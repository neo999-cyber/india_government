## A3.2 How much capacity was reclassified, and what it does to a headline

From CEA *General Review 2025* Table 1.2 (retrieved this run — see A1.0 for the URL and retrieval evidence), the position at **31.03.2019**, the year-end immediately after the decision:

| column | MW |
|---|---|
| Hydro (large) | 45,399 |
| Thermal | 226,279 |
| Nuclear | 6,780 |
| RES (excl. large hydro) | 79,412 |
| Total | 357,871 |

**The reclassification moved 45,399 MW across the line in one instrument, with no construction.** Arithmetic, operand by operand:
- "Renewable capacity" before the decision: 79,412 MW → 79,412 ÷ 357,871 × 100 = **22.19%** of installed capacity.
- "Renewable capacity" after: 79,412 + 45,399 = 124,811 MW → 124,811 ÷ 357,871 × 100 = **34.88%**.
- The single decision therefore added **45,399 MW** to the renewable total, a **+57.17%** increase on the pre-existing renewable base (45,399 ÷ 79,412 × 100), and **+12.69 percentage points** to the renewable share of installed capacity, in one day, without a turbine being installed.

**But — and this is the finding that stops the obvious inference — the reclassification also restates the base year.** The relevant test is whether an announced *growth* figure spans the change on mixed bases. Checked against the shipped record L-0052, whose summary reads "Renewable capacity rose from 76.38 GW in March 2014 to 274.68 GW by March 2026":
- CEA GR Table 1.2 at 31.03.2014: RES 35,850 MW + Hydro 40,531 MW = **76,381 MW = 76.38 GW.** The record's 2014 baseline is already on the post-2019 (hydro-inclusive) basis.
- Derived endpoint on the same basis: 31.03.2024 RES+Hydro = 143,645 + 46,928 = 190,573 MW; plus FY2024-25 additions (RE incl. SHP 28,723.67 + Hydro large 799.99 = 29,523.66) = 220,096.66 MW at 31.03.2025; plus FY2025-26 additions (50,905.25 + 3,620 = 54,525.25) = **274,621.91 MW ≈ 274.62 GW at 31.03.2026** — against the record's 274.68 GW. (Capacity-addition figures from CEA *Executive Summary March 2026*, section 2, retrieved this run.) The ~60 MW residual is within the provisional-vs-final margin the source itself flags with an asterisk.

So **L-0052's growth figure is internally consistent: both endpoints are on the hydro-inclusive basis, and the reclassification does not inflate its ratio.** What it does do is make the 2014 figure a *retrospective restatement*: 76.38 GW was not a number anyone in March 2014 would have called renewable capacity, because large hydro was not then renewable. The officially published renewable capacity at 31.03.2014 was 35,850 MW.

**A counter-intuitive consequence worth recording, since it cuts against the pattern rather than for it:** on the narrow (pre-2019) basis the growth multiple is *larger*, not smaller. 35,850 MW (31.03.2014) → 236,525 MW ("Wind, Solar & Other RE", 30.06.2026) = **6.60×**. On the wide basis, 76,381 → 288,589 = **3.78×**. The reclassification raises the *level* and the *share* while lowering the *growth multiple*. Anyone choosing a basis to maximise a headline would therefore choose differently depending on which headline they wanted — which is itself evidence against a simple "chosen for the headline" story.

## A3.6 The standing "reclassified headline" promotion test — verdict on evidence

The instrument holds two instances of a reclassified-headline pattern (state roads notified as national highways; heliports counted as airports under UDAN) and has declined to write a record on two, setting a promotion test: **a third instance in a third sector, OR one instance shown to have been chosen for the headline.** Assessed on what I retrieved, not on what would be convenient.

**Does it qualify as a third instance in a third sector?** Yes on sector — electricity generation is neither roads nor aviation. And structurally the move is the same shape: an existing, already-built stock (45,399 MW of large hydro at 31.03.2019) is redefined into a target category without construction, and the category's headline total rises 57.17% overnight.

**Is it shown to have been chosen for the headline?** **No.** Stating this precisely: *the documents I retrieved contain no statement of a headline motive, and I located no document that states one.* What the announcing primary (PIB 1567817) actually gives as its purpose is, in its own words, a market-and-viability package — "DISOMS are reluctant sign Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) Hydro Power due to higher tariff", answered with an HPO inside the non-solar RPO, a 40-year project life, an 18-year debt repayment period, a 2% escalating tariff, and budgetary support for flood moderation and enabling infrastructure at stated rupee-per-MW caps. Scanner needle, so this is checkable: `node tools/scan-text.mjs pib_1567817.txt target renewable capacity headline achieve --variants` returns `headline: 0`, `achieve: 0`, `target: 0 (variant "targets": 3)` — and all three "targets" hits are the HPO trajectory, not a renewable-capacity target. `capacity: 12`, of which the only target-adjacent hit is the *context* sentence "160 GW capacity addition by 2022 from infirm sources of power like solar and wind", used as an argument for grid stability, not as a total to be topped up.

**Three findings that cut AGAINST reading this as a headline move, which I record because they weaken the case I might otherwise want to make:**

1. **The reclassification cannot help the biggest headline in this field.** The NDC limb and the 500 GW target are stated in **non-fossil** capacity (see A4). Large hydro was *always* non-fossil — it is not a fossil fuel and never was. Moving it from "conventional" to "renewable" changes nothing whatever in the non-fossil numerator or denominator. If the object were the 40%-by-2030 target that the same PIB release names in its own Background paragraph ("40% of the total capacity from non-fossil fuel sources by 2030 to honour its Nationally Determined Contribution"), this instrument would be useless for it. That is a strong structural argument against a headline motive, and it comes from the announcing document itself.
2. **The reclassification lowers the growth multiple even as it raises the level** (A3.2): 6.60× on the narrow basis versus 3.78× on the wide one, 2014 to 2026. A party optimising for a headline has to pick which headline, and the two point opposite ways.
3. **CEA's principal statistical series did not adopt the new definition.** *General Review 2025*, published six years after the decision, still keeps Hydro in a column of its own and still defines RES to exclude it. So the reclassification did not propagate into the authority's core historical series at all. Contrast the highways case as the instrument frames it, where the renamed stock is absorbed into the headline length series. Here the headline series is unchanged and only the summary presentations moved — and even those (CEA's Executive Summary) publish both bases side by side rather than switching.

**What it IS, on the evidence:** a **definitional** reclassification with an operative regulatory purpose — the creation of a Hydro Purchase Obligation inside the non-solar RPO, notified with a nine-year trajectory (2021-22 to 2029-30) on 29.01.2021, which places a binding purchase duty on distribution companies that did not exist before. It is not jurisdictional: no transfer of ownership, funding responsibility or administrative control accompanies it (the retrieved primaries describe none). It has a real market consequence independent of any counting effect.

**Recommendation to the authoring stage, stated as a recommendation and not a conclusion:** the sector limb of the promotion test is met; the "chosen for the headline" limb is not met and, on the structural argument in point 1, is affirmatively hard to sustain. If the test is read disjunctively this promotes; if the intended standard is that a third instance must be of the *same kind* as the first two, this one differs materially on point 3 — the headline series did not move. **Do not stretch it.** The honest formulation available from these documents is narrower and, I think, more interesting than the pattern it was tested against: *the same government publishes, concurrently, at least four incompatible boundaries for the word "renewable", swinging the answer by up to 52,065 MW or 9.5 BU a month, and does not designate a preferred one.* That is a finding about ambiguity that is left standing, not about a headline that was moved.
