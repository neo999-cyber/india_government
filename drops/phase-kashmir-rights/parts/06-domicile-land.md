## 1. THE DOMICILE INSTRUMENT — a definitional replacement, not a numerical change

### 1.1 Where "domicile" actually lives — and it is not where most readers assume

The word "domicile" was not introduced by a land law, a residency law, or a citizenship law. It was inserted
**into a recruitment statute**: the **Jammu and Kashmir Civil Services (Decentralization and Recruitment) Act
(Act No. XVI of 2010)**, at item 14 of the schedule to `S.O. 1229(E)` (R1). That single fact governs everything
in §2.

`S.O. 1229(E)` is made "In exercise of the powers conferred by section 96 of the Jammu and Kashmir
Reorganisation Act, 2019 (34 of 2019)". Its paragraph 1(2) reads "It shall come into force at once".

### 1.2 The definition, verbatim from the gazette (R1, pp. 52–54)

First, the hook. Item 14, section 2 of the 2010 Act:

> "(i) After clause (c), insert –– '(ca) "Domicile" means Domicile as defined in section 3A of this Act.'"

Then the new **section 3A**, whose marginal heading is itself the answer to the "what does it attach to"
question — **"Domicile for purposes of appointment to any service in Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir"**:

> "3A. (1) Any person who fulfils the following conditions shall be deemed to be a domicile of the Union
> territory of Jammu and Kashmir **for the purposes of appointment to any post carrying a pay scale of not more
> than Level-4 (25500)** under the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir or under a local or other authority
> (other than cantonment board) within the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir:-
> (a) who has **resided for a period of fifteen years** in the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir **or** has
> **studied for a period of seven years and appeared in Class 10th/12th examination** in an educational
> institution located in the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir; or
> (b) who is **registered as a migrant by the Relief and Rehabilitation Commissioner (Migrants)** in the Union
> territory of Jammu and Kashmir.
>   (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), following persons shall be deemed to be domicile
> under sub-section (1):-
> (a) **children of those Central Government Officials, All India Services Officers, Officials of Public Sector
> Undertaking and Autonomous body of Central Government, Public Sector Banks, Officials of Statutory bodies,
> Officials of Central Universities and recognised Research institutes of Central Government who have served in
> Jammu and Kashmir for a total period of ten years**; or
> (b) children of parents who fulfil any of the conditions in sub-section (1); or
> (c) children of such residents of Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir as reside outside Union territory of
> Jammu and Kashmir in connection with their employment or business or other professional or vocational reasons
> but their parents fulfil any of the conditions provided in sub-section (1)."

**All four limbs the brief asked for are confirmed verbatim: 15-year residence, 7-year study + Class 10/12
examination, migrant registration, and children of central-government officers with a qualifying period of
ten years.**

**A fifth limb the brief did not name, and it is not in the March order.** `S.O. 3808(E)` of **26 October 2020**
(R2), at item 25 of its schedule, adds:

> "Section 3A. –– After sub-section (2), insert:- '(3) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1),
> **spouse of a domicile shall also be deemed to be a domicile** for the purposes of sub-section (1)'."

So the spouse limb is seven months younger than the rest and sits in a *different* order from the one everyone
cites. Any authored account of the domicile criteria that stops at the March 2020 order is incomplete.

**Machinery, verbatim (R1, p. 54), new section 3B:**

> "3B. (1) **Tehsildar** within his territorial jurisdiction shall be the Competent Authority for issuance of
> domicile certificate: Provided that the Government of Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir may notify any
> other officer to be Competent Authority… (3) Any person aggrieved by the order of the Competent Authority may
> file appeal before the concerned **Deputy Commissioner** who shall decide appeal… within a period of **sixty
> days**."

**The eligibility rule, verbatim (R1, p. 54), new section 5A:**

> "5A. Subject to the provisions of this Act, no person shall be eligible for appointment to a post carrying a
> pay scale of not more than Level-4 (25500) unless he is a domicile of the Union territory of Jammu and
> Kashmir."

**Three days later this was widened.** Per R7, MHA to Lok Sabha, 20 September 2020, verbatim: *"The Government
has notified the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation (Adaptation of State Laws) Second Order, 2020 on 03.04.2020
vide which Domicile norms for purposes of appointment to **any post** in Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir
had been notified."* The substitution of "any post" for the Level-4 cap is **RELAYED (L1)** as to its exact
words. **Stage 3 must render the March 2020 text and the April 2020 widening as two dated states of the same
provision, not one.** The three-day gap is itself the finding: the first published version of the rule
guaranteed domiciles only the bottom four pay levels.

### 1.3 The replacement of "permanent resident" — established from the deletions, not from the old text

I could not retrieve section 6 of the J&K Constitution or the State Subject notifications of 1927 and 1932
(L6). **I therefore establish what "permanent resident" attached to the only way I honestly can: by reading
the list of places from which the two Adaptation Orders delete the phrase.** That is direct primary evidence of
the old status's reach, and it is stronger than a paraphrase of a text I do not hold.

**In `S.O. 1229(E)` (31 March 2020) the phrase "permanent resident" is deleted or replaced in exactly two
statutes, and the two do different things:**

| Statute | Provision | Operation, verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| J&K Civil Services (Decentralization and Recruitment) Act 2010 | s.6(i) and s.7(i) | "for '**permanent resident of the State**' substitute '**Domicile of Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir**'"; and in each, "Omit clause (ii)" |
| **J&K Property Rights to Slum Dwellers Act 2012** | s.2(28) and s.3(1) | "omit '**who is a permanent resident of the State of Jammu and Kashmir and**'" — with **no substitution of "domicile"** |

**In `S.O. 3808(E)` (26 October 2020) the phrase is deleted from the J&K Development Act 1970 at three
points — and again nothing is put in its place:**

| Provision | Operation, verbatim |
|---|---|
| Development Act 1970, s.2(da) | "omit '**being permanent resident of the State**'" |
| Development Act 1970, s.2(ea) | "omit '**being permanent resident of the State**'" |
| Development Act 1970, **s.17(1)** | "In sub-section (1), omit '**permanent resident of the State**'" |

**This is the whole finding in one table.** Where the old status governed *employment*, it was **substituted**
by domicile. Where it governed *property* — slum-dwellers' property rights in 2020, and the Development
Authority's power to dispose of land in 2020 — it was **deleted and not replaced by anything**.

**"Permanent resident" and "domicile" are therefore not two values of one variable. They are two different
objects:**

| | Permanent resident (pre-5 Aug 2019) | Domicile (from 31 Mar 2020) |
|---|---|---|
| Source of the definition | The State's own constitutional instrument (not retrieved here) | A **recruitment Act**, section 3A, inserted by a **Union executive order** |
| Author | The State legislature / the State's constituent instrument | The Central Government under s.96, J&K Reorganisation Act 2019 |
| Stated purpose | not established from primary text here | expressly "**for the purposes of appointment to any post**" |
| Attaches to immovable property | **Yes** — evidenced by the deletions above | **No** — see §2 |
| Attaches to public employment | Yes | Yes |
| Certifying authority | not established here | **Tehsildar**, appeal to Deputy Commissioner in 60 days |

**No series bridges the two.** There is no instrument anywhere in this material that reports permanent
residents and domiciles on one basis, and the qualifying conditions differ, so a count of one is not a
restatement of a count of the other. Any "number of permanent residents → number of domiciles" line is a splice
across a definitional replacement and must not be drawn.

## 2. WHAT DOMICILE ATTACHES TO — the point both cases turn on, settled from the gazette

**Domicile governs eligibility for public employment. It does not govern land purchase. Nothing in either
Adaptation Order makes domicile a condition of buying land.** I establish this three ways, all primary.

**(i) The definition is expressly purpose-limited.** s.3A(1): "shall be deemed to be a domicile … **for the
purposes of appointment to any post**". s.5A is about "eligible for appointment". The marginal headings are
"Domicile for purposes of appointment to any service" and "Requirement of domicile for being eligible for
certain posts". The statute it sits in is a recruitment Act.

**(ii) The land law that replaced the old regime uses a different gate word: "agriculturist".**
`S.O. 3808(E)` inserts a new **section 133-H into the J&K Land Revenue Act, Samvat 1996**, with the marginal
heading **"Transfer of land to non-agriculturist barred"**, verbatim:

> "133-H.— (1) Save as provided in this Act,- (a) no sale (including sale in execution of a decree of a Civil
> court or for recovery of arrears of land revenue…), gift or exchange, or (b) no mortgage of any land or
> interest therein, in which the possession of mortgaged property is delivered to the mortgagee, or (c) no
> agreement made by instrument in writing for the sale, gift, exchange, or mortgage of the land **shall be valid
> in favour of a person who is not an agriculturist**: Provided that **the Government or an officer authorized by
> it in this behalf may grant permission to an agriculturist to alienate the land to a non-agriculturist** by way
> of sale, gift, exchange or mortgage or for such agreement on such conditions as may be prescribed…"

and it defines the gate:

> "Explanation: For the purpose of this section, the expression- (a) '**agriculturist**' means a person:- (i) who
> **cultivates land personally in the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir as on such date as may be notified by
> the Government**; or (ii) **such category of persons as may be notified by the Government from time to time**.
> (b) '**land**' means the land recorded as agricultural land including village abadideh, by whatever name
> called, in the revenue records being used for agriculture purposes or for purposes subservient to agriculture
> or for pasture or **being used for residential purposes in rural areas**."

**The word "domicile" appears nowhere in 133-H.** The protected category is occupational, not personal-status:
somebody who personally cultivates land in J&K. And the second limb of the definition — "such category of
persons as may be notified by the Government from time to time" — means **the Government can enlarge the class
of "agriculturists" by notification**, without amending anything.

The carve-outs sit immediately after, and they are wide:

> "133-I. …the Government may, by notification in the official Gazette, allow transfer of land … in favour of
> eligible **public trust** established under law for **charitable purpose** and which is non-profitable in nature."
> "133-J. …in favour of a person or an institution for the purpose of promotion of **healthcare or senior
> secondary or higher or specialized education**…"
> "133-K. …in favour of **any person, institution or corporation, for such industrial or commercial or housing
> purposes or agricultural purposes or any other public purpose** as may be notified by the Government for
> industrial and commercial development of the union territory."

with a use-condition:

> "133-L. Any non-agriculturist in whose favour land has been transferred … shall, **irrespective of transfer of
> such land, continue to be a non-agriculturist** … and he shall **put the land to such use for which land has
> been transferred within a period of five years** … (2) In case such person fails to put the land for its
> intended use or diverts … the land so purchased by him shall vest in the Government free from all
> encumbrances."

And a commencement hook that stage 3 must not miss: **133-H(9) — "The provision of this section shall come into
effect from a date to be notified by the Government in the Official Gazette."** Whether and when that date was
notified is **NOT ESTABLISHED here** (the J&K gazette is unreachable). So the only surviving restriction on
agricultural-land transfer is one whose commencement I cannot confirm.

**(iii) Non-agricultural land is simply outside the bar.** 133-H bites only on land "recorded as agricultural
land". The Development Act 1970's own permanent-resident condition on disposal (s.17(1)) was **deleted with no
replacement**. So for non-agricultural land there is no residence-based, domicile-based or status-based
restriction left in the retrieved text at all.

**(iv) The Government's own answers to Parliament are consistent with this and never say otherwise.** Twice —
R11 (RS \*250, 11 Aug 2021) and R14 (LS 3716, 21 Dec 2021) — an MP asked in terms whether "**no domicile or
permanent resident certificate is required to purchase non-agricultural land in the UT**". Both times the
answer neither confirmed nor denied it, and instead said only, verbatim:

> "As per the adapted land laws of Jammu and Kashmir, the Government may, by notification in the official
> gazette, allow transfer of land, for public purposes such as education, charitable purpose and healthcare."

**That answer describes 133-I and 133-J — the discretionary carve-outs — and says nothing about the general
rule.** A reader of the reply alone would come away believing land transfer to outsiders requires a government
notification for a public purpose. The gazette says the general rule for non-agricultural land is that there is
no such requirement. **The reply is not false; it is non-responsive, twice, to the same question. That is a
first-class finding and a strong candidate for the L-0092 shape** (a presentational finding — see §9).

**The single sentence stage 3 should carry:** *domicile is an employment qualification; land purchase in J&K
is gated by the agriculturist/non-agriculturist distinction for agricultural land and by nothing at all for
non-agricultural land; conflating the two is the most common error in this subject and both the government's
case and its critics' case become incoherent if it is made.*

### 3.3 The two quantities the brief asked to be kept apart — and where the second actually lives

necessarily contains at least:
- persons qualifying under the **migrant limb**, 3A(1)(b), many of whom were permanent residents;
- **children of central-government officials** under 3A(2)(a) — some of whom were, some of whom were not;
- **children of parents** who qualify, 3A(2)(b) and (c) — a derivative route from an existing PRC family;
- persons who were PRC-eligible but held no PRC in their own name (the reply's phrase is "dependents/**holders**
  of erstwhile Permanent Resident Certificates", so the denominator is certificate-holding, not entitlement);
- and, yes, persons with no prior claim at all.

**Treating 1,22,671 as "outsiders granted domicile" would be exactly the laundering this instrument exists to
prevent. Stage 3 must not author it as a figure.**

**But Quantity B was eventually answered — by a different legislature, five years later.** Per **L5 (RELAYED,
T4)**: in **April 2025**, in the restored J&K Legislative Assembly, the Revenue Minister (the portfolio is held

granted to "non-state subjects"** in the preceding two years, out of roughly **35.12 lakh** issued in that

did not disaggregate in five years of parliamentary answers was produced within months of the J&K Assembly
resuming. It is therefore **not `not-collected`** — the holder plainly had it. See §6.

**Three cautions on the 83,742, all of which stage 3 must carry:**
1. It is **T4, relayed** — the assembly reply itself is unreachable (§0.3).
2. "Non-state subjects" is **the reply's own term, and it is a pre-2019 term**. Whether it means "persons who
   held no PRC", "persons who would not have qualified for a PRC", or something narrower is **not established**.

3. The stated period is "**the past two years**" — i.e. roughly 2023–2025, **not** the period since 2020. So it

## 4. LAND LAW — what was repealed, on what authority, and what replaced it

### 4.1 A correction to the brief's premise, and it matters for citation

The brief refers to "the J&K Reorganisation (Adaptation of Central Laws) Third Order 2020, dated 26 October
2020" as the land instrument. **The land changes are not in that order.** The gazette issue of 26 October 2020
(R2) contains **two** orders, back to back in one file:

- **`S.O. 3807(E)` — UT of J&K Reorganisation (Adaptation of CENTRAL Laws) Third Order, 2020.** Its schedule is
  three central Acts: the **Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016**; the **SARFAESI Act**; and the
  **Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973**. (That RERA is being adapted into J&K in the same breath as the land
  repeals is itself worth carrying.)
- **`S.O. 3808(E)` — UT of J&K Reorganisation (Adaptation of STATE Laws) FIFTH Order, 2020.** Its schedule is
  **26 State Acts**, and **this is where every land repeal and every land amendment sits.**

Widely-repeated press accounts ("the Third Order repealed 12 of 26 laws") merge the two. **Stage 3 must cite
`S.O. 3808(E)`, the Fifth Order (State Laws), for the land changes.**

### 4.2 The authority, and its expiry date — verbatim from the Act (R3)

Section 96, J&K Reorganisation Act 2019 (OCR of the MHA-hosted scan, cleaned of obvious character errors and
marked as such):

> "96. For the purpose of facilitating the application in relation to the successor Union Territories, of any
> law made before the appointed day, as detailed in Fifth Schedule, the Central Government may, **before the
> expiration of one year from that day**, by order, make such adaptations and modifications of the law, **whether
> by way of repeal or amendment**, as may be necessary or expedient; and thereupon every such law shall have
> effect subject to the adaptations and modifications so made **until altered, repealed or amended by a competent
> Legislature or other competent authority**."

**Two dates follow arithmetically and both are load-bearing.** The appointed day is 31 October 2019. The power
expired one year later. **The land orders are dated 26 October 2020 — five days before the power lapsed.** The
repeal of a body of land law dating to 1936–1976 was executed in the last week of a one-year executive window.
That is a fact about the *process*, and it belongs to the caseAgainst at full strength. Equally, the closing
words — "until altered, repealed or amended by a competent Legislature" — belong to the caseFor at full
strength, and they are the reason the April/October 2025 assembly answers (§3.3, §5.3) exist at all.

### 4.3 What `S.O. 3808(E)` repealed outright — verbatim "Repeal as a whole"

Ten of its 26 items are total repeals, and eight of the ten are land statutes:

| Item | Act | Vintage |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **The Jammu and Kashmir Alienation of Land Act** | V of Samvat 1995 (1938) |
| 4 | **The Jammu and Kashmir Big Landed Estates Abolition Act** | XVII of Samvat 2007 (1950) |
| 5 | The Jammu and Kashmir Common Lands (Regulation) Act, 1956 | XXIV of 1956 |
| 6 | The Jammu and Kashmir Consolidation of Holdings Act, 1962 | V of 1962 |
| 12 | The Jammu and Kashmir Prevention of Fragmentation of Agricultural Holdings Act | XXV of 1960 |
| 13 | The Jammu and Kashmir Prohibition on Conversion of Land and Alienation of Orchards Act | VIII of 1975 |
| 14 | **The Jammu and Kashmir Right of Prior Purchase Act** | II of Svt. 1993 (1936 A.D.) |
| 15 | The Jammu and Kashmir Tenancy (Stay of Ejectment Proceedings) Act | XXXIII of 1966 |
| 16 | The Jammu and Kashmir Utilization of Lands Act | IX of Samvat 2010 (1953) |
| 17 | The Jammu and Kashmir Underground Public Utilities (Acquisition of Rights of User in Land) Act | IV of 2014 |

A **second, overlapping** repeal list is inserted into the Land Revenue Act itself as a new **section 140**,
which adds two Acts not in the schedule list — the **Land Improvement Schemes Act 1972** and the **J&K Tenancy
Act, Svt. 1980**. Section 140(2) required the Board of Revenue to ensure "**final adjudication by the 31st
March, 2021** of all the residual matters on fast track basis", by negotiation, mediation, conciliation,
arbitration or Lok Adalat "where no Government interest is involved". New **section 140-A** allowed
regularisation of unauthorised occupation arising from the repeals at "not less than the fifty percent of the
circle rate", with a hard stop: "**no such regularization shall be permitted after 31st December, 2021**".

**Whether either deadline was met is NOT ESTABLISHED. No instrument I retrieved reports it, and the J&K
Board of Revenue is unreachable (§0.3). Flag it: two statutory deadlines with no published outturn.**

### 4.4 The Land Grants Act — not repealed, restructured

Item 9 keeps the **J&K Land Grants Act (XXXVIII of 1960)** but converts a governmental power into a delegable
rule-making power. Verbatim: in s.4(1), "substitute '**may grant land on lease**' with '**may make rules for
grant of land on lease**'", omit the first and last provisos, and insert a new s.4(3):

> "(3) The Government may notify the **competent authority or authorities** for granting lease under sub-section
> (1) to different categories of persons for such purposes and in such areas as may be specified…"

Leasing is therefore now governed by rules and notified competent authorities. **The rules themselves are not
retrieved** (J&K gazette unreachable).

### 4.5 The Development Act 1970 — the removal, and the Strategic Areas power

Beyond the three deletions of "permanent resident" (§1.3), item 7 of `S.O. 3808(E)` does four further things
that stage 3 should treat as a single package:

**(a) Strategic Areas — verbatim, new s.3(3):**

> "(3) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, the Government may **on the written request of an Army
> officer not below the rank of Corp Commander**, declare an area as **Strategic Area** within a local area, **only
> for direct operational and training requirements of armed forces**, which may be **excluded from the operation
> of this Act and rules/regulations made thereunder** in the manner and to the extent specified in the
> declaration and **the Government may satisfy itself about the reasons cited** for declaring the area as
> strategic area and will have such area notified accordingly with such conditions as may be required."

and a new definition, s.2(m): "'Strategic Areas' means an area notified as Strategic Area under sub-section (3)
of section 3 of this Act."

**On the brief's question "who designates":** the answer from the text is **two-step**. The **Army initiates**,
in writing, at not below Corps Commander rank ("Corp Commander" is the gazette's own spelling). The
**Government of the UT declares**, and the text imposes on it a satisfaction duty ("may satisfy itself about the
reasons cited"). **The armed forces do not themselves declare, and the text does not authorise "direct
construction" by them in terms** — what it does is *exclude the notified area from the Development Act and its
rules*, i.e. from master-plan, zonal-plan and development-permission control. That is the accurate reading and
it is narrower than the common paraphrase; stage 3 should use the narrower one.

A consequential amendment confirms the reach: item 19 amends the **J&K Control of Building Operations Act
1988**, s.3(2)(b), to read "in the Municipal Area **excluding any strategic area notified under the Jammu and
Kashmir Development Act, 1970**". So a strategic area is also outside municipal building control.

**Whether any Strategic Area has been notified, where, or how large, is NOT ESTABLISHED. No figure exists in
anything I retrieved.** See §6, absence D5.

**(b) Master plans override agricultural-conversion law — verbatim, new s.11A(1):**

> "Upon coming into operation of the master plan or a zonal plan, the land use permitted in the area covered
> thereunder shall only be as provided in terms of such master or zonal plan. **The provisions of the Jammu and
> Kashmir Agrarian Reforms Act, 1976, Jammu and Kashmir Land Revenue Act, Samvat 1996 or any other law for the
> time being in force requiring any permission to change the usage of any land, shall not be applicable** to any
> land so covered."

**This is the quiet one.** The agricultural-conversion protections re-enacted as Land Revenue Act s.133-A are
switched off inside any area covered by a master or zonal plan. The protective regime and the planning regime
were amended in the same order, and the planning regime wins.

**(c) A new industrial machinery.** A whole new **Chapter VII-A, "Jammu and Kashmir Industrial Development
Corporation"** (ss.22-A to beyond 22-G) is inserted into the Development Act: a body corporate able "to
acquire, hold and dispose of property, both movable and immovable", twelve Directors of whom **five official
Directors plus four more are nominated by the J&K Government** and who "hold office **during the pleasure of
the Government**", and machinery for industrial areas, industrial estates and commercial centres.

**(d) Land acquisition modernised.** Throughout, "Land Acquisition Act, 1990" is replaced by "the **Right to
Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 (30 of
2013)**" (Development Act ss.2(l), 16; Evacuees' Property Act ss.14-A, 14-B). This is a genuine strengthening
of compensation and consent standards and belongs to the caseFor.

### 4.6 The Agrarian Reforms Act 1976 — kept, and given a transfer bar of its own

Item 2 retains the Act (the government's stated reason for repealing the Big Landed Estates Abolition Act — see
§4.7) but substitutes s.28-A, verbatim:

> "28-A.— (1) Save as otherwise provided in this section, **no person who is vested with ownership rights in land
> under this Act shall transfer such land or rights therein in any manner whatsoever to any person other than
> Government or its agencies and instrumentalities**; Provided that nothing in this sub-section shall prohibit:
> (a) transfer of such land ownership whereof has been vested in a prospective owner under section 8 **after the
> expiry of fifteen years** from the date such land has been vested in the State under section 4 of the Act
> subject to the provisions of sections 133-H, 133-I, 133-J, 133-K and 133-L of the Jammu and Kashmir Land
> Revenue Act…; (b) grant of lease…; (c) entering into **Contract Farming**…; (d) transfer of land in the form of
> simple mortgage for securing loan for purposes of improvement of the land. (2) Any transfer … in
> contravention of sub-section (1) shall be **null and void** … be dispossessed … by a Revenue Officer not below
> the rank of Tehsildar and the land shall vest in the State…"

It also inserts s.7(8): "**On and from such date as may be notified by the Government, no application for
resumption of land shall be entertained under this section**" — a shutdown switch on resumption, with the date
left to the executive. **Whether that date has been notified is NOT ESTABLISHED.**

### 4.7 The government's own account of the repeal, verbatim (R11, RS \*250, 11 August 2021)

> "(b) & (c): Under the Jammu and Kashmir Big Landed Estates Abolition Act, 1950, the land was transferred to
> the tillers. The Act also prescribed a limit to right of ownership of **182 standard kanals (22.75 acres)**.
> However, this Act has been repealed and there is another legislation i.e. The Jammu & Kashmir Agrarian Reforms
> Act, 1976, for transfer of land to tillers which is in force. This Act prescribes ceiling restriction of **100
> standard kanals (12.5 acres)**."

Arithmetic checks: 182 ÷ 8 = 22.75 and 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5, so the reply's own conversion implies **1 acre = 8
standard kanals**, consistently. **This is the government's strongest single argument on land and stage 3 must
carry it at full strength: the repealed Act's ceiling was *looser* than the surviving Act's, so on the ceiling
measure the protective regime was not weakened by the repeal.**

### 4.8 The Land Revenue Act, otherwise

`S.O. 3808(E)` item 11 is the largest single item in the order (~33,600 characters). Besides 133-A to 133-L it:
creates a **Board of Revenue** (new s.5A) chaired ex officio by the Financial Commissioner Revenue with two
Secretary-rank members appointed by the UT Government; inserts **Chapter X-A "Land Leasing"** (s.119-B:
"Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law, the Government may through a notification … formulate
procedures and conditions for **leasing in or leasing out of agricultural land**"); inserts **Chapter X-B
Regional Planning**; defines "**Agricultural land**", "**Agriculture and Allied Activities**" and "**Occupant**"
for the first time; converts the tenancy vocabulary ("tenant", "protected tenant", "occupancy tenant") into
"**occupant**" throughout; and inserts **s.133-C(5)**, an ouster clause, verbatim:

> "Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force, **nothing done or purporting
> to have been done by the Collector, or the officer authorised by him** in this behalf, under sections 133-A,
> 133-B, 133-BB or 133-C **shall be called in question in any Court and no Court shall entertain any suit or
> proceeding or grant any injunction** with respect to any action or thing or any matter for which a proceeding
> has been taken under the said provisions."

**A civil-court ouster inserted by executive order into the land-conversion enforcement chain is a governance
finding in its own right and should not be lost inside the land-transfer story.**

### 5.2 The arithmetic, shown, including where it fails

- **1 + 57 + 127 = 185.** R19 internally consistent. ✔
- **378 + 253 = 631.** ✔ **Rs 90.48 cr + Rs 39.49 cr = Rs 129.97 cr.** ✔
- **Area reconciles exactly at 20 marla = 1 kanal:** 212k 13m + 173k 7m = 385 kanal 20 marla = **386 kanal**, plus

- **The relayed year series does NOT reconcile.** The two outlets between them give 2020 = 1, 2021 = 57, 2022 =

### 5.3 The unit is not stable, and neither is the level

**Four units in five answers: properties → plots → persons → persons → persons + area + value.** A count of
plots, a count of persons and an area are three different objects. The brief's warning is exactly right, and
the numbers show why it matters: **"11 transactions" and "N hectares" are different arguments** — and until
October 2025 **no Indian instrument of any kind published the area at all.**

**And the levels do not reconcile across instruments:**
- August 2021: **2 persons since August 2019**.
- April 2023: **57 persons in calendar 2021 alone**.

Both are official; both are sourced to the same producer, the J&K administration. Either the 2021 answer's
"properties" is a much narrower object than the 2023 answer's "land", or the base was silently revised, or the
registration data caught up. **No document says which. This is an unannounced restatement of exactly the kind
phase 11 documented in the MHA incident table (break 1), and it should be recorded as one.**

**Two further non-comparabilities:**
- The December 2021 answer (7 plots) **states no period at all.** It is a stock with no clock.
- The August 2021 and March 2022 answers use "**after abrogation of Article 370**" / "**since August, 2019**",
  while the April 2023 answer uses calendar years starting **2020**. So the 2019 stub is inside two answers and
  outside a third.

### 5.4 The definitional trap in "outside the UT of Jammu and Kashmir" — and it is severe

**31 October 2019 changed who counts as an outsider.** Before that date, a person from Kargil or Leh was a
resident of the same State and, if a permanent resident, held the same land rights as a person from Srinagar or
Jammu. After it, Ladakh is a separate Union territory, and **a Kargili buying land in Kashmir is now, by
construction, a "person from outside the UT of Jammu and Kashmir."**

Per **L4 (RELAYED, single outlet, T4 — treat with care)**, roughly **350 of the 631** counted non-resident
buyers are **Ladakhis, chiefly from Kargil**, with the remainder from Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra,
Punjab, Jharkhand, Delhi, Bihar and one from West Bengal. **If that holds, a clear majority of the headline
"outsiders" figure consists of people from the erstwhile same State, reclassified by an administrative-unit
change rather than by any movement of population.**

**This is the phase's periodisation rule biting directly on a headline number, and stage 3 must carry it as a
definitional caution on the series whether or not it hardens the 350.** Note also that the same reclassification
runs the other way: R19 records **zero** outside purchases in Ladakh, and after 31 October 2019 a Kashmiri
buying in Leh would also be an "outsider".

### 5.5 What the Union publishes about government land — and the asymmetry that exposes

The Union does publish land quantities for J&K, in hectares, district by district — just not for private
transfers to non-residents.

**R17 (RS US Q.384, 20 July 2022): State land acquired by the Government** (Khalsa Sarkar, Kahchrie, Shamilat
etc.) "for various public purposes such as roads, including National Highways, railways, schools/colleges,
playgrounds, parks, buildings, soil waste management, **border fencing/border out posts**, industrial estates
etc.", **district-wise for all 20 districts** across 2019-20, 2020-21 and 2021-22 (to June).

- MHA's **stated total: 2,359.45 hectares**.
- **The table does not sum to it.** Adding the 20 district rows gives 2019-20 = **1,071.40**, 2020-21 =
  **574.55**, 2021-22 (to June) = **712.40**, total **2,358.35 hectares** — **1.10 hectares short of the stated
  total**. I recomputed this and it is not a transcription slip on my side. **Small, but it is an arithmetic
  defect on the face of an official reply and belongs in the record.**

Same reply, **Roshni Act 2001**: "A total of State land measuring **8,565.40 hectares** was transferred under
Roshni Act 2001 to individuals, institutions and entrepreneurs", and after the High Court judgment of
**09.10.2020** the mutations were cancelled and "**Land measuring 1,363.95 hectares has been retrieved**", with
"more than 70 review petitions" filed and the matter sub judice.

**R18 (RS US Q.391, 20 July 2022):** "Land measuring **331.90 hectare** has been acquired by the **Central
Government or its agencies** for various projects like national highways etc. **in Kashmir** from August 2019
till 13/7/2022", under the National Highways Act 1956 and the RFCTLARR Act 2013, with a district-wise table of
**pending compensation totalling Rs 111.016 crore** (Pulwama 8.656, Baramulla 19.00, Ganderbal 46.17, Anantnag
37.19).

**The asymmetry, stated plainly for stage 3:** in hectares, over roughly the same window, the state acquired
**2,359 ha** and central agencies **332 ha** in Kashmir alone, while the entire published private transfer to
non-residents is **19.5 ha** across the whole UT over six years. **The quantity that dominates the argument is
not the quantity that dominates the land.** Both cases can use this; neither can ignore it.

## 8. THE TWO TRAPS FROM PHASE 11 — where each bites

### Trap (1) — a source that looks independent but is media-derived from official reporting is not a check

**It bites at the single most load-bearing point in the whole subject.** The figures of 631 non-residents, 386
kanal, Rs 129.97 crore and 83,742 domicile certificates to non-state subjects — the two quantities that would
settle the argument — reach this instrument **only through Greater Kashmir, Kashmir Life and ETV Bharat, and
every one of those outlets is reporting an official reply it did not produce.** Two outlets agreeing is not two
sources; it is one source read twice.

**Say it in the required words: these press accounts are media-derived from official reporting and are not a
check on it.** They are T4 and must render T4. The only thing that would upgrade them is the J&K Assembly's own
record, which is unreachable from here.

The same trap, one level down: **there is no non-official land-transfer or domicile dataset for J&K anywhere in
this material.** Not an NGO series, not an academic panel, not a registry scrape. The subject has exactly one
producer.

### Trap (2) — check who PRODUCES the series a ministry presents as its own

**It bites on every Union figure in §3 and §5, and the ministry says so itself.** Every domicile and land
figure MHA has given Parliament carries the preface, verbatim: "**As per the information provided by the
Government of Jammu & Kashmir**" (R4, R5, R6, R11, R12, R13, R14, R16, R17, R18, R19).

**MHA is a conduit, not a producer.** And the producer is the J&K administration, which since 31 October 2019
reports to the Lieutenant Governor and thence to MHA — the structural point phase 11 recorded at break 5 of
`01-periodisation.md`. **The ministry that answers for the figure and the ministry the producer answers to are
the same ministry.**

**The proof that this is not a formality is arithmetic.** R19 (MHA, April 2023) gives 2020 = 1, 2021 = 57,
2022 = 127. L3 (J&K Assembly, October 2025) gives the identical 2020 = 1, 2021 = 57, 2022 = 127. **Same
numbers, because it is the same J&K Revenue Department dataset surfacing through two different legislatures.**
The Union and the territorial answers are not two observations of the world; they are one dataset with two
covers. Anyone who treats an MHA reply and a J&K Assembly reply as mutual corroboration is double-counting a
single instrument.

**One important qualification, and it cuts the other way.** The J&K Assembly reply is *richer* than anything the
Union ever published from the same dataset — it adds area, value, district detail and buyer origin. So while
the two are not independent, **the change of legislature demonstrably changed what the producer was made to
disclose.** That is not corroboration; it is a fact about interrogability, and it is what makes D1 and D2
`not-published` rather than `not-collected`.

## 9. THE LEDGER QUESTION — tested against `differentFacts` criterion (c)

**I read the criterion text in `schemas/ledger.schema.json` before answering.** Verbatim, the operative
condition: *"(c) OPERATIVE — no single measure exists, or could be constructed from available data, that places
both sides' facts on one ledger. An UNBUILT comparison FAILS (c): if a common denominator exists in principle
and merely has not been built, that is a gap in the instrument, not a property of the argument. A counterpart
that is DECLARED UNMEASURABLE passes (c). In practice (c) admits only arguments that cross a category
boundary — a quantity against a legal ruling, a constitutional process, or a stated absence."*

**Answer: it depends on which record stage 3 authors, and the two split cleanly. Stage 3 should not give both
the same flag.**

### 9.1 On LAND — `differentFacts` should be **FALSE**

The government's fact is a count of transfers. The critics' fear is about the scale and direction of transfer.
**Those share a ledger in principle, and as of October 2025 the ledger has actually been built**: the J&K
Assembly reply reports the same event set in **persons, area (kanal) and value (rupees)** simultaneously, and
land area is a denominator both sides accept. 386 kanal = 19.53 ha against a UT of 42,241 km² is a single
measure on which both sides' claims can be laid, and it is not close to ambiguous.

The precondition (a) also fails: both sides are talking about the **same underlying quantity** — land
transferred to persons from outside the UT — and disagree about its **weight and its trajectory**, not about
its value. That is a weighting case.

**Per the schema line "PERMITTED AND MEANINGFUL ON FALSE", stage 3 should write a `differentFactsNote` on the
false flag**, saying: both sides accept the transfer figures; what divides them is (i) whether a small stock is
evidence of a small flow or of an early one, and (ii) whether the counted category means what its name says
(§5.4). The temptation to mark this true comes from the *units* disagreeing across answers, which is an
instrument defect, not a property of the argument. **An unbuilt comparison fails (c); this one is not even
unbuilt.**

### 9.2 On DOMICILE — `differentFacts` should be **TRUE**

Here the two sides genuinely do not share a measure, and the failure is structural rather than clerical.

- The caseFor's strongest facts are **legal-status facts**: that West Pakistan refugees, Valmikis and Gorkhas
  could not hold immovable property or state employment; that a woman marrying a non-resident lost status.
  These are facts about **who was excluded from a category** — and the excluded population was never enumerated
  by any instrument in this material. **There is no stock count of permanent residents, and D8 establishes that
  the object "person who held permanent-resident status but did not convert" is `never-defined`.**
- The caseAgainst's strongest facts are **process facts**: that the definition was replaced by executive order
  under a power that expired within one year, at a time when the affected legislature did not exist.
- The one quantity that would join them — certificates to previously-non-permanent-residents, by year — is
  **`not-published`** (D1), and the only figure that exists (83,742) is on a different period and a different
  category name.

**That is criterion (c) satisfied in its stated form:** the argument crosses a category boundary — *a
constitutional process against a stated absence* — which is precisely the case the schema says (c) admits.
Granting the government's 31,08,682 in full leaves the critics' case untouched, and granting the critics'

`revisitTrigger`.**

**No — and the reason is worth stating exactly.** The nearest candidate is the J&K Assembly's Revenue

## 10. BOTH CASES, AT THEIR STRONGEST, IN THEIR OWN TERMS

### 10.1 THE CASE FOR

*Written to be as strong as the evidence allows, using the government's preferred data.*

For seventy years, Indian citizens living in Jammu and Kashmir were denied, on the basis of where their
families came from, two things every other Indian had: the right to own immovable property where they lived,
and the right to apply for the public jobs their taxes funded. The Adaptation Orders did not take a right from
anyone. **They gave rights to people who had none.**

Look at what the orders actually delete. In the **J&K Property Rights to Slum Dwellers Act 2012** — a statute
whose entire purpose is to give the poorest urban residents title to the ground under them — `S.O. 1229(E)`
strikes the words "**who is a permanent resident of the State of Jammu and Kashmir and**" from both the
definition of a beneficiary and the operative grant. Before 31 March 2020, a family that had lived in a Jammu
slum for three generations could be denied title to its own home because of an ancestral registration in 1927.
**That was the protective regime, in operation, on the ground.** The Union government did not amend it. It
removed the bar.

The same is true across the board. MHA told the Rajya Sabha on 24 March 2021 (R20), verbatim: *"After 5th of
August, 2019, all the provisions of Constitution of India have been applied to the Union territory of Jammu and
Kashmir. All civil rights which are available to any other citizen of the country are now available to the
**Valmiki community** as well. They can now exercise their voting rights in Elections and **can seek employment
to public posts as the provision of Domicile of Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir has been made available to
them**."* Valmikis were brought to J&K as sanitation workers in the 1950s and, seventy years on, their
grandchildren were barred from any state job except sanitation. That is not an abstraction. Domicile ended it.
The same logic reaches West Pakistan refugees and Gorkhas, and MHA's own J&K division still runs a standing
scheme headed "**Financial Assistance to families of West Pakistan Refugees settled in Jammu and Kashmir**"
(R21) — an acknowledgement that these people were the State's residents in every sense but the legal one.

**And the domicile test is not a loophole; it is demanding.** Fifteen years of residence, or seven years of
schooling *plus* sitting the Class 10 or 12 board examination in the territory. Children of central-government
officials qualify only after their parent has served **ten years** in J&K. These are among the strictest
residence thresholds attached to any employment preference in India. On the government's own published figures
(R4), the result is exactly what a status-conversion exercise should look like: of **32,31,353** certificates
issued to 31 December 2020, **31,08,682 — 96.2 per cent — went to dependents or holders of erstwhile Permanent
Resident Certificates.** The overwhelming majority of domicile certificates are the same people, re-papered.

On land, the alarm has been tested against the record and has not survived it. The government has answered on
this repeatedly and consistently. **Two persons** as of August 2021 (R12). **Seven plots** as of December 2021
(R13). **34 persons** as of March 2022 (R16). **185 persons** across three full years (R19) — and in the Union
territory of Ladakh, **zero**. Even taking the most recent and most complete figure available, from the
opposition-led J&K Assembly itself rather than from the Union: **631 buyers, 386 kanal, Rs 129.97 crore, over
six years.** That is **19.5 hectares** in a territory of **42,241 square kilometres** — **0.00046 per cent**, one
part in two hundred thousand. In the same reply the government stated that **no land has been allotted to
outsiders for shopping malls, hospitals or colleges since October 2019**. There is no demographic change here
because there is nothing large enough to change anything.

Nor was the protective floor removed. **The ceiling on landholding is tighter now than it was.** MHA to the
Rajya Sabha, 11 August 2021 (R11): the repealed Big Landed Estates Abolition Act 1950 capped ownership at
**182 standard kanals (22.75 acres)**; the surviving Agrarian Reforms Act 1976, which does the same
tiller-transfer work, caps it at **100 standard kanals (12.5 acres)**. And agricultural land remains barred to
non-agriculturists outright under the new **section 133-H** — "**Transfer of land to non-agriculturist
barred**" — with dispossession and vesting in the State as the penalty for evasion, plus a new
"**Prohibition on transfer of certain lands**" in Agrarian Reforms Act section 28-A. What was repealed was
statutory undergrowth from 1936, 1938, 1950 and 1953 whose work had been done or had been superseded, replaced
by a single consolidated Land Revenue Act with a Board of Revenue, a defined "agricultural land", a defined
"agriculture and allied activities", digital land passbooks and a route to contract farming.

Meanwhile the orders imported protections J&K did not have. Every reference to the old State Land Acquisition
Act 1990 was replaced with the **Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation
and Resettlement Act, 2013** — the strongest compensation and rehabilitation statute in Indian law, which the
people of J&K had been denied while every other Indian enjoyed it. The **Real Estate (Regulation and
Development) Act, 2016** was extended in the same gazette issue.

Finally, on process. Section 96 of the Reorganisation Act — passed by both Houses of Parliament — expressly
authorised the Central Government to adapt existing laws "**whether by way of repeal or amendment**", precisely
so that a territory could not be left with a body of law that no longer fitted its constitutional position.
The power was **time-limited to one year** and was allowed to lapse. And the section says in terms that
everything it does stands only "**until altered, repealed or amended by a competent Legislature**". Nothing was
put beyond democratic reach. The J&K Assembly can change any of it tomorrow. That it has not — while it has
freely used its floor to demand and obtain the very figures the critics say are hidden — is itself an answer.

### 10.2 THE CASE AGAINST

*Written to be as strong as the evidence allows, using its own preferred data.*

Begin with who did this, and when. **The definition of who belongs to Jammu and Kashmir was rewritten by an
executive order of the Union Cabinet, published in the Gazette on 31 March 2020, at a moment when the
territory's own legislature did not exist and had not existed since November 2018.** No member from Jammu, from
Kashmir, or from Ladakh voted on it, spoke to it, or amended it. It came into force "at once". The power used —
section 96 of the Reorganisation Act — is on its face a **transitional tidying power**, given "for the purpose
of facilitating the application" of existing law to a new administrative unit. It was used to repeal the
agrarian settlement of a society.

And look at the clock. Section 96 expired one year after the appointed day of 31 October 2019. **The land
orders are dated 26 October 2020 — five days before the power lapsed.** Ten land statutes repealed outright,
the Land Revenue Act rewritten across thirty-three thousand words, a new industrial corporation created, a
military designation power inserted, all in the final week of a window after which it would have had to go to a
legislature. That is not tidying. **That is a deadline being beaten.**

Consider what was repealed. The **Right of Prior Purchase Act of 1936**, which gave neighbours and co-sharers
first refusal on village land. The **Alienation of Land Act of 1938**. The **Big Landed Estates Abolition Act of
1950**, the instrument of one of the most thoroughgoing land reforms in Asia, which transferred land to the
tiller without compensation and is the reason rural Kashmir has the lowest landlessness in India. The
**Prohibition on Conversion of Land and Alienation of Orchards Act of 1975**, which protected the saffron and
apple economies. The **Common Lands (Regulation) Act**, the **Consolidation of Holdings Act**, the **Prevention
of Fragmentation Act**, the **Utilization of Lands Act**, the **Tenancy Act**. These were not colonial relics.
They were the settlement on which the accession-era arrangement rested, and they were revoked without a single
vote cast by anyone they governed.

Now examine the protection said to survive. Section 133-H bars transfer to a "non-agriculturist" — but the
statute defines an agriculturist as one who "cultivates land personally in the Union territory … **as on such
date as may be notified by the Government**" **or** "**such category of persons as may be notified by the
Government from time to time**." The protected class is whatever the executive says it is, by notification, with
no legislative check. The bar itself "**shall come into effect from a date to be notified by the Government**"
— a protection whose commencement is discretionary. And it is riddled by design: sections 133-I, 133-J and
133-K let the Government permit transfer to **"any person, institution or corporation, for such industrial or
commercial or housing purposes or agricultural purposes or any other public purpose."** Housing. A protection
that exempts housing is not a protection against settlement.

**More importantly, ask what protection was simply deleted.** In the Development Act 1970 — the statute
governing every development authority in the territory — the words "**permanent resident of the State**" were
struck out of section 17(1), the disposal power, and out of two definitions, **and nothing was put in their
place.** Not "domicile". Nothing. For non-agricultural land there is now **no residence-based restriction of any
kind**. And in the same order, new section 11A provides that inside any master or zonal plan area, the
conversion protections of the Agrarian Reforms Act and the Land Revenue Act "**shall not be applicable**". The
protective statute and its override were enacted on the same day, in the same gazette, by the same authority.

The government has been asked this directly and has not answered it. **Twice** — Rajya Sabha Starred Question
\*250 on 11 August 2021 and Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 3716 on 21 December 2021 — Members asked in terms
whether "no domicile or permanent resident certificate is required to purchase non-agricultural land". **Both
times the Ministry replied only that the Government "may, by notification in the official gazette, allow
transfer of land, for public purposes such as education, charitable purpose and healthcare."** That describes
the exceptions and conceals the rule. A citizen reading Hansard would conclude that outside transfer requires
special permission. The gazette says the opposite.

Then there is the military power. On the **written request of an Army officer not below Corps Commander rank**,
an area may be declared a **Strategic Area** and thereby **excluded from the operation of the Development Act
and all rules under it** — and, by a consequential amendment, from municipal building control as well. **How
many such areas have been declared, where, and how large, has never been published.** In a territory with one
of the densest military deployments on earth, a power to remove land from civilian planning law on a service
request, with no published register, is not a technicality.

And now the counting. The government's reassurance rests on numbers whose object changes every time they are
given. **Two properties** in August 2021. **Seven plots** in December 2021, with no period stated at all.
**34 persons** in March 2022. Then, in April 2023, **57 persons in the year 2021 alone** — twenty-eight times
the figure the same Ministry gave for the whole period ending August 2021. **These are not a series. They are
four different objects sharing a headline**, and not one of the four is the object that matters, which is area.
**No Indian government instrument published the area of land transferred to non-residents until the J&K
Assembly, restored after six years, forced it out in October 2025.**

And when the number finally arrived with a definition attached, the definition consumed it. Of 631
"non-residents", **around 350 are reported to be Ladakhis, most from Kargil** — people who until 31 October 2019
lived in the same State and held the same rights, reclassified as outsiders by the stroke that created the
Union territory. **The government's own headline figure is majority-composed of a statistical artefact of the
reorganisation it is being used to defend.**

The same pattern governs domicile. The single figure that would settle whether demographic change is under way
— **certificates issued to persons who were not previously permanent residents** — was never published by the
Union in five years, though the Union published its complement. It emerged only in April 2025, when the
restored Assembly asked: **83,742 domicile certificates to "non-state subjects" in two years.** Two years. Not
since 2020. **The years 2020 to 2023 remain blank to this day.** And in December 2021 the Rajya Sabha was
promised the details of commercial land deals — "date, name of the party/company who purchased" — with the
words "**The Information is being collected and will be laid on the Table of the House.**" It never was.

So the two sides are not measuring the same object, and that is not an accident of research. The government
offers a small number and calls it reassurance. The critics ask for a series — by year, by district, by area,
by whether the buyer was previously a permanent resident — and that series does not exist. **The reassurance
and the fear are not tested against one another because the state has not produced the thing that would test
them.** A right to buy that only 631 people have exercised is not the point. The point is that the framework
under which the next 631,000 might is now set by executive order, its protections commence at executive
discretion, its exemptions cover housing, and its measurement stopped in 2022.

### 10.3 Do the two cases rest on different facts, or on different weightings?

**Split, exactly as in §9 — and stage 3 must not paper over the split.**

- **On land: DIFFERENT WEIGHTINGS of the same facts.** Both sides accept 631 / 386 kanal / Rs 129.97 crore.
  Both accept the repeal list. Both accept that the surviving ceiling is tighter. They divide on whether a small
  stock over six years is evidence of a small flow or of an early one, on whether "housing" as a permitted
  purpose is a loophole or a normal planning category, and on whether a framework should be judged by its
  present outturn or by its permissions. **A single ledger — area transferred, by year, against total area —
  exists and would resolve the trajectory question. It has been built once, by the J&K Assembly.**
- **On domicile: DIFFERENT FACTS.** The caseFor's core facts are legal-status facts about excluded populations
  that no instrument enumerates. The caseAgainst's core facts are process facts about who legislated and when.
  **Neither contradicts the other. Both can be granted in full simultaneously**, and no measure exists or could
  be constructed from available data that places them on one ledger. This is the (c) case.

## 12. FOR STAGE 3 — bearing on existing records, and candidate material

**No phase-11 record is re-authored here. Two bear on this material and take AMENDMENTS, not rewrites:**

- **`L-0123` — "Two J&K security quantities lost their only legislative route in 2018, and it did not resume in
  2024."** **AMENDMENT, and it cuts against the record's generality.** L-0123's finding is that the restored
  assembly is barred from the subject matter, so the route did not resume. **That holds for security. It does
  not hold for domicile and land**, which are Revenue Department subjects within the UT legislature's
  competence — and the restored assembly demonstrably exercised the route, twice, in 2025 (L3, L5), extracting
  the two quantities the Union had not published. **The amendment is a scope qualification: the 2018
  termination and 2024 non-resumption are subject-matter-specific, not chamber-wide.** Note also R10 (MHA to
  Rajya Sabha, 11 Dec 2019, verbatim): *"The powers to make laws under the State List and Concurrent List that
  relate to land and property are vested with the Legislative Assembly of the Union Territory of Jammu and
  Kashmir."* That is the Union's own statement of the competence boundary, given six weeks after the appointed
  day, and it is the primary support for the amendment.
