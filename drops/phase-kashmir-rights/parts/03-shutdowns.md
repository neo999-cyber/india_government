1. **The blackout is a discrete dated object with a duration, not a count.** L-0081 says

3. **A `withheld` on the J&K case, fully specified.** Named requester, specific request,

4. **The J&K review architecture is different from every other state's.** The Supreme Court

## 2. THE LEGAL INSTRUMENTS

### 2.1 The 2017 Rules as made — RETRIEVED (R1)

Made "in exercise of the powers conferred by **section 7** of the Indian Telegraph Act,
1885" — the rule-making power, not the substantive one. The substantive power is
section 5(2), and it appears in the Rules only as the standard the Review Committee tests
against (rule 2(6)). **This distinction matters and is routinely got wrong in secondary
accounts**, including in a search summary I discarded during this run.

Verbatim architecture:

- **Rule 2(1).** Directions "shall not be issued except by an order made by the Secretary
  to the Government of India in the Ministry of Home Affairs" or "the Secretary to the
  State Government in-charge of the Home Department" — the *competent authority*. In
  unavoidable circumstances an officer not below Joint Secretary, duly authorised, may
  issue; subject to confirmation by the competent authority **within 24 hours**, failing
  which the order "shall cease to exist."
- **Rule 2(2).** The order "shall contain reasons" and a copy goes to the Review Committee
  "latest by next working day."
- **Rule 2(3).** Conveyed to designated officers "by an officer not below the rank of
  Superintendent of Police."
- **Rule 2(4).** Nodal officers "in every licensed service area or State or Union
  territory."
- **Rule 2(5).** Review Committee. Centre: **Cabinet Secretary** (Chairman), Secretary
  Legal Affairs, Secretary DoT. State: **Chief Secretary** (Chairman), Law
  Secretary/Legal Remembrancer, "Secretary to the State Government (other than the Home
  Secretary)."
- **Rule 2(6).** The Committee "shall meet within five working days of issue of directions
  ... and **record its findings** whether the directions issued under sub-rule (1) are in
  accordance with the provisions of sub-section (2) of section 5 of the said Act."

**Four first-class findings about the text as made, all from the bare rules:**

1. **There is no maximum duration anywhere in the 2017 Rules.** The "7 days extendable by
   7" figure that circulates in secondary accounts is **not in the notified text**. I read
   the whole notification; it is not there.
2. **There is no publication requirement anywhere in the 2017 Rules.** Not of the order,
   not of the reasons, not of the Review Committee's findings.
3. **There is no periodic review.** The Committee meets once, within five working days.
   The seven-working-day periodic review is a *judicial* graft — Bhasin direction (f),
   §5.1 — expressly made "till this gap is filled."
4. **The Review Committee is wholly executive and, in the 2017 text, has no power to set
   aside.** It "records its findings." All three members are serving civil servants; the
   chair is the head of the same executive that issued the order. The only oversight
   mechanism in the statute is the issuing authority's own colleagues.

### 2.2 The 2020 amendment — RETRIEVED (R2)

G.S.R. 694(E), 10 November 2020, under section 7. A single insertion after rule 2(2):

> "(2A) The suspension order issued by the competent authority under sub-rule (1) shall
> not be in operation for more than fifteen days."

**The operative finding is what it does not say.** It caps the life of *an order*. It says
nothing about issuing a fresh order on day sixteen. A fifteen-day cap on each of a series
of successive orders is not a cap on the shutdown. §5.3 shows the J&K series running to
146 numbered orders in 2020 alone; on the retrieved sample (R8) the orders reference and
supersede each other in an unbroken chain. **The cap constrains the document, not the
condition.** Stage 3 should not describe the 2020 amendment as a limit on shutdown length
without that qualification.

### 2.3 The Telecommunications Act 2023 — RETRIEVED (R5)

Section 20(2): on public emergency or in the interest of public safety, the Central or a
State Government or a specially authorised officer may, if satisfied it is necessary, "in
the interest of the sovereignty and integrity of India, defence and security of the State,
friendly relations with foreign States, public order, or for preventing incitement to the
commission of any offence, **subject to such procedure and safeguards as may be
prescribed**, and for reasons to be recorded in writing, by order — ... (b) direct that any
telecommunication service or class of telecommunication services ... shall be suspended."

Section 20(4): "The action specified under sub-section (1), sub-section (2) and
sub-section (3) shall be for **such duration and in such manner as may be prescribed**."

Section 20(3), noted because it is directly on this subject and is easy to miss: "The
press messages, intended to be published in India, of correspondents accredited to the
Central Government or a State Government shall not be intercepted or detained, unless
their transmission has been prohibited under clause (a) of sub-section (2)." This protects
*interception and detention* of accredited correspondents' messages; it does not exempt
them from a suspension under clause (b).

**Did the regime change?** The grounds broadened on the face of the statute: section 5(2)
of the 1885 Act conditions the power on "the occurrence of any public emergency, or in the
interest of the public safety"; section 20(2) keeps that opening condition but then lists
the same five heads. The material change is that **duration and safeguards are now
expressly delegated to rules** rather than being absent from the parent Act.

### 2.4 The successor rules — DRAFT RETRIEVED (R3), FINAL RETRIEVED (R4)

Draft G.S.R. 519(E), 28 August 2024. **Final: Telecommunications (Temporary Suspension of
Services) Rules, 2024, G.S.R. 724(E), 22 November 2024**, in supersession of the 2017
Rules. The review architecture **survives, and is strengthened in three specific ways**:

| | 2017 (as amended 2020) | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Publication | **absent** | **rule 3(2): "Any suspension order issued under sub-rule (1) shall be published"** |
| Duration cap | rule 2(2A), inserted 2020 | rule 3(2)(b)(iii), in the rules as made: "a specified duration, not exceeding fifteen days" |
| Geographic/service limits | absent | rule 3(2)(b)(ii): "clearly defined geographical area and type of telecommunication service required to be suspended" |
| Least-intrusive test | absent | **rule 3(4): no order "unless the authority issuing such order has considered that the objectives ... cannot be achieved by any other reasonable means"** |
| Copy to Review Committee | "by next working day" | within 24 hours |
| Review Committee composition | 3 executive officers | **identical, 3 executive officers** |
| Review Committee power | "record its findings" | rule 5(3) record findings **and rule 5(4) "may pass an order setting aside such suspension order"** |
| Periodic review | absent | **still absent** |

So: **the 2024 Rules write three of the Bhasin directions into subordinate legislation —
publication, proportionality-as-necessity, and a durational limit — and give the Review
Committee, for the first time, the power to quash.** They do **not** write in Bhasin
direction (f), the seven-working-day periodic review, and they do **not** add a single
non-official to the Committee, which the Standing Committee had recommended (L4,
RELAYED). Stage 3 should treat the 2024 Rules as the strongest single item in the
caseFor and should also note that they came **four years and ten months** after the
judgment they codify.

### 2.5 The Union-territory hole, and the Special Committee

**A structural point that phase 12 owns and that no all-India record can carry.**

In both the 2017 and the 2024 Rules the "competent authority" is defined as the Union Home
Secretary *or* "the Secretary to the State Government in-charge of the Home Department."
**Neither instrument names a Union territory as an ordering authority, and neither
provides for a UT review committee** — rule 5(2) of the 2024 Rules says "Every State
Government shall constitute a review committee." The nodal-officer rule, by contrast,
expressly says "every service area, or State or Union territory." The drafter reached for
"Union territory" in one rule and not in the other.

What actually happens is settled by the retrieved order R8: Government Order
No. Home-07 (TSTS) of 2021 is signed by **Shaleen Kabra IAS, Principal Secretary to the
Government, Home Department**, who styles himself in terms — "[Competent Authority under
sub-rule (1) of Rule 2 of the Temporary Suspension of Telecom Services (Public Emergency
or Public Safety) Rules, 2017]". **NOT ESTABLISHED HERE:** whether the bridge is the
General Clauses Act 1897 (s.3(58)/s.3(60)) reading "State Government" onto a UT
administrator. `indiacode.nic.in` fails DNS from this environment and I did not retrieve
the General Clauses Act. I state the gap and decline to fill it from memory.

**What follows regardless of the bridge, and is worth recording:** after 31 October 2019
both the ordering authority and the reviewing committee for J&K are officers of a
Union-administered territory. The 2017 design placed the order with a State Home
Secretary and the review with a State Chief Secretary — two officers of an elected state
government. In J&K after 31 October 2019 they are not.

Then the Supreme Court displaced even that. In **FMP (11 May 2020)** the Court held
(para 23, RETRIEVED):

> "since the issues involved affect the State, and the nation, the Review Committee which
> consists of only State level officers, may not be in a position to satisfactorily address
> all the issues raised. We, therefore, find it appropriate to constitute a Special
> Committee ..."

— comprising the Union Home Secretary (Chair), the Secretary, Department of
Communications, and the Chief Secretary, UT of J&K. **J&K is the only territory in India
with a bespoke, Union-headed shutdown review body created by judicial order.** R8 confirms
it operated: it "held its 6th meeting on 04.02.2021," and the order lifting the speed
restriction was passed "keeping in view the advice of the Special Committee."

**Read against the record, the Special Committee cuts both ways and stage 3 should say
so.** For: a body chaired by the Union Home Secretary is a higher-level review than the
statute provides anywhere else, and it demonstrably produced a relaxation. Against: the
petitioners asked the Court to lift a restriction and received instead a committee of
executive officers — one of whom, the Chief Secretary, already chaired the statutory
Review Committee, and another of whom heads the ministry whose Secretary is the Union
competent authority. The reviewers and the ordering authorities are drawn from the same
three offices.

### 2.6 Section 144 CrPC / BNSS 163 as the parallel instrument — RETRIEVED (R10)

BNSS 2023 section 163 reproduces CrPC 144 in substance: a District Magistrate,
Sub-divisional Magistrate or specially empowered Executive Magistrate may "by a written
order stating the material facts of the case" direct any person to abstain from an act;
may be passed ex parte in emergency (163(2)); may be directed "to the public generally"
(163(3)); **shall not remain in force more than two months, extendable by State Government
notification to six months** (163(4)); rescindable on the Magistrate's motion or on
application (163(5)-(7)).

**Is a communications curb ordered under section 144 counted anywhere? No.** Three points,
all load-bearing:

1. Bhasin **forbids** using section 144 for internet suspension where the Suspension Rules
   apply. Direction (j)-(n) and para 106 constrain section 144; the Court held the
   Suspension Rules to be the specific mechanism. So the *legal* answer is that such an
   order should not exist.
2. But section 144 / BNSS 163 orders **restricting movement and assembly** were the other
   half of the August 2019 restriction (R6 para 4: "the District Magistrates, apprehending
   breach of peace and tranquillity, imposed restrictions on movement and public
   gatherings by virtue of powers vested under Section 144, Cr.P.C."), and **Bhasin
   direction (a) requires those to be published too** — "all orders in force and any
   future orders under Section 144, Cr.P.C **and** for suspension of telecom services."
3. **No count of section 144 / BNSS 163 orders exists at any level.** Not official, not
   unofficial. The trackers count internet shutdowns; nobody counts movement orders. This
   is the cleanest `not-collected` in the whole subject: the orders are issued by
   individual magistrates, there is no central register, and if a holder were compelled
   tomorrow there is no single body that could produce the series. **Candidate absence,
   `not-collected`, and it is J&K-specific in the sense that the August 2019 restriction
   had two legs and the corpus currently records only one.**

2019 was published** (§5.3). The only primary trace is the SG's undated submission on
16.10.2019 that mobile and landline had been partly relaxed (R6). Every specific 2019
restoration date in circulation is press-derived. **Stage 3 must not assert them as T1,
and must not assert them at all without a retrieved source.** I deliberately do not
reproduce the commonly cited dates here, because reproducing them would launder them.

**The formal 4G restoration.** R8 revokes the speed restriction; it does not use the words
"4G restored." SFLC's J&K page (RELAYED, §4) says 4G returned on **6 February 2021**;
Access Now's dataset says **5 February 2021**. The order is dated 05.02.2021. The
discrepancy is between the order's date and the day service actually changed, and I cannot
resolve it. **A one-day disagreement is not noise here — it moves the headline duration
figure.**

### 3.3 Duration is the sharper measure — and it has at least four defensible values

State the definition or the number is meaningless.

| Definition | Start | End | Duration | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1. Total blackout** — no internet of any kind | 04.08.2019 | 14.01.2020 (first fixed-line restoration, Home-03) | **~163 days** | Both endpoints RETRIEVED (R6, R7) |
| **D2. To any mobile internet in the Valley** | 04.08.2019 | 24.01.2020 (Home-05, 2G all districts, whitelist only) | **~173 days** | Both endpoints RETRIEVED |
| **D3. To an open (non-whitelisted) internet** | 04.08.2019 | 04.03.2020 (Home-17) | **~213 days** | Both endpoints RETRIEVED |
| **D4. To unrestricted-speed mobile internet** | 04.08.2019 | 05.02.2021 (Home-07 of 2021) | **~551 days** | Both endpoints RETRIEVED |

**D4 is the figure the world cites, and it is the one that requires the most care**, because
between 04.03.2020 and 05.02.2021 there *was* internet — open, at 2G, on postpaid and
verified prepaid, on fixed line without speed limits from 15.04.2020, and in two districts
at full speed before the end. Calling that period "shutdown" is a definitional choice, not
a factual error; Access Now's own dataset classifies the tail as `shutdown_type: Throttle`.
**Recommendation for stage 3: if a duration is asserted, assert D4 with the definition
attached — "restriction on high-speed mobile internet, 4 August 2019 to 5 February 2021" —
and record D1 and D3 in the same breath.** The corpus's own rule is that a quantity
without its definition is not a quantity.

Note the arithmetic disagreement in the sources themselves: Access Now records
`8/5/2019 – 2/5/2021, duration 551`; SFLC's J&K page says "552 days"; both are internally
consistent, they differ on whether the start is 4 or 5 August and whether endpoints are
inclusive. This is a **worked instance of the general problem**, not a defect in either.

## 4. THE COUNTING DISAGREEMENT — BY DEFINITION, NOT BY NUMBER

### 4.1 SFLC.in — the unit is the ORDER, and the source is the NEWSPAPER

Unit, from the homepage disclaimer, verbatim:

> "a single order of shutdown which will be counted as one may be imposed in multiple
> districts, thereby increasing the district count but keeping the state count the same."

Definition: "a Government-imposed disablement of access to the Internet as a whole within
a particular locality or localities for any duration of time" — blanket only; content and
platform blocks excluded.

Sources, from the FAQ, verbatim and decisive:

> "**At the moment, almost all of our raw data is collected from reports published in
> national and regional newspapers.** ... **As the Government does not formally announce
> Internet shutdowns to the public before or after they happen, we currently rely entirely
> on secondary sources for information.**"

> "**our data is as reliable as the sources it comes from. We consider newspaper reports of
> Internet shutdowns to be more or less accurate by default, and therefore do not verify
> every report separately unless we have a reason to do so.**"

Duration: a **free-text** field, labelled "Duration (hours)" in the UI, returning `"24"`,
`"24 hours"`, `"0"`, `"None"`, `""`. Not computed.

Extensions: **no stated rule**; observed practice is that successive extension orders
become separate entries (four entries for one continuous Delhi disruption in July 2026).

J&K: total **449** entries; **2019 = 55, 2020 = 116, 2021 = 79, 2022 = 43**. The August
2019 blackout is **one** of those 55 entries (record 715): 20 districts, "Preventive",
**Duration (hours): None**, published date 03 Aug 2019, window **"-" to "Present"**,
**status Active** as of retrieval in August 2026 — and its cited source is a **News18
article**, not a government order.

**Read that last sentence twice.** The single most consequential internet shutdown in
Indian history is, in the most-cited tracker, one row with no end date, no duration, a
status of "Active" seven years later, and a newspaper as its authority.

**A qualification the subagent established from the data, and it matters:** SFLC's current
J&K entries *do* attach scanned government orders (`01TSTSof2025.pdf`,
`02TSTSof2025.pdf` — the J&K Home Department's own TSTS PDFs). The methodology page has
not been updated to say so. **So the tracker changed its evidentiary basis for J&K after
the orders started being published, and did not say so.** The 2019 corpus, including the
flagship entry, remains press-derived.

### 4.2 Access Now / #KeepItOn — the unit is a DECISION

> "A **shutdown instance** refers to any disruption event lasting longer than one hour, or
> a series of disruption events that we attribute to the same or similar circumstances,
> justifications, methods, and perpetrators."

> "a shutdown instance most often results from **one consequential decision made by someone
> in a position of authority**. ... a government that makes numerous snap decisions over
> the course of ongoing protests would end up being linked to numerous shutdown instances,
> while a **predetermined, single policy** of shutdowns during a school exam period would
> end up being linked to **one shutdown instance**."

Scope is **wider** than SFLC's: full shutdowns, **throttling**, and service/platform
blocking all count. Duration is measured in **days**, minimum 1. Geography is a
categorical attribute, not a multiplier. The reports address the aggregation problem
head-on:

> "**Other than a minimum duration of one hour, shutdown instances can be defined at any
> length. As a result, with our current approach, total counts of shutdown instances by
> country group together shutdowns of all durations.**"

J&K figures: 2019 report — "at least 121 internet shutdowns in India in 2019 ...
**shutdowns in Jammu and Kashmir comprised about 68%**", with a boxed feature "KASHMIR:
THE WORLD'S SECOND-LONGEST SHUTDOWN IN 2019 ... **175 days**". 2020 report — the J&K
administration "issued internet shutdown orders **every two weeks** in 2020", and "both
the union government of India and the Jammu and Kashmir administration have **refused to
publish previous internet shutdown orders from 2019**." 2021 report — "**85 were in Jammu
and Kashmir**"; "**551 days | 8/5/2019 – 2/5/2021**"; "the longest shutdown on record,
according to our documentation."

**The dataset's own classification of the flagship datum:** the ~551-day Kashmir row is
tagged `info_source: News media article`, `shutdown_type: Throttle`, with the jkhome.nic.in
order PDFs appearing only in the corroborating field and only for the 2021 tail. Across
all India rows the source split is `News media article` 398 / `CSO KIO partners` 271 /
`Other` 246 — and **the `info_source` field has no "government order" value at all.**

**Access Now's J&K 2019 count is 81. SFLC's is 55.** The two disagree by 47% even though
one feeds the other.

### 4.3 Top10VPN — the "hours" unit is not its own measurement

> "The nature, duration and severity of each internet outage are **sourced from** Netblocks
> real-time graphic data and reports, IODA, **the SFLC.IN Internet Shutdown Tracker**, and
> OONI's ... tools."

Cost figures come from the NetBlocks COST tool over the Brookings method. **For India,
Top10VPN's hours are downstream of SFLC's free-text duration field.** Any economic-loss

Retrieved via a Wayback snapshot of 31 December 2025; the live site is unreachable.

### 4.4 Internet Society Pulse — the only measurement-first instrument

Borrows Access Now's definition, but detects from traffic data and *then* corroborates:
"we cross check to see if there are media reports from reputable sources **or** orders
issued by authorities." India 421 of 908 tracked shutdowns since 2018. Its own caveat is

### 4.5 Not established

**IODA** — the Georgia Tech site returns an empty 3 KB SPA shell; nothing about its unit or

the *retrieved* Access Now formulation is the weaker "the longest shutdown on record,

### 4.6 TRAP (1), APPLIED, IN THOSE WORDS

**A source that looks independent and is media-derived from official reporting is not a
check.** Here the derivation is one step longer and one step worse: the trackers are not
media-derived from *official reporting* — during the period that matters most there was no
official reporting to derive from. They are **media-derived from press coverage of an
event the press could not cover**, because the same blackout that is being counted
disabled the reporting from which the count is built.

Specifically:
- **SFLC is not an independent check on the government's account of J&K shutdowns for
  2019.** It is a check on newspaper coverage of them, self-declared: "we rely entirely on
  secondary sources."
- **Access Now is not an independent check on SFLC.** It names SFLC as a data source, its
  2025 India rows link directly to SFLC's media files, and the free-text description of
  its August 2019 row is SFLC's description verbatim.
- **Top10VPN is not an independent check on either.** It names SFLC in its methodology.
- **Only ISOC Pulse detects independently of both press and government** — and it is the
  least-cited of the four, and its own economic model is not regional.

So of the four "independent" instruments generally offered as a counterweight to the
official silence, **three trace to one press-derived compilation and one of them says so in
writing.** P-54 currently says the trackers are "unchallenged but also unaudited"; the
sharper statement available after this retrieval is that **they are not mutually
independent**, which means their agreement is not corroboration. That is a candidate
amendment to P-54 and it is a stronger finding than anything in the current text.

**The counterweight, stated honestly:** the trackers' own position is unanswerable on its
own terms — a count assembled from public orders, press reports and user submissions is
necessarily a floor, because unpublished orders cannot be counted at all. Being derivative
is not being wrong. And after 2020, for J&K specifically, SFLC's entries *are*
order-based, which is exactly the improvement the publication direction was meant to
produce. The criticism bites hardest on precisely the period the criticism is about: 2019.

### 4.7 The official side of the count

There is **no official count of internet shutdowns in India, and none for J&K.** L-0081
already records this all-India as `not-collected`. The J&K-specific position is different
and better: since January 2020 the J&K Home Department has published its own numbered
suspension orders, so a **count of published J&K orders is directly derivable** — §5.3.
It is not a count of shutdowns, and stage 3 must not present it as one. It is a count of
*orders in a numbered series*, which is a different and cleaner object.

Parliamentary answers on the official position: see §7.

### 5.2 The 2019 orders: the `withheld` test is met, before the Supreme Court — RETRIEVED

This is the sharpest finding in the part and it is entirely from the primary judgment.

> "**the orders are not before us.** The Petitioners and Intervenors claim that the orders
> were not available, which is why they could not place them on record." (para 11)

> "while the non-availability of orders was not denied by the Respondent-State, **they did
> not produce the said orders.** In fact, **when this Court by order dated 16.10.2019 asked
> them to produce the orders, the Respondent-State placed on record only sample orders,
> citing difficulty in producing the numerous orders** which were being withdrawn and
> modified on a day-to-day basis. The Respondent-State also claimed that the plea to
> produce orders by the Petitioners was an expansion of the scope of the present
> petitions." (para 12)

> "when we asked the learned Solicitor General about the non-supply of orders issued by the
> authorities relating to the restrictions imposed, particularly with respect to the cell
> phone services as well as Section 144 proceedings, **he claims privilege over those
> orders**." (order of 16.10.2019, reproduced at para 8)

> "In the present case, while the State initially claimed privilege, it subsequently
> dropped the claim and produced certain sample orders, citing difficulty in producing all
> the orders before this Court. In our opinion, **this is not a valid ground to refuse
> production of orders before the Court.**" (para 18)

**Applying the schema test, in order:**

- `not-collected`? **No.** The orders demonstrably existed — samples were produced.
- `not-published`? Insufficient. The test is whether release was *specifically requested
  and refused*.
- **`withheld`? YES, and all three required elements are named:**
  - **NAMED REQUESTER:** Anuradha Bhasin and Ghulam Nabi Azad, petitioners in WP(C)
    1031/2019 and 1164/2019 — **and the Supreme Court of India itself**.
  - **SPECIFIC REQUEST:** the prayer in WP(C) 1164/2019 and IA 157139 for a mandamus
    "directing Respondent Nos. 1 and 2 to produce all orders by which movement of all
    persons has been restricted since 04.08.2019" and "production of all orders by way of
    which communication has been blocked in State of Jammu and Kashmir" (para 13); and the
    Court's own **order dated 16.10.2019** directing production.
  - **DATE:** 16 October 2019 (the Court's order); the refusal is on the record at the
    same hearing (privilege claimed by the Solicitor General) and again in the final
    disposal at para 12 (only sample orders produced).
- `never-defined`? No.

**`reasonDisputed`: true.** **`disputeKind`: normative** — the factual claim (that orders
existed and were numerous and were being modified daily) is not contested; what is
contested is whether administrative difficulty justifies non-production, and the Supreme
Court held in terms that it does not. *An `evidentiary` reading is available and should be
considered:* the State's position moved from "privilege" to "difficulty", and privilege
and difficulty are different claims about different facts. My recommendation is
`normative`, with the shift recorded in the `why` text.

**Route:** unusually, there is one and it is enforceable rather than requestable — Bhasin
direction (a) itself. Same structure as L-0081's route but with a *specific* refusal
behind it rather than a general non-practice.

The FMP judgment's table (R7) lists the orders passed "since 10.01.2020" and names
thirteen: Home-03, 04, 05, 08, 09, 13, 16, 17, 20, 21, 22, 28 and 34. **Home-06 and
Home-07 of 2020 fall inside that window and are not in it.** I retrieved both from the
archive and read them.

**R13 — Government Order No. Home-06 (TSTS) of 2020, dated 26.01.2020** (Wayback capture
of `jkhome.nic.in/pdf/G.O No. 06(TSTS) of 2020 dt. 26.01.2020.pdf`, capture 2021-09-24;
scanned, read visually). Subject: "Temporary suspension of Telecom Services— **confirmation**
reg;". Reference: "**Order No. 48 of 2020 dated 25.01.2020 issued by IGP, Kashmir Zone
(Authorized Officer)**". Verbatim:

> "the IGP, Kashmir Zone, being the Authorized Officer under the Temporary Suspension of
> Telecom Services (Public Emergency or Public Safety) Rules, 2017, has issued an order on
> 25.01.2020, giving following directions to the Telecom Service Providers (TSPs) /
> Internet Service Providers (ISPs):
> 1. **to suspend the mobile data services** w.e.f 25th (1800 Hrs.) & 26th January, 2020
>    (1800 Hrs.) in the Kashmir Valley;
> 2. **to suspend the Voice calling services and SMS facility across the Kashmir Valley**,
>    on 26th January, 2020, effective from 0600 Hrs. to 1800 Hrs."

confirmed by the Principal Secretary Home as of "absolute necessity ... in the interest of

**R14 — Government Order No. Home-07 (TSTS) of 2020, dated 28.01.2020** (same route,

suspending 2G data services in district Anantnag following "the encounter between the

likelihood of misuse of data services by the anti-national elements", restored by the
Authorized Officer's further order of 28.01.2020.

**Three findings, and they are among the most consequential in this part.**

1. **A Valley-wide suspension of VOICE CALLING and SMS on 26 January 2020 — sixteen days
   after Anuradha Bhasin — is not an internet shutdown and therefore appears in no
   tracker.** The Suspension Rules cover "telecom services", which the DoT's own
   parliamentary framing includes as telephone, mobile telephone and internet access. Every
   tracker in §4 counts *internet*. **Voice and SMS suspensions are counted nowhere, by
   anyone, official or unofficial.** This is a clean, well-evidenced **`not-collected`**
   and it is genuinely new to the corpus: not "nobody has published the count" but "the
   quantity has never been gathered, and the instruments that exist are defined so as to
   exclude it."
2. **What is published is the confirmation, not the order.** In both cases the operative
   direction to the service providers was issued by the **Inspector General of Police,
   Kashmir Zone**, as "Authorized Officer", in *his own numbering series* — Order No. 48 of
   2020, Order No. 56 of 2020. The Home Department publishes only its own subsequent
   confirmation under the first proviso to rule 2(1). **The document that actually
   suspended the service is not in the published series and I could not locate it in any
   archive.** So the J&K published-order count in §5.3 is a count of confirmations of an
   unpublished police series, and Bhasin direction (a) — "publish all orders ... for
   suspension of telecom services" — is met at one remove. I do not know how many of the
   287 published orders are confirmations of this kind; the two I read both are, and the
   restoration-trajectory orders (R7's table, R8) are not.
   *Caution:* the IGP's numbers 48 and 56 two days apart indicate a **general** IGP order
   series, not a suspension-specific one. **Do not read "at least 56 suspension orders by
   27 January 2020" out of this.** Nothing here supports that.
3. **On the omission from the FMP table: a fair reading is available and should be
   stated.** Home-06 and Home-07 are of a different *kind* from the thirteen tabulated
   ones — they are confirmations of short, local, emergency suspensions, whereas the table
   tracks the progressive restoration. The table may reasonably have been a restoration
   chronology rather than a complete return, and the judgment does not claim completeness.
   **But the consequence stands regardless of intent:** the picture of "the orders which
   have been passed since 10.01.2020" that the Supreme Court had before it when it declined
   to lift the restriction **omitted a Valley-wide cut of voice calls and SMS**, and the
   Court's own reasoning at para 12 turns on the steps taken since Bhasin. Stage 3 should
   state the omission and the innocent reading together, and should not assert
   suppression.

**On the rank question, flagged and not resolved.** Rule 2(1)'s first proviso permits
issuance by "an officer, not below the rank of a **Joint Secretary to the Government of
India**, who has been duly authorised by the Union Home Secretary or the State Home
Secretary." Both retrieved orders describe the IGP, Kashmir Zone as "the Authorized Officer
under the ... Rules, 2017". **I did not retrieve any instrument authorising him, and I make
no claim about whether an Inspector General of Police satisfies that rank condition.** I
record the question because it is on the face of two primary documents and because the
Standing Committee's reported finding that DoT does not even know which States have
constituted review committees (L4) suggests the authorisation layer is not centrally
documented either. **NOT ESTABLISHED — do not author on it.**

### 5.4 The RTI chain — a second, later, cleaner `withheld` — RELAYED (L3)

Everything in this subsection is from two Internet Freedom Foundation posts. **IFF is the
applicant, not an observer.** T4 throughout. I could not verify any of it against the
J&K Home Department's own records because every J&K host is unreachable (§0.3), and I did

**Cycle 1 (2023).** RTI to the J&K Home Department, **22 June 2023**, seeking (among other

**Cycle 2 (2024–25).** RTI **IFF/RTI/2024/112 dated 15 May 2024**, same subject matter.
PIO response **6 June 2024**: same refusal, same unparticularised section 8. First Appeal
**6 July 2024**; hearing **7 July 2024**; **FAA order 5 August 2024** again directing
release and again noting the PIO should have specified and justified the clause. **The PIO
did not comply.** Compliance letter **15 October 2024**. PIO reply **29 October 2024**:

**This is the strongest `withheld` in the material, and it is J&K-specific.**

- **NAMED REQUESTER:** the Internet Freedom Foundation.
- **SPECIFIC REQUEST:** RTI application IFF/RTI/2024/112 to the Home Department,
  Government of Jammu and Kashmir, for the findings of the Review Committee on internet
  suspension orders in the UT since 10 January 2020.
- **DATE:** 15 May 2024 (request); refused 6 June 2024; ordered released 5 August 2024;
  refused again 29 October 2024.

**`reasonDisputed`: true. `disputeKind`: EVIDENTIARY** — and this is where the J&K case
differs categorically from L-0081's all-India absence, which is `normative`. The
department gave **two mutually inconsistent reasons**: in June it withheld the findings as
*exempt under section 8*, which asserts that they exist and are held; in October it said
they were *not available in the records*, which asserts they are not. Only one of those
can be true. The stated reason is contradicted by evidence, from the same holder, in the
same file, five months apart. That is the schema's definition of `evidentiary` exactly.

**A further consequence that stage 3 should weigh.** If the October 2024 answer is the
true one, then the Review Committee findings for J&K between January 2020 and February
2024 **do not exist** — which would mean the only statutory oversight mechanism was not
producing the record rule 2(6) requires. That is a materially worse finding than
non-disclosure, and I cannot resolve which reading is correct. **Both readings should be
stated; neither should be chosen.**

### 5.5 How the J&K case differs from L-0081's `not-published`

| | L-0081 (all-India) | J&K (phase 12) |
|---|---|---|
| Class | `not-published` | **`withheld`**, twice: §5.2 (2019 orders) and §5.4 (Review Committee findings) |
| Requester | none named — a general practice | **named**: petitioners + the Supreme Court (2019); IFF (2024) |
| Request | none | **specific and dated** |
| Refusal | non-practice | **express**, and in 2024 in defiance of an appellate direction |
| `disputeKind` | `normative` | §5.2 `normative`; **§5.4 `evidentiary`** |

## 6. THE MEASURING INSTRUMENT WAS DISABLED BY THE THING IT MEASURES

**This is the central structural fact of the subject and it must be a first-class finding,
not a caveat.**

The corpus's fixed periodisation says the August 2019 communications blackout degraded
media-derived series specifically while leaving official series formally intact — the two
source families break differently at the same moment. On this subject that general rule
becomes a specific and unusually clean pathology:

- **The event being measured is the event that disabled one of the two instruments.**
  Every shutdown tracker for India is, on its own account (§4.1), built from newspaper
  reports. On 4 August 2019 internet, mobile and landline in the Valley were all cut
  (R6 para 3), the Srinagar edition of the Kashmir Times could not be distributed on
  5 August and could not be published from 6 August (R6 para 4). **The instrument that
  counts shutdowns runs on the connectivity the shutdown removed.**
- **The other instrument — the official one — produced nothing either, and for an unrelated
  reason.** The orders existed but were not published (§5.3) and were not produced when
  demanded (§5.2). So the official series was not "formally intact but unpublished"; on
  this subject there was no official series at all, and L-0081 already records that as
  `not-collected`.
- **The result is a measurement hole with two independent causes, closing at different
  times.** The media-derived instrument recovered as connectivity returned through 2020.
  The official one began emitting in January 2020 when the J&K Home Department started
  publishing (§5.3). Neither recovered *backwards*: nothing published in 2020 reconstructs
  the 2019 orders, and no tracker retro-fitted its 2019 entries. **The August 2019 blackout
  is measured, to this day, by a single tracker row citing a news website, with no end date
  and a status of "Active" (§4.1).**
- **And the trackers' derivative structure (§4.6) means the hole does not get filled by
  redundancy.** Three of the four instruments trace to the same press-derived compilation.
  Where the press could not report, all three are dark together.

The honest statement of the epistemic position: **for the period 4 August 2019 to roughly
mid-January 2020, this instrument has no measurement of the thing it is describing that is
independent of either (a) a government that did not publish its orders or (b) a press that
could not file.** Everything after January 2020 is far better evidenced. Stage 3 should
carry that asymmetry explicitly into any `confidence` value: high after January 2020,
medium at best before it.

## 9. THE LEDGER QUESTION — `differentFacts` CRITERION (c)

I read `schemas/ledger.schema.json` `properties.differentFacts` in full first, as
instructed. Criterion (c), operative: *no single measure exists, or could be constructed
from available data, that places both sides' facts on one ledger. An UNBUILT comparison
FAILS (c). A counterpart that is DECLARED UNMEASURABLE passes (c). In practice (c) admits
only arguments that cross a category boundary — a quantity against a legal ruling, a
constitutional process, or a stated absence.*

**Answer: (c) is satisfied. `differentFacts` should be TRUE on a J&K shutdowns record.**

Working it properly:

- **(a) precondition — do the two sides cite different quantities?** Yes. The caseFor's
  facts are a *security* series: incidents, casualties, the absence of the mass-casualty
  unrest of 2008, 2010 and 2016, and the stagedness of restoration. The caseAgainst's
  facts are a *duration* (551 days on D4), a *population* (over 12 million), and a
  **stated absence** — unpublished orders, therefore no possible proportionality review.
- **(b) precondition — does either contradict the other?** No. Grant the State every
  incident figure it put before the Supreme Court in FMP (R7 para 13) in full: the
  caseAgainst is untouched, because it is not a claim about whether violence fell. Grant
  the 551 days and the unpublished orders in full: the caseFor is untouched, because it is
  not a claim that the orders were published.
- **(c) operative — could a common measure be constructed from available data?** No, and
  the reason is structural rather than a gap in the instrument. To place both sides on one
  ledger you would need a counterfactual: violence that would have occurred without the
  shutdown, against rights foregone. **The first half is unmeasurable in principle** — no
  control territory, no counterfactual — and, decisively, **the government's own position
  is that it has never assessed the impact** (§7, L4, if retrieved). **The second half is
  a declared absence**: the orders are unpublished and were withheld, so the proportionality
  ledger the comparison would need *cannot be built by anyone*, which is precisely the
  caseAgainst's point.

That is not an unbuilt comparison. It is the schema's own admitted case: **a quantity
against a stated absence**, where the counterpart is declared unmeasurable. The worked
negative in the schema — the OECD Producer Support Estimate netting two economic
quantities by construction — has no analogue here, because there is no construction that
nets days-without-internet against attacks-that-did-not-happen.

**`differentFactsNote` should say, in substance:** the security case rests on incident
counts the UT administration produces and the Union relays; the rights case rests on a
duration and on an absence of published orders. Both are arithmetically correct. They
cannot be netted because the netting would require the counterfactual violence the
government has not measured and the proportionality record the government did not publish
— and the non-existence of the second is the rights case's central claim, so building the
common ledger would require the very thing whose absence is being litigated.

## 10. BOTH CASES, EACH AT ITS STRONGEST

### FOR

A shutdown is a preventive instrument against rumour-driven mass mobilisation, and the
theatre has a documented history of exactly that. The Supreme Court itself, in the
judgment most often cited against the state, accepted the premise: "Modern terrorism
heavily relies on the internet ... The internet is being used to support fallacious proxy
wars by raising money, recruiting and spreading propaganda/ideologies. The prevalence of
the internet provides an easy inroad to young impressionable minds" (Bhasin para 39,
quoted back by the Court in FMP para 22, both RETRIEVED). The period following August 2019
did not produce the mass-casualty street unrest of 2008, 2010 and 2016. Restoration was
not indefinite and was not a single switch: it was staged, documented, and each stage was
tied to an assessment — fixed line to hospitals and essential services on 14 January 2020,
2G postpaid to five Jammu districts the same day, all districts by 24 January, the
whitelist expanding from 329 to 481 to 1,674 sites and then abolished entirely on 4 March
2020, fixed line unthrottled on 15 April 2020, two districts to full speed before the end,
and the speed restriction revoked on 5 February 2021 on the advice of a Special Committee
which had by then met six times. Every one of those dates is on a published order and was
retrieved for this part. The state is under no obligation to publish operational security
orders in real time while an operation is running; the Supreme Court, asked in FMP to lift
the restriction outright, **declined**, and constituted a review body instead — a court
that thought the restriction indefensible would not have done that. And the state has
since moved further than the judgment required: the Telecommunications (Temporary
Suspension of Services) Rules 2024 write publication, a fifteen-day cap, a
least-intrusive-means test and a power of quashing into binding subordinate legislation.
The J&K Home Department has published its numbered suspension orders continuously since
January 2020 — at least 287 of them — which is more than most Indian states have done, and
publication is now a step in the order's own distribution list.

### AGAINST

The longest shutdown ever recorded anywhere, imposed on a population of more than twelve
million, and the orders that imposed it were never published — so proportionality review
was impossible **by design rather than by oversight**. That is not a characterisation; it
is the record. When the Supreme Court ordered production on 16 October 2019 the Union
first claimed privilege and then produced samples, and the Court held in terms that
difficulty "is not a valid ground to refuse production of orders." The published series
begins in January 2020, after the judgment; the Department's website was publishing other
orders throughout 2019, so the mechanism existed and the communications orders were kept
off it. The fifteen-day cap introduced in November 2020 caps a document, not a condition —
the J&K series ran to 146 numbered orders in 2020 alone. A court that has to reiterate a
direction four years later is telling you the direction was not followed, and the
reiteration was itself in a **J&K compliance** proceeding, not a fresh case. When a civil
society organisation asked the J&K Home Department for the Review Committee's findings,
it was refused under an unparticularised exemption, won on appeal, was refused again in
defiance of the appellate order, and was then told the records were "not available" — a
department that cannot decide whether the documents are secret or non-existent has
established, on either branch, that the only statutory oversight mechanism produced
nothing reviewable. And the deepest point is the one about knowledge: **the blackout
disabled the instruments that would have measured its own effects.** Every tracker of
Indian shutdowns is built, by its own written admission, from newspaper reports; the
newspapers could not publish. The government produced no count, has never assessed the
impact, and told Parliament the records are not centrally held. So the state imposed the
longest shutdown on record, did not publish the orders, did not measure the consequences,
and the only independent counters were counting a thing they could not see.

#### 11.A.1 The referent question — ANSWERED, and the answer is the interesting one

**The TRAI "Jammu & Kashmir" Licensed Service Area INCLUDES Ladakh, and its boundary did
NOT change on 31 October 2019.** Three independent establishments:

1. **TRAI says so, once, in passing.** *Recommendations on Improving Telecom Coverage and
   Backhaul Infrastructure in far-flung areas of Ladakh*, 24 April 2023, para 2.15:
   "information as per census data of villages ... from all TSPs that are providing mobile
   services in **Ladakh falling in the J&K Licensed Service Area (LSA)**."
2. **The licence definition was never amended.** DoT's Unified Access Service Licence
   Annexure-VI (footer "Final UASL circle (28.01.2004)") and the Unified Licence
   Annexure-V both define service area 08 as "**Entire area falling within the State of
   Jammu & Kashmir including the autonomous council of Ladakh**" — a State that no longer
   exists and a body the 2019 Act superseded. Diagnostic detail: **the same annexure
   expressly carries forward the Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh Reorganisation
   Acts of 2000 in its own text, and contains no reference anywhere to the Jammu and
   Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019.** The drafter knew how to record a reorganisation in
   this table and did not record this one. Both annexures enumerate 22 service areas; there
   is no Ladakh entry.
3. **Arithmetic identity inside a single TRAI report.** QPIR Jan–Mar 2026: service-area
   J&K access subscribers 14.28 M (Table 1.2) = State/UT J&K 13.72 + Ladakh 0.56
   (Table 1.5). Internet: service-area J&K 11.99 M (Table 1.40) = J&K UT 11.59 + Ladakh
   0.40 (Table 1.41). Same identity holds in QPIR Apr–Jun 2020.

**What this means for the corpus, and it is a first-class finding.** The corpus's fixed
periodisation says any series whose geographic denominator is "Jammu and Kashmir" changes
referent on 31 October 2019 and must break. **The TRAI J&K series is the exception that
tests the rule: it does not break, because its denominator was never the administrative
unit.** It is a licensing boundary frozen in 2004. The consequence is that from 31 October
2019 onward **a TRAI "Jammu & Kashmir" figure and, say, an MHA "Jammu and Kashmir" figure
are figures about different territories**, and nothing in either publication says so.
Stage 3 must not place a TRAI J&K circle series beside any post-2019 J&K administrative
series without declaring that they are on different denominators. This is a
`provenance`-shaped finding: a referent divergence that is silent on both sides.

**TRAI has published no note, footnote, definition or erratum on the composition of the
J&K service area, before or after 2019.** It publishes a standing composition note naming
seven other multi-State service areas ("Andhra Pradesh includes Telengana, Madhya Pradesh
includes Chhatishgarh, Bihar includes Jharkhand ..."). **J&K/Ladakh is never in that note,
in any report, before or after 2019.**

Ladakh first appears in a QPIR only in the Jan–Mar 2020 edition (published 17 September
2020) — **eight months after the reorganisation** — and then only as a *label on a merged
row with no separate value*, at State/UT level. It gets its own numbers in the Apr–Jun 2020
wireless tables, reverts to merged rows for three quarters, and is fully split only much
later. At service-area level nothing ever changed.

### 13.8.2 THE FINDING — shutdown orders in J&K lie outside the elected government's competence entirely

Three retrieved provisions of the J&K Reorganisation Act, in sequence:

> **s.32(1).** "the Legislative Assembly may make laws ... with respect to any of the
> matters enumerated in the State List **except the subjects mentioned at entries 1 and 2,
> namely 'Public Order' and 'Police'** respectively or the Concurrent List ..."

> **s.53(1).** "There shall be a Council of Ministers ... to aid and advise the Lieutenant
> Governor in the exercise of his functions **in relation to matters with respect to which
> the Legislative Assembly has power to make laws** except in so far as he is required ...
> to act in his discretion ..."

> **s.53(2).** "The Lieutenant Governor shall, in the exercise of his functions, **act in
> his discretion** in a matter: (i) **which falls outside the purview of the powers
> conferred on the Legislative Assembly**; or ... (iii) related to All India Services and
> Anti Corruption Bureau"

and, closing the loop:

> **s.53(3).** "The question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers
> to the Lieutenant Governor **shall not be inquired into in any court**."

**Chain the three and the result is unambiguous.** Public Order and Police are excluded
from the Assembly's competence by s.32(1). Whatever is outside the Assembly's competence is
outside the Council of Ministers' advisory role under s.53(1), and is a matter on which the
Lieutenant Governor acts **in his discretion** under s.53(2)(i). A telecom suspension is
ordered on grounds of "public emergency or public safety" and, on the retrieved J&K orders
themselves, "in the interest of sovereignty and integrity of India, the security of the
State/UT and for maintaining public order" (R15, R16). **It is squarely inside the excluded
subjects.**

Therefore: **an internet or communications shutdown in Jammu and Kashmir is not a decision
of the elected government of Jammu and Kashmir, and cannot be, as a matter of statute.**
Since the 2024 assembly election restored a Council of Ministers, this is not a
transitional fact about a period of direct rule — **it is the standing constitutional
position of the Union territory.** And s.53(3) forecloses even judicial inquiry into
whether ministerial advice was tendered.

*A second, independent route to the same place, flagged with its caveat:* telecommunications
is a Union List subject, so a suspension direction would be outside the Assembly's
competence on that ground too. **I did not retrieve the Seventh Schedule and I rest the
finding on s.32(1), which I did retrieve and which excludes Public Order and Police in
terms.** The second route is offered only to show the conclusion is overdetermined.

### 13.8.3 What this does to the record

**It sharpens §2.5 into something authorable.** §2.5 said that after 31 October 2019 the
ordering and reviewing officers for J&K are officers of a Union-administered territory
rather than of an elected state government. That was true but soft. The statutory position
is harder: **on this subject there is no elected-government competence to be displaced.**

**It belongs in the caseAgainst, and it is stronger than anything already there** — but it
must be stated as a *structural* fact, not as an allegation. The Union did not seize a
power from the J&K Council of Ministers; Parliament, in the same Act that created the UT,
placed Public Order and Police outside the Assembly's reach. **That is a legislative design
choice, retrievable in the statute, and it means the accountability route that exists in
every State — an elected Home Minister answerable to a State Assembly for a shutdown — does
not exist in J&K at all.**

**It also strengthens the caseFor, honestly read.** If shutdown orders are by design a
Union-discretion matter in a UT with an active security situation, then the Supreme Court's
construction of a Special Committee headed by the **Union** Home Secretary (§2.5, R7) was
not an oddity but the only review body that could match the actual locus of the power. The
Court's stated reason — that a committee "which consists of only State level officers, may
not be in a position to satisfactorily address all the issues" — reads differently once
s.32(1) is in view.

**And it bears on where any absence route must be addressed.** §5.4's RTI chain ran against
the **J&K Home Department**, which answered that the Review Committee findings were "not
available in the records". §13.6.2 shows the review record sits with the **GAD**. §13.8.2
now shows the underlying power is exercised in the Lieutenant Governor's discretion. **A
route that names only the Home Department may be addressed to the wrong holder**, and
stage 3 should say so when it writes the `wouldFill` text rather than copying L-0081's
all-India route.

### 13.9.2 `jkhome.nic.in` IS GONE — and this is the finding, not the artefact

`jkhome.nic.in` returns **NXDOMAIN** — the name does not exist in DNS — from **three
independent public resolvers**: 1.1.1.1, 9.9.9.9 and 208.67.222.222. (8.8.8.8 returns

decisive: **`jkhighcourt.nic.in`, a sibling `.nic.in` host, resolves and returns 200 through
the same resolver in the same command.** So this is not a `.nic.in` delegation problem and
not the resolver artefact. The name has been removed.

**Corroboration from the Internet Archive.** The last successful capture of the site root is
**3 April 2025** (`https://jkhome.nic.in/`, status 200). Captures of any `jkhome.nic.in` URL
stop in **April 2025**. There are none in the sixteen months since. Today is 3 August 2026.

**What this means, stated carefully.**

1. **The publication venue for J&K suspension orders no longer exists.** Every one of the
   ~287 orders inventoried at §5.3, both Review Committee orders at §13.6.2, the 4G
   restoration order R8, and the two confirmations R13/R14 — **all of them were published
   on `jkhome.nic.in`, and all of them are now reachable only through the Internet
   Archive.** Every J&K primary document in this part was retrieved from the Archive. That
   was a convenience when I did it; it is now the only route.
2. **It directly undercuts the Union's own answer to Parliament.** The child's retrieval
   (`03b-parliamentary-answers.md`) has the Ministry of Home Affairs telling the Rajya Sabha
   in July 2022 that suspension orders "are uploaded on the official website of the
   Government of Jammu and Kashmir." That was the Union's answer to a question about
   whether it holds shutdown data — it pointed at a website. **The website is gone.** A
   compliance discharged by pointing at a URL is a compliance that ends when the URL does.
3. **It creates a third absence class-shape that the schema's four do not cleanly cover, and
   stage 3 must handle it deliberately.** These orders are not `not-collected` — they were
   collected. Not `never-defined`. Not straightforwardly `not-published` — they *were*
   published. And not `withheld` on the post-2020 series — nobody refused them; they were
   posted. **They were published and then became unreachable.** On the schema's own test —
   *producibility under compulsion* — the right question is whether the J&K Home Department
   still holds them, which I cannot determine, and the honest classification is
   **`not-published`** for the *current* state with the history stated in the `why`: they
   were published from January 2020 and the venue ceased to resolve between April 2025 and
   August 2026. **Do not write `withheld` for these** — there is no named requester and no
   refusal for the post-2020 series.
4. **It is a caseAgainst item and a serious one, but it must not be over-read.** I have
   **not** established intent, a takedown, or a deliberate removal. A government website
   being migrated, consolidated, or allowed to lapse is common and unremarkable in itself.
   **What is established is the effect:** the documentary record of India's compliance with
   the Supreme Court's publication direction, for the territory the direction was made
   about, is currently preserved by a private foundation in California and by nobody else I
   could find. Stage 3 should state the effect and expressly decline to state a cause.
5. **The `wouldFill` route for any J&K absence must be rewritten.** L-0081's route is "an
   RTI to state and UT home departments". For J&K that route now has to name a holder whose
   website does not resolve, whose RTI portal (`rtionline.jk.gov.in`) does not answer, and
   whose review records sit with a different department (§13.6.2). **A route that says "see
   jkhome.nic.in" is a placeholder route, and the brief is explicit that a placeholder route
   is worse than none.**

**What I could not do:** find a successor site. `jk.gov.in`, `jkpolice.gov.in` and
`rtionline.jk.gov.in` all resolve but return no HTTP response from here, so I cannot
establish whether the Home Department's orders have been migrated to `jk.gov.in` or
anywhere else. **NOT ESTABLISHED — the orders may well be published today at an address I
cannot reach. Stage 3 must not assert that publication has ceased. What is established is
that the address the Union gave Parliament no longer exists.**

## 13.11 One more sample, and what the published J&K series actually consists of

**Two consequences for §5.3's count of ~287 published orders.**

1. **The published series is predominantly a series of confirmations, not of originating
   orders.** Stage 3 should describe it that way. The document that directs the service
   providers is the police order; the document that is published is the Home Department's
   confirmation of it. Rule 3(2) of the 2024 Rules requires "any suspension order issued
   under sub-rule (1)" to be published, and sub-rule (1) is the provision under which the
   Authorized Officer acts. **On the retrieved documents, the order published is the
   confirming one. Whether that discharges the rule is a legal question this part does not
   answer.**
2. **The police numbering is definitively not a count, and the two series are not
   commensurable.** IGP Kashmir Zone reached **1395(TSTS) by 13 September 2020**; ADGP Jammu
   Zone reached **1382(TSTS) by 3 November 2024**; IGP Kashmir Zone was at **137(TSTS) by
   13 January 2025**. Against a Home Department series that reached 146 in all of 2020 and
   11 in all of 2024. **No quantity may be derived from the Authorized Officers' numbering**
   — the magnitudes are file references, and the zones number independently and reset
   annually. This supersedes nothing above but hardens the caution first entered at §5.3a
   and repeated at §13.6.3.

**And it adds one substantive point to the caseFor that the earlier text understates.** Each
of the five confirmations states a specific triggering event, a specific district, and a
specific clock window measured in hours — Pulwama for seven hours and ten minutes; Anantnag
for one day; Kathua for six and a half hours; Ganderbal for ten and a half. Whatever is
true of August 2019, **the instrument as used from 2020 onward is short, local and
reason-bearing on its face**, and that is visible in the primary documents rather than
asserted by anyone.
