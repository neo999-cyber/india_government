A record that runs them together loses the fact that the Union spent 87 days operating a State that had been constitutionally reconstituted but not yet dissolved.

### 1.2 C.O. 272, verbatim and complete

From **R2, Appendix II**, page 369-370 (RETRIEVED, T1). The footnote to the Appendix reads: *"Published with the Ministry of Law and Justice, (Legislative Department) notification No. G.S.R. 551 (E), dated the 5th August, 2019, Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II, Section 3, Sub-section (i)."*

> **THE CONSTITUTION (APPLICATION TO JAMMU AND KASHMIR) ORDER, 2019 — C.O. 272**
>
> In exercise of the powers conferred by clause (1) of article 370 of the Constitution, the President, **with the concurrence of the Government of State of Jammu and Kashmir**, is pleased to make the following Order:—
>
> 1. (1) This Order may be called the Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019.
> (2) It shall come into force at once, and shall thereupon **supersede the Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 1954** as amended from time to time.
>
> 2. All the provisions of the Constitution, as amended from time to time, shall apply in relation to the State of Jammu and Kashmir and the exceptions and modifications subject to which they shall so apply shall be as follows:–
>
> To article 367, there shall be added the following clause, namely:
>
> "(4) For the purposes of this Constitution as it applies in relation to the State of Jammu and Kashmir–
> (a) references to this Constitution or to the provisions thereof shall be construed as references to the Constitution or the provisions thereof as applied in relation to the said State;
> (b) references to the person for the time being recognized by the President on the recommendation of the Legislative Assembly of the State as the Sadar-i-Riyasat of Jammu and Kashmir, acting on the advice of the Council of Ministers of the State for the time being in office, shall be construed as **references to the Governor of Jammu and Kashmir**;
> (c) references to the **Government of the said State** shall be construed as **including references to the Governor of Jammu and Kashmir acting on the advice of his Council of Ministers**; and
> (d) in proviso to clause (3) of article 370 of this Constitution, the expression **"Constituent Assembly of the State referred to in clause (2)" shall read "Legislative Assembly of the State"**."

**Four operative moves, and they are not equally famous.** (d) is the one everybody cites. But (b) and (c) do the second half of the work: (b) converts every reference to the elected Sadar-i-Riyasat into the appointed Governor, and (c) makes "Government of the said State" **include** the Governor acting on his Council's advice. On 5 August 2019 there was no Council of Ministers — the Assembly had been dissolved on 21 November 2018 — and the Governor's own functions had been assumed by the President on 19 December 2018. So the entity whose "concurrence" the Order's own preamble recites was, at the moment of recital, the President.

### 1.3 C.O. 273, verbatim and complete

From **R2, Appendix III**, page 370-371 (RETRIEVED, T1). Footnote: *"Published with the Ministry of Law and Justice, (Legislative Department) notification No. G.S.R. 562(E), dated the 6th August, 2019, Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II, Section 3, Sub-section (i)."* (The Appendix's own heading carries a typographical error, "DECLRATION", reproduced here as printed.)

> **DECLRATION UNDER ARTICLE 370(3) OF THE CONSTITUTION — C.O. 273**
>
> In exercise of the powers conferred by clause (3) of article 370 read with clause (1) of article 370 of the Constitution of India, the President, **on the recommendation of Parliament**, is pleased to declare that, as from the **6th August, 2019**, all clauses of the said article 370 shall cease to be operative except the following which shall read as under, namely:—
>
> "370. All provisions of this Constitution, as amended from time to time, without any modifications or exceptions, shall apply to the State of Jammu and Kashmir notwithstanding anything contrary contained in article 152 or article 308 or any other article of this Constitution or any other provision of the Constitution of Jammu and Kashmir or any law, document, judgment, ordinance, order, by-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage having the force of law in the territory of India, or any other instrument, treaty or agreement as envisaged under article 363 or otherwise."

**Note what C.O. 273 says the recommendation was.** Article 370(3)'s proviso required "the recommendation of the Constituent Assembly of the State". C.O. 272(2)(d) made that read "Legislative Assembly of the State". C.O. 273 recites the recommendation of **Parliament** — not of a Legislative Assembly. The bridge is the 19 December 2018 Proclamation, under which "the powers of the Legislature of the State shall be exercisable by or under the authority of Parliament". So the substitution effected by C.O. 272 was consumed one day later by a body that was standing in for the substituted body.

**The full circuit, stated once, flatly and without adjectives.** The Constituent Assembly of J&K dissolved in 1957. C.O. 272 read "Constituent Assembly" as "Legislative Assembly". The Legislative Assembly had been dissolved by the Governor on 21 November 2018. Its powers had been vested in Parliament by the President's Proclamation of 19 December 2018. The Union's own Parliament therefore supplied the recommendation, and the Union's own President supplied the "concurrence of the Government of the State". **At no point in the chain did any body chosen by the electorate of Jammu and Kashmir act.** That is not an argument; it is a list of dated instruments, all four of them retrieved.

### 1.4 What C.O. 272 did to Article 35A, and whether that is a distinct instrument

**It is not a distinct instrument. It is a consequence of C.O. 272 paragraph 1(2), and no instrument anywhere names Article 35A.**

Article 35A was introduced by the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 1954 (C.O. 48)**. The judgment (R3, paragraph 435) reproduces the operative words of C.O. 48, which inserted after Article 35:

> "35A. Saving of laws with respect to permanent residents and their rights – Notwithstanding anything contained in this Constitution, no existing law in force in the State of Jammu and Kashmir, and no law hereafter enacted by the Legislature of the State— defining the classes of persons who are, or shall be, permanent residents of the State of Jammu and Kashmir; or conferring on such permanent residents any special rights and privileges or imposing upon other persons any restrictions as respects— employment under the State Government; Acquisition of immovable property in the State; Settlement in the State; or Right to scholarships and such other forms of aid as the State Government may provide. Shall be void on the ground that it is inconsistent with or takes away or abridges any rights conferred on the other citizens of India by any provision of this Part."

So Article 35A protected four things and only four: **State-Government employment, acquisition of immovable property, settlement, and State scholarships/aid** — and it protected them by immunising the *State's own permanent-resident laws* from Part III challenge. It did not itself define a permanent resident; s.6 of the Constitution of J&K and the State's Permanent Resident laws did that.

C.O. 272 paragraph 1(2) provides that the Order "shall thereupon **supersede** the Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 1954 as amended from time to time." **Article 35A fell because its parent Order was superseded.** There is no separate order abolishing it, no separate resolution, and no separate section of the Reorganisation Act.

**This has three consequences a record must carry.**
1. **Article 35A's removal has no instrument of its own and therefore no separate date, no separate authority and no separate reasons.** It dates to 5 August 2019 only derivatively.
2. **The Supreme Court did not decide the validity of Article 35A's removal.** The eight questions framed for determination (R3, paragraph 86, reproduced at §4.1 below) do not include it. Article 35A appears in the judgment only in the *recitals of counsel's submissions* (paragraphs 77 and 85, both footnoted to written submissions of intervening senior advocates) and in Kaul J's narrative. It is not in any conclusion of any of the three opinions. **Anyone who says the Supreme Court upheld the abrogation of Article 35A is over-reading the judgment.** The Court upheld C.O. 272 paragraph 2 in part and C.O. 273 in full; 35A went by paragraph 1(2), which nobody framed an issue on.
3. **The counterfactual is unmeasured and unmeasurable.** Because 35A protected State laws from challenge rather than creating rights directly, what changed on 5 August 2019 is that the J&K permanent-resident laws became *challengeable* — and separately, they were repealed by the Reorganisation Act's Fifth Schedule and replaced by domicile rules. Which of those two did the work on any given entitlement is not established by any document I retrieved. Domicile and land law are another child's subject in this phase; the boundary I draw is that **the instrument that removed the shield is mine, and the instruments that replaced the underlying rules are theirs.**

### 1.5 The one textual discrepancy between two T1 reproductions of C.O. 272

I have C.O. 272 paragraph 2 from two independent official sources. **They differ.**

- **R2** (Legislative Department, Constitution of India, Appendix II), clause (4)(c): *"…acting on **the advice** of his Council of Ministers…"*
- **R3** (Supreme Court judgment, page 256), clause (4)(c): *"…acting on **the aid and advice** of his Council of Ministers…"*

Two words. Nothing turns on it substantively — "aid and advice" is the Constitution's own formula in Articles 74 and 163 and the shorter form is read the same way. **It is recorded because it is a fact about the evidence, not about the world: the operative text of the instrument at the centre of the dispute is reproduced differently by the Legislative Department and by the Supreme Court, and the Gazette original was not retrieved by me from either host, so I cannot say which reproduction is faithful.** A record quoting C.O. 272(4)(c) verbatim must name which reproduction it is quoting.

### 1.6 A first-class finding: the official Constitution still carries the substitution the Court struck down — and carries it garbled

**R2 is dated 1 May 2024 — four and a half months after the judgment of 11 December 2023.** Its text of Article 370(3) reads, verbatim, at page 233:

> "Provided that the recommendation of the ¹[clause (2) of Legislative Assembly of the State] shall be necessary before the President issues such a notification."

with footnote:

> "1. Subs. by C.O. 272, dated the 5-8-2019, s.2. for "Constituent Assembly of the State referred to in clause (2)" (w.e.f. 5-8-2019)."

Three things are wrong with that page, and all three are visible on its face.

1. **The Supreme Court held on 11 December 2023 that "Paragraph 2 of CO 272 by which Article 370 was amended through Article 367 is ultra vires Article 370(1)(d)"** (R3, conclusion 514(g)). The Legislative Department's edition, published afterwards, prints the substitution as good law and makes no reference to the judgment anywhere in the article, the footnotes, or the preface.
2. **The printed substitution is textually corrupt.** C.O. 272(4)(d) directed that the expression "Constituent Assembly of the State referred to in clause (2)" *shall read* "Legislative Assembly of the State" — a whole-phrase substitution. The edition has applied it as though only the words "Constituent Assembly of the State referred to in" were replaced, leaving "clause (2) of" stranded in front. The result — "the recommendation of the clause (2) of Legislative Assembly of the State" — is not English and is not what C.O. 272 said.
3. **The preface presents the Appendices as neutral reference.** Verbatim: *"The Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019 and the declaration under article 370(3) of the Constitution have been provided respectively in Appendix II and Appendix III for reference."* No note that any part of Appendix II has been declared ultra vires.

This is a `not-published` absence of a specific and unusually clean kind: **the correction exists, was made by the highest court, is public, and has not been carried into the official text.** The route is real and named in §7.

**Both sides of it, in their own terms.** The Legislative Department's position, which it has not stated but which is the obvious one: the Court held paragraph 2 ultra vires *and in the same breath held that the President could have achieved the identical result under Article 370(1)(d)*, and that C.O. 273 is valid; Article 370 is inoperative either way; the appendix is printed "for reference" and the footnote records what the instrument did, not what a court later said about it. The contrary position: a pocket edition of the Constitution issued by the Government of India is the document a citizen, a magistrate and a law student read, and it currently tells them that a proviso reads in a way five judges of the Supreme Court unanimously said it cannot; and the garbling shows the entry was never checked at all. **These rest on the same facts, weighted differently — this is not a `differentFacts` case.**

## 2. THE JAMMU AND KASHMIR REORGANISATION ACT, 2019 — THE INSTRUMENT ITSELF

Everything in §2 is from **R1**, the Gazette text, RETRIEVED and read directly. **Phase 11 flagged twice that it could not retrieve the bare Act and that neither s.32(1) nor s.46(1) "may be quoted as verbatim primary" (part 10 §1, L-0123 sources, P-86 sources). That gap is now closed. §2.4 states the amendments this implies for the live records; it does not re-author them.**

Structure, counted from R1: **103 sections in 14 Parts, plus five Schedules.** The Fifth Schedule contains four tables.

### 2.1 The boundary I am drawing against L-0123

L-0123 is titled "Two J&K security quantities lost their only legislative route in 2018, and it did not resume in 2024". It uses s.32(1) and s.46(1)(c) **as answerability**: whether a security quantity can be asked about and answered. Its scope note is explicit — "the assembly, the elections and the restoration of statehood are outside this record's subject and are not scored here."

**My record is the reorganisation as an act of institutional design.** The same two sections appear, but for a different property: not "can a question be asked" but "what kind of legislature was created". Concretely, this part owns:

- the **four-way disposition of the statute book** (§2.3), which L-0123 does not touch at all;
- the **creation of the two Union territories and the change of constitutional class** (Article 1 First Schedule entry moved from "THE STATES" to "UNION TERRITORIES");
- the **permanent narrowing of legislative competence as a design feature of the new unit**, alongside the parallel provisions — s.53(2) on the Lieutenant Governor's discretionary domain, s.55 on rules for the conduct of business, s.73 on failure of constitutional machinery — which together define what the UT legislature *is*;
- **statehood restoration** (§6), which L-0123 expressly excludes.

The overlap is exactly two section numbers, used for two different purposes. **No amendment to L-0123 is proposed on scope. The amendment proposed at §2.4 is purely evidentiary — the sources on L-0123 and P-86 say the Act text was not retrieved, and it now has been.**

### 2.2 The territorial and constitutional surgery, verbatim

- **s.2(a)**: *"'appointed day' means the day which the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint"*. **The Act as passed did not name 31 October 2019.** The date was fixed by executive notification, S.O. 2889(E) of 9 August 2019 (established from R3 paragraph 12, which is the Court's own recital; I did not retrieve S.O. 2889(E) itself).
- **s.3**: forms the UT of Ladakh "comprising the following territories of the existing State of Jammu and Kashmir, namely:— 'Kargil and Leh districts'". **Two districts named, and nothing else.**
- **s.4**: forms the UT of Jammu and Kashmir comprising "the territories of the existing State of Jammu and Kashmir **other than those specified in section 3**." So the UT of J&K is defined as a residue.
- **s.5**: the Governor of the existing State becomes the **common Lieutenant Governor** of both UTs "for such period as may be determined by the President."
- **s.6**: in the **First Schedule to the Constitution**, under "I. THE STATES", *"entry 15 shall be deleted"* and entries 16 to 29 renumbered 15 to 28; under "II. UNION TERRITORIES", new entries 8 (Jammu and Kashmir) and 9 (Ladakh) inserted. **This is the whole of the downgrading, in three lines of a schedule amendment.**
- **s.8**: Fourth Schedule to the Constitution (Council of States) — entry 21 deleted, entries renumbered, new entry *"31. Jammu and Kashmir ……4"*. The four Rajya Sabha seats survive the change of class.
- **s.10**: five Lok Sabha seats to the UT of J&K, one to the UT of Ladakh.
- **s.13**: *"the provisions contained in article 239A, which are applicable to 'Union territory of Puducherry', shall also apply to the 'Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir'."* **The template is Puducherry.**
- **s.14(3)**: total directly-elected seats **107**. **s.14(4)**: *"until the area of the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir under the occupation of Pakistan ceases to be so occupied and the people residing in that area elect their representatives — (a) twenty four seats … shall remain vacant and shall not be taken into account for reckoning the total membership of the Assembly"*. So 107 minus 24 = **83 fillable seats as enacted**; delimitation later took the figure to 90, which is another child's subject.
- **s.32(1)**, verbatim, now primary: *"Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Legislative Assembly may make laws for the whole or any part of the Union territory of Jammu and Kashmir with respect to any of the matters enumerated in the State List **except the subjects mentioned at entries 1 and 2, namely "Public Order" and "Police"** respectively or the Concurrent List in the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution of India in so far as any such matter is applicable in relation to the Union territories."*
- **s.46(1) proviso (c)**, verbatim, now primary: *"Provided that the Lieutenant Governor shall, after consultation with the Speaker of the Legislative Assembly, make rules— … (c) for prohibiting the discussion of, or the asking of questions on, any matter which affects the discharge of the functions of the Lieutenant Governor in so far as he is required by this Act to act in his discretion."* Note the verb: **"shall … make rules"**, not "may". On the face of the Act the Lieutenant Governor is *obliged* to make rules under (a), (b) and (c) after consulting the Speaker. Phase 11 could not establish whether such rules exist (part 10, COULD NOT ESTABLISH). **That question is now sharper, not answered: the Act mandates the rules; whether they were made remains unestablished, and the mandatory wording makes their absence, if they are absent, a separate fact worth having.**
- **s.53(2)**, which phase 11 never reached: the Lieutenant Governor *"shall, in the exercise of his functions, act in his discretion in a matter: (i) which falls outside the purview of the powers conferred on the Legislative Assembly; or (ii) in which he is required by or under any law to act in his discretion or to exercise any judicial functions. (iii) **related to All India Services and Anti Corruption Bureau**: Provided that if any question arises whether any matter is or is not a matter as respects which the Lieutenant Governor is by or under this Act required to act in his discretion, **the decision of the Lieutenant Governor in his discretion shall be final**, and the validity of anything done by the Lieutenant Governor shall not be called in question on the ground that he ought or ought not to have acted in his discretion."* **s.53(3)**: *"The question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the Lieutenant Governor shall not be inquired into in any court."*

  **s.53(2)(i) read with s.32(1) is the load-bearing join, and it is a closed loop.** Public Order and Police fall outside the Assembly's powers by s.32(1); anything outside the Assembly's powers is the Lieutenant Governor's discretion by s.53(2)(i); a dispute about whether something is in his discretion is decided by him, finally, by the proviso; and s.46(1)(c) obliges him to make rules barring questions on matters affecting his discretionary functions. **The bar on questioning and the allocation of subject matter are the same provision seen twice.** This is the structural fact L-0123 records the consequences of; the mechanism belongs here.
- **s.55(1)**: the Lieutenant Governor makes rules for allocation of business **"on the advice of the Council of Ministers"** — a different and narrower formula than s.46(1), where no advice is required and only consultation with the Speaker is.
- **s.73**: on a report from the Lieutenant Governor or otherwise, the President may **"suspend the operation of all or any of the provisions of this Act"**. This is the President's-rule analogue for the UT and is the section under which S.O. 4484(E) of 13 October 2024 revoked President's rule (R4, MHA AR 2024-25 para 15.5, retrieved).

The Act does contain a transfer provision, **s.100 "Transfer of pending proceedings"**, and it is not limited to courts: *"Every proceeding pending immediately before the appointed day before a court (other than High Court), **tribunal, authority or officer** … shall … stand transferred to the **corresponding** court, tribunal, authority or officer of that Union territory."* But s.100(3)(b) defines "corresponding" as *"(i) the court, tribunal, authority or officer in which, or before whom, the proceeding would have laid **if it had been instituted after the appointed day**; or (ii) in case of doubt, such … as may be determined after the appointed day by the Government or administration of that Union territory, or the Central Government…"*.

**So the machinery exists and cannot reach the SHRC's complaints.** A complaint that would have lain before the J&K State Human Rights Commission could not have been instituted after the appointed day at all, because Table-3 entry 117 repealed the Commission's enabling Act on that very day. There is no body in which it "would have laid", so limb (i) is empty; limb (ii) leaves it to the UT administration or the Central Government to nominate a substitute, and I retrieved no document showing any such determination was made. **The correct statement is not "no savings provision" but "a general transfer provision whose own definition of a corresponding authority cannot be satisfied where the Act itself abolished the authority, with a discretionary fallback that no retrieved document shows was exercised."** I state this as an amendment for stage 3 to weigh; I have not re-authored L-0121. It slightly *weakens* the bare wording of the existing `caseAgainst` and slightly *strengthens* the finding, because it shows Parliament turned its mind to pending proceedings in general and the abolished commissions still fell through.

## 4. IN RE: ARTICLE 370 OF THE CONSTITUTION — 11 DECEMBER 2023

All of §4 from **R3**, RETRIEVED, T1, read in full. Citation: 2023 INSC 1058, WP(C) No. 1099 of 2019 with twenty-two connected matters. Bench of five: **Dr D Y Chandrachud CJI, Sanjay Kishan Kaul, Sanjiv Khanna, B R Gavai and Surya Kant JJ.** Three opinions: the CJI's, for himself, Gavai and Surya Kant JJ; a concurring opinion of Kaul J; a three-page concurring opinion of Khanna J. **Result: writ petitions and SLPs disposed of; no petition allowed.**

### 4.1 The eight questions the Court framed for determination — verbatim, paragraph 86

> a. Whether the provisions of Article 370 were temporary in nature or whether they acquired a status of permanence in the Constitution;
> b. Whether the amendment to Article 367 in exercise of the power under Article 370(1)(d) so as to substitute the reference to the "Constituent Assembly of the State referred to in clause (3) of Article 370 by the words "Legislative Assembly of the State" is constitutionally valid;
> c. Whether the entire Constitution of India could have been applied to the State of Jammu and Kashmir in exercise of the power under Article 370(1)(d);
> d. Whether the abrogation of Article 370 by the President in exercise of the power under Article 370(3) is constitutionally invalid in the absence of a recommendation of the Constituent Assembly of the State of Jammu and Kashmir as mandated by the proviso to clause (3);
> e. Whether the proclamation of the Governor dated 20 June 2018 … and the subsequent exercise of power on 21 November 2018, under Section 53(2) of the Constitution of Jammu and Kashmir to dissolve the Legislative Assembly are constitutionally valid;
> f. Whether the Proclamation which was issued by the President under Article 356 of the Constitution on 19 December 2018 and the subsequent extensions are constitutionally valid;
> g. Whether the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act 2019 … is constitutionally valid bearing in mind: i. The first proviso to Article 3 …; and ii. The second proviso to Article 3 …;
> h. Whether during the tenure of a Proclamation under Article 356, and when the Legislative Assembly of the State is either dissolved or is in suspended animation the status of the State of Jammu and Kashmir as a State under Article 1(3)(a) of the Constitution and its conversion into a Union Territory under Article 1(3)(b) constitutes a valid exercise of power.

**Note what is absent from the framing: Article 35A.** It is not question (a) to (h). See §1.4.

### 4.2 The fourteen conclusions — the operative text, paragraph 514

Reproduced in compressed form; the sub-paragraphs that matter are verbatim.

| (a) | J&K "does not retain any element of sovereignty after the execution of the IoA and the issuance of the Proclamation dated 25 November 1949"; no "internal sovereignty" distinguishable from other States. **"Article 370 was a feature of asymmetric federalism and not sovereignty."** |
| (b) | The challenge to the s.92 and Article 356 Proclamations "does not merit adjudication" — the petitioners did not challenge them until the special status was abrogated. |
| (c) | Exercise of power by the President after an Article 356 Proclamation **is subject to judicial review**; the challenger must prima facie establish mala fides or extraneous exercise, then the onus shifts to the Union. |
| (d) | Parliament's power under Article 356(1)(b) is not confined to law-making; it extends to non-law-making powers of the State Legislature. |

### 4.3 What the Court DECIDED about the Article 367 route — and it is the opposite of the common summary

**The Court did rule on the Article 367 route, and it ruled against it.** Conclusion (g) is unambiguous: paragraph 2 of C.O. 272, in so far as it added clause (4) to Article 367 to make "Constituent Assembly" read "Legislative Assembly", **is ultra vires**. Kaul J agreed in his conclusion (g): *"Article 367, which is an interpretation clause, cannot be used to alter the character of a provision. Therefore, Paragraph 2 of C.O. 272, which amends Article 367(4) is ultra vires Article 370."* Khanna J agreed: *"Paragraph (2) of C.O. 272 by which Article 370 was amended by taking recourse to Article 367 is ultra vires and bad in law."*

**The reasoning, paragraphs 383–385**, is worth having because it is the strongest statement in the judgment of the point the petitioners were making:

> "CO 272 changes the language to the proviso to Article 370(3) in two ways. First, it changes the recommending body from the Constituent Assembly to the Legislative Assembly; and second, it makes a new arrangement at variance with that specific Constituent Assembly." … "Both these changes are not insignificant because they modify the essential character of the proviso by substituting a particular type or kind of body with another type or kind entirely. There are myriad differences between a Constituent Assembly and Legislative Assembly. A Constituent Assembly is tasked with framing a Constitution in exercise of constituent power… Not having been entrusted with the responsibility to do this, **the Legislative Assembly cannot be equated to the Constituent Assembly.**"

**And then it did not matter.** The route was struck down and the outcome preserved by two moves: (i) conclusion (h) — the President could have applied the whole Constitution under Article 370(1)(d) *without* the State's concurrence, because the effect equals an Article 370(3) exercise; (ii) conclusion (j) — the President could declare under Article 370(3) *without* any recommendation at all, because the proviso's recommending body no longer existed. Khanna J states the structure in one sentence: the paragraph is *"ultra vires and bad in law, **albeit can be sustained in view of the corresponding power under Article 370(1)(d)**."*

**Precision that matters for the record.** The common formulation "the Court did not rule on the Article 367 route" is **wrong**. The correct formulation is: *the Court held the Article 367 route invalid, and held that the same result was independently reachable without it, so the invalidity changed nothing.* A record that says the Court declined to decide this is misstating the judgment; a record that says the Court upheld the route is also misstating it.

### 4.4 The self-concurrence problem — the Court named it, then held it did not arise

This is the passage that most directly meets the case against, and the Court's own words go a long way toward it before turning. **Paragraph 426**, verbatim:

> "The judgment of the Constitution Bench in Damnoo (supra) holds that the fundamental character of representative democracy underlines the provisos to Article 370(1)(d) and 370(1)(b) by which the concurrence and consultation of the Government of the State is required before the President issues an order… the power under Article 370(1)(b) and 370(1)(d) could only be exercised with the collaboration between the Union and the State. **The purpose which the condition seeks to serve (collaboration between the federal units and representative democracy) would be lost if the President secures his own concurrence while exercising the power.**"

And the Court's own statement of who actually concurred, **paragraph 421**, verbatim:

> "The CO states that the President issued the CO 'with the concurrence of the Government of the State of Jammu and Kashmir'. The phrase Government of the State as it occurs in Article 370 was defined in CO 1965 to mean the Governor on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers. **However, it was the President giving concurrence** since the Governor had by then dissolved the Legislative Assembly of State and the President by the 2018 Proclamation assumed to himself 'all the functions of the Government of the said State and all powers vested in or exercisable by the Governor of that State under the Constitution'."

**So the Court found, as a fact, that the Union concurred with itself.** It then held (paragraph 427) that this was not invalid, on six grounds, of which the operative ones are: applying the whole Constitution under 370(1)(d) "is the same as" an Article 370(3) exercise except that the former is reversible; the President can act unilaterally under 370(3); "the principle of consultation and collaboration underlying the provisos to Article 370(1)(d) would not be applicable where the effect of the provision is the same as Article 370(3)"; and therefore *"the concurrence of the Government of the State under the second proviso to Article 370(1)(d) was not required to be secured in the first place"*; and, finally, since it was not required, securing it from the Union "is not mala fide."

**The shape of that answer should be recorded exactly, because it is unusual.** The Court did not hold that the Governor validly represented the State. It held that **no representation of the State was needed**, which makes the recital in C.O. 272's own preamble — "with the concurrence of the Government of State of Jammu and Kashmir" — surplusage. The instrument asserts a concurrence the Court says was unnecessary and which the Court says the Union gave to itself.

### 4.5 What the Court expressly DECLINED to decide

**One question, and it is question (h): whether a State can be converted into a Union Territory.**

Conclusion (m) is the whole of it: *"In view of the statement we do not find it necessary to determine whether the reorganisation of the State of Jammu and Kashmir into two Union Territories of Ladakh and Jammu and Kashmir is permissible under Article 3."* The trigger is a statement of counsel: *"The Solicitor General stated that the statehood of Jammu and Kashmir will be restored (except for the carving out of the Union Territory of Ladakh)."*

Kaul J puts the linkage in a materially different form — his conclusion (l): *"The challenge to Section 4 of the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganization Act on the touchstone of Article 3 is not required to be debated **on account of the assurance on behalf of the Government of India that the Statehood of Jammu & Kashmir would be restored on elections being held**."*

**These two formulations are not the same and the difference is load-bearing.** The CJI's conclusion (n) states two separate obligations — elections **by 30 September 2024**, and restoration of statehood **"at the earliest and as soon as possible"** — with no stated dependence between them. Kaul J's conclusion (l) states restoration as conditional on elections being held. **On the CJI's formulation, holding elections does not discharge the statehood undertaking; on Kaul J's, the condition for it has been satisfied since October 2024.** I retrieved no document that reconciles them. Any record on statehood restoration must say which opinion it is reading.

**Two further things the Court did not decide, both by omission rather than by declining:**
- **The validity of the removal of Article 35A** (§1.4). Not framed, not decided.
- **What follows if statehood is not restored.** Conclusion (n) attaches a date to the elections and no date to the restoration. The Court issued a *direction* on elections ("We direct that steps shall be taken by the Election Commission of India…") and a *statement in the imperative without an addressee* on statehood ("Restoration of statehood shall take place at the earliest and as soon as possible"). **No body is named, no date is fixed, and no consequence is specified.** That asymmetry inside a single sub-paragraph is the single most consequential textual fact in this part.

### 4.6 The concurring opinions — including a divergence that Khanna J's opinion describes as agreement

**Kaul J's conclusions (paragraph 112)** track the majority on outcome but not on premise. His conclusion (a), verbatim:

> "In light of this Court's prior finding in Prem Nath Kaul, **the State of Jammu and Kashmir retained an element of internal sovereignty** despite Maharaja Hari Singh signing the IoA with the Dominion. Article 370 of the Constitution recognized this internal sovereignty by recognizing the Constituent Assembly of the State"

and his conclusion (c): "Article 370(3) contained the mechanism to bring the temporary arrangement to an end, and in turn, **to de-recognize the internal sovereignty of the State** and apply the Constitution of India in toto".

**The CJI's conclusion (a) is the direct negative**: J&K "does not have 'internal sovereignty' which is distinguishable from the powers and privileges enjoyed by other States"; "Article 370 was a feature of asymmetric federalism and not sovereignty."

**Khanna J's three-page opinion says both judgments "uniformly agree that Article 370 of the Constitution of India was a feature of asymmetric federalism and not sovereignty", and concurs with both**: *"I find it difficult to state that I agree with the reasoning in one and not the other. I, therefore, respectfully concur with the two judgments."*

**Record this precisely and do not editorialise it.** On the face of the two opinions, one says internal sovereignty existed and was de-recognised in 2019; the other says it never existed after 1949. A third opinion characterises them as uniform and concurs with both. **Whether this is a genuine doctrinal split or two routes to one destination is a question about the judgment that the judgment does not answer, and it is not for this instrument to settle. What the instrument can record is that the ratio on the sovereignty question is not single.**

**Khanna J also entered the one caution about UT conversion that anybody on the bench entered**, paragraph 6, verbatim:

> "Union Territories are normally geographically small territories, or may be created for aberrant reasons or causes. **Conversion of a State into Union Territory has grave consequences, amongst others, it denies the citizens of the State an elected state government and impinges on federalism. Conversion/creation of a Union Territory from a State has to be justified by giving very strong and cogent grounds. It must be in strict compliance with Article 3 of the Constitution of India.**"

He said this in the same opinion in which he agreed the question need not be determined. **A standard was stated and expressly not applied.**

**Kaul J's Epilogue (paragraphs 113–125)** recommends *"the setting up of an impartial truth and reconciliation commission"* to *"investigate and report on the violation of human rights both by State and non-State actors perpetrated in Jammu & Kashmir at least since the 1980s and recommend measures for reconciliation"*, "set up expediently, before memory escapes", "time-bound", with the manner left to the Government. He acknowledges the objection himself: *"I am alive to the challenge that recommending the setting up of a truth and reconciliation is beyond the realm of this Court."*

**The finding, stated flatly.** The Court set 30 September 2024. **The final phase of polling was held on 1 October 2024, votes were counted on 8 October 2024, and the ECI's own stated date for completion of the election was 10 October 2024.** On the face of the retrieved documents, the election was not completed by 30 September 2024.

**Both readings, in their own terms, because this is genuinely arguable and the record should not pick.**

*The reading on which the direction was complied with.* The direction is that **"steps shall be taken by the Election Commission of India to conduct elections … by 30 September 2024"** — the object of "by 30 September 2024" is grammatically the taking of steps, and the ECI announced the full schedule on 16 August 2024, issued the Phase-3 gazette notification on 5 September 2024, and held two of three phases in September. On any reading the election was in progress on 30 September. The residual slippage is one polling day and a counting date, both driven by ordinary electoral logistics, and the counting date moved only because the Haryana poll moved for a reason recorded in R9 that has nothing to do with J&K (a religious festival affecting the Bishnoi community). The elected Assembly was constituted and President's rule revoked within a fortnight of the deadline.

*The reading on which it was not.* A judicial direction to conduct elections by a date is not discharged by having conducted some of them by that date. Five judges chose a specific date in a case about the removal of an elected legislature, and the date passed with the poll unfinished and no vote counted. **The ECI's own original schedule, announced on 16 August 2024, already put counting on 4 October — so the body under the direction planned, from the outset, to finish after the date.** No extension was sought or granted so far as any document I retrieved shows. And the ECI's press material (R10) does not mention the direction, the deadline, or the judgment at all: the release celebrates the poll at length and never says it was court-ordered.

**These rest on the same facts, differently weighted. Not a `differentFacts` case.** Both sides accept every date in the table.

**Absence found here, and it is small but clean:** *no document I retrieved — not the ECI releases, not the MHA Annual Reports, not any of 2,839 parliamentary answers — records the Government or the Election Commission addressing the 30 September 2024 date at all.* Neither claiming compliance nor explaining the slippage. See §7.

## 8. THE LEDGER QUESTION — `differentFacts` criterion (c), answered explicitly

**I read the criterion in `schemas/ledger.schema.json` before answering.** It reads: three conditions, of which (c) is operative — *"no single measure exists, or could be constructed from available data, that places both sides' facts on one ledger. An UNBUILT comparison FAILS (c)… A counterpart that is DECLARED UNMEASURABLE passes (c). In practice (c) admits only arguments that cross a category boundary — a quantity against a legal ruling, a constitutional process, or a stated absence."*

**Answer: `differentFacts` is TRUE for the core reorganisation record, and it is one of the cleanest instances the criterion describes.**

**(a) — do the two cases cite different underlying quantities, measures or events?** Yes. The case FOR rests on (i) a **count** — 106 Central Acts applied by Table-1, 337 State-law entries disposed of, the whole Constitution applied — (ii) a **legal ruling**, the upholding of C.O. 273 and of the whole-Constitution application, and (iii) a **normative claim** about equal citizenship. The case AGAINST rests on (i) a **constitutional process** — who supplied the concurrence and who supplied the recommendation, established from four dated instruments — (ii) a **declined adjudication**, conclusion (m), and (iii) a **statutory competence limit**, s.32(1) read with s.53(2). These are not the same objects weighted differently. **(a) satisfied.**

**(b) — does granting one side's strongest fact in full leave the other's case intact?** Yes, and demonstrably so **within a single document**. The judgment itself grants both: it holds C.O. 273 valid and the Constitution of J&K redundant, *and* it finds as a fact at paragraph 421 that "it was the President giving concurrence", *and* it says at paragraph 426 that the purpose of the concurrence requirement "would be lost if the President secures his own concurrence". Neither side needs to deny anything the other asserts. **(b) satisfied.**

**(c) — could a single measure be constructed from available data that places both sides' facts on one ledger?** **No, and not because it merely has not been built.**

The test the criterion sets is whether a common denominator exists *in principle*. Try to construct one and it fails at the category boundary every time:
- A **rights-and-entitlements outcome measure** — what residents can now do that they could not before — would take the whole of the FOR case and none of the AGAINST case, because the AGAINST case is not a claim about outcomes. Granting every outcome the FOR side claims leaves "the Union concurred with itself" exactly where it was.
- A **procedural-legitimacy measure** does not exist, is not constructible, and could not be: its inputs would be a court's decision to decline a question and the identity of a consenting authority, neither of which is a quantity.
- The **counterpart to the 106 is declared unmeasurable by the highest available authority**: conclusion (m) says the Court "do[es] not find it necessary to determine" whether a State may be converted into a Union Territory. **That is a counterpart declared unadjudicated, which the criterion says passes (c).** There is no number, and no ruling, on the other side of the count.

The criterion says (c) "admits only arguments that cross a category boundary — a quantity against a legal ruling, a constitutional process, or a stated absence." **This argument crosses all three at once**: a count of laws against a constitutional process (who consented), against a legal ruling that was expressly not made, against a stated commitment with no date. **`differentFacts`: TRUE.**

### 8.1 Does any single instrument place both sides' facts on one ledger?

**One document holds both sets of facts. No document places them on a common measure. That distinction is the answer, and it should be stated in those words.**

The judgment of 11 December 2023 is the single instrument that contains, in one text: the count of what was applied; the holding that the process was valid; the finding that the Union concurred with itself; the holding that the Article 367 route was ultra vires; the refusal to decide the Union-Territory question; and the undertaking that statehood will be restored. **A record that wanted one source for both cases has one.** But the judgment does not adjudicate between them on a shared metric — it disposes of the petitions by holding that most of the AGAINST case's facts, granted in full, do not lead where the petitioners said they led, and by declining the one question on which they might have. **A common document is not a common ledger.**

Draft, for stage 3 to use or discard: *"The case for counts instruments and outcomes — 106 Central Acts applied by the Fifth Schedule, the whole Constitution applied by C.O. 272, a temporary provision ended by C.O. 273 and upheld by a unanimous Constitution Bench. ## 9. THE TWO CASES, EACH AT ITS STRONGEST, IN ITS OWN TERMS AND ON ITS OWN PREFERRED EVIDENCE

Written to be usable as `caseFor` / `caseAgainst`. Each uses the evidence its own side would choose.

### 9.1 The case FOR

Article 370 was, in the Constitution's own heading, a **temporary** provision, placed in Part XXI among "Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions" — and a unanimous Constitution Bench so held, from "the historical context for the inclusion of Article 370 and the placement of Article 370 in Part XXI" (conclusion (e)). Its own mechanism for ending, Article 370(3), vested an unqualified power in the President; the Court held the power "did not cease to exist upon the dissolution of the Constituent Assembly" (conclusion (f)) and that the President "can unilaterally issue a notification that Article 370 ceases to exist" (conclusion (h)). The process was not executive fiat: **both Houses of Parliament passed resolutions recommending the declaration, on 5 and 6 August 2019, and both Houses passed the Reorganisation Bill.** Article 3's first proviso requires the State legislature's *views*, and the Court held those views are "recommendatory" (conclusion (l)) — a holding about Article 3 generally, not a concession made for this case.

The result reaches individuals. Before 5 August 2019 the residents of J&K stood outside a long list of Central statutes. MHA's own answer of 7 February 2024 lists what that meant in practice: the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act 2009, the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition Act 2013, the Forest Rights Act 2006, the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989, the Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014, the Juvenile Justice Act 2015, the National Commission for Minorities Act 1992 and the National Commission for Safai Karmacharis Act 1993 — *"Now, residents of the region are enjoying rights which are available to citizens in other parts of the country."* **The Fifth Schedule's own arithmetic bears that out: 106 Central Acts, of which 91 were applied by deleting the words "except the State of Jammu and Kashmir" from statutes that already protected every other Indian.** Article 35A had made those exclusions unchallengeable; its removal restored the ordinary position that a law discriminating between citizens can be tested against Part III.

The Court examined every challenge and rejected each: sovereignty (a), the Proclamations (b) and (f), the scope of Article 356 (d), the whole-Constitution application (i), C.O. 273 (j). Where it found a defect — the Article 367 route — it held the identical result was independently available under Article 370(1)(d) and Article 370(3), so nothing turned on it: as Khanna J put it, *"ultra vires and bad in law, albeit can be sustained in view of the corresponding power under Article 370(1)(d)."* **Not one petition was allowed.** And the Union's undertaking on statehood was honoured in the part that had a date attached to it: the assembly was elected in three phases in September–October 2024, counted on 8 October, and President's rule revoked on 13 October 2024 — an elected government in Srinagar for the first time in six years, on the Election Commission's own account without a single repoll, with turnout above the Lok Sabha 2024 figure and with polling stations set up in 469 locations on the Line of Control and International Border.

### 9.2 The case AGAINST

**The concurrence of the State was given by the Union to itself, and the Supreme Court found this as a fact.** Paragraph 421, verbatim: *"the CO states that the President issued the CO 'with the concurrence of the Government of the State of Jammu and Kashmir'… **However, it was the President giving concurrence** since the Governor had by then dissolved the Legislative Assembly of State and the President by the 2018 Proclamation assumed to himself 'all the functions of the Government of the said State'."* Paragraph 426, verbatim: *"The purpose which the condition seeks to serve (collaboration between the federal units and representative democracy) **would be lost if the President secures his own concurrence** while exercising the power."* The Court did not hold that the Governor validly stood in for the State; it held that **no consent of the State was needed at all** — which leaves C.O. 272's own recital asserting a concurrence the Court says was both unnecessary and self-given.

**The recommendation was supplied by the same route.** Article 370(3)'s proviso required the recommendation of the Constituent Assembly of the State. C.O. 272 changed that to the Legislative Assembly. The Legislative Assembly had been dissolved by a Union-appointed Governor on 21 November 2018 and its powers vested in Parliament by the President on 19 December 2018. C.O. 273 therefore recites the recommendation of **Parliament**. **At no point did any body chosen by the electorate of Jammu and Kashmir act on any of it** — and the Court itself held the interpretive device that made this possible was **ultra vires**, in terms that concede the objection: *"the Legislative Assembly cannot be equated to the Constituent Assembly"*; *"An interpretation clause cannot be used to bypass the procedure laid down for amendment."* The vice was named and then held not to matter.

**A State was reduced to a Union Territory, and the Court declined to say whether that is permitted.** Question (h) was framed for determination and answered with conclusion (m): *"In view of the statement we do not find it necessary to determine whether the reorganisation… is permissible under Article 3."* The statement in view of which it was not determined was counsel's, not a statute's and not an order's — and **as at August 2026 it remains unperformed.** Khanna J, concurring, set the standard that was not applied: conversion of a State into a Union Territory *"has grave consequences… it denies the citizens of the State an elected state government and impinges on federalism… has to be justified by giving very strong and cogent grounds. It must be in strict compliance with Article 3."*

**The narrowing of the new legislature is permanent on the face of the Act and self-sealing.** s.32(1) removes "Public Order" and "Police" from the Assembly's competence. s.53(2)(i) makes everything outside the Assembly's competence the Lieutenant Governor's discretion. The proviso to s.53(2) makes **his own decision on the scope of his discretion final and unreviewable on that ground**, and s.53(3) bars any court from inquiring what advice Ministers gave. s.46(1)(c) obliges him to make rules **prohibiting questions** on matters affecting his discretionary functions. **The subject-matter allocation and the bar on questioning it are the same provision seen twice**, and phase 11 documented three occasions in the restored Assembly's first year on which questions or debates were refused on exactly these grounds — including a question about ex-gratia to families of victims of violence.

**And the disposition of the statute book chose.** The same Fifth Schedule, on the same day, repealed the **J&K Protection of Human Rights Act 1997** (Table-3 entry 117, extinguishing the State Human Rights Commission with roughly 630 complaints pending), the **J&K Accountability Commission Act 2002** (Table-3 entry 1), the **J&K State Vigilance Commission Act 2011** (entry 164) and the **J&K State Commission for Women Act 1999** (entry 23) — while **retaining the J&K Public Safety Act 1978** (Table-4 entry 123), the preventive-detention statute. **Four accountability institutions repealed; the detention power kept.** That is not an inference from tone; it is five entries in one schedule.

**Finally, the undertaking has no date and has never had one.** "Once the situation normalises" (Home Minister, Lok Sabha, 6 August 2019). "At an appropriate time" (MHA, Rajya Sabha, 15 December 2021). "Will be restored" (Solicitor General, Supreme Court, 11 December 2023). "At the earliest and as soon as possible" (the Court, same day). **Seven years, four formulations, no date** — and **in MHA's own published record of 2,839 parliamentary answers across 152 sitting dates, the subject has not been addressed at all since 15 December 2021.** MHA's Annual Report for 2024-25 mentions neither Article 370 nor statehood anywhere in the document.

### 9.3 Where the two cases actually diverge

**They do not disagree about a single fact in §9.1 or §9.2.** Every proposition on both sides is drawn from a retrieved primary document, and no proposition on either side contradicts one on the other. **They disagree about what a constitutional process is for.** The FOR case treats the process as instrumental — the question is whether the power existed and whether the outcome is lawful and beneficial. The AGAINST case treats the process as constitutive — the question is who was entitled to decide, and a valid outcome reached by the wrong decider is a different thing from a valid outcome. **That is a disagreement about the criterion of success, which is exactly why no common measure can be built, and it is the substance of the (c) finding in §8.**

## 10. THE TWO UNDECIDED ENUM SHAPES — my material produces one of each

**I propose no new enum value.** Per the brief, where my material produces a record of the L-0086 shape or the L-0092 shape I say so and say which written definition I would file it on.

### 10.1 An L-0086-shape record: the statehood undertaking

**Shape match: in force, testable in principle, awaiting external adjudication.** The undertaking stands and has never been withdrawn (in force). Whether statehood has been restored is a binary observable requiring no methodology (testable in principle). Applications to enforce it are pending before the Supreme Court (awaiting external adjudication). **This is the L-0086 shape almost exactly, and L-0086 is currently filed `too-early`.**

**Which written definition I would file it on, and my reservation.** On the definitions as written, `too-early` is the only value that fits: *"the measure is in force but has not run long enough for its stated objective to be testable."* The alternatives all fail on their own text — `no-objective` fails because an objective *was* stated four times; `contested` fails because the evidence supports only one reading of whether restoration has occurred (it has not); `failed` fails because an undertaking framed as "at the earliest and as soon as possible" cannot be said to have missed a deadline it never had; `reversed` fails because the enacting authority has not withdrawn anything.

**My reservation, stated so the decision-makers have it.** `too-early` is a strange word for a commitment now in its seventh year, made in Parliament in 2019, restated to the Supreme Court in 2023, and still undated in 2026. **The strain is not that the value is wrong on its text but that its text was written for a measure that needs time to work, and this is an undertaking that needs a decision.** That is precisely the gap the L-0086 shape names, and it is why the record should carry an `assessmentNote` saying the value may change on review. **I would file `too-early` and flag it. I would not invent a value.**

### 10.2 An L-0092-shape record: the official Constitution's uncorrected Article 370

**Shape match: a presentational finding.** Nothing in the world changed; what changed is what an official document says about the world (§1.6). The Legislative Department's edition of 1 May 2024 prints the C.O. 272 substitution as operative, four and a half months after five judges held it ultra vires, and prints it in a form — "the recommendation of the clause (2) of Legislative Assembly of the State" — that is not what C.O. 272 directed. **L-0092 is currently filed `contested`.**

**Which written definition I would file it on.** `contested`, on its written text: *"the evidence supports more than one defensible reading and the record does not choose between them."* The two readings are both available on the same document and are set out at the end of §1.6 — an appendix-for-reference-with-a-historical-footnote reading, and an official-text-misstates-the-law reading. **This is the same mechanism L-0092's own `assessmentNote` describes** ("a précis reading and a misrepresentation reading are both available on the same documents"). I would file `contested` with an `assessmentNote` in the same terms as L-0092's, and I would **not** file `failed` — nobody announced an objective for a pocket edition.

## 13. TRAPS FROM PHASE 11 — where they bite in this part

**Trap 1 — "a source that looks independent and is media-derived from official reporting is not a check."** It bites in exactly one place, and it is worth naming because the source is prestigious. **PIB releases R9 and R10 are not independent of the Election Commission; they are the Election Commission's press notes carried under a PIB byline.** R9 says so on its face — it opens *"The Election Commission of India had announced the schedule … vide Press Note No. ECI/PN/128/2024"*. So my election dates are **the ECI's own account of the ECI's own conduct, relayed by a government press agency**, at a moment when the ECI was under a judicial direction with a date attached. That does not make the dates wrong — they are administrative facts a rival would contradict instantly — but **PIB is not a check on the ECI and must not be presented as corroboration of it.** The proper check, `eci.gov.in`'s own record, returned 403 (§12 item 12).

The trap does *not* bite on R1, R2 or R3, which are the instruments themselves, nor on R7, which is the Ministry's own answers used as evidence of what the Ministry said.

**Trap 2 — "check WHO PRODUCES the series a ministry presents as its own."** It bites cleanly, on a document I retrieved while sweeping for statehood answers. **LS Unstarred Q.1995 of 11 March 2025, "REORGANISATION OF UNION TERRITORY OF LADAKH"**, asks whether health, education and energy infrastructure improved "since the reorganisation". MHA answers **"Yes sir"** and lays three before/after tables — hospital beds 824→1554, ICT labs 2→148, distribution transformers 1182→2781, and so on — introduced with the words, verbatim: *"The details thereof **as reported by Union Territory of Ladakh** are as under."*

**The Ministry of Home Affairs presents to Parliament, as evidence that the reorganisation worked, figures produced by the administration the reorganisation created — an administration run by a Lieutenant Governor appointed by the same Union, with no legislature at all in Ladakh's case.** The producer, the subject and the beneficiary of the claim are one authority. MHA discloses the source in the sentence, which is more than the J&K security tables do (P-86), and that disclosure should be credited. But **the answer contains no baseline definition, no note of what "before reorganization" means as a date, and no independent series.** Anyone tempted to read these as an outcome measure of the reorganisation should be stopped at this footnote. **I have not authored any of these figures and stage 3 should not.**

**A third trap, from this part's own material.** The communications blackout from August 2019 degraded media-derived series while leaving official series formally intact — the phase's standing rule. **It has a specific consequence here that is not obvious: the constitutional instruments of 5–6 August 2019 were issued during the period in which the population they applied to could not communicate.** That is a fact about the record of *reaction*, not about the instruments: there is no contemporaneous documentary record from within Kashmir of the response to C.O. 272 and C.O. 273, and there could not be. The official record of that week — the Gazette, the parliamentary resolutions, PIB — is complete and retrievable; the non-official record of the same week is structurally missing. **Two source families, same moment, opposite completeness.** Any record of August 2019 that draws only on what is retrievable is drawing only on the Union's side of the ledger, and should say so.
