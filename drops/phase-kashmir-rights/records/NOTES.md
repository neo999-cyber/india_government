## 1. THE DISCIPLINING-MEASURE FINDING — YES, and it is one series

Phase 9 found one: the OECD Producer Support Estimate, which defeated both partisan framings on farm
support because it nets budgetary support against implicit price transfers by construction. Phase 11 found
none. **Phase 12 has one, and it is NCRB's J&K detenu row in *Prison Statistics India*.**

**The series, thirteen years of it, from ten archive-retrieved volumes and three from the live host:**

> 182 (2009) · 409 (2010) · 239 (2011) · *no figure for 2012* · 72 (2013) · 35 (2014) · 90 (2015) ·
> **432 (2016)** · 212 (2017) · 283 (2018) · **404 (2019)** · 228 (2020) · 252 (2021) · **546 (2022)**

**It contradicts the chronology both sides share, and neither side quotes it.**

- **Against the government's framing.** The official case is that militancy collapsed after August 2019 and
  the territory normalised. Preventive detention in J&K's own prisons reached **its highest recorded level in
  2022 — 546, higher than 2016 and higher than 2019** — three years after the reorganisation, on the
  government's own statistical agency's own table.
- **Against the critics' framing.** The critical case treats 2019 as the great repression. **2016 exceeds it
  (432 against 404)**, and 2016 is the Burhan Wani year, before the reorganisation and under a State
  government. The same spike appears independently in the parliamentary "Detained" series at 395 against 7
  two years earlier — a fifty-six-fold rise, tabled by the Ministry itself.
- **And the 2019→2020 fall of 404 to 228, which both sides would read as a story, is partly an artefact of
  address.** PSI attributes a detenu to the State that holds the body. In the same interval the out-of-State
  detenu counts in the two receiving States also fell. PSI cannot distinguish release from transfer from
  COVID decongestion, and says so nowhere.

**How it differs from the PSE, stated so the claim is not over-read.** The PSE *nets* two quantities both
sides quote selectively. This series does not net anything — it **corrects a shared chronology**. It is a
corrective measure rather than a netting one, and that is a weaker form of the same virtue. Three caveats
limit it and are carried on the series itself: it is all preventive-detention laws and can never yield a PSA
figure; it is a 31 December stock, and J&K released 339 detenus during 2021 while holding 252 at year end; and
it excludes the detenus J&K moved out of the territory.

**Two runners-up, named because they are close and because naming them shows why they fall short.**

- **The land-area denominator.** 386 kanal — about 19.5 hectares, one part in 216,000 of the territory —
  transferred privately to non-residents over six years, against **2,359 hectares** of State land acquired
  for public purposes and **332 hectares** acquired by central agencies in Kashmir alone over roughly the same
  window. That discomfits both sides: demographic change by land purchase is not happening at scale, and the
  land question in J&K is not principally about outsiders buying. **It falls short because the two transfers
  are different acts** — a hostile reader will say public acquisition and private purchase are not
  commensurable, and they would have a point.
- **Working Paper I of the Delimitation Commission.** It prints population, area, density and seats in
  adjacent columns on one page, so both cases are computable from the criticised body's own document. **It
  falls short for the opposite reason: it produces three answers, not one** — 17.17 per cent on population, an
  inversion on area, 1.31 per cent on registered electors. An embarrassment of common measures is not a
  disciplining measure.

### One arithmetic defect found by stage 4, corrected — and its origin is in `parts/`

   253 in 2018 — with NCRB's foreign detenus accounting for the whole of each gap. **`bridgeExists` is set
   true on the strength of it**, and the implication is not stated by either instrument: the parliamentary
   series silently excludes foreign nationals, who in 2014 were **80 per cent of the detenus actually held**.
   2016 and 2017 are consistent and **not verified**, and 2019 cannot be tested because the dates differ.
2. **L-0146's `partly`.** It is the only scored-against-an-objective record in the drop and the objective is
   declared in `assessmentNote` because no field holds it. `contested` was considered and rejected: the facts
   are agreed and only their weight is argued, and the case against's central claim contradicts the case for
   rather than sitting beside it.
3. **The 197-day gap in L-0149.** Both endpoints come from the successor body's own published index — zero
   decisions against any J&K public authority for any order date up to 14 May 2020, earliest 15 May 2020.
   **It is a rare instance in this phase of an absence with hard edges on both sides**, and it should be
   checked, because most absences here have soft edges and this one does not.

4. **PR-47 is the only pair in the drop whose gap is computable**, and the contrast is worth reading as a
   finding about the phase rather than about the pair. Fourteen of fifteen pairs have `gapComputable: false`
   because the two sides are on different units, different populations, different dates or different
   instruments, or because one side does not exist. PR-47's two sides are **two columns of one annexure,
   printed side by side by the holder, in one unit, for one recipient and the same years** — which is what a
   published two-sided account looks like, and how rare it is in this material is the point. Note that the
   two figures the gap yields — 1,605.80 crore rupees unreleased and a 53.64 per cent release rate — are
   **derived and stated nowhere in the answer**, and are labelled as such on both series, on P-99 and in the
   pair's own notes.

## 8. RETRIEVAL DISCIPLINE — CARRIED FORWARD, NOT AS A METHOD NOTE BUT BECAUSE IT CHANGED FINDINGS

Every document entered as a source below is one a part records as **RETRIEVED**. RELAYED documents are graded
T4 regardless of the institution behind them — Amnesty, JKCCS, the Internet Freedom Foundation's own RTI
account, the J&K Assembly replies of 2025, and every press account of a court order. **No URL appears in any
record that no part records as fetched.**

Two corrections that changed gradings rather than merely method:

- **The "dead host" failures across this phase and phase 11 were largely a DNS resolver artefact.** `dig
  @1.1.1.1` then `curl --resolve` reaches `ncrb.gov.in`, `indiacode.nic.in`, `egazette.gov.in`, `sansad.in`,
  `jkhighcourt.nic.in` and the Supreme Court's document host. **The browser User-Agent is not the fix — the
  `--resolve` is**, and that correction is stated in P-88's sources so it is not propagated wrongly. This is
  what let the PSA's official consolidated text, thirteen PSI volumes and the Reorganisation Act Gazette be
  retrieved, and it is why several phase-11 T4 gradings are recoverable (AMENDMENTS.md, L-0121 and L-0123).
- **Three failure modes must be distinguished and this phase's earlier text collapsed them.** NXDOMAIN = the
  name is gone, a finding about the world. Resolves-but-no-HTTP = a finding about this environment. SERVFAIL =
  a resolver artefact, a finding about nothing. `jkhome.nic.in` is the first, on three independent resolvers
  with a sibling `.nic.in` host as a working control — which is what makes L-0140 authorable at all, and what
  bounds it to the effect rather than the cause.
