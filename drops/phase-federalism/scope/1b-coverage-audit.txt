REQUIREMENT A: THE 18 FEDERALISM-TAGGED LEDGER RECORDS

L-0012: Goods and Services Tax
  Date: 2017-07-01 | Term: T1
  Domains: macro, federalism
  Assessment: partly
  Summary: Replacement of a fragmented web of central and state indirect taxes with a single destination-based GST, governed by a GST Council with the Centre holding one-third of voting weight and states two-thirds, decisions requiring a 75% majority. Launched with four slabs plus cess rather than the single-rate ideal. GSTN technical failures, compliance burden and delayed exporter refunds marked the first years. Collections grew steadily to a record ₹22.08 lakh crore in FY2024-25 (+9.4% YoY), with a record single month of ₹2,36,716 crore in April 2025. Compensation ended June 2022 amid continuing state demands. GST 2.0, effective 22 September 2025, collapsed the structure to 5% and 18% plus a 40% demerit rate, with ~₹48,000 crore estimated revenue foregone.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether GST with a 75%-majority GST Council rule, four (now two) slabs, and expiring compensation (to June 2022) achieves unified national market and higher revenue buoyancy.

L-0040: MGNREGA under an inheriting government
  Date: 2014-05 | Term: T1
  Domains: welfare, employment, federalism
  Assessment: contested
  Summary: A statutory employment guarantee enacted in 2005, publicly disparaged by the Prime Minister in February 2015 as a living monument to the previous government's failures, retained and heavily used throughout the period.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether a flagship state-of-origin welfare scheme survives political reversal and continues to be deployed by the inheriting government.

L-0051: Discom reform: UDAY and its successors
  Date: 2015-11 | Term: T1
  Domains: infrastructure, macro, federalism
  Assessment: failed
  Summary: UDAY (2015) restructured distribution company debt with states absorbing 75%, followed by the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (2021) — the latest in a cycle of bailouts running back through IPDS and R-APDRP.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether infrastructure cost-sharing between Centre and states through successive bailout schemes achieves fiscal sustainability.

L-0066: The three farm laws: passage, protest and repeal
  Date: 2020-06-05 | Term: T2
  Domains: macro, federalism, governance
  Assessment: reversed
  Summary: Three agricultural marketing laws promulgated by ordinance during the COVID lockdown, enacted in September 2020, and repealed in November 2021 after roughly fifteen months of protest. The only complete propose-resist-repeal arc in this record.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether Centre-promulgated agricultural reform survives state and farmer opposition.

L-0069: MSP, procurement and the legal guarantee demand
  Date: 2014-05 | Term: T1
  Domains: welfare, federalism
  Assessment: no-objective
  Summary: Minimum support prices are announced for 23 crops but procurement reaches roughly 6% of farmers and is concentrated in a handful of states. Farm unions have demanded a statutory MSP guarantee since the repeal; the committee promised to examine it has not reported in four years.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether MSP is a universal guarantee or a state-variable subsidy masked by national announcement.

L-0071: Groundwater depletion in the grain belt
  Date: 2014-05 | Term: T1
  Domains: environment, federalism
  Assessment: no-objective
  Summary: About 76% of Punjab's assessed groundwater blocks are classified over-exploited, with annual draft near 35 billion cubic metres against a sustainable 20, and the water table falling roughly a metre a year in central districts.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether Centre-state coordination on resource management addresses state-level depletion crises.

L-0091: India's school closures, 2020-2022
  Date: 2020-03-25 | Term: T2
  Domains: education, federalism
  Assessment: contested
  Summary: Indian schools were never classified fully open for 707 consecutive days - 23 months and 5 days, from 25 March 2020 to 1 March 2022. Across UNESCO's wider 25-month tracking window of 775 days, India had 47 days fully open. On UNESCO's own definition of closure duration, which counts fully closed and partially open days together, India's 93 weeks is the unique global maximum across 210 countries, against a global mean of 40.6. On full closure alone India's 25 weeks ranks 58th. The affected population, 294,893,120 children, is the largest of any country in the world.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether federalised education governance (states' competence) coordinates crisis response across states.

L-0094: A national teacher-vacancy statistic, tabled for a decade and then withdrawn
  Date: 2014-07-30 | Term: T1
  Domains: governance, federalism, education
  Assessment: no-objective
  Summary: From 2014 the Ministry routinely tabled all-India totals for sanctioned, in-position and vacant school teaching posts in both Houses, drawn from Annual Work Plan and Budget and Project Approval Board returns. The last full national figure was given on 02.08.2023 (Rajya Sabha Unstarred 1460); the last vacancy-only national figure on 07.02.2024 (Rajya Sabha Unstarred 525). Since then no answer in either House has carried a sanctioned or a vacancy number at any level. The Ministry continues to compute it: it furnished 63,26,207 sanctioned and 9,59,148 vacant for 2023-24, and 69,85,760 and 9,82,662 for 2024-25, to the Standing Committee on 7 January 2025, printed in the 368th Report of 08.08.2025.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether Centre has standing to count state-staffed positions and whether national aggregation is published.

L-0098: RTE section 12(1)(c): the 25 per cent private-school quota
  Date: 2014-04-01 | Term: T1
  Domains: education, governance, federalism
  Assessment: partly
  Summary: Section 12(1)(c) of the RTE Act requires private unaided schools to reserve 25 per cent of entry-class seats for children from disadvantaged groups and weaker sections, with the state reimbursing a notified per-child cost. Central co-funding of that reimbursement began in 2014-15. By 2021-22 the Ministry reported 48.97 lakh children in schools for which reimbursement was claimed. On the Ministry's own count 19 of 36 States and UTs were admitting children under the provision as at April 2023 - roughly half the country has never operated it.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether Centre-mandated private-school quota with federal reimbursement reaches half the states.

L-0099: Karnataka's neighbourhood rule and the collapse of quota participation
  Date: 2019-01-30 | Term: T1
  Domains: education, governance, federalism
  Assessment: contested
  Summary: The Karnataka RTE (Amendment) Rules 2018, gazetted 30 January 2019, provided that admission under section 12(1)(c) would be available only where there is no government or aided school in the defined neighbourhood offering the relevant class. Private schools admitting children under the provision in Karnataka fell from 12,165 (2018-19) to 219 (2019-20) - a 98.2 per cent collapse in one year, against a national pattern of flat or rising participation. The Karnataka High Court upheld the rule on 31 May 2019.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether state amendment of Centre-mandated RTE 12(1)(c) quota can be enforced through local definitions.

L-0100: NEP 2020's three-language formula and Tamil Nadu's refusal
  Date: 2020-07-29 | Term: T2
  Domains: federalism, governance, education
  Assessment: contested
  Summary: NEP 2020 paragraph 4.13 retains the three-language formula while removing every named language from it: 'no language will be imposed on any State', the three being 'the choices of States, regions, and of course the students themselves, so long as at least two of the three languages are native to India'. Tamil Nadu, whose Assembly resolved on 23 January 1968 'that the Three-Language Formula shall be scrapped', has refused to implement it. The Union's own position, stated in Parliament in March 2026, is that 'Tamil Nadu is not implementing it in its entirety'.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether NEP 2020's nominally-flexible three-language formula binds dissenting states.

L-0101: Samagra Shiksha funds withheld from states that have not adopted NEP 2020
  Date: 2024-04-01 | Term: T3
  Domains: federalism, governance, education
  Assessment: contested
  Summary: Tamil Nadu, Kerala and West Bengal received nil central Samagra Shiksha release in FY2024-25, against 1,876.16, 141.66 and 311.29 crore respectively the year before. All three are the three States that have not signed the PM-SHRI memorandum of understanding; 33 of 36 States and UTs have. The Union's stated position is that 'implementation of Samagra Shiksha is implementation of NEP, 2020' and that Tamil Nadu 'has not agreed to implement NEP, 2020 in its entirety'. Nothing in NEP 2020 authorises the conditionality; the linkage runs through the PM-SHRI memorandum and the 2021 realignment of Samagra Shiksha to the policy.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether untied transfers can be withheld to enforce policy alignment when no statutory authority exists.

L-0108: The West Bengal teacher-recruitment panel annulled, and the anti-cheating law that does not reach it
  Date: 2016 | Term: T1
  Domains: governance, federalism, education
  Assessment: failed
  Summary: On 3 April 2025 the Supreme Court upheld the Calcutta High Court's judgment of 22 April 2024 setting aside en bloc the entire 2016 selection process of the West Bengal Central School Service Commission for Assistant Teachers of Classes IX-X and XI-XII and for non-teaching Group C and Group D staff. Six weeks before the High Court ruled, Parliament had enacted the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act 2024, whose Schedule does not cover any state school service commission, state public service commission, state teacher eligibility test or state teacher recruitment board.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether Centre can protect state recruitment integrity and what happens when national anti-cheating legislation excludes state instruments.

L-0125: The mechanism of 5 and 6 August 2019: who supplied the concurrence, and who supplied the recommendation
  Date: 2019-08-05 | Term: T2
  Domains: governance, federalism, kashmir
  Assessment: contested
  Summary: Article 370 was rendered inoperative by two Presidential orders four days apart. C.O. 272 of 5 August 2019 superseded the Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order 1954, applied the whole Constitution to the State, and added a clause to Article 367 directing that the expression 'Constituent Assembly of the State referred to in clause (2)' in the proviso to Article 370(3) be read as 'Legislative Assembly of the State'. C.O. 273 of 6 August 2019 then declared, on the recommendation of Parliament, that all clauses of Article 370 cease to be operative. A five-judge Constitution Bench held on 11 December 2023 that the Article 367 route was ultra vires and that the identical result was independently available without it, and disposed of every petition.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether Article 370's repeal was constitutionally valid and whether its process can be questioned after effect.

L-0127: The undertaking to restore statehood: seven years, six statements, no date
  Date: 2019-08-06 | Term: T2
  Domains: governance, federalism, kashmir
  Assessment: awaiting-adjudication
  Summary: The commitment to restore statehood to Jammu and Kashmir has been stated six times, in three forums, by the Union Home Minister, by two Ministers of State, by the Solicitor General and by the Supreme Court itself. It has never been withdrawn and has never been dated. As at August 2026 statehood has not been restored, and applications to enforce the undertaking are pending before the Supreme Court.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether an indefinite deferral of statehood after explicit commitment can be sustained.

L-0138: The Union keeps no count of shutdowns because police is a State subject, and in Jammu and Kashmir it is not
  Date: 2021-03-10 | Term: T2
  Domains: governance, federalism, kashmir
  Assessment: no-objective
  Summary: Both Union bodies with responsibility for telecom suspension have stated on the record that they keep no record of shutdown orders issued by States and Union Territories, and the reason both give is that police and public order are State subjects. Jammu and Kashmir is not a State: the Reorganisation Act places Public Order and Police outside the Legislative Assembly's competence entirely, and everything outside that competence is the Lieutenant Governor's discretion, on which no court may inquire what advice was tendered. So for the territory where suspensions have been most frequent and most prolonged, the constitutional ground the Union gives for not counting does not apply to it.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether constitutional absence of state police competence in a Union Territory creates accountability gap for telecom shutdowns.

L-0146: The third tier in Jammu and Kashmir was elected once, and every elected term has since lapsed with no successor election
  Date: 2018-11-17 | Term: T2
  Domains: governance, federalism, kashmir
  Assessment: partly
  Summary: Jammu and Kashmir had no constitutionally grounded third tier until 2019, because Parts IX and IXA did not apply to it. Since then panchayats, Block Development Councils, municipal bodies and a new directly elected District Development Council in every district have all been elected. All of them have also expired: the municipal bodies in October and November 2023, the panchayats and BDCs on 9 January 2024, and the District Development Councils on 24 February 2026. Not one successor election has been held.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether third-tier terms can lapse entirely without successor elections or whether the territory has abandoned elected local government.

L-0147: The J&K local tier has no State Finance Commission report, no local-bodies audit, and its Finance Commission grants fell to zero and then stopped appearing
  Date: 2011-06-24 | Term: T2
  Domains: governance, federalism, kashmir
  Assessment: no-objective
  Summary: Jammu and Kashmir enacted a State Finance Commission Act in 2011 requiring a commission to be constituted as soon as may be and every fifth year thereafter. No document establishes that any such commission was ever constituted, reported or had its report acted on. The Comptroller and Auditor General's own database returns zero local-bodies audit reports for the territory as either State or Union Territory, while returning them for other states. And the Ministry of Panchayati Raj's own devolution index scores the territory at 27.85 against a national average of 43.89. And the Union's own Finance Commission grants to the territory's Gram Panchayats were released at 53.64 per cent of allocation across the 2015-20 award, with the whole of the final year's 1,049.49 crore rupees released at zero - after which the territory has no row at all in the successor award's allocation table.
  Federal Question Settled: Whether Finance Commission grants exist for territories without a constituted Finance Commission and whether local-governance finance reaches gram panchayats.
