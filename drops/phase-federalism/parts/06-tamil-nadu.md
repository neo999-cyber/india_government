Derived facts, all computable from the Court's own dates:
- **Ten** bills had assent withheld *simpliciter* on 13.11.2023 — the Governor conveyed **no message**
  for reconsideration under the first proviso to Article 200. **Two** (Nos. 25/2022 and 26/2022) were
  reserved for the President in the first round and **never re-enacted**; they are the two bills whose
  Presidential status the judgment records as **"Status not on record"** (A-1 below).
- Pendency of the ten withheld bills at the Governor: **minimum 199 days, maximum 1,400 days**;
  the full sorted set is 199, 199, 382, 382, 546, 546, 546, 564, 1,121, 1,400 days.
- **Every subject-matter is a university.** All twelve bills are university or higher-education
  statutes. This is not a general assent dispute; it is a dispute over control of universities,
  which is why the Governor's stated ground was Entry 66 of List I. That is the same
  Union-versus-State education fault line as L-0100/L-0101, arriving through a different Article.
- The **Governor took charge on 18.11.2021** (judgment paragraph 3). Bill No. 2/2020 had by then
  already been pending **675 days** with his predecessor. The judgment does not disaggregate the
  pendency by incumbent, and neither do I.
- **Timing against the litigation.** The writ was filed **31.10.2023**; the Court issued notice
  **10.11.2023**; the Governor acted on all twelve bills **13.11.2023** — **13 days** after filing and
  **3 days** after notice. The Court's word for this (paragraph 4) is that he "forthwith, took a
  decision."
- **Second round.** The Assembly reconvened in special session and repassed the ten bills
  **18.11.2023**, forwarding them the same day. The Governor reserved them for the President
  **28.11.2023** — **10 days**. The contrast between 10 days in the second round and 199–1,400 days
  in the first is on the face of the Court's own table.
- **Presidential disposal of the ten reserved bills**: assent **withheld to seven**, **granted to
  one** (No. 48/2022), **two awaited**. Time from reservation (28.11.2023) to Presidential action:
  79, 80, 82, 82, 90, 90, 90 and 111 days. The two awaited had been pending **497 days** at the date
  of judgment.

**F-2. The 8 April 2025 holding, verbatim from the conclusion (paragraph 434).** The Court held: the
Governor has three options under Article 200 (assent / withhold / reserve) and the first proviso is
**not a fourth option** but is "intrinsically and inextricably attached to the option of withholding
of assent"; **"Neither the concept of 'pocket veto' nor that of 'absolute veto' finds place within
the constitutional scheme"**; a bill returned in the second round after reconsideration must be
assented to unless "materially different"; and **"the reservation by the Governor of the ten Bills
for the consideration of the President in the second round was illegal, erroneous in law and is thus
liable to be set aside. As a result, any subsequent action taken upon the said Bills by the
President also does not survive and is thus set aside."**

**F-3. The Article 142 relief — deemed assent, and its exact date.** Conclusion item (IX): the ten
Bills, "having been pending with the Governor for an unduly long period of time, and the Governor
having acted with clear lack of bona fides in reserving the Bills… immediately after the
pronouncement of the decision of this Court in *State of Punjab*", **"are deemed to have been
assented to by the Governor on the date when they were presented to him after being reconsidered"**
— which paragraph 435(c) fixes as **18.11.2023**. So the operative constitutional fact is: ten Tamil
Nadu Acts took effect by a date the Court assigned retrospectively, seventeen months before the
judgment that assigned it.

**F-4. The timelines the Court prescribed (conclusion item XIV, and paragraph 250).** Verbatim, four
limits, breach of which "would make the inaction of the Governors subject to judicial review":
(i) withholding or reserving **on the advice of the Council of Ministers** — "forthwith, subject to a
maximum period of one-month"; (ii) withholding **contrary to** that advice — return with a message
within "a maximum period of three-months"; (iii) reserving **contrary to** that advice — "within a
maximum period of three months"; (iv) a bill presented after reconsideration — assent "forthwith,
subject to a maximum period of one-month". Separately, at paragraph 391, the Court prescribed that
**the President must decide an Article 201 reference within three months** of receipt, adopting the
Ministry of Home Affairs' own guideline period; delay beyond it requires reasons recorded and
conveyed to the State.

**F-5. The Article 143 Presidential Reference and what it did.** Retrieved: the Supreme Court's own
PDF of the advisory opinion, **Special Reference No. 1 of 2025, *In re: Assent, Withholding or
Reservation of Bills by the Governor and the President of India*, 2025 INSC 1333**, 111 pages,
delivered **20 November 2025** by a five-judge bench (CJI **B.R. Gavai**, **Surya Kant**, **Vikram
Nath**, **P.S. Narasimha**, **A.S. Chandurkar** JJ), from
`https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2025/39157/39157_2025_1_1501_66169_Judgement_20-Nov-2025.pdf`.
The President made the reference on **13 May 2025** — **35 days** after the 8 April judgment — putting
**fourteen questions**. The opinion's operative paragraph 165 answers them. Against the 8 April
holding it goes, item by item:
- **165.2 — reversed.** "The Governor enjoys discretion in choosing from these three constitutional
  options and **is not bound by the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers**."
- **165.3 and 165.6 — reversed.** The Article 200 function "is not justiciable", as is the
  President's under Article 201; only "in glaring circumstances of inaction that is prolonged,
  unexplained, and indefinite" may the Court issue "a limited mandamus… without making any
  observations on the merits."
- **165.5 and 165.7 — reversed.** "In the absence of constitutionally prescribed time limits… it
  would not be appropriate for this Court to judicially prescribe timelines." Same for the President.
- **165.10 — reversed, in terms.** "The exercise of constitutional powers and the orders of the
  President/Governor cannot be substituted in any manner under Article 142, and **we hereby clarify
  that the Constitution, specifically Article 142 even, does not allow for the concept of 'deemed
  assent' of Bills.**" Paragraph 128: deemed consent at the expiry of a judicially set timeline "is
  virtually a takeover, and substitution, of the executive functions by the Judiciary."
- **165.1 — retained.** Three options; the first proviso restricts them rather than adding a fourth.
- The opinion holds (paragraph 78, and paragraph 79 following) that *State of Tamil Nadu* "is based
  on a **partial reading**" of *Samsher Singh*, *Valluri* and *Hoechst*.

**F-6. The one thing the opinion expressly did NOT do — and this is the finding that matters.** At
paragraph 26 the Court states: **"The questions do not require this Court to vacate, amend, or
modify the final relief granted by this Court in *State of Tamil Nadu*"** — the reference goes "at
the most, [to] clarification on the propositions of law laid down in it, which have ramifications
for the governance of all States, i.e., **beyond the parties that were before it in that lis**."
Paragraph 27 accepts the Attorney General's and Solicitor General's submission that the reference
"seeks clarification of constitutional principles for future governance, and not the setting aside
of an already awarded decree, or the relief granted therein."

**The consequence, stated precisely: the ten Tamil Nadu Acts stand, and the reasoning that produced
them does not.** Tamil Nadu keeps the decree and loses the doctrine. Every other State — and Tamil
Nadu itself on the next bill — is now governed by 165.2, 165.3, 165.5 and 165.10, under which there
is no timeline, no justiciable merits review, no binding aid-and-advice, and no deemed assent. The
remedy Tamil Nadu obtained on 8 April 2025 is, by the Court's own opinion of 20 November 2025,
unavailable to anyone including Tamil Nadu. **This is a case-state fact of the first order and it is
established from two Supreme Court PDFs, neither party's account of them.**

**F-7. What the record does not contain.** The judgment records no reason given by the Governor for
the 199–1,400 days of inaction — the Court notes at paragraph 4 that he acted only after notice
issued. The Presidential reasons for withholding assent to seven bills are not in either document.
The status of Bills 25/2022 and 26/2022 is recorded by the Supreme Court itself as **"Status not on
record"** — a Court declaring an absence in the Union's own return. See A-1.

### B. The Article 131 suit over Samagra Shiksha — a new figure L-0101 does not carry

**F-8. Tamil Nadu's claim is ₹2,291 crore, and that is a THIRD number for the same money.** Tamil
Nadu filed an original suit under **Article 131** in the Supreme Court — *State of Tamil Nadu v.
Union of India* — reported as **Diary No. 28793 of 2025**, filed **20 May 2025**, seeking a direction
to the Union to pay **"over ₹2,291 crore"** said to be due under Samagra Shiksha, with future
interest. The Supreme Court, per **Prashant Kumar Mishra and Manmohan JJ**, declined to list it
during partial working days on **9 June 2025**, holding there was no urgency.
**Grade: T4 for all of it.** The plaint is not public; the Supreme Court's case-status portal at
`https://www.sci.gov.in/case-status-diary-no/` returned HTTP 200 but is **CAPTCHA-gated** (22
occurrences of "captcha" in the retrieved HTML), so the diary number and filing date could not be
confirmed against the Court's own register. Sources are LiveLaw and India Legal (see §Sources).

**Why this is a finding and not a footnote.** L-0101 already holds the Union's figures for the same
dispute: **₹2,151.59 crore allocated** for FY2024-25 and **₹362.81 crore released**. Tamil Nadu sues
for **₹2,291 crore**. Three numbers, one pot of money, and **they are not reconcilable by
arithmetic** because they are not measuring the same thing:
- **₹2,151.59 crore** is the Union's *approved central share of the FY2024-25 Annual Work Plan and
  Budget* — an allocation, which on the Union's own doctrine is an intention, not a debt.
- **₹362.81 crore** is *cash released*, an accounting fact checkable in PFMS.
- **₹2,291 crore** is Tamil Nadu's *claim of what is due*, which necessarily contains a
  counterfactual (what the rules would have produced absent the PM-SHRI condition) and, on the news
  accounts, appears to span more than one year. **Its workings are not published.** Tamil Nadu is
  the plaintiff; this is the plaintiff's number.
This is the same shape part 07 established for West Bengal — **"released" (Union) versus "dues
outstanding" (state)** — appearing here in a **justiciable** form for the first time. That is the
one thing that makes the Tamil Nadu instance different: if the suit is ever heard, a court will have
to make the two definitions meet on one record. Until then the gap between ₹2,151.59 and ₹2,291
crore is **unadjudicated**, and this part does not adopt either figure.

**The definitional caveat, and it is load-bearing.** FC-XVI's own note under Table 8.4 states:
**"Inter-se shares of FC-11 to FC-14 are exclusive of the States' shares of service taxes."** Until

5.39 → 5.31 → 4.97 → 4.02 → 4.08 → 4.097 as though it were one measurement is running a
four-value segment on one definition and a two-value segment on another.** This must go in the
series `caveat` and as a `break` at the FC-14/FC-15 boundary. FC-XVI itself adds a second break note
for FC-15: "all shares must be recalculated after dropping Jammu & Kashmir" — J&K's inter-se share
goes **1.29 → 1.30 → 1.55 → 1.85 → 0.00** across the same five columns, which is the same J&K-leaves-
the-denominator effect part 07 flagged on the *vertical* share (42→41).

**F-12. The comparator, confirmed from the same table — and part 07 is right.** Reading down

**But the same table also contains the strongest fact against Tamil Nadu's case, and it must be

unchanged"* under FC-15, while Telangana, Karnataka and Kerala "saw a sharp decline."
**So the FC-XIII→FC-XIV fall of 0.82→0.66 (a drop of 19.5 per cent, not 24 — see the correction
below) is real, and the FC-XIV→FC-XV period, which is the period of this instrument's T2 and T3,
shows Tamil Nadu's ratio essentially FLAT while three other southern States fell sharply.**

**F-14. Why the higher-income State loses share — the Commission's own reasoning, quoted.** FC-XVI

for differences in fiscal capacity among States by devolving more to States with lower per capita
GSDP."* Para 8.94: the Commission expressly declines poverty and SC/ST-share indicators because
*"per capita GSDP is a more direct measure of the State's fiscal capacity to provide public
services."* And para 8.61, on the whole formula: *"each of these five FCs has disbursed 75 per cent
or more of the divisible pool based on equity."*

higher-income State receiving less per rupee is the formula performing its constitutional function,
not failing it.** FC-XVI para 8.65 gives the aggregate: the eight high-per-capita-GSDP States held

cent**. Verbatim: *"the devolutions have had a progressive tilt under all five FCs relative to equal
per capita shares."*

**F-17. Tamil Nadu's GST complaint, as the Commission records it — and a correction to this part's
own brief.** FC-XVI para 7.41 sets out three State concerns about GST and **attributes them by
name**. Tamil Nadu appears in three of the four propositions: loss of fiscal autonomy because rate
changes now need Council approval (with Karnataka, Kerala, Telangana, West Bengal); loss of
*"control over a large part of their tax base"* (with Chhattisgarh and Haryana); and **"Tamil Nadu
has complained about the lack of buoyancy of SGST"**. But the proposition that *"GST's
destination-based nature shifted revenue to consuming States, resulting in a permanent loss of
revenues"* is attributed to **Himachal Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Haryana, Uttarakhand, and
Punjab — Tamil Nadu is NOT in that list.**
**This is a finding against the brief's own framing.** The brief selected Tamil Nadu partly because
"as a manufacturing state it sits on the losing side of destination-based GST." On the most recent
authoritative compilation of what the States actually argued, **Tamil Nadu did not make the
producing-state destination argument to the Sixteenth Finance Commission.** It made a *buoyancy*
argument and an *autonomy* argument instead. The producing-state argument in this phase's record
belongs to Gujarat (47th Council), Karnataka (54th Council) and the six States FC-XVI names.
**The authoring stage must not put the destination-principle case in Tamil Nadu's mouth.**

**F-18. Tamil Nadu's own post-compensation shortfall figure, and where it lives.** FC-XVI para 7.41,
final sentence: *"many States have noted that the cessation of transfers to States from GST
compensation cess on 30 June 2022, has led to a sudden fiscal imbalance in their budgets, **with

**Unit string: "₹ crore, estimated shortfall in FY2024-25 attributed to the cessation of GST
compensation".** This is the closest thing retrieved to "Tamil Nadu's own compensation-gap
computation" that part 01 forwarded. **Its epistemic status is exactly the same as the 42nd-meeting

the table, with no workings published and no adjudication.** The Commission reports it; it does not
verify it, does not say what it is a shortfall *against*, and does not print the arithmetic. A
counterfactual — what compensation would have been paid had the guarantee continued — is not a
measurable quantity, and **"nearly ₹20,000 crore" is a round number in a sentence, not a
computation.** It is reported here and **not adopted**.

**R-1. "Ten Tamil Nadu bills, deemed assented by the Court and the doctrine withdrawn seven months
later"** — `type: episode`, `date: 2020-01-13` (the earliest bill forwarded), `dateEnd: 2025-11-20`,
`term: T2→T3`, `domains: [federalism, governance]`, `assessment: contested`, `differentFacts:

*Summary skeleton.* Twelve university bills passed by the Tamil Nadu Assembly between 13 January
2020 and 28 April 2023 sat with the Governor for between 199 and 1,400 days. He acted on all twelve
on 13 November 2023 — three days after the Supreme Court issued notice on the State's writ petition
— withholding assent to ten without any message for reconsideration and reserving two for the
President. The Assembly repassed the ten in a special session on 18 November 2023; the Governor
reserved them for the President ten days later. On 8 April 2025 the Supreme Court held the second-
round reservation illegal, set aside the President's consequent actions, and under Article 142
declared the ten bills deemed assented as of 18 November 2023. On 13 May 2025 the President referred
fourteen questions under Article 143. On 20 November 2025 a Constitution Bench held that the
Governor is not bound by ministerial advice under Article 200, that the function is not justiciable,
that courts may not prescribe timelines, and that Article 142 does not permit deemed assent — while
expressly declining to disturb the relief already granted to Tamil Nadu.

*`caseFor` (the Union's case, in its own terms).* Article 200 confers a constitutional function on
the Governor with no time limit written into it, and a Constitution Bench has now held that the
absence is deliberate: "In the absence of constitutionally prescribed time limits, and the manner of
exercise of power by the Governor, it would not be appropriate for this Court to judicially
prescribe timelines." A two-judge bench had read into Article 200 a timetable the Constitution does
not contain, and then enforced it with a remedy — deemed assent — that converts judicial review into
substitution: "virtually a takeover, and substitution, of the executive functions by the Judiciary."
The bills at issue transferred the appointment of Vice-Chancellors from the Governor as Chancellor
to the State Government, a subject on which Entry 66 of List I gives the Union a genuine interest,
and reserving such bills for the President is the constitutional route for resolving exactly that
question. The five-judge opinion also left Tamil Nadu's own relief intact, which is the opposite of
vindictiveness.

*`caseAgainst` (Tamil Nadu's case, in its own terms).* The measured facts are not in dispute and
they were supplied by the Governor himself: 199 to 1,400 days of silence, ended three days after the
Supreme Court issued notice, and a second-round reservation completed in ten days. Whatever Article
200 permits, a function performed in ten days when a court is watching and not at all for nearly
four years when it is not is not a considered exercise of discretion. The bills were passed by an
elected legislature, repassed without material change after the first refusal, and every one of them
concerns universities the State funds. And after 20 November 2025 the position is that this can
recur with no timeline, no merits review and no remedy beyond a "limited mandamus" for inaction that
is "prolonged, unexplained, and indefinite" — a standard the Court did not define. Tamil Nadu's
Acts survive; the mechanism that produced them does not, and no State can use it again.

*`assessmentNote`.* Contested because **the two sides do not disagree about a single fact.** Every
date is admitted; the dispute is entirely about what Article 200 permits, and the Supreme Court has
given two answers to that in seven months. `differentFacts: false`.

**R-4. "₹30,085 crore a year reaches Tamil Nadu without passing through Tamil Nadu's budget, and its

*Summary skeleton.* The Government of India transfers funds directly to State Implementing Agencies
in Tamil Nadu — societies, corporations and missions authorised by the State to receive central money
— without routing them through the State Budget or the State Treasury. The amount rose from ₹3,914.73

entitlement of the State treasury, and the State's own accounting choices determine what its Finance

*(First-class findings. `withheld` requires a **named requester, a specific request and a date** —

**A-1. The Presidential disposal of Bills 25/2022 and 26/2022 — `not-collected`, and the Supreme
Court itself says so.** Paragraph 13 of 2025 INSC 481 records the President's action on these two

date for eight of the other ten bills to the day, so the column exists and is populated; the two
gaps are gaps in what was placed on record, not in the table's design. **This is a Court declaring
an absence in the Union's own return, which is the strongest form the finding can take.**
`reasonKind: not-collected`. Closes if the Union discloses the disposal.

**A-2. The Governor's reasons for the 199–1,400 days — `never-defined`.** Neither judgment records

will ever produce this quantity. `reasonKind: never-defined`.

**A-3. The Presidential reasons for withholding assent to seven Tamil Nadu bills — `not-published`.**
The dates are in the Court's table; the reasons are in neither judgment. Article 201 requires no
published reasons, and 2025 INSC 1333 §165.6 holds the President's Article 201 function
**not justiciable**. *Not* graded `withheld`: I identified no named requester, specific request and
date. `reasonKind: not-published`.

**A-4. Tamil Nadu's workings behind the ₹2,291 crore Article 131 claim — `not-published`.** The

(the plaint), and it is closed. `reasonKind: not-published`.

**A-6. GST transaction data withheld from the Comptroller and Auditor General — `withheld`, and it

report series certifies every other year's accounts without such a caveat, so the caveat is an event,

## Definitional disagreements

**D1. "The Supreme Court decided the assent question" — two decisions, one outcome, opposite
doctrines, and the disagreement is about WHAT A JUDGMENT IS.**

| | 2025 INSC 481 (8 Apr 2025, 2 judges) | 2025 INSC 1333 (20 Nov 2025, 5 judges) |
|---|---|---|
| Is the Governor bound by aid and advice under Art. 200? | Yes, as a general rule | **No** — §165.2 |
| Is the Art. 200 function justiciable? | Yes | **No**, except limited mandamus for prolonged, unexplained, indefinite inaction — §165.3 |
| May the Court set timelines? | Yes — 1 month / 3 months / 3 months / 1 month; 3 months for the President | **No** — §165.5, §165.7 |
| May Art. 142 produce deemed assent? | Yes, "in exceptional cases like the one at hand" | **No** — §165.10, "the Constitution, specifically Article 142 even, does not allow for the concept of 'deemed assent'" |
| Do the ten Tamil Nadu Acts stand? | Deemed assented 18.11.2023 | **Untouched** — §26: the reference does not "vacate, amend, or modify the final relief granted" |

**Resolution.** These are not two measurements of one quantity. They are a **decree** and a
**doctrine**, and Indian constitutional law lets the first survive the destruction of the second.
The correct statement of the position is: *ten Tamil Nadu university Acts are in force by force of
an Article 142 direction whose reasoning the Supreme Court has since held impermissible, and no
other State can obtain the same relief.* Anyone who states only one half is quoting selectively —
including, symmetrically, either government.

**D2. "Due" versus "released" versus "allocated" — the Samagra Shiksha instance.** Three numbers,
three definitions, no arithmetic bridge: **₹2,151.59 crore** (Union, *approved central share of the
Annual Work Plan and Budget* — an intention); **₹362.81 crore** (Union, *cash released* — an
accounting fact); **₹2,291 crore** (Tamil Nadu, *due* — a claim containing a counterfactual). Part
07 established this shape for West Bengal (Union "released" ₹0.00 vs State "dues outstanding"
₹97,807.91 crore–₹1.75 lakh crore). **The Tamil Nadu instance adds one thing part 07's does not: a
pending Article 131 suit, which is a forum with jurisdiction to choose between the definitions.**
Neither figure is adopted here.

*(D3 → D6 appended in the completion pass. **D3 and D4 are the two the earlier text already
promised** — F-20(iii) refers forward to "D3 below" and F-23 to "See D4". They are supplied here.)*

**D3. The back-to-back GST loan — one rupee, three official characterisations, all three authored by
the Union.**

| Instrument | What the same money is called there | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Union Budget, *Statement of Transfer of Resources to States and UTs*, head II.2 / I.b | **A transfer to States** — ₹1,10,208 crore FY2020-21 and ₹1,47,865.55 crore FY2021-22, with no grant/loan distinction drawn in the total (part 04, §7) | Inflates the Union's published transfers to States by the full amount |
| Tamil Nadu Finance Accounts, **Major Head 6004 – Public Debt** | **Borrowing by the State** — ₹6,241 crore FY2020-21, ₹8,095.25 crore FY2021-22, cumulative ₹14,336 crore (F-29, S-12) | Recorded as the State's debt receipt |
| Department of Expenditure decision, quoted verbatim by the CAG | **Not the State's debt "for any norms which may be prescribed by the Finance Commission"** (F-29) | Excluded from debt/GSDP, outstanding liabilities, fiscal deficit and loans-from-GoI in every audited series |

**Resolution: there is none, and that is the finding.** These are not three measurements of one
quantity by three parties — they are **three definitions issued by one party**, the Union, for three
different purposes: to maximise a published transfers total, to place a repayment obligation on the
State's books, and to keep it out of the fiscal-rule arithmetic. The State's auditor records the
third and footnotes it on every affected table; the State's own accounts carry the second; the
first appears nowhere in Tamil Nadu's documents at all. **The correct statement is: the back-to-back
loan is simultaneously counted as a transfer to Tamil Nadu, as Tamil Nadu's debt, and as not
Tamil Nadu's debt, and no instrument reconciles the three.** Compare part 01's compensation record
and part 04's PR-5; **this part adds only that the third characterisation is quoted by a
constitutional auditor from the Union's own decision, which makes it citable rather than inferred.**

**D4. "For every rupee Tamil Nadu contributes it gets back X paise" — four constructions, four
different answers, and the phrase names none of them.** *(This analysis stands whether or not the
figure is ever sourced; per F-23 and A-8 the figure itself is **HELD as unestablished**, and nothing
below should be read as computing it.)*

The sentence has an implicit numerator and an implicit denominator and **every published Tamil Nadu
quantity fits more than one slot**. The four constructions:

1. **Tax collected in Tamil Nadu → transfers received.** Fails on the numerator's meaning, not its
   availability: Union tax collection is attributed to the place of **assessment or registration**,
   not of economic incidence. A company headquartered in Chennai pays corporation tax "from" Tamil
   Nadu on profits earned across India, and one headquartered in Mumbai does the reverse. **Under
   GST the problem is definitional rather than empirical** — IGST is settled to the **destination**
   State by statute, so "GST collected in Tamil Nadu" is not a quantity that means what the sentence
   needs it to mean. Note the irony: this construction requires exactly the destination principle
   that F-17 and F-33 establish **Tamil Nadu has not argued and its auditor has not measured**.
2. **Share of national GDP → share of devolution.** This is the construction FC-XVI itself uses when
   it adopts **"Contribution to GDP" at 10 per cent** (F-15), and it is a defensible reading of
   "contributes". It is also the construction under which the answer moves the least, because
   FC-XVI's own aggregate (F-14) is that the eight high-per-capita-GSDP States held 33.7 per cent of
   the 2011 population and received 21.2–23.7 per cent of devolution.
3. **Share of population → share of devolution.** This is `tn-devolution-population-ratio` and it is
   **fully measured and unambiguous: 0.66–0.67 under FC-XIV and FC-XV** (F-11). Expressed as paise it
   is **66 or 67 paise per rupee — but of population share, not of contribution**, and anyone
   quoting it as a contribution ratio has substituted the denominator.
4. **All Union revenue attributed to Tamil Nadu → all Union money reaching Tamil Nadu.** The
   numerator is unavailable (see 1) and **the denominator is unstable by roughly thirty per cent**:
   in FY2023-24, Tamil Nadu's share of Union taxes was ₹46,072 crore and grants-in-aid ₹25,342 crore
   (₹71,414 crore together), while a further **₹30,085.53 crore reached the State outside its budget
   entirely** (F-24). **Including the direct-to-SIA channel raises the "money received" denominator
   by 29.6 per cent; the ₹30,085.53 crore is on its own 65.3 per cent as large as the entire share of
   Union taxes.** *(Both percentages are my arithmetic on the CAG's figures, not printed anywhere.)*

**The disagreement, stated precisely: constructions 2 and 3 are measurable and give different
answers; construction 1 is not measurable in principle under GST; and construction 4 is measurable
only after a choice about the direct-to-SIA channel that changes the result by nearly a third.** A
single "X paise" figure conceals four choices. **The instrument's rule applies without modification:
report the constructions, hold the figure.**

**D5. Two definitional breaks inside one publisher's own series — and the rule they generate.**
Both are set out at P-G: **off-budget borrowing** for FY2021-22 is ₹27,669.88 crore in one edition
and ₹1,241.96 crore in another because TANGEDCO was dropped from the definition; **GSDP** growth for
FY2020-21 is 3.73 per cent in one edition and 2.58 per cent in two others, alongside a changing source
attribution. **The disagreement here is not between the Union and a State — it is between an
institution and its own earlier self**, which is the hardest kind for a reader to detect because both
numbers carry the same imprimatur. **Rule for the authoring stage: a Tamil Nadu ratio-to-GSDP must
carry the edition it was computed in, and no Tamil Nadu off-budget-borrowing series may cross the
FY2022-23 edition boundary.** This is the same class of problem as part 02's Statement 18
title-reassignment warning and part 04's silent reclassification of GST compensation into the
transfers headline — **three instances now, in three different publications, which makes it a
property of Indian fiscal publishing rather than an accident.**

**D6. "Shortfall" — three incompatible senses inside this one part, and they must never be added.**

| Sense | Instance | What it is measured against | Status here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Against a **statutory guarantee** | ₹455.16 / ₹2,445.71 / ₹3,009.76 crore (F-27) | Base-year revenue compounded at 14 per cent, a rate written into the GST (Compensation to States) Act 2017 | **Defined, with published workings.** Adopted as reported. |
| Against a **Commission's projection** | ₹30,426 crore cumulative FY2015-20, and **+₹31,887 crore the other way** FY2021-24 (F-26) | A forecast, which can be wrong in either direction and demonstrably was | **Defined but not a grievance measure** — it conflates forecasting error with conduct. Reported, with the sign flip. |
| Against a **counterfactual** | "nearly ₹20,000 crore in 2024-25" (F-18) | What compensation *would have been paid* had a guarantee that expired on 30 June 2022 continued | **Not a measurable quantity.** Reported, **not adopted.** |

**All three are called "shortfall" in Tamil Nadu's public argument and only the first has a
denominator anyone can check.** The authoring stage must give each a distinct field or a distinct
record, and **must not produce any total.**


## Disciplining measure

*(Per quantity. The question is: **is there an instrument that puts BOTH sides' facts on ONE
ledger?** Candidates named in the brief: the Supreme Court judgment (both pleadings on the record),
the Finance Commission report (the State's memorandum and the Union's submission between two
covers), RBI's* State Finances*. The **basis-versus-evidence caveat** is answered explicitly for
each.)*

**Q1. The assent facts — dates, durations, dispositions. DISCIPLINED, and this is the strongest
instrument in the whole part.**

2025 INSC 481 paragraph 13 is a **single table containing the State's dates and the Governor's and
President's dates side by side, admitted by both parties, in a document neither party wrote.** The
State supplied the forwarding dates; the Governor supplied the action dates in his own return of
1 December 2023 (recorded at paragraph 15); the Court tabulated them. Neither party could deny the
other's column, because each column is the other party's own admission. **This passes the
independence test in the strong form part 03 identified for the CAG's cess audit** — the Court
performs an operation neither party would perform, namely putting the two date-sets in one row so
that the *interval* becomes visible. The 199-to-1,400-day range is not in either party's
submission; it is a **subtraction the instrument makes possible and the parties do not make**.
And the table's own honesty is demonstrable: it records **"Status not on record"** for two bills
rather than silently omitting them (A-1). An instrument that publishes its own gaps is doing the
disciplining job.

**Limit.** The judgment disciplines the *chronology* completely and the *reasons* not at all. There
is no column for why. After 2025 INSC 1333 §165.3 and §165.5 there never will be, because the
function is now non-justiciable and untimed. **So: the facts are disciplined and the justification
is now permanently outside every instrument.** That is a finding about the disciplining measure
itself — it worked once, on this record, and the same Court has since removed the jurisdiction that
made it work.

**Q2. What the law now is. DISCIPLINED — by a five-judge bench over a two-judge bench.** The
hierarchy is the instrument. 2025 INSC 1333 is a Constitution Bench advisory opinion; 2025 INSC 481
is a Division Bench judgment. The opinion states its own limits at paragraph 26 (it does not vacate
the decree) and its own reach at paragraph 165 (it governs "the governance of all States… beyond
the parties that were before it in that lis"). **Both propositions come from one document, so there
is no version-versus-version problem here at all.** Anyone asserting either "the Supreme Court
struck down the Tamil Nadu judgment" or "the Tamil Nadu judgment still stands" is quoting half of
paragraph 26.

**Q3. Tamil Nadu's ₹2,291 crore against the Union's ₹2,151.59 / ₹362.81 crore. NOT DISCIPLINED,
BUT UNIQUELY DISCIPLINABLE.** No instrument today puts these on one ledger: the Union's figures are
in a Lok Sabha answer (L-0101's sources), Tamil Nadu's is in an unpublished plaint. **But an
Article 131 suit is precisely the instrument that would force it** — both pleadings on one record,
a court obliged to say which definition of "due" governs. It has not been heard. **This is the one
place in this phase where the disciplining instrument exists in principle and is merely dormant**,
and it is already flagged as L-0101's `revisitTrigger`.

*(Q4 → Q8 appended in the completion pass. **Q4 is the one F-16 refers forward to.** Q5 is the
material change the CAG evidence makes to this section and it is the most important answer in the
part.)*

**Q4. The Finance Commission report as the instrument that puts both sides' asks between two covers.
DISCIPLINED AS TO POSITIONS, NOT AS TO FACTS — a shared ledger of arguments, exactly as part 01 found
for the GST Council minute book.**

FC-XVI Chapter 8 prints, in one place, **every State's recommended criterion weights alongside the
Union's submissions and the Commission's own choice** — Tamil Nadu's asks (per-capita-GSDP distance
at 35 per cent adjusted for purchasing power parity, contribution to GDP at 12.5, tax effort at 2.5,
demography at 15) sit in the same chapter as the criteria the Commission actually adopted (F-15,
F-16). Chapter 7 does the same for the cess question, naming which States argued what (F-17). **No
State can misreport its own submission and no State can pretend another State's submission was not
made**, because the compilation is by the umpire.

**But test it against the phase's crux and it fails the same way part 01's minute book fails.** Every
*factual* input in that chapter originates with one side: the population data are the Registrar
General's, the GSDP data are MoSPI's, the tax collections are the Department of Revenue's, and the
Commission does not audit any of them. The States supply **positions**; the Union supplies **facts**;
the Commission supplies **weights**. **A shared ledger of arguments is not a shared ledger of
facts**, and F-16's grading follows from this: the Commission's *summary* of Tamil Nadu's memorandum
is T1, and the *content* is a party's submission.

**The one place the Commission does more than compile — and it is worth naming.** FC-XVI's own note
under Table 8.4 (*"Inter-se shares of FC-11 to FC-14 are exclusive of the States' shares of service
taxes"*) and its recalculation note dropping Jammu and Kashmir from the FC-15 column are the
Commission **declaring the breaks in its own comparative table**. That is an instrument publishing
its own discontinuities, which is the same behaviour that made the Supreme Court's paragraph-13 table
trustworthy at Q1 (it printed "Status not on record" rather than omitting two rows). **An instrument
that publishes its own gaps is doing part of the disciplining job even where it cannot do the rest.**

**Q5. The Comptroller and Auditor General's State Finances Audit Report. DISCIPLINED — and it is the
only instrument in this part that produces facts NEITHER government supplied. This is the material
change the CAG evidence makes to this section.**

Part 03 set the standard: the CAG's cess-to-reserve-fund audit **passes** the independence test
because *the auditor performs a subtraction the payer will not perform, producing a fact neither
party supplied*. Two items in the Tamil Nadu audit corpus are exactly that shape, and they are not
equally strong. **Grade per table, never per report** (P-H).

**(a) The direct GoI → State Implementing Agency quantum — PASSES IN THE STRONG FORM, and it is the
strongest instance in this part.** The number, ₹30,085.53 crore for FY2023-24 and the nine-year series
behind it, has this structure:
- the **input comes from the payer** — the PFMS portal of the Controller General of Accounts, a Union
  system ⚐;
- the **comparator comes from the recipient** — the State's Annual Finance Accounts ⚑, which the same
  auditor certifies;
- the **operation is the auditor's** — setting the two side by side and asking whether the second
  captures the first;
- and the **output appears in neither party's own account of itself.** It is not in the Tamil Nadu
  budget, not in the State's headline fiscal aggregates, and not in the Union's *Statement of Transfer
  of Resources to States* as a Tamil Nadu row.

**The finding it produces — that the State's "receipt and expenditure as well as other fiscal
variables/parameters derived from them did not present the true and fair picture to that extent" — is
a fact about the relationship between two governments that neither government states anywhere.**
That is the definition of independent evidence rather than a common basis, and it is met here more
cleanly than anywhere else in this part. It also has the property part 03 valued: **the party that
would never perform this operation is the one it embarrasses — both of them.**

**(b) The printed `5 (4-3)` column — PASSES, but in a weaker form than (a), and the weakness must be
stated.** Table 2.7 prints a subtraction column that **no Finance Commission publishes** (no
Commission tests its own projections ex post) and that **no Union budget document publishes** (the
Union reports releases, not variances against a Commission's forecast) and that **Tamil Nadu's own
budget does not publish either**. The column heading is literally `5 (4-3)`. **An auditor printing a
subtraction that neither party performs, in a document both use, is the part-03 shape.**

Three qualifications, all of which cut:
- **The source line is "Details furnished by the Finance Department"** ⚑ — the compilation is a
  party's. *But a control exists and I ran it:* the actual-devolution column is reproduced exactly in
  **Table 2.4 of the same report**, which is drawn from the CAG-certified Finance Accounts —
  FY2019-20 26,393, FY2022-23 38,731, FY2023-24 46,072, identical in both tables. **So the actual
  column is corroborated against audited accounts and the projection column is the Commission's own
  published number. The inputs are Union-origin even though the compilation is the State's.**
- **The difference measures the Commission's forecasting error as much as the Union's conduct**, and
  no retrieved document decomposes the two. An instrument that produces an uninterpretable fact has
  disciplined the arithmetic and not the argument.
- **The sign flips and stays flipped**, which is the single strongest indicator that this instrument
  is not simply reproducing the complainant's case. **An instrument that only ever produces numbers
  favourable to the party that compiled the inputs would be suspect; this one produces
  −₹30,426 crore and then +₹31,887 crore.** The flip is evidence *of* the instrument's independence,
  even as it weakens the grievance.

**(c) Two further items pass in the strong form and should not be lost in the general finding.** The
**GST protected-revenue recomputation** (F-27) — the auditor going back to the statutory base year
and compounding it itself rather than accepting either party's shortfall claim, and publishing the
multiplication. And the **deficit restatement** (F-31) — the auditor correcting the *State's* own
number upward every year and titling a chart "Persistent understatement". **An instrument that
corrects the complainant is the same instrument that convicts the respondent, and its credibility on
the second rests on its willingness to do the first.**

**The limit of the whole instrument, stated plainly.** The CAG's State Finances Audit Report can
audit **what Tamil Nadu received and how Tamil Nadu accounted for it**. It cannot audit **what the
Union collected**, because that is a different report series with a different addressee — which is
why part 03's trap ("Tamil Nadu has no independent measurement of Union cess collections and cannot
have one") survives everything in this section. **And where the Union declined to supply data, the
auditor's power stopped**: A-6, the GST transactions the CAG asked for and did not get, certifying a
State's accounts on a test basis as a one-time exception. **The disciplining instrument in this part
is the strongest one the phase has found, and the Union can still switch off any part of it by not
answering.**

**Q6. RBI's *State Finances: A Study of Budgets*. NOT AN INDEPENDENT INSTRUMENT — it is a common
basis, and RBI says so itself.** The brief named it as a disciplining candidate and it must be
answered. RBI's own Explanatory Note, as recorded in the recovered stage-2 material (section C):

> "This Report is based on the receipts and expenditure data presented in the **budget documents of
> 31 State governments** and union territories (UTs) with legislature… supplemented with… Finance
> Accounts of the States published by [the] CAG… **The analysis conforms to the data presented in
> State budgets and the accounting classification thereof.**"

with Statement 17's footer reading *"Source: Budget documents of the State governments."*

**Retrieval note, and it matters: I did not fetch this document in this session.** It was retrieved
by a stage-2 grandchild from RBI's own HTML publication mirrors on `www.rbi.org.in`
(`rbidocs.rbi.org.in` stalls on the TLS handshake), and it belongs to **part 04's** source list, not
to this part's. **It is carried here as a reasoned position, not entered as a source of this part.**

**Why the conclusion nonetheless holds and is important.** RBI *State Finances* restates 31 States'
own budget numbers on one accounting classification. That is genuinely valuable — it makes States
comparable — but **comparability is a common basis, not independent evidence.** It cannot audit what
the Union actually released, it inherits every State's own definitions, and on this part's own
findings it would inherit **Tamil Nadu's understated deficit** (F-31), **whichever off-budget-
borrowing definition the State was using that year** (P-G), and **the complete absence of the
₹30,085.53 crore direct-to-SIA channel** (F-24), which is not in any State budget by construction.
**The same caveat attaches to the FC-commissioned *Tamil Nadu State Government Finances* study**,
whose source note reads *"State Budget Documents of Tamil Nadu (Various Years)"* — F-19 already
grades it on both axes, and Q5(b)'s corroboration control is the reason the audit series and the
budget-document series must be carried as two things (CS-TN-7).

**Q7. The general result for this part, stated as the phase's crux question answers it.**

| Instrument | Puts both sides on one ledger? | Common basis or independent evidence? |
|---|---|---|
| Supreme Court judgment, 2025 INSC 481 ¶13 | **Yes, completely, as to chronology** | **Independent evidence** — each column is the other party's admission and the *interval* is the Court's operation (Q1) |
| Supreme Court, Article 143 opinion | **Yes, as to what the law is** | n/a — it is the law, not a measurement (Q2) |
| Article 131 suit | **Would, and is dormant** | Would be independent, if heard (Q3) |
| Finance Commission report | **Yes, as to positions** | **Common basis** — every factual input is the Union's (Q4) |
| **CAG State Finances Audit Report** | **Yes, and it is the only fiscal instrument here that does** | **Independent evidence** on the SIA quantum in the strong form, on the `5 (4-3)` column in a weaker form, on the protected-revenue recomputation and the deficit restatement in the strong form (Q5) |
| RBI *State Finances* | Comparable, not confronted | **Common basis** — the States' own budget documents restated (Q6) |
| Tamil Nadu government documents ⚑ / Union releases about Tamil Nadu ⚐ | No | Party evidence, both directions |

**The single sentence.** *In the constitutional dispute Tamil Nadu has an instrument that disciplined
the facts completely and has since had the jurisdiction behind it withdrawn; in the fiscal dispute it
has an instrument — its own auditor — that produces facts neither government supplies, and the Union
can limit it by declining to answer.* **Both halves of that sentence are established from documents
neither government wrote.**

**Q8. What would have to be retrieved to close what is left open.** In descending order of value:
(i) the **Union-side by-State disclosure of direct-to-SIA transfers**, to test whether A-9's absence
is a publication failure or a retrieval failure; (ii) the **conditions attached to the XIV FC
performance grants**, without which R-7 must not be authored (A-7); (iii) **the same six-edition CAG
sweep for West Bengal and Bihar**, which would settle whether F-31's "persistent understatement" is a
Tamil Nadu attribute or a property of State budgeting — the route is recorded at P-E and costs one
`dig` and twelve page fetches per State; (iv) **whether the FY2018-19 GST-data caveat at A-6 appears
in other States' reports**, which would convert R-6 from a Tamil Nadu record into a national one;
(v) the **plaint in the Article 131 suit**, which is closed by construction (A-4, P-B).
