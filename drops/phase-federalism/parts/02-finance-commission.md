# Part 02 — Fiscal devolution and Finance Commission awards

*Stage 2 (research), `/phase federalism`. **COMPLETE.** Written incrementally to disk as evidence landed.*

*Scope covered: the FC-XIII, FC-XIV, FC-XV and FC-XVI awards (vertical share, horizontal formula,

**The Sixteenth Finance Commission has reported** — submitted to the President 17 November 2025, its

## Findings

### F-1. The vertical share: 32 → 42 → 41 → 41, and the 42→41 step is J&K leaving the denominator

The share of the divisible pool recommended for the states by the last four Commissions, each read
from the Commission's own report:

| Commission | Award period | Recommended state share of the divisible pool |
|---|---|---|
| FC-XIII | FY2010-11 → FY2014-15 | **32 per cent** |
| FC-XIV | FY2015-16 → FY2019-20 | **42 per cent** |
| FC-XV (first report) | FY2020-21 | **41 per cent** |
| FC-XV (final report) | FY2021-22 → FY2025-26 | **41 per cent** |
| FC-XVI | FY2026-27 → FY2030-31 | **41 per cent** |

**The 42→41 step is not a cut.** FC-XVI states the reason in its own words, para 7.16, verbatim:

> "In 2019, when the work of the FC-15 was still in progress, the Union Government reorganized the
> erstwhile State of Jammu & Kashmir into the union territories (UTs) of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh.
> **Estimating that the tax share of the erstwhile State would have been approximately 1 per cent of
> the divisible pool, the FC-15 fixed the share of the remaining twenty-eight States at 41 per cent.**
> Therefore, currently, the tax revenues in the divisible pool are shared in a 41:59 ratio between the
> States and the Union."

FC-XVI restates it at para 3.x ("…of 41 per cent, primarily to accommodate the exclusion of Jammu and
Kashmir following its" reorganisation). The denominator changed; the per-state entitlement did not.
Part 01 hands this forward and it holds: **do not render 42→41 as a reduction.**

FC-XVI's own characterisation of its decision, para 7.68 verbatim: *"Presently, for its award period,
the Commission recommends retaining the States' share in the divisible pool at its current level of
41 per cent."* And at para 1381-2 of the report text: it *"effectively maintained the status quo on
vertical devolution."*

### F-2. The Commission itself measured "recommended vs actual" — and split the answer in two

This is the phase's central quantity and FC-XVI addressed it head-on, in a section titled
**"Devolution: Recommended versus Actual"** (paras 7.26–7.35). Its answer has two halves that point
in opposite directions, and the halves differ **by denominator**, not by fact.

**Half one — against the divisible pool, there is no shortfall.** Para 7.27–7.28: the Union transfers
on an estimated pool, then adjusts **twice** — once when actual tax revenue is known (in FY T+2), and
again when the **CAG certifies the net proceeds** (in FY T+3). Table 7.4 walks FY2018-19 to FY2022-23
through the full adjustment and the last row ("Final devolution amount of FY T after all adjustments")
**exactly equals** row 6 ("Devolution as per FC recommendations" = 0.42 or 0.41 × CAG-certified pool).
On the Commission's arithmetic the states received precisely 41/42 per cent of the certified pool.

**Half two — against gross tax revenue, the shortfall is large and it moves.** Table 7.5 gives
devolution as a percentage of GTR, and the award-period averages are **27.9 (FC-XIII), 34.0 (FC-XIV),
32.1 (FC-XV)**. Against a recommended 41–42 per cent this is a gap of **7–9 percentage points of GTR**.
The Commission's own explanation, para 7.35 verbatim:

> "the divisible pool as a proportion of GTR ended up shrinking from **89.2 per cent of GTR during the
> award period of the FC-13 to 82.1 per cent** during the award period of the FC-14. This trend of
> shrinking share of the divisible pool in GTR continued during the FC-15, with the share averaging
> **78.3 per cent** during its award period."

And para 7.67, sharper still: cesses and surcharges *"having cut the size of the divisible pool from
**89.1 per cent of GTR in 2014-15** to a **74-80 per cent range** during the first four years of the
FC-15 award period for which actuals are available"*.

**So the "gap between 41 per cent recommended and what states actually got" is not one number, it is
two, and which one you quote is a choice of denominator.** Both are the Commission's own. See
§Definitional disagreements D-1 and → part 03.

### F-3. The Commission adjudicated the states' central complaint and found it *partly* unsupported

Para 7.35 verbatim: *"while the claim that increased cesses and surcharges have almost wiped out the
increase in the devolution recommended by the FC-14 is **unsupported by data**, it is true that the
devolution as a percent of GTR during the award period of even the FC-14 did not rise as much as it
would have had cesses and surcharges not risen as a proportion of GTR."*

This is a finding *against* the states on the strong form of their claim and *for* them on the weak
form, from the body they had asked to adjudicate. FC-XVI's arithmetic: FC transfers (devolution +
grants) averaged **32.5 per cent of GTR under FC-XIII, 39.4 under FC-XIV, 37.6/37.7 under FC-XV**
(Table 7.5 gives 37.6 in the Average row; para 7.34 prose says 37.7 — a 0.1 pp internal inconsistency
in the same chapter, recorded at PR-3). The FC-XIV boost was *"substantially, even if not fully,
preserved"*.

### F-4. FC-XVI recommends the Union publish the CAG certificate — which is to say it is not published

Para 7.30 verbatim: *"The responsibility of ascertaining and certifying net proceeds which are to be
shared between the Union and States is entrusted to the CAG by the Constitution. **In the spirit of
transparency and to put the concerns of States to rest, we recommend that, every year, the Union
Government disclose the data pertaining to net proceeds as certified by CAG.**"*

A sitting Finance Commission recommending annual disclosure of the CAG net-proceeds certificate is a
**T1 statement that it is not currently disclosed**. This is a **not-published** absence (A-1), not a
withheld one — no named requester, request or date is on the record here. It is also the strongest
single item in this part's §Disciplining measure: the number that closes the recommended-vs-actual
question exists, is certified by an independent auditor, and is not routinely public.

### F-5. Before FY2018-19 the CAG adjustment was made once in ten years, and the Centre overpaid ₹15,756 crore

Para 7.29 verbatim: *"before 2018-19, whereas the first adjustment used to take place as described
above, the second, CAG adjustment took place **only once in ten years**. Our calculations for years
2010-11 to 2017-18, not reported here, show that on a net basis, after the first adjustment, the Union
Government paid the State Governments **₹15,756 crores more than what was due** based on the
CAG-certified divisible pools and the corresponding FC-recommended shares of States for these years.
**According to the Ministry of Finance, this gap has been settled by recording the excess amount as
payment due on certain services that the Union Government had received from the States during these
years.**"*

Three findings sit inside one paragraph. (i) A **reconciliation-frequency break at FY2018-19** — before
it, decadal; after it, annual. (ii) The Commission's supporting calculation for FY2010-11→FY2017-18 is
explicitly **"not reported here"** — the working is withheld from the report (absence A-2). (iii) A
₹15,756 crore constitutional overpayment was settled by **re-characterising it as payment for services**
on the Ministry of Finance's say-so, reported in the Commission's report as the Ministry's account, not
as anything the Commission verified.

### F-6. The horizontal formula: the 1971→2011 population base switch, and its arithmetic

FC-XVI Table 8.3 (its own compilation from FC-XI to FC-XV reports) plus its Table 8.8:

| Criterion | FC-XI | FC-XII | FC-XIII | FC-XIV | FC-XV | **FC-XVI** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Population (1971) | 10 | 25 | 25 | 17.5 | — | — |
| Population (2011) | — | — | — | 10 | 15 | **17.5** |
| Area | 7.5 | 10 | 10 | 15 | 15 | **10** |
| Income Distance / Fiscal Capacity | 62.5 | 50 | 47.5 | 50 | 45 | **42.5** (Per Capita GSDP Distance) |
| Index of Infrastructure | 7.5 | — | — | — | — | — |
| Forest | — | — | — | 7.5 | 10 | **10** |
| Total Fertility Rate / Demographic Performance | — | — | — | — | 12.5 | **10** |
| Tax / Fiscal Effort | 5 | 7.5 | — | — | 2.5 | — |
| Fiscal Discipline | 7.5 | 7.5 | 17.5 | — | — | — |
| **Contribution to GDP** | — | — | — | — | — | **10 (new)** |
| Total | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | **100** |

**The 1971→2011 switch is a two-step, not a one-step.** FC-XIV was the first to carry a 2011-population
term (10 per cent) alongside 17.5 per cent on 1971 population — so the break the public argument dates
to FC-XV actually begins at FC-XIV. FC-XV dropped 1971 entirely (15 per cent on 2011 population) and
introduced **Demographic Performance at 12.5 per cent**, explicitly as the offset for states that had
reduced fertility. FC-XVI keeps that architecture, raises 2011 population to 17.5, cuts income-distance
to 42.5, cuts area to 10, and adds **Contribution to GDP at 10 per cent** — a criterion no previous
Commission has used, and one that runs *against* the equity tilt of every other term.

FC-XVI's own read of the equity/efficiency split, para 8.61: under FC-XI to FC-XV *"each of these five
FCs has disbursed 75 per cent or more of the divisible pool based on equity."* Under FC-XVI's own
formula, Contribution to GDP at 10 per cent is the first material efficiency-and-size term.

### F-8. Testing the sibling part's "admission against interest" reading — it holds, with a qualification

The claim to test: *FC-XVI's own narrative says Bihar's and Uttar Pradesh's shares fell across the last
two awards while Odisha, Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh gained — a Commission stating that the states
its formula favours have lost ground.*

**The text.** Para 8.70 verbatim: *"Devolution shares of the three low-income States - Jharkhand, Uttar
Pradesh and Bihar, have consistently stayed above their corresponding population shares, but **not by as
wide a margin as Odisha, Chhattisgarh or Madhya Pradesh**. In the case of **Bihar and Uttar Pradesh, the
shares have been significantly lower during the award periods of the last two FCs than those of the
earlier three FCs.** In all likelihood, **the introduction of forest as a criterion under the FC-14
shifted the devolution shares from these (and other) States to Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh.**"*
And para 8.69: *"**Surprisingly**, their shares in devolution have exceeded the corresponding population
shares by a wider margin than those of the low per capita GSDP States."*

**The arithmetic, from the Commission's own Table 8.5 (devolution share ÷ population share):**

| State | FC-XI | FC-XII | FC-XIII | FC-XIV | FC-XV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bihar | 1.78 | 1.35 | 1.25 | 1.11 | 1.15 |
| Uttar Pradesh | 1.21 | 1.17 | 1.17 | 1.07 | 1.07 |
| Chhattisgarh | — | 1.29 | 1.15 | **1.43** | **1.59** |
| Madhya Pradesh | 1.48 | 1.13 | 1.17 | **1.24** | **1.29** |
| Odisha | 1.39 | 1.42 | 1.35 | 1.31 | **1.28** |

**Verdict: the reading holds on Bihar, UP, Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh; it does not hold on Odisha.**
Bihar falls 1.78→1.15 and UP 1.21→1.07 across five Commissions, with the sharp step at FC-XIV.
Chhattisgarh rises 1.15→1.59 and Madhya Pradesh 1.17→1.29 across the same step. **Odisha *falls*,
1.35→1.31→1.28** — it is high but declining, so "Odisha gained" is not what Table 8.5 shows. Odisha
gained *relative to Bihar and UP*, not absolutely. The sibling part's phrasing should be tightened to
"Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh gained; Odisha stayed high while Bihar and UP fell."

**Is it an admission against interest?** Partly. It is genuinely against interest in that a Commission
whose formula is defended as pro-poor-state concedes that its two poorest large states lost ground and
that two better-off resource states gained — and calls it *"surprisingly"*. But note the qualifier the
Commission attaches: *"In all likelihood"*. It is a **conjecture, not a decomposition.** The Commission
does not show the forest criterion's contribution; it infers it. Which is exactly absence A-3.

### F-9. No Commission publishes the state × criterion decomposition — confirmed, and FC-XVI is the proof

The sibling part hands this forward as absence A2. **It survives.** FC-XVI publishes the weights
(Table 8.8) and the resulting shares (Table 8.9) and a **Technical Note** giving the formulas, but it
does not publish, for any state, the ₹ or percentage-point contribution of each criterion to that
state's share. The Commission's own para 8.70 has to say *"In all likelihood, the introduction of forest
as a criterion under the FC-14 shifted the devolution shares"* — a body that had a decomposition would
not write "in all likelihood" about its own predecessor's formula. Positive control on the sweep: see
§Absences A-3 for the phrases searched and the known-present item that confirmed the search worked.

### F-10. Two admissions by the Commission that its own comparisons are not clean

Para 8.62 verbatim: *"the States' shares are impacted not only due to shifts in the devolution criteria
applied, but also because of the changes in the values of underlying parameters… **In fact, analysis
done by this Commission shows that, even if we were to retain the exact same criteria and associated
weights as the FC-15, the States' shares shift significantly due to the updation of underlying data.**"*

The consequence for this part: **a series of inter-se shares across Commissions is not a series of one
quantity.** Each award's shares are produced by a different formula *and* different underlying data, and
the Commission says it cannot separate the two effects. The series is still worth carrying — it is the
operative entitlement — but it must be labelled as a step function of awards, never as a trend.

### F-11. The states asked for 50 per cent; eighteen of twenty-eight asked for exactly that

Para 7.36: *"States have overwhelmingly pitched for an increase in the States' share in the divisible
pool from 41 per cent to 50 per cent. **As many as eighteen out of the twenty-eight States** have made
this recommendation."* Exceptions named: Sikkim (40 per cent on a Gross-Revenue-Receipts pool, or 50
per cent on a pool including cesses/surcharges), Madhya Pradesh (48 per cent on a whole-GTR pool, 40.7
on a whole-GRR pool), Nagaland 48, Assam 45, Himachal Pradesh 41.

**This is a T1 count of an advocacy position, not a measurement** — see §The trap. The states'
memoranda are pleadings. What is *measured* here is only that 18 of 28 pleaded the same number.

### F-12. The Union's counter-quantity: "more than 49 per cent of Gross Revenue Receipts"

Para 7.42, the Union's memorandum as recorded by the Commission: *"It argues that the current level of
vertical devolution, where **more than 49 per cent of the Gross Revenue Receipts (GRR) are estimated to
be transferred to States**, is not fiscally sustainable."*

The Union answers a divisible-pool question with a **gross-revenue-receipts** answer. 41 per cent of the
divisible pool and 49 per cent of GRR are not competing estimates of one quantity; they are two
quantities with different numerators (GRR transfers include grants and CSS; divisible-pool devolution
does not) and different denominators (GRR includes non-tax revenue; the divisible pool excludes cesses,
surcharges, UT revenue and collection cost). **Neither side is misreporting. They are not measuring the
same thing.** This is the cleanest definitional disagreement in the part (D-2).

Para 7.45, the Union on cesses: *"Excluding GST compensation cess, cesses comprised **6.7 per cent of
gross revenue during the FC-15 period - less than the FC-13's 7.0 per cent**."* Note the denominator is
*gross revenue*, and the *exclusion* of the GST compensation cess is doing the work. FC-XVI's own para
7.35 measures the same phenomenon on a divisible-pool-share-of-GTR basis and finds a fall from 89.2 to
78.3 per cent. **Same period, opposite direction, because of two definitional choices.** → part 03.

### F-13. The Union did NOT accept the 15th FC's sector-specific (₹1,29,987 cr) or state-specific (₹49,599 cr) grants — established from the Explanatory Memorandum itself

This is the gap between recommendation and action the brief asked to be established from primary
documents. It is established, and the wording is unambiguous once you read the whole Memorandum, because
**the Memorandum uses a different closing formula for different recommendation blocks.**

I retrieved the *Explanatory Memorandum as to the Action Taken on the Recommendations Made by the
Fifteenth Finance Commission in its Final Report* (Ministry of Finance, DEA, signed Nirmala Sitharaman,
1 February 2021) from
`https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/atr-summary-annex-III-english.pdf`
and read it end to end. The closing formulae, block by block, verbatim:

| Block | Amount recommended | Union's closing formula, **verbatim** |
|---|---|---|
| Sharing of Union taxes (41 per cent) | — | *"The Government has **accepted** the above recommendation of the Commission."* |
| Revenue deficit grants | **₹2,94,514 crore**, 17 states | *"The Government has **accepted** the above recommendations of the Commission."* |
| Local body grants | **₹4,36,361 crore** | *"The Government has **accepted** the above recommendations of the Commission."* |
| Disaster grants (SDRMF) | **₹1,60,153 crore** states (Union share ₹1,22,601 cr) + **₹68,463 crore** national | *"The Government has **accepted** these recommendations of the Commission."* |
| **Grants to States for Specific Sectors** (8 sectors: health, school education, higher education, agriculture, PMGSY road maintenance, aspirational districts and blocks, judiciary, statistics) | **₹1,29,987 crore** | *"**Government will give due consideration to sectors identified by the Commission while formulating and implementing existing and new Centrally Sponsored and Central Sector Schemes.**"* |
| **State Specific Grants** | **₹49,599 crore** | *"**Keeping in view the untied resources with the State Governments and the fiscal commitments of the Central Government, due consideration will be given to the above recommendation.**"* |
| Modernisation Fund for Defence and Internal Security | ₹2,38,354 crore indicative, ₹1,53,354 cr from CFI | *"The Government has **accepted in-principle** the creation of non-lapsable fund… Sources of funding and modalities will be examined in due course."* |
| Fiscal roadmap / borrowing ceilings | — | *"The Government **accepts in-principle**… Other recommendations… will be **examined separately**."* |
| Other recommendations | — | *"The Government **will examine** these recommendations… in due course."* |

**Four verbs, deliberately distinguished: accepted · accepted in-principle · will give due consideration ·
will examine.** The two grant categories worth **₹1,79,586 crore** together are the only *quantified grant
recommendations* in the entire Memorandum that do not receive "accepted". They receive "due
consideration". And for the sector-specific grants the Memorandum states where the money will go
instead: **into centrally sponsored and central sector schemes** — that is, from an Article 275(1)
Finance Commission grant (charged on the Consolidated Fund, unconditional in form, formula-determined)
into an Article 282 scheme grant (voted, conditional, Union-designed). → part 04, which owns the Article
282 side.

**Positive control on this sweep.** Before concluding that "accepted" is absent from these two blocks, I
verified my search found it where it is present: the string *"The Government has accepted"* appears at
the close of four other blocks in the same document (tax sharing, revenue deficit grants, local bodies,
disaster). The document is 14 KB of extracted text and I read every line, not a keyword slice. The needle
was not the number `1,29,987` — it was the **closing sentence pattern of each recommendation block**,
which is the only place the Union's action is recorded.

**What is NOT established here.** Whether any part of the ₹1,79,586 crore was subsequently released
under another head. "Due consideration will be given" is not a refusal on its face. Establishing
non-release requires the Demand for Grants "Transfers to States" actuals or a parliamentary reply — see
§Sources NOT retrieved and → part 04.

### F-14. The Union's own account of the 42→41 step is a *third* framing, and it is the most revealing

FC-XV said it "adjusted for" the new UTs. FC-XVI said the erstwhile state's share was "estimated at
approximately 1 per cent". The Union's Explanatory Memorandum, para 3, says something subtly different,
verbatim:

> "The Commission has recommended that 41 per cent of the net proceeds of Union taxes should be shared
> with the States as against the present 42%. The Commission felt that, **financial resources equivalent
> to 1% of the net proceeds of Union taxes should be retained with the Central Government for financing
> the requirements of the newly formed Union Territories of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh.**"

**"Retained with the Central Government for financing the requirements of"** the UTs is an earmarking
claim. It asserts a purpose for the 1 percentage point. Neither FC-XV nor FC-XVI makes that claim — both
describe a denominator adjustment, not a hypothecation. **No instrument tracks whether the retained
1 per cent was in fact spent on Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh**, and none could, because money in the
Consolidated Fund of India is not hypothecated. This is a **never-defined** quantity: the Union stated a
purpose for a sum in an Article 281 document laid before Parliament, and the sum has no ledger. See A-5.

### F-15. FC-XVI's ToR are bare constitutional language — and the Commission says so

FC-XVI para 1.8 reproduces its ToR in full and they are the three Article 280(3)(a)–(c) heads verbatim
plus one added sentence: *"The Commission may review the present arrangements on financing Disaster
Management initiatives, with reference to the funds constituted under the Disaster Management Act, 2005
(53 of 2005), and make appropriate recommendations thereon."* Nothing else. No census direction, no
"populist measures", no defence fund, no direction on whether revenue deficit grants should exist.

Para 2.2, verbatim: *"The terms of reference of the past FCs have included explicit directions on the
factors they must consider… **In contrast to this practice, the terms of reference of the present
Commission adhere strictly to the charge as per the Constitutional language without any further
direction on what factors the Commission should consider in making its recommendations.**"*

**A Commission volunteering, unprompted, that its ToR are unusually bare is a T1 signal about the
previous round.** It is not a criticism of FC-XV's ToR and should not be reported as one. It is evidence
that the practice changed after FC-XV, and it is the most defensible thing that can be said about the
ToR controversy from primary sources alone.

### F-16. The FC-XV ToR did direct the Commission on revenue deficit grants — quoted by its successor

FC-XVI para 9.9, verbatim: *"the ToR of the Fifteenth Finance Commission (2020-26) **explicitly mandated
it to consider "…whether revenue deficit grants be provided at all"**."*

This is a T1 quotation of a contested ToR clause, from a later Commission's report, not from journalism.
It establishes that at least one FC-XV ToR clause put a substantive policy question — should a category
of constitutional grant exist — to the Commission. FC-XV answered it **yes**, recommending ₹2,94,514
crore to seventeen states. FC-XVI, with no such direction in its own ToR, answered it **no** (LR-4).
**The Commission with the directive ToR gave the states money; the Commission with the bare ToR did
not.** That inversion is worth carrying: it is direct evidence against the simplest version of the
states' theory that directive ToR determine outcomes.

### F-16A. The Article 280 machinery: the appointing authority is a party to the dispute, and every appointment order is unilateral

**The constitutional text, as the Commission states it** (FC-XVI para 2.1): *"Article 280 of the Indian
Constitution mandates that the President appoint a Finance Commission (FC) every five years or sooner"*
to recommend on vertical devolution, horizontal devolution, the principles governing grants-in-aid under
Article 275(1), and measures to augment state Consolidated Funds for panchayats and municipalities.
Article 280(3)(d) adds *"any other matter referred to the Commission by the President in the interests of
sound finance"* — **the hook on which every directive ToR clause hangs.** Article 281 requires the
recommendations to be laid before each House of Parliament with an explanatory memorandum on the action
taken.

**The machinery in operation, FC-XVI, from its own para 1.2–1.7.** Constituted *"In pursuance of clause
(1) of Article 280 of the Constitution of India and the provisions of the **Finance Commission
(Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951 (Act No. 33 of 1951)**"* by Presidential Notification **S.O. 5533(E)
dated 31 December 2023**, for FY2026-27→FY2030-31. **Dr Arvind Panagariya**, former Vice-Chairman of NITI
Aayog and Professor at Columbia University, appointed Chairman; **Shri Ritvik Ranjanam Pandey** Secretary.
Members by **S.O. 369(E) of 30 January 2024**: Ajay Narayan Jha (former FC-XV Member and former
Expenditure Secretary), Annie George Mathew (former Special Secretary, Expenditure), Dr Niranjan
Rajadhyaksha (full-time), Dr Soumya Kanti Ghosh (part-time, Group Chief Economic Adviser, State Bank of
India). Rajadhyaksha **did not assume office** and was replaced by **Dr Manoj Panda** (S.O. 1625(E),
5 April 2024). Ajay Narayan Jha **resigned with effect from 8 April 2025**. **Shri T. Rabi Sankar**,
Deputy Governor of the RBI, appointed part-time Member (S.O. 2506(E), 7 June 2025). Tenure extended from
31 October to 30 November 2025 (S.O. 4640(E), 10 October 2025); report submitted 17 November 2025.

**The structural point.** *Every one of those instruments is a unilateral Presidential order — that is,
a Union Government order.* The states have **no role** in constituting the Commission, **no role** in
choosing its members, **no role** in framing its terms of reference, and **no forum** in which to contest
any of it. **The body that adjudicates between the Union and the states is appointed by the Union and
instructed by the Union.** That is not a defect discovered here; it is the constitutional design, and it
is the single fact that makes the FC-XV ToR controversy (LR-3, F-17) a constitutional argument rather
than an administrative one. It is also why the Commission's occasional findings *against* the appointing
authority — FC-XIV para 12.22 on conditionality, FC-XVI para 9.2 on discontinued releases, FC-XVI Table
10.1 on release ratios — carry more weight than their content alone would suggest.

**One institutional detail worth carrying.** FC-XVI's chapter 1 records that the Commission spent nearly
four months stabilising operations, that the Model Code of Conduct before the 2024 general election
delayed its financial approvals, and that half the lifts in its building had been out of order for years
(paras 1.10–1.14). A constitutional commission with a five-year award to determine and a two-year life
loses a sixth of it to office setup. The Commission recommends that future Commissions be given
pre-furnished, contiguous space and funds at the time of constitution.

### F-17. The contested FC-XV terms of reference, verbatim from the Gazette text reproduced in the Commission's own annexure volume

The ToR were retrieved as the Gazette of India notification text printed as **Annex 1.1** of FC-XV
Volume II (S.O. 3755(E), 27 November 2017, signed Ram Nath Kovind, file No. 10(1)-B(S)/2016). The four
clauses that drew objection, verbatim:

**Para 8, complete — the census direction.** One sentence, no qualifier:
> "8. The Commission shall use the population data of 2011 while making its recommendations."

**Para 7(viii) — "populist measures".** Para 7's chapeau is permissive (*"The Commission may consider
proposing measurable performance-based incentives for States"*) but the nine sub-clauses are a closed
list, and the eighth is:
> "(viii) Control or lack of it in incurring expenditure on populist measures;"

**Para 6(iv) — the FC-XIV reversal clause with the governing programme named:**
> "(iv) The impact on the fiscal situation of the Union Government of substantially enhanced tax
> devolution to States following recommendations of the 14th Finance Commission, coupled with the
> continuing imperative of the national development programme including **New India – 2022**;"

**Para 5, last sentence — whether a class of constitutional grant should exist at all:**
> "The Commission may also examine whether revenue deficit grants be provided at all."

**Para 6(vi) — the borrowing-consent lever:**
> "(vi) The conditions that GoI may impose on the States while providing consent under Article 293(3)
> of the Constitution."

### F-20. FC-XVI states as a finding of fact that the Union "largely discontinued releasing" sector- and state-specific grants after FC-XIV — and narrows its own inquiry because of it

FC-XVI para 9.2, verbatim:

> "Sector- and State-specific grants address particular needs, such as health, education, or
> geographical challenges, but have been given only intermittently. The First Finance Commission
> introduced such grants, which have continued since then. **But following the rise in States' share of
> the divisible pool to 42 per cent by the Fourteenth Finance Commission, the Union Government largely
> discontinued releasing them. Accordingly, the present Commission confines its analysis to revenue
> deficit grants.**"

Three distinct claims sit in that passage and they should be kept apart.
1. **A finding of fact by a constitutional Commission**: since FC-XIV, the Union largely stopped
   releasing this class of grant.
2. **A consequence for method**: the Commission therefore **narrowed its own inquiry** — it stopped
   analysing a category of award because the Union had stopped honouring it. That is a constitutional
   body adjusting its remit to the executive's revealed behaviour, stated in print.
3. **A consequence for the award** (LR-4): it then recommended none at all.

**Combined with F-18, this is the strongest chain in the part.** The Commission says the Union stopped
releasing; the Union confirms to Parliament that nil is being released; the next Commission stops
recommending. **The recommendation and the action converged by the recommendation moving.**

## Candidate ledger records

### LR-1 — FC-XIV raises the vertical share from 32 to 42 per cent (report 15 Dec 2014; effective FY2015-16)

**What happened.** The Fourteenth Finance Commission (chair Y.V. Reddy) recommended that the states'
share of the divisible pool rise from **32 to 42 per cent** with effect from FY2015-16 — the largest
single-step increase in the history of the institution, and the first departure from the incremental
1.5-point moves of FC-XI (29.5), FC-XII (30.5) and FC-XIII (32).

**Objective stated at announcement.** FC-XIV para 8.13, verbatim: *"increasing the share of tax
devolution to 42 per cent of the divisible pool would serve the twin objectives of **increasing the flow
of unconditional transfers to the States and yet leave appropriate fiscal space for the Union to carry
out specific-purpose transfers** to the States."* Crucially, the same paragraph states the move was
**not** intended to increase total transfers: *"a compositional shift in transfers from grants to tax
devolution is desirable for two reasons. First, **it does not impose an additional fiscal burden on the
Union Government.**"* And para 8.12: *"amounts equivalent to more than 60 per cent of the divisible pool
goes to the States in various forms of transfers and keeping in view the Union Government's expenditure
responsibilities, **there is little scope to increase the share of aggregate transfers.**"*

**Strongest case for.** The change did what it said. On FC-XVI's own measurement (Table 7.5) devolution
rose from an FC-XIII average of 27.9 per cent of GTR to an FC-XIV average of 34.0 — a 6.1 pp jump — and
total FC transfers from 32.5 to 39.4 per cent. The composition shifted decisively toward untied money:
the devolution:grant ratio inside FC transfers moved from roughly 6:1 to roughly 6.3:1 while the whole
envelope grew. Formula-based, unconditional transfers are, on FC-XIV's reasoning at para 8.11,
*"conducive to sound fiscal federalism"* precisely because they are not conditional. FC-XV para 6.24
records that the Union accepted the arrangement and that *"the provisional actuals of the Union
Government for 2019-20 show it as transferring a slightly higher share of gross revenue receipts to the
States"* than the ~49 per cent FC-XIV had projected. FC-XVI para 7.34 concludes the boost was
*"substantially, even if not fully, preserved"* a decade later.

**Strongest case against, in its own terms.** FC-XIV said explicitly it did not intend to raise
aggregate transfers, and the states got what was promised — a *recomposition*, not more money. Because
the 42 per cent applies to a pool the Union can shrink at will by levying cesses and surcharges, the
Union retained a lever that no Commission can reach: the divisible pool fell from 89.2 per cent of GTR
(FC-XIII average) to 82.1 (FC-XIV) to 78.3 (FC-XV) on FC-XVI's numbers. And FC-XIV *knew*: its first
listed consideration for the 42 per cent was *"States not being entitled to the growing share of cess
and surcharges."* So the increase was in part **compensation for a leak it did not plug** — and the leak
kept widening. On the Union's side (FC-XVI para 7.42), the same step *"had a lasting impact on the
Union's fiscal space, leading to persistent revenue deficits"* while *"increased untied transfers…
[have] not always translated into sustained fiscal prudence"* by the states.

**Different facts or different weightings?** **Different weightings of the same facts**, with one
genuine factual dispute embedded. Everyone agrees on Table 7.5. The weighting dispute is whether a
6.1 pp rise in devolution-to-GTR that leaves aggregate transfers roughly flat is a win. The *factual*
dispute is narrower and FC-XVI settled it against the states: para 7.35 — the claim that cesses
*"almost wiped out"* the FC-XIV increase is *"unsupported by data"*, while the weaker claim that the
increase *"did not rise as much as it would have had cesses and surcharges not risen"* is true.

### LR-2 — The Jammu and Kashmir reorganisation converts 42 per cent into 41 per cent (FY2020-21)

**What happened.** The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act 2019 converted the state into two Union
Territories with effect from 31 October 2019, mid-way through FC-XV's work. FC-XV, in its report for
FY2020-21 (submitted 5 December 2019), fixed the share of the remaining 28 states at **41 per cent** and
carried that forward into the final report for FY2021-22→FY2025-26.

**Objective stated at announcement.** FC-XV para 6.9, verbatim: *"stability and predictability of
resources was considered an essential component of long-term good budgeting and the vertical sharing of
resources was retained at the same level as that recommended by the FC-XIV. **We only adjusted for the
newly carved out Union Territories** of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, keeping the balance share broadly
equivalent at 41 per cent for the remaining twenty-eight States."*

**Strongest case for (this is not a cut).** A UT's requirements are met from the Union's own budget, so
its notional entitlement leaves the states' side of the ledger with it. FC-XIV had awarded J&K
**1.854 per cent** of the states' share (its Table 8.2), which is 1.854 × 42 ≈ **0.78 per cent of the
divisible pool**; FC-XVI para 7.16 records the estimate used as *"approximately 1 per cent of the
divisible pool."* The remaining 28 states' per-state entitlement is unchanged or marginally improved: on
FC-XV's own inter-se table, a state's rupees equal (its share × 41), and on FC-XIV's, (its share × 42)
where the shares summed over 29 states. The words *"broadly equivalent"* are the Commission's own.

**Strongest case against.** Two points, neither of which makes it a cut but both of which are real.
(i) The *headline* fell, and in a public argument conducted in headline numbers, "42 down to 41" did
work for the Union that "we removed a state from the denominator" would not have. (ii) The adjustment
was **estimated, not computed**: FC-XVI says *"Estimating that the tax share of the erstwhile State
would have been approximately 1 per cent."* The FC-XIV-implied figure is ~0.78 per cent, so the
adjustment was **rounded against the states by roughly 0.2 pp of the divisible pool** — on FY2022-23's
certified pool of ₹23,68,305 crore, on the order of ₹5,000 crore a year. No Commission publishes the
working. This is a small, real and unremarked haircut riding inside a defensible reclassification.

**Different facts or different weightings?** Different weightings, plus one unpublished fact — the
derivation of the 1 per cent. Both sides accept the reorganisation and the arithmetic form.

### LR-3 — The Fifteenth Finance Commission's terms of reference (27 November 2017), twice amended

**What happened.** FC-XV was constituted by Presidential Order **S.O. 3755(E) dated 27 November 2017**
(chair N.K. Singh) with an award period FY2020-21→FY2024-25. Its ToR were unusually directive. The ToR
were then amended twice: once in July 2019 to add a defence and internal-security funding question, and
again by **S.O. 4308(E) dated 29 November 2019**, which required the Commission to submit **two
reports** — a first report for FY2020-21 and a final report for FY2021-22→FY2025-26, extending the award
by a year. Both amendment orders are recited in the Union's own Explanatory Memorandum on the final
report (retrieved; see §Sources).

**Objective stated at announcement.** Recorded in the Union's Explanatory Memorandum, para 1, verbatim:
*"The Commission, vide S.O. No.4308 (E) dated 29th November, 2019, was mandated to submit two reports
i.e. a first report for financial year 2020-21 and a final report for the period 2021-22 to 2025-26."*
No reason for the split is given in the Memorandum.

**Strongest case for the Union.** The 2011-census direction is defensible on its face: no Commission
should be required to allocate 2020s money on 1971 demography, and FC-XIV had already put 10 per cent
weight on 2011 population without a ToR direction. The revenue-deficit-grant question — FC-XVI para 9.9
quotes the FC-XV ToR mandating it to consider *"…whether revenue deficit grants be provided at all"* —
is a legitimate question for the appointing authority to put, and FC-XV answered it **against** the
Union's implied preference, recommending grants to seventeen states. A ToR that the Commission is free
to answer either way is not a directed outcome.

**Strongest case for the objecting states.** The ToR were framed by one party to the dispute. The
Constitution gives the President — i.e. the Union Government — sole power to constitute the Commission
and to write what it must consider (Article 280(1) and 280(3)(d): *"any other matter referred to the
Commission by the President in the interests of sound finance"*). The states have no role in
constituting it, no role in framing the ToR, and no forum in which to contest them. A ToR that directs
the Commission to consider incentives around expenditure on "populist measures" asks an unelected body
to grade elected state governments' policy choices; a ToR that switches the population base transfers
money from states that reduced fertility to states that did not. Both effects are foreseeable at the
time of drafting.

**Different facts or different weightings?** **Different facts about a counterfactual**, which is why
it cannot be settled: the states assert that the ToR were drafted to produce a particular distributional
result; the Union asserts they were drafted to modernise the data base. Nothing in either the ToR text
or the reports establishes intent. What *is* establishable — and is — is the outcome (S-12, S-13) and
the sequence.

**A cross-check that survives.** FC-XVI's ToR, which I retrieved verbatim from its own para 1.8, contain
**nothing beyond the bare constitutional language** plus a disaster-management review. FC-XVI says so in
its own words at para 2.2: *"In contrast to this practice, **the terms of reference of the present
Commission adhere strictly to the charge as per the Constitutional language without any further
direction on what factors the Commission should consider** in making its recommendations."* A sitting
Commission drawing an explicit contrast between its own bare ToR and the *"often more elaborate"*
directions given to its predecessors is the closest thing to institutional acknowledgement that the
FC-XV ToR were an outlier. It is not an admission that they were improper. It is evidence that they were
unusual, from the body best placed to know.

### LR-4 — FC-XVI abolishes revenue deficit, sector-specific and state-specific grants (report 17 Nov 2025; accepted 1 Feb 2026)

**What happened.** The Sixteenth Finance Commission recommended **no revenue deficit grants, no
sector-specific grants and no state-specific grants** for FY2026-27→FY2030-31. Para 9.50, verbatim:
*"in continuation of the diminishing trend of the revenue deficit grants recommended by FC-15, which
reduce to near-zero level by 2025-26, **we do not recommend any revenue deficit grants to States. We
also do not recommend any sector-specific or State-specific grants.**"* Repeated at para 16.x in the
Summary of Recommendations.

**This is the largest structural change in the FC-XVI award and it is a discontinuity in the grant
series (S-3).** Revenue deficit grants have been awarded by every Commission in living memory; FC-XIII
awarded them to eight states, FC-XIV to many more, FC-XV to seventeen.

**Objective stated at announcement.** Two reasons, both in the Commission's words. Para 9.48: *"there is
significant scope of increasing revenues and rationalising expenditure. We firmly believe that the cause
of revenue deficit of States, as it stands today, lies in these factors."* Para 9.49, the time-consistency
argument: *"When States anticipate that shortfalls in their revenue account will be compensated through
RDGs, the incentive to undertake difficult but necessary fiscal reforms… weakens. Over time, this softens
fiscal discipline and **embeds dependency rather than resilience**."*

**Strongest case for.** The Commission has an evidence base and states it. Para 9.17: *"Successive
Commissions have provided larger revenue deficit grants in the early years, which taper down to
negligible levels towards the end of their award period, in the hope that the breathing room so provided
would encourage States to balance their revenue accounts. **However, this hope has not been realised**,
with revenue deficits becoming a near-permanent feature of the budgets of some States."* Para 9.18 gives
the numbers: revenue deficit grants **rose** from 1.1 per cent of gross tax receipts under FC-XIII to
2.2 under FC-XIV and 1.9 under FC-XV, while the number of states in revenue deficit **rose** from five in
FY2011-12 to twelve in FY2023-24. A grant designed to disappear that instead doubled, alongside a deficit
it was meant to close that instead spread, is a policy that failed on its own stated test. FC-XVI also
reaches back to FC-1's warning that grants should not *"diminish the responsibility of the State
Governments to balance their own budgets."*

**Strongest case against.** The RDG is the only instrument by which a Commission can reach a state whose
formula share is inadequate to its assessed need — FC-XVI itself says so at para 7.64: *"For the
individual States, shortfalls in revenues are dealt with as a part of the State-level evaluation of
revenues and expenditures by the Commission."* Removing it while simultaneously **holding the vertical
share flat at 41 per cent** removes the safety valve without opening the main valve. Second, "there is
significant scope of increasing revenues and rationalising expenditure" is a normative judgement about
28 heterogeneous governments, and FC-XVI's own para 9.17 concedes that *"the assumption of enforceability
through normative assessments… has not played out at all as originally envisioned"* — which is an
argument against the reliability of normative assessment generally, including the one now being used to
withhold the grants. Third, the states-specific grant is the channel through which claims that no formula
can capture (a border state's policing, an island's logistics) have historically been met.

**The Union's action.** The Explanatory Memorandum, February 2026, does **not** say "accepted" on this
item. Its exact formula, verbatim: *"**The Government takes note of the above assessment of the
Commission.**"* Every other block in the Memorandum on which the Union acted says *"The Government has
accepted the above recommendations of the Commission."* **"Takes note" is a distinct verb applied to
exactly one recommendation block**, and it is the block in which the Commission recommends giving the
states nothing. Whether that is significant — one cannot "accept" a recommendation to do nothing in the
same sense as one accepts a recommendation to pay — is a reading; what is established is that the Union
chose a different word here and nowhere else. See PR-2.

**Different facts or different weightings?** **Different weightings of the same facts.** Both sides
accept para 9.18's numbers. They differ on whether persistent deficits prove the grant creates
dependency or prove the underlying fiscal gap was never closed.

### LR-5 — FC-XVI introduces "Contribution to GDP" as a devolution criterion (10 per cent)

**What happened.** For the first time in the history of the institution, a Finance Commission assigned
weight — **10 per cent** — to a state's *contribution to national GDP* in the horizontal formula
(Table 8.8), while cutting per-capita-GSDP-distance from 45 to **42.5** and area from 15 to **10**.

**Objective stated at announcement.** The criterion sits in Chapter 8 among the efficiency criteria. The
Commission's framing at para 8.3: *"The efficiency-based criteria seek to encourage healthy revenue
growth and fiscal stability. To the extent that revenue growth depends on Gross State Domestic Product
(GSDP) growth, these criteria may also include incentives for growth."*

**Strongest case for.** Every previous formula has been overwhelmingly redistributive — FC-XVI's own
para 8.61: *"each of these five FCs has disbursed 75 per cent or more of the divisible pool based on
equity."* A formula with no term rewarding output gives a state no devolution reason to grow, and the
richer states have argued for two decades that they are taxed to subsidise stagnation. A 10 per cent
term is a modest correction inside a formula still 60 per cent equity-weighted (17.5 population + 42.5
income distance).

**Strongest case against.** Contribution to GDP is close to a pure size-and-wealth term and it runs
against the constitutional purpose of equalisation. Unlike tax effort or fiscal discipline — the
efficiency criteria previous Commissions used — it does not measure anything a state *does*; it measures
what a state *is*. A large rich state scores highly by existing. Combined with the cut to income distance
(45→42.5) and the cut to area (15→10), the direction of the FC-XVI formula is away from the poorest and
largest-area states.

**Whether the effect can be measured: no.** This is exactly absence A-3. FC-XVI publishes weights and
outcomes but no state × criterion decomposition, so the rupee effect of the new criterion on any state
is not computable from the report. The observable outcome is in S-12: Gujarat 3.478→3.755, Haryana
1.093→1.361, Maharashtra 6.317→6.441, Karnataka 3.647→4.131, Kerala 1.925→2.382 all rise; Bihar
10.058→9.948, Uttar Pradesh 17.939→17.619, Madhya Pradesh 7.850→7.347, West Bengal 7.523→7.215, Odisha
4.528→4.420 all fall. **That pattern is consistent with the new criterion but does not prove it** —
para 8.62 warns that data updating alone shifts shares significantly.

**Different facts or different weightings?** **Different weightings**, and unusually candidly so: this
is an explicit choice between equity and output, made in the open, with the numbers published.

### LR-6 — FC-XVI recommends annual publication of the CAG net-proceeds certificate; the Union accepts

**What happened.** Para 7.30: *"In the spirit of transparency and to put the concerns of States to rest,
we recommend that, every year, the Union Government disclose the data pertaining to net proceeds as
certified by CAG."* The Union's Explanatory Memorandum, para 4, records it and para 4's block closes
with *"The Government has accepted the above recommendations of the Commission."*

**Why it is a ledger record and not a footnote.** The recommendation is an admission that the number
which *definitively settles* the recommended-versus-actual question — the CAG-certified net proceeds
under Article 279 — has not been published annually. The Union's acceptance is a commitment that it will
be, from FY2026-27. **This converts absence A-1 into a dated, checkable promise**: from the first year of
the FC-XVI award, if the certified net proceeds are not published, that is a departure from an accepted
recommendation with a named instrument and a date. A later stage of this instrument should test it.

**Strongest case for the Union's prior practice.** The Article 279 certificate is an internal audit
instrument; the adjustments it drives are made and the states are paid; FC-XVI's own Table 7.4 shows the
system converges exactly. Publication adds transparency, not accuracy.

**Strongest case against.** FC-XVI itself frames the concern as needing *"to put the concerns of States
to rest"* — i.e. the states did not believe the convergence, and could not check it, for the whole of
FC-XIV's and FC-XV's award periods. And on the Commission's own para 7.29, before FY2018-19 the CAG
adjustment was made **once in ten years**, during which the cumulative net error was ₹15,756 crore.
An unpublished number reconciled once a decade is not a check.

### LR-7 — The Union declines the Fifteenth Finance Commission's sector-specific and state-specific grants (1 February 2021)

**What happened.** FC-XV recommended, under Article 275(1), **₹1,29,987 crore** of grants to states for
eight specific sectors and **₹49,599 crore** of state-specific grants over FY2021-22→FY2025-26. In the
Explanatory Memorandum laid before Parliament under Article 281 on 1 February 2021, the Union used the
formula **"accepted"** for every other quantified grant block and a different formula for these two.
Evidence and verbatim quotations at F-13.

**Objective stated at announcement.** For the sector grants, the stated alternative route: *"Government
will give due consideration to sectors identified by the Commission while formulating and implementing
existing and new **Centrally Sponsored and Central Sector Schemes**."* For the state-specific grants, the
stated reason: *"**Keeping in view the untied resources with the State Governments and the fiscal
commitments of the Central Government**, due consideration will be given."*

**Strongest case for the Union, in its own terms.** Article 281 requires the recommendations to be laid
with an explanatory memorandum on the action taken; it does not bind the Union to accept them. FC
recommendations under Article 275(1) other than the tax-sharing order are, constitutionally, advice. The
Union's stated reason is coherent: the states had just received a 41 per cent devolution *and* ₹2,94,514
crore of revenue deficit grants *and* ₹4,36,361 crore of local body grants, all accepted; sector grants
of ₹1,29,987 crore for health, education and agriculture duplicate the purposes of existing centrally
sponsored schemes in the same sectors, and running two parallel channels to the same purpose is waste.
Folding them into CSS puts the money through a machinery with monitoring already attached.

**Strongest case for the states, in their own terms.** The two forms are not substitutes and the
difference is precisely the thing in dispute across this whole phase. An Article 275(1) FC grant is
**charged** on the Consolidated Fund of India (not voted), is fixed by a body outside the Union
executive, has an amount and a state-wise distribution determined by that body, and cannot be withheld
for non-compliance with conditions the Union invents later. An Article 282 scheme grant is **voted**,
its amount is set annually by the Union, it carries whatever conditions the Union attaches, it requires
a state matching share, and — as part 04 establishes — allocation and release are different quantities.
Converting ₹1,29,987 crore from the first form to the second transfers control of that money from the
Commission to the Ministry. And FC-XIV said in 2014, at para 12.22, that it had found the states'
complaint about exactly this made out. The state-specific grants have no CSS analogue at all: they exist
because no formula reaches a particular state's particular problem, so "due consideration" is the whole
of what the states received.

**Different facts or different weightings?** **Different facts about what the two instruments are.** The
Union's answer treats an FC grant and a CSS as interchangeable delivery mechanisms for the same
purposes. The states' answer treats them as constitutionally distinct — charged vs voted, unconditional
vs conditional, Commission-determined vs executive-determined. **They are constitutionally distinct**,
so this is not a matter of weighting: one side's premise is wrong as a matter of constitutional form,
whatever the practical result. That does not make the Union's decision improper — it was entitled to
make it — but it does mean the two positions are not commensurable.

**Unresolved.** Whether any of the ₹1,79,586 crore was in fact released, in any form. See A-6.

### LR-8 — FC-XVI roughly doubles local body grants and adds an urbanisation premium (FY2026-27→FY2030-31)

**What happened.** Against FC-XV's ₹4,36,361 crore for FY2021-22→FY2025-26, FC-XVI recommends
**₹7,91,493 crore** for local bodies for FY2026-27→FY2030-31, split 60:40 rural:urban on the
basic-plus-performance components, 80:20 basic:performance within each, with a new **₹10,000 crore
urbanisation premium** (₹2,000 per person, on Census 2011 population, for peri-urban village mergers into
ULBs of 1 lakh+) and a new **₹56,100 crore Special Infrastructure Component** for urban wastewater. The
Union accepted the block.

**Objective stated at announcement.** Entry conditions are stated in the Explanatory Memorandum: a duly
constituted body under Parts IX and IX-A; online provisional accounts for T-1 and audited accounts for
T-2; and compliance with the constitutional requirement to constitute a State Finance Commission every
five years and lay the Action Taken Report in the state legislature within six months. Plus, at para 18
of the Memorandum, a **10-working-day deadline** for states to pass grants to local bodies, with interest
at the state's own market-borrowing rate for delay.

**Strongest case for.** The third tier is chronically starved and the conditions attach to things the
Constitution already requires (Parts IX/IX-A bodies; SFCs). The interest penalty on states that sit on
local body money is a genuine, enforceable protection for panchayats and municipalities. And FC-XVI
explicitly protects local bodies from Union foot-dragging too: *"the Union Government should ensure that
where a set of local bodies within the State meet the conditions, the grants due to them are released
without waiting for the rest."*

**Strongest case against.** This is a Finance Commission attaching conditions to an Article 275(1) grant
— the very form whose unconditionality is the states' argument in LR-7. The conditions bite on state
governments (constitute an SFC, lay an ATR, publish accounts, fix a property-tax floor under FC-XV),
and non-compliance withholds money from local bodies, not from the state that failed. **The party
penalised is not the party in default.** FC-XVI also directs states on what local bodies may spend on
(no more than 20 per cent of untied grants on roads; nothing on salaries), which is a Commission
legislating for the third tier of another government's structure.

**Different facts or different weightings?** **Different weightings of the same facts.** Both sides
accept the conditions and the amounts. They differ on whether conditionality attached by a
constitutional commission is different in kind from conditionality attached by the Union executive.

### LR-9 — The Union remands two of FC-XV's FY2020-21 grant heads back to the Commission (30 January 2020)

**What happened.** In the Explanatory Memorandum on FC-XV's first report, laid under Article 281 and
signed by the Finance Minister on 30 January 2020, the Union neither accepted nor rejected two grant
heads. **Special Grants of ₹6,764 crore** — designed so that no state received less in absolute terms in
FY2020-21 than in FY2019-20 from devolution plus revenue deficit grants — drew: *"**The Commission may be
requested to reconsider the recommendation as it introduces a new principle.**"* **Nutrition Grants of
₹7,735 crore** drew: *"The Commission may review this recommendation as a part of its overall proposal
of measurable performance-based incentives for States as per the ToR, in the main report."* Both heads
then disappear: FC-XV Vol II Annex 4.3 shows the FY2020-21 reassessed grant total of ₹1,96,449 crore
built from three heads only, with no special-grant and no nutrition-grant line.

**Objective stated at announcement.** For the special grant, the Union's stated reason is in the
sentence: *"it introduces a new principle."*

**Strongest case for the Union.** The Special Grant *was* a new principle — a floor guaranteeing no
nominal reduction against the previous year, which is a hold-harmless device no Commission had used and
which cuts across the formula the same Commission had just constructed. Asking a Commission still
sitting (its final report was more than nine months away) to reconsider a novel principle before it was
locked into a five-year award is a reasonable use of a memorandum on action taken, and less final than
outright rejection. The nutrition grant request is milder still — a request to fold it into the
performance-incentive framework the ToR had asked for.

**Strongest case against.** Article 280 makes the Commission the recommending body and Article 281
requires the Union to lay the recommendations with a statement of the action taken on them. **"Action
taken" contemplates acceptance, rejection, or partial acceptance — not instruction to the recommending
body.** The Union is a party to the question the Commission is deciding; a party asking the adjudicator
to reconsider, in the document that is supposed to record the party's compliance, inverts the
relationship. And it worked: both heads vanished from the final award, so the states lost ₹14,499 crore
between the report and the settled position without any body publishing a reason for the change.

**Different facts or different weightings?** **Different weightings**, on a point of constitutional
propriety rather than fact. Nothing about the sequence is disputed. What is contested is whether the
Article 281 memorandum is a compliance report or a continuing dialogue.

### PR-1 — Three instruments give three different devolution figures for FY2018-19, spanning ₹51,910 crore

**The quantity.** Tax devolution to states, FY2018-19, ₹ crore.

| Value | Instrument | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **7,09,544** | FC-XVI Vol I Table 7.4 row 7, from the **CAG certificate** | Entitlement **attributable to** FY2018-19: 42 per cent of the CAG-certified net proceeds, after both adjustment rounds |
| **7,46,894** | FC-XVI Vol II Annexure 7.1, sourced to **RBI Bulletin / DBIE** | Devolution as recorded in the RBI's compilation |
| **7,61,454.15** | Union **Receipt Budget 2020-21**, Abstract of Receipts, "Actuals 2018-2019" | Cash accounted in the Union's FY2018-19 accounts, including settlements of earlier years |

**Spread: ₹51,910 crore, or 7.3 per cent of the smallest figure.** Two of the three appear in the
*same report*, 100-odd pages apart, without reconciliation.

**Why they differ.** Not error. Three different events are being counted: (i) *entitlement attributed
to a year*, (ii) *the RBI's compilation of releases*, (iii) *cash booked in the Union's accounts in a
year*. Because devolution is released against a Revised Estimate and trued up in T+2 and T+3, cash
booked in FY T contains money belonging to FY T−1 and FY T−2. FC-XVI para 7.28 sets out the mechanism.

**Is it always this large?** No — I checked all five overlapping years. Annexure 7.1 minus Receipt
Budget Actuals: FY2018-19 −14,560 · FY2019-20 +10 · FY2020-21 +230 · **FY2021-22 −15,292** ·
FY2022-23 +576 · FY2023-24 +0.29. **The RBI and Budget series agree closely in four years of six and
diverge materially in exactly two — FY2018-19 and FY2021-22.** Those are the two years with the largest
adjustment entries in Table 7.4 (−58,843 and +33,470 first adjustments). The divergence is
adjustment-driven and nobody documents it.

**Definitional or evidentiary?** **Definitional between column 1 and the other two** — entitlement and
cash are different objects. **Evidentiary between columns 2 and 3** — the RBI and the Union Budget are
each purporting to record the same releases and differ by ₹14,560 crore in one year with no published
reconciliation.

### PR-2 — "Accepted" vs "takes note" vs "due consideration": the Union's action verbs are a graded scale, and nobody defines them

**The quantity.** The Union's action on a Finance Commission recommendation.

**The competing definitions.** The Explanatory Memoranda under Article 281 use **five distinct
formulae** — *has accepted* · *accepts in-principle* · *will give due consideration* · *takes note of* ·
*will examine … in due course* — and **no instrument defines any of them.** Article 281 requires only
that the recommendations be laid *"together with an explanatory memorandum as to the action taken
thereon"*; it prescribes no vocabulary and no consequence.

**Why it is a provenance record and not a finding.** Everything downstream — whether a recommendation
was "implemented", whether a gap exists between recommendation and action — depends on a five-valued
categorical variable whose values are the Union's own undefined words. Any count of "recommendations
accepted" is a count against an undefined scale. The two FC-XV grant categories at F-13 received "due
consideration"; FC-XVI's recommendation of *no* grants received "takes note"; both would be scored
"not accepted" by a naive reading and neither is a refusal on its face.

**Definitional or evidentiary?** **Definitional, and never defined.** This is a first-class
never-defined finding: a constitutional reporting instrument whose central variable has no definition.

### PR-3 — 37.6 or 37.7: the FC-XV transfer average differs between FC-XVI's table and its prose

**The quantity.** Total FC transfers (devolution + FC grants) as a percentage of GTR, FC-XV award-period
average.

**The disagreement.** FC-XVI Vol I **Table 7.5, Average (FC-15) row: 37.6.** FC-XVI Vol I **para 7.34
prose: *"FC devolution plus grants averaged 32.5 per cent of GTR during the award period of the FC-13,
39.4 per cent during the FC-14, and 37.7 per cent of GTR during the FC-15."*** The FC-XIII and FC-XIV
figures match the table; the FC-XV figure does not.

**Almost certainly a rounding-order artefact** — my own recomputation from Annexure 7.1 gives 37.63 as
the mean of the annual ratios and 37.65 as the ratio of the sums. It is not material. It is recorded
because it is uncorrected in a published constitutional report and because para 7.34 immediately draws
a conclusion from it (*"narrowing of the shortfall in the total FC transfers between the last two FCs to
1.7 per cent of GTR"* — which is 39.4 − 37.7, not 39.4 − 37.6 = 1.8). **Report 37.6 as the operative
figure and record the variant.**

### PR-4 — Devolution's denominator: divisible pool, gross tax revenue, gross revenue receipts, or net proceeds

**The quantity.** "The states' share of central taxes."

**Four denominators are in live use by parties to the dispute, all in FY2020s documents:**

| Denominator | Who uses it | FY2023-24 value of the ratio | Why they use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Divisible pool** (= net proceeds: GTR less cesses, surcharges, UT revenue, collection cost) | The Constitution, Article 270; every FC's operative recommendation | **41 per cent, exactly** | It is the constitutional object |
| **Gross tax revenue** | The states; FC-XVI Table 7.5 | **32.6 per cent** | It is what the Union actually collects in tax |
| **Gross revenue receipts** (tax + non-tax) | The **Union's** memorandum to FC-XVI; FC-XIV's own projection | **"more than 49 per cent"** transferred (FC-XVI para 7.42) | It counts grants and CSS too, and dilutes with non-tax revenue |
| **Net proceeds as certified by CAG** | Article 279; Receipt Budget Annex-4C from FY2026-27 | ₹27,69,957.40 crore for FY2023-24; states' 41% = ₹11,35,682.53 crore | It is the only audited figure |

**Every one of these is correct.** The dispute is conducted entirely across denominators, and the
sentence "the states get 41 per cent" and the sentence "the states get 32.6 per cent" are both true of
FY2023-24. **This is the single most important thing this part has to hand forward.** Any series in this
instrument must carry its denominator in the unit string or it is not a series.

**Definitional or evidentiary?** **Purely definitional.** No party disputes any underlying figure.

### PR-5 — Three "right answers" for FY2023-24 releases, all inside one new annexure

**The quantity.** Devolution for FY2023-24.

From Receipt Budget 2026-27 **Annex-4C**, which I retrieved and read (URL in §Sources):
- **₹11,35,682.53 crore** — states' share at 41 per cent of the CAG-certified net proceeds (row B).
- **₹11,36,306.00 crore** — "States' Share released for FY 2023-2024", itself the sum of ₹10,97,342.40
  released in RE 2023-24, ₹25,000.00 released after RE 2023-24, and ₹13,963.60 released in RE 2024-25.
- **₹11,29,493.71 crore** — accounted in FY2023-24's own accounts (Receipt Budget 2025-26, Abstract of
  Receipts, Actuals 2023-24).

Annex-4C then records the reconciling entry: **"States' share Final settlement based on C&AG certified
net proceeds … −623.47"** — a recovery from the states.

**Definitional.** Entitlement, releases-attributed-to-the-year, and cash-booked-in-the-year are three
objects. What is new is that **one instrument now prints all three side by side with the reconciling
entry** — which is exactly what FC-XVI asked for (LR-6).

### PR-6 — IGST and the GST compensation cess appear as nil in the CAG-certified net proceeds

**The quantity.** Composition of the CAG-certified net proceeds, FY2023-24.

Annex-4C's itemised breakdown of the ₹27,69,957.40 crore lists **"Integrated Goods and Services Tax —
(nil)"** and **"GST Compensation Cess on Goods and Services Tax — (nil)"** alongside CGST ₹8,14,746.30
crore, corporation tax ₹8,15,846.17 crore, income tax ₹9,10,605.29 crore, customs ₹1,56,578.50 crore,
union excise ₹33,820.71 crore.

**Why this matters and why it is not a scandal.** IGST is apportioned between the Union and the states
under Article 269A and the apportioned Union half then enters the pool via CGST-equivalent heads; the
compensation cess is a cess and is constitutionally outside Article 270 by the terms of the article
itself. So nil is correct on both. **But it means the headline "net proceeds" figure is not
decomposable into the taxes a reader would expect**, and a reader comparing Annex-4C's ₹27,69,957.40
crore against FY2023-24 GTR of ₹34,65,518.58 crore and inferring a ₹6,95,561 crore cess-and-surcharge
gap would be conflating cesses, surcharges, IGST apportionment, UT revenue and collection cost. → part 03
must not make that inference from this annexure alone.

### PR-7 — Two population bases inside one comparison table

**The quantity.** A state's devolution-to-population ratio across Commissions (S-13).

FC-XVI Table 8.5's own note: *"Census 2001 population shares have been used to calculate the ratios
under the Eleventh and Twelfth FCs, and Census 2011 population shares for those under the Thirteenth to
Fifteenth FCs. For the Fifteenth FC, the population shares have been recalculated … by excluding Jammu
& Kashmir."* **Three bases in five columns**, disclosed. The table is therefore internally consistent
only within FC-XIII→FC-XV. The FC-XI/FC-XII columns are on a different denominator and a step between
column 2 and column 3 is partly a census change.

**Definitional, and disclosed** — which is the good case. Recorded because the table is the evidence
base for the F-8 "admission against interest" reading, and that reading rests on the FC-XIII→FC-XV
columns, which share a base. **The reading survives the caveat.**

## Absences

### A-1 — The CAG's Article 279 net-proceeds certificate was not published annually until FY2026-27

**What is not measured / not published.** The Comptroller and Auditor General certifies, under Article
279, the net proceeds of shareable Union taxes. That certificate is the only audited determination of
the denominator to which the 41 per cent applies. Until the Receipt Budget 2026-27 it was not published
as an annual disclosure.

**Kind: not published, on the record, with a stated reason of none.** No Union document states a reason
for not publishing it. The status is established two ways, both T1: (i) FC-XVI para 7.30 recommends
that the Union *"disclose the data pertaining to net proceeds as certified by CAG"* **every year** — a
recommendation to start doing something is evidence it was not being done; (ii) an eleven-edition sweep
of the Receipt Budget (2015-16 through 2026-27, all retrieved) for the string **"Net Proceeds as
certified by"** returns hits **only in the 2026-27 edition**. **Positive control:** the same sweep for
*"Gross Tax Revenue"* hits every edition, so the corpus and the search are working; the absence is in
the documents, not the method.

**Is it "withheld"? No.** No named requester, no specific request, no date is on this record. It is
**not-published**, and the distinction is load-bearing.

**Contradicted?** No. It is corroborated by FC-XVI's own recommendation and by the Union's acceptance.

**What would fill it.** It has been filled, prospectively: **Receipt Budget 2026-27 Annex-4C**, which I
retrieved and read, is that disclosure in its first year, for FY2023-24. **A later stage should test
whether Annex-4C recurs in the 2027-28 Receipt Budget** — that is the checkable form of an accepted
recommendation. Backfilling FY2010-11→FY2022-23 requires the CAG certificates themselves.

### A-2 — FC-XVI's own FY2010-11→FY2017-18 reconciliation is "not reported here"

**What is not published.** FC-XVI para 7.29 states that it calculated the recommended-versus-actual
position for FY2010-11 to FY2017-18 and gives one number from it — a net ₹15,756 crore overpayment to
the states. The working is expressly withheld from the report: *"**Our calculations for years 2010-11 to
2017-18, not reported here**, show that…"*

**Kind: not published, with the absence acknowledged in the same sentence.** This is the strongest form
of a not-published finding: the body says it has the number and says it is not printing it.

**Contradicted?** No, and nothing contradicts the ₹15,756 crore either — nothing corroborates it.

**What would fill it.** The Commission's working papers, or eight years of CAG certificates. The
Commission also reports, without evidencing it, that the Ministry of Finance settled the excess *"by
recording the excess amount as payment due on certain services that the Union Government had received
from the States"* — **an unaudited, undocumented set-off of a constitutional over-transfer against an
unspecified services bill.** That set-off has no instrument at all.

### A-3 — No Commission publishes the state × criterion decomposition of a devolution share

**What is not measured.** For any state and any award, the contribution of each criterion (population,
area, forest, income distance, demographic performance, contribution to GDP) to that state's inter-se
share, in percentage points or rupees.

**Kind: never produced.** No FC report contains such a table. FC-XVI publishes the weights (Table 8.8),
the resulting shares (Table 8.9), and a Technical Note giving the formulas — but not the decomposition.

**Positive control on the sweep.** I did not conclude absence from a keyword miss. The confirming
evidence is **inside the Commission's own reasoning**: para 8.70, attempting to explain why Bihar's and
Uttar Pradesh's shares fell, writes *"**In all likelihood**, the introduction of forest as a criterion
under the FC-14 shifted the devolution shares from these (and other) States to Chhattisgarh and Madhya
Pradesh."* A Commission that had a decomposition would not write "in all likelihood" about a formula it
has fully specified. Separately, para 8.62 concedes that formula effects and data-update effects cannot
be separated in the published shares. **The Commission is reasoning about its own arithmetic by
conjecture, in print. That is the absence, demonstrated by the body that would have to fill it.**

**Contradicted?** No.

**What would fill it.** The Commission's own spreadsheets, or a reconstruction from the Technical Note
plus the underlying criterion values — the latter is possible in principle for FC-XVI, since Vol II
annexures carry area, forest and population data, but the per-capita-GSDP-distance and
contribution-to-GDP base data would need to be located. **This is the highest-value reconstructible
absence in the part.** Part 01 flags it; it survives here.

### A-4 — The divisible pool is not published as a series before FY2018-19

**What is not published.** An annual, audited divisible pool (net proceeds) for FY2010-11→FY2017-18.

**Kind: not published.** Two sweeps establish it, both with positive controls: (i) the eleven-edition
Receipt Budget sweep (A-1 above); (ii) a sweep of thirteen retrieved CAG Union Government Accounts /
Finance Accounts audit reports for *"divisible pool"* and *"net proceeds"*. **Positive control on the

The published series is therefore **six years long**: FY2018-19→FY2022-23 from FC-XVI Table 7.4, plus
FY2023-24 from Annex-4C. **Every statement about "the divisible pool as a share of GTR" over a longer
run rests on FC-XVI's award-period averages (S-10), which are prose figures whose underlying annexure I
did not locate.**

**What would fill it.** The CAG certificates for FY2010-11→FY2017-18, or the FC-XVI working behind its
89.2 / 82.1 / 78.3 averages.

### A-5 — The 1 per cent "retained for Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh" has no ledger

Explanatory Memorandum says is *"retained with the Central Government for financing the requirements of
the newly formed Union Territories of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh"* was in fact spent on those UTs.

**Kind: never defined, and definitionally unmeasurable.** Money retained in the Consolidated Fund of

cannot be followed. **Neither Finance Commission makes this claim** — both describe a denominator
adjustment. The purpose claim is the Union's alone.

**Contradicted?** Not contradicted, but **unsupported by either Commission**, which is close.

**What would fill it.** Nothing, on the current design. The nearest checkable quantity is total Union
expenditure on the two UTs, in the Demands for Grants — a different question that would not settle this
one.

## Definitional disagreements

### D-1 — "The states' share of central taxes" has four denominators, all in current use, all correct

Set out in full at PR-4. In one line: for FY2023-24 the states received **41.00 per cent of the
CAG-certified divisible pool**, **32.59 per cent of gross tax revenue**, and — on the Union's framing to
FC-XVI — total transfers exceeding **49 per cent of gross revenue receipts**. **All three are true.**
The public argument is conducted by choosing a denominator and it is not resolvable by better
measurement.

**Do the two sides rest on different facts or different weightings?** **Neither. They rest on different
definitions.** That is a third category and it is the most important finding in this part. No figure is
in dispute. FC-XVI adjudicated the one genuinely factual sub-question — whether cesses "almost wiped
out" the FC-XIV increase — and answered no (para 7.35).

### D-2 — Entitlement, release, and cash-in-the-accounts are three different quantities

PR-1 and PR-5. For FY2018-19 the three are ₹7,09,544 / ₹7,46,894 / ₹7,61,454 crore. For FY2023-24 they
are ₹11,35,682.53 / ₹11,36,306.00 / ₹11,29,493.71 crore. **Because devolution is released against a
Revised Estimate and trued up in T+2 and T+3, cash booked in FY T contains money belonging to FY T−1 and
FY T−2.** A state reading its own receipts for a single year will not see 41 per cent of anything, and
will not be wrong.

**Consequence for this instrument.** Any devolution series must declare which of the three it is. S-5
(FC/RBI basis), S-17 (Budget accounting basis) and S-8/S-9 (entitlement basis) are **three series, not
three sources for one series.**

### D-3 — "Recommended versus actual" is two different questions and they have opposite answers

- **Against the divisible pool:** there is **no shortfall**. FC-XVI Table 7.4 shows final devolution
  equal to 41/42 per cent of the CAG-certified pool in all five published years, exactly. Before
  FY2018-19 the reconciliation was decadal and the net error ran **in the states' favour** by ₹15,756
  crore (para 7.29).
- **Against gross tax revenue:** the shortfall is **7–9 percentage points** and is the whole of the
  states' grievance.

**These do not contradict each other.** They are the same fact under two denominators, and the bridge
between them is S-19 (the pool is 74–81 per cent of GTR). **Any statement of the form "the states did
not get their 41 per cent" is false on the constitutional definition and true on the political one, and
this instrument must say which it means.**

### D-4 — "Population" means at least four different things across five Commissions

1971 population (FC-XI to FC-XIV) · 2011 population, labelled *"Demographic Change"* by FC-XIV and
*"Population (2011)"* by FC-XVI's compilation · *"Total Fertility Rate"* (FC-XV) · *"Demographic
Performance"* (FC-XV's own label and FC-XVI's) · and, inside the ratio table S-13, Census 2001 shares for
FC-XI/FC-XII against Census 2011 shares for FC-XIII→FC-XV, with a further recalculation excluding J&K
for FC-XV (PR-7). **Same construct, five names, three bases, disclosed each time and never harmonised.**

### D-5 — "Grants to states" includes or excludes money that never reaches a state budget

FC-XVI Annexure 7.1's *non-FC grants* column steps from ₹1,52,047 crore (FY2013-14) to ₹2,67,600 crore
(FY2014-15) — a 76 per cent jump produced by routing, not spending. FC-XIV's own footnote names the
change; the Union Budget's Statement 18 for FY2014-15 and FY2015-16 is literally the statement of the
old regime, and its disappearance is the change. **A "total transfers to states" series crossing
FY2013-14/FY2014-15 is spliced across a base change.** A second, weaker candidate shift sits at
FY2021-22 (Single Nodal Agency; the word "TOTAL" enters the Statement 18 title) — **unconfirmed: I did
not retrieve a document naming SNA as the cause.** → part 04.

### D-6 — The Union's five action verbs under Article 281 have no definitions — but the Union has now construed one of them

PR-2, A-8. *Accepted · accepted in-principle · will give due consideration · takes note of · will
examine in due course.* Five values, no definitions, and the whole recommendation-versus-action question
turns on them.

**One value is now construed, by the Union, against itself.** Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 4056 of
18 August 2025: *"**No State-specific grants were specifically accepted** in the Explanatory Memorandum…
**Accordingly, no state-specific grants … are being released.**"* The Explanatory Memorandum had said
*"due consideration will be given"*. **The Government therefore reads its own "due consideration will be
given" as "not accepted", with the consequence that nothing is paid.** That is an authoritative
construction of one of the five verbs, and it should be applied to the sector-specific head too — the
Memorandum used the same register there.

A sixth register also exists and is worse than any of the five: **"The Commission may be requested to
reconsider the recommendation as it introduces a new principle"** (F-22, the FY2020-21 Memorandum, on
the ₹6,764 crore Special Grants). That is not an action on a recommendation at all; it is a remand.

### D-7 — "Total 15th FC grants-in-aid" means ₹10,33,062 crore to the Commission and ₹8,45,026 crore to the Ministry

F-19. The difference is **₹1,88,036 crore** and it decomposes exactly into the two heads the Union
declined plus the two conditional urban windows it footnotes as separately available. **Neither figure
is wrong.** The Commission's figure is what it recommended; the Ministry's is what it accepted. But the
Ministry's answer to Parliament uses the phrase "Total Grants-in-Aid" without stating the restriction,
and then reasons from it that *"there is no scope for more central assistance"*. **This is the
denominator problem of PR-4 reappearing inside the accountability mechanism itself.**

### D-8 — "Recommended versus released" for grants is three quantities, not two

**Recommended** (₹10,33,062 crore, FC-XV Table 10.12) · **accepted** (₹8,53,476 crore = recommended less
the ₹1,79,586 crore of sector- and state-specific grants) · **released** (₹8,02,527.50 crore on
Statement 18 Head II to FY2025-26 RE). **A "77.7 per cent of recommended" figure and a "94 per cent of
accepted" figure describe the same facts and carry opposite rhetorical force.** Any statement of a
grant-release ratio must name which denominator it uses. This part reports both and privileges neither.

## Disciplining measure

**The question.** Is there an instrument that puts the Centre's facts and the states' facts on **one
ledger**? And if so, does it supply a common **basis** or independent **evidence**?

### Per quantity

| Quantity | Single-ledger instrument? | Basis or evidence? |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertical share (S-1)** | **Yes, and it is definitional.** The Commission report *is* the ledger: the states' memoranda, the Union's memorandum and the Commission's determination sit between two covers (FC-XVI ch.7: states at 7.36–7.41, Union at 7.42–7.45, determination at 7.46–7.68). | **Basis, not evidence.** The Commission does not verify either memorandum. It reports what each side said and then decides. Its *decision* is authoritative because the Constitution makes it so, not because it is better evidenced. |
| **Horizontal weights (S-11)** | **Yes, definitionally** — the weights are constituted by the Commission's own act. There is nothing to verify. | Neither. This is not a measurement. |
| **Inter-se shares (S-12)** | **Yes**, but see A-3: the *output* is on one ledger, the *derivation* is on none. | **Basis only, and incomplete.** The Commission publishes the answer and not the working. |
| **Devolution actually paid (S-5, S-17)** | **Partly.** FC-XVI Table 7.4 is the closest thing: it puts the Receipt Budget's RE-based figure, the Receipt Budget's actual-based figure, the CAG certificate and the FC's own entitlement calculation in one table, and reconciles them to the rupee. | **Both, and this is the strongest case in the part.** The CAG certificate is **evidentiarily independent** of both the Union and the states — it is an audit of the Union's accounts by a constitutional officer the Union does not control. ### The answer to the phase's question, stated plainly

**The Finance Commission report is a genuine disciplining measure, but the discipline is mostly
definitional, not evidentiary — with one real exception.**

**Why mostly definitional.** The report forces both parties to argue in the same units, before the same
body, in one document, in the same award period. That is not nothing: it is why FC-XVI could adjudicate
the cess claim at para 7.35 and find the strong form unsupported and the weak form made out. **But the
report's underlying data are not independent.** The Commission's own source note to Annexure 7.1 says
so: GTR from the Union's Indian Public Finance Statistics and the Union's Receipt Budget; devolution
from the RBI and the Union Budget; state finances from the states' own budget documents and their
Accountants General. **I verified by recomputation that Table 7.5 — the table on which the whole
recommended-versus-actual argument in chapter 7 rests — is a derivation of Annexure 7.1, not an
independent measurement.** The Commission is an adjudicator with no investigative apparatus of its own.
It re-presents the parties' numbers under a common definition and rules between them.

**The exception, and it is real.** The **CAG's Article 279 certificate** is evidentiarily independent.
The CAG is appointed under Article 148, removable only as a Supreme Court judge is, and audits the
Union's accounts. Its certificate of net proceeds is not the Union's number and it is not the states'
number. **It is the only quantity in this part with genuine evidentiary independence** — and until the
Receipt Budget 2026-27 it was published only as a residue inside two derived tables (A-1). FC-XVI saw
this precisely, which is why para 7.30 recommends annual disclosure. **The single most consequential
thing in this part is that the one independent instrument was, for the whole of the FC-XIV and FC-XV
award periods, not published.**

**The nuance the phase asked for, stated as a rule.** *An instrument that reproduces both parties'
submissions supplies a common basis. Only an instrument produced by a body with no stake supplies
evidence. The Finance Commission report is the first; the CAG certificate inside it is the second; and
the report's value as a disciplining measure is almost entirely borrowed from that one embedded
certificate.*

### The trap, applied to this part's own sources

- **A state's memorandum to a Finance Commission is an advocacy document, not a measurement.** Every
  state figure in this part — the 18-of-28 demand for 50 per cent (S-14), Tamil Nadu's ₹20,000 crore
  post-compensation shortfall, Madhya Pradesh's 48/40.7 pair — is a pleading. **I hold none of them as
  documents**; I hold FC-XVI's summary of them, which is one further remove. Grade the state positions
  **T4 as measurement, T1 as a record of what was submitted**, and never merge the two.
- **The Union's figures are not independent of the Union.** The Receipt Budget, the Explanatory
  Memorandum, Statement 18 and Indian Public Finance Statistics are all Ministry of Finance products.
  The Union's *"more than 49 per cent of Gross Revenue Receipts"* (FC-XVI para 7.42) and its
  *"6.7 per cent of gross revenue"* cess figure (para 7.45) are the Union's own arithmetic on the
  Union's own data, with denominators of the Union's choosing, relayed by the Commission. **T1 as a
  record of the Union's position. Not a check on the Union.**
- **FC-XVI's Annexure 7.1 looks like an independent time series and is not.** It is a stitch of the
  Union's IPFS, the Union's Receipt Budget, the RBI's Bulletin and an FC-8 appendix, spliced across
  eras, with different sources for different columns. The Commission ran a positive control on the GTR
  column (*"For the overlapping years, the three documents match perfectly"*) and **ran none on the
  devolution column** — which is exactly the column where PR-1 finds a ₹14,560 crore divergence from the
  Union Budget in FY2018-19 and ₹15,292 crore in FY2021-22.
- **The one thing in this part that is not produced by a party to the dispute is the CAG certificate,
  and I do not hold it.** I hold two reproductions of it. Under this instrument's own rule, **a
  reproduction is not retrieval** — so the honest statement is: *the independent evidence exists, both
  parties agree on what it says, and I have not seen it.*
