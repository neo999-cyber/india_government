## HEADLINE FINDINGS

1. **The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 does not cover a single state teacher-recruitment or teacher-eligibility exam, and does not cover CTET either.** Its Schedule names six central bodies. Coverage follows the *conducting* authority, not the employer — so NTA-run KVS/NVS teacher recruitment is inside, CBSE-run CTET is outside, and every state TET and state teacher recruitment is outside. **(T1, direct)**
2. **The number "25,753" — the canonical figure for the West Bengal annulment — appears nowhere in either judgment.** Neither the Calcutta HC judgment of 22.04.2024 (282 pp.) nor the Supreme Court judgment of 03.04.2025 (41 pp.) contains it. The judgments' own table of appointment letters sums to **25,735**. Both judgments also contain an internal arithmetic error in the Class IX–X row. **(T1, direct — full-text search of both PDFs)**
3. **Three of five state anti-paper-leak Acts were drafted around the *hiring* exam and left the *licensing* exam outside** — Haryana, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand cannot reach HTET/UTET/JTET. Only **UP (2024)**, by writing "either qualifying or competitive" into the definition, and **Gujarat (2023)**, by naming the State Examination Board in its Schedule, reach eligibility tests. **(T1)**
4. **CTET's fixed 60% absolute cut produces a 3.4× swing in pass rates across seven consecutive editions** (10.5% to 35.4% combined). Since the cut is unmoderated, this is paper-difficulty variance, not candidate-quality variance. Paper II (upper primary) bottomed at **7.56%** in January 2024. **(T1 result notices; arithmetic mine)**
5. **CTET was not held at all in calendar year 2025** — 20th edition December 2024, 21st edition February 2026. **(T1, direct)**
6. **Litigation, not budget, is the stated cause of frozen UP recruitment.** UP's basic education minister told the Vidhan Sabha on 28 Feb 2023 that of 69,000 assistant-teacher posts only **6,696 had been filled in 20 months**, and that "the recruitment drives are affected as cases against it are pending in courts… at present there is no proposal under consideration regarding… conduct of teacher recruitment examination." **(T3)**

## A. THE ELIGIBILITY-TEST ARCHITECTURE

### A1. Statutory basis

**RTE Act 2009, s.23(1)** — *"Any person possessing such minimum qualifications, as laid down by an academic authority, authorised by the Central Government, by notification, shall be eligible for appointment as a teacher."*
**T1 (direct)** — https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19014/1/the_right_of_children_to_free_and_compulsory_education_act_2009.pdf

**Structural point: s.23(1) does not mention a TET.** The TET exists only because NCTE — the notified academic authority — chose to make it a minimum qualification. It is delegated legislation, not statute. That is why it can be, and has been, changed by letter.

s.23(2) second proviso (inserted by RTE (Amendment) Act 24 of 2017) gave every teacher in position as on **31 March 2015** four years to acquire minimum qualifications. **T1 (direct)**

### A2. NCTE Notification of 23 August 2010

Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part III–Sec. 4, No. 215, 25 August 2010; notification dated 23.08.2010, F.No. 61-03/2010/NCTE(Acad.), signed Hasib Ahmad, Member-Secretary.
**T1 text via mirror** (ncte.gov.in was unreachable; retrieved from Punjab SEB's PSTET document store) — https://pstet.pseb.ac.in/EligibilityDoc/NCTE-Guidelines-23-aug-2010.pdf

Operative clause, appearing identically for Classes I–V and VI–VIII as limb (b), conjoined by "AND":
> *"(b) Pass in the Teacher Eligibility Test (TET), to be conducted by the appropriate Government in accordance with the Guidelines framed by the NCTE for the purpose."*

The **29 July 2011 amendment** is confirmed as to existence and date by two official sources retrieved (ctet.nic.in introduction page, **T1 direct**, https://ctet.nic.in/introduction/; and Rajya Sabha PSC 368th Report, **T2**) but **its text was not retrieved** — ncte.gov.in refused every connection.

### A3. NCTE Guidelines of 11 February 2011 — the 60% rule

NCTE Notification No. 76-4/2010/NCTE/Acad dated 11.02.2011.
**T1 (direct, quoted in CBSE's own bulletin):** *"As per NCTE Notification No.76-4/2010/NCTE/Acad dated 11.02.2011: A person who scores 60% or more in the TET exam will be considered as TET pass."* — CTET September 2026 Information Bulletin, §9, https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3443dec3062d0286986e21dc0631734c9/uploads/2026/05/202605111250310617.pdf

The rationale, verbatim from the Guidelines (**T1 via mirror**, https://mastering.mkcl.org/tet/images/NCTERules.pdf): the TET *"would bring national standards and benchmark of teacher quality in the recruitment process"*; would *"induce teacher education institutions… to further improve their performance standards"*; and *"would send a positive signal to all stakeholders that the Government lays special emphasis on teacher quality."*

Two properties that matter:
- **The 60% cut is absolute, not norm-referenced.** No scaling, no vacancy linkage.
- **Category relaxation is permissive on the school management, not on the examining body** (Guidelines para 9(a); CTET bulletin §9(a)).
- Qualifying confers **no right to appointment** (para 9(b)).

⚠️ **Date discrepancy:** CBSE's 2021 public notice cites the same letter as *"dated 10/02/2011"*; CBSE's 2025 FAQ and the PIB release say 11.02.2011. Three-to-one favours **11 February 2011**; treat "10/02/2011" as a typo, not a second document.

### A4. Lifelong TET validity (2021) — the chain, precisely

The brief's "around 3 June 2021" is the *ministerial announcement*. The instrument is six days later.

- **3 June 2021, 1:41 PM — PIB Release ID 1723992, Ministry of Education.** **T1 (direct)** — https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1723992
  > *"Government has decided to extend the validity period of Teachers Eligibility Test qualifying certificate from 7 years to lifetime with retrospective effect from 2011. The respective State Govts./UTs will take necessary action to revalidate/issue fresh TET certificates to those candidates whose period of 7 years has already elapsed."*
- **9 June 2021 — the operative instrument: NCTE letter No. NCTE-Reg1011/78/2020-US(Regulation)-HQ/99954-99992**, substituting the second sentence of para 11 of the 2011 Guidelines. Quoted verbatim in **CBSE Public Notice No. CBSE/CTET/2021 dated 21.06.2021**, **T1 (direct)** — https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3443dec3062d0286986e21dc0631734c9/uploads/2022/04/2022042551.pdf
  > Old: *"The Validity Period of TET qualifying certificate for appointment shall be decided by the appropriate Government subject to a maximum of seven years for all categories."*
  > New: *"The Validity Period of TET qualifying certificate for appointment, **unless otherwise notified by the appropriate Government**, would remain valid for life."*
  CBSE added that it *"shall not issue any revised Marks Statement and CTET Eligibility Certificates pertaining to previous examinations."*

**Two findings usually missed:** (a) the ministerial announcement **preceded** the NCTE instrument; (b) lifelong validity is a **default subject to state override**, not a floor. **The NCTE General Body resolution behind the 9 June letter was not found** — the "NCTE General Body" attribution in the brief is **unverified**.

## B. COURT ACTIONS THAT HALTED OR VOIDED TEACHER RECRUITMENT

### B1. West Bengal SSC 2016 — the largest teacher-recruitment annulment in Indian history

**The Supreme Court judgment. T1 (direct, full text).**
*State of West Bengal v. Baishakhi Bhattacharyya (Chatterjee) & Ors.*, **2025 INSC 437**, Civil Appeal arising out of SLP (C) No. 9586 of 2024 with ~150 connected appeals, **decided 3 April 2025**, 41 pages. Bench: **Sanjiv Khanna, CJI** and **Sanjay Kumar, J.** (judgment authored by CJI Khanna).
https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2024/18822/18822_2024_1_1501_60635_Judgement_03-Apr-2025.pdf

**What the 2016 selection covered (¶ bullets, T1):** 12,905 Assistant Teachers Classes IX–X; 5,712 Assistant Teachers Classes XI–XII; 2,067 Group C; 3,956 Group D — **24,640 declared vacancies**.

**The core holding (¶20, verbatim):**
> *"In our opinion, this is a case wherein the entire selection process has been vitiated and tainted beyond resolution. Manipulations and frauds on a large scale, coupled with the attempted cover-up, have dented the selection process beyond repair and partial redemption. The credibility and legitimacy of the selection are denuded."*

**The legal test the Court applied (¶19 bullets, verbatim):**
> *"Despite the inconvenience caused to untainted candidates, when broad and deep manipulation in the selection process is proven, due weightage has to be given to maintaining the purity of the selection process."*
> *"Individual notice and hearing may not be necessary in all cases for practical reasons when the facts establish that the entire selection process is vitiated with illegalities at a large scale."*

**Tainted vs untainted (¶¶45–49, T1):**
- ¶45 — tainted candidates: services **terminated** and **must refund all salaries**; *"Since their appointments were the result of fraud, this amounts to cheating."*
- ¶46 — untainted candidates: *"the entire selection process has been rightly declared null and void due to the egregious violations and illegalities, which violated Articles 14 and 16… As such, the appointments of these candidates are cancelled. However, candidates who are already employed **need not be asked to refund** or restitute any payments made to them. However, their services will be terminated."*
- ¶47 — cancelled candidates who previously held other government posts may apply to rejoin within three months; **no break in service, seniority preserved, no wages for the interregnum**; supernumerary posts may be created.
- ¶48 — **only Ms. Soma Das** (disabled) continues on humanitarian grounds; other differently-abled appointees continue **with wages only until fresh appointments are completed**.
- ¶49 — non-tainted candidates may re-apply with age relaxation.
- ¶51 — *"we uphold the impugned judgment cancelling en bloc / entire selection process but have made certain modifications."*

**THE NUMBERS — a first-class counting problem. T1 (direct), arithmetic mine.**

The universal figure **25,753 does not appear in either judgment.** I searched the full text of both.

The judgments' own table (SC ¶37; HC ¶271) of WBSSC recommendations vs Board appointment letters:

|---|---|---|---|
| AT Classes IX–X | 11,425 | 12,946 | **1,071** (SC) / **1,539** (HC) — actual difference **1,521** |
| AT Classes XI–XII | 5,557 | 5,756 | 199 ✓ |
| Group C | 2,067 | 2,483 | 416 ✓ |
| Group D | 3,881 | 4,550 | 669 ✓ |
| **Total** | **22,930** | **25,735** | — |

- Rows 2–4 are internally consistent. **Row 1 is not, in either judgment, and the two courts print different wrong numbers for it** (SC: 1,071; HC: 1,539; arithmetic: 1,521). This is an error in the judicial record, reproduced and varied.
- **Total appointment letters per the Board's own figures = 25,735.** The canonical **25,753** differs by **18**. I found no official document using 25,753 — it is used by WBSSC/WBBSE in compliance proceedings and universally in reporting (e.g. PTI, 19 Dec 2025, https://news.careers360.com/wbbse-untainted-school-teachers-continue-working-till-august-31-supreme-court-directive-slst/amp), and the Calcutta HC (Justice Amrita Sinha, 19 Nov 2025) is reported as treating 1,806 tainted candidates as being "among the 25,753". **Report this as a counting/transcription discrepancy of unresolved origin, not a substantive dispute** — but the judgments themselves support 25,735.
- The Court **rejected** WBSSC's and the Board's late attempt to reconcile the figures (¶37): *"We reject this late attempt by WBSSC and the Board to reconcile the figures."*

**How many were actually tainted — a second, larger discrepancy. T1 (direct).**
WBSSC's own admission recorded at ¶26: **1,498 out-of-panel appointments + 926 rank-jumping + 4,091 OMR mismatches − 239 overlapping = 6,276 illegal appointments.** (Arithmetic verified: 1,498+926+4,091−239 = 6,276.) A further table (¶26A) gives 463 rank-jumped + 749 out-of-panel = 1,212 on further verification; CBI identified 8,163 OMR mismatches of whom **4,273 had been appointed**.

**Yet the "tainted" list WBSSC actually published contains only 1,806 names.** WBSSC published it, the Calcutta HC (Justice Amrita Sinha) found it inadequate on **19 November 2025** and ordered a fuller list with parents' names, addresses, category of taint, school and district; WBSSC re-published on **27 November 2025**. **T3/T4** — https://www.deccanherald.com/india/west-bengal/adhering-to-calcutta-high-court-order-wbssc-re-releases-tainted-candidate-list-with-additional-details-3812128 ; https://www.indiatvnews.com/jobs/news/west-bengal-ssc-releases-names-of-1-806-tainted-candidates-from-2016-slst-adhering-to-calcutta-hc-order-2025-11-27-1019277
**The gap between 6,276 admitted illegal appointments and 1,806 published tainted names is unexplained in anything I retrieved. This is a live, first-class finding.**

**The Calcutta High Court judgment. T1 (direct, full 282 pp.).**
*Baishakhi Bhattacharyya (Chatterjee) v. State of West Bengal*, Division Bench of **Debangsu Basak** and **Md. Shabbar Rashidi, JJ.**, **22 April 2024**, WPA 18585/2021 and connected. *The West Bengal Board of Primary Education & Anr. v. - ¶190: CBI found irregularities in **264 candidates** (grace marks) and **96 candidates** who did not secure qualifying marks; *"In view of such identification, the allegation of fraud and corruption pertaining to the entire recruitment process, is not sustainable and the appointment of the 32,000 teachers cannot be interfered with."*

The DB observed: *"Such alteration of marks and number of candidates cannot be construed to be mere typographical errors. No documents were produced to establish such alteration."* **This is the origin of the 36,000-vs-32,000 divergence — a judicial correction, not two events.**
- Stay of the DB judgment was sought immediately after delivery and **refused**.

**The doctrinal contrast is the point:** on near-identical facts (2016 WB teacher recruitment, CBI probe, allegations of manipulation), the SC annulled 25,735 appointments where taint could not be segregated, and the HC upheld ~32,000 where CBI could identify only 360 tainted individuals. The operative variable is **segregability**, exactly as ¶19 of the SC judgment frames it.

## C. THE LEGISLATIVE RESPONSE

### C1. The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 — **the Schedule excludes every teacher exam in the country except NTA-conducted ones**

**T1 (direct, full text as on 21 July 2025)** — https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/20100/1/A2024-01.pdf

**ACT NO. 1 OF 2024. Assented [12th February, 2024.]** — confirming the brief's date against secondary sources that say 25 February.
**Commencement: 21 June 2024**, vide **S.O. 2422(E) dated 21 June 2024** (footnote 1 of the Act).
**Rules: the brief's "rules notified 21 June 2024" is wrong.** 21 June 2024 is the *commencement* date. The **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Rules, 2024** were made by **G.S.R. 342(E) dated 23 June 2024**, DoPT, published in the Gazette of India Extraordinary Part II §3(i), No. 319, **24 June 2024**. **T3** (secondary reproducing the gazette text verbatim) — https://www.staffnews.in/2024/06/public-examinations-prevention-of-unfair-means-rules-2024.html

**s.2(k):** *"'public examination' means any examination conducted by the public examination authority, **as specified in the Schedule**, or conducted by such other authority as may be notified by the Central Government."*

**THE SCHEDULE, verbatim and complete:**
> **THE SCHEDULE** — [See section 2(k)]
> ANY EXAMINATION CONDUCTED BY—
> 1. Union Public Service Commission.
> 2. Staff Selection Commission.
> 3. Railway Recruitment Boards.
> 4. Institute of Banking Personnel Selection.
> 5. Ministries or Departments of the Central Government and their attached and subordinate offices **for recruitment of staff**.
> 6. National Testing Agency.
> 7. Such other authority as may be notified by the Central Government.

**Coverage conclusion (mine, on the T1 text):**
- **No state teacher-recruitment body is listed. No state TET body is listed. No state at all is listed.**
- **CTET is NOT covered.** CBSE is an autonomous body, not an "attached and subordinate office", and entry 5 is confined to *"recruitment of staff"* of ministries/departments — CTET is neither. Only entry 7 could bring it in, and **the Schedule as printed in the 21 July 2025 consolidation shows no added authority**.
- **Coverage follows the conducting authority, not the employer.** NTA is in the Schedule, so **an NTA-conducted teacher-recruitment exam for KVS/NVS is inside the Act**, while CBSE-conducted CTET for the same schools is outside. That is an artefact of drafting, not policy.
- The Act's own **Statement of Objects and Reasons ¶4** concedes the design: *"The Bill shall serve as a model draft for States to adopt at their discretion. This would aid States in preventing the criminal elements from disrupting conduct of their State level public examinations."* **T1**

**This is a first-class finding.** Of the teacher-examination failures documented above — WB SSC 2016, Bihar TRE-3, UPTET 2021, REET 2021, RPSC Senior Teacher 2022, Maharashtra TET 2018/2020, Haryana HTET 2015, Gujarat TAT 2018, OTET 2019 — **the central anti-paper-leak Act would have reached none of them.**

Penalties (T1): s.10(1) 3–5 years and fine up to ₹10 lakh; **s.11(1) organised crime: 5–10 years and fine not less than ₹1 crore**, with attachment and forfeiture of institutional property under s.11(2); s.9 all offences cognizable, non-bailable, non-compoundable; s.12 investigation by DSP/ACP and above, with power to refer to a central agency; s.15 overriding effect. s.3 defines unfair means to include leakage of question paper or answer key, unauthorised possession of an OMR sheet, tampering with OMR sheets, altering assessment, and tampering with shortlisting/merit documents.

**Uttar Pradesh — U.P. Act No. 8 of 2024. T1 (direct).** https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/21000/1/8of2024_english.pdf
Assented **6 August 2024**; s.1(3) *"deemed to have come into force with effect from 15th day of July, 2024."* **The decisive words, s.2(1)(g)(i):**
> *"'public examination' includes,— (i) any examination **either qualifying or competitive** for recruitment or regularization or promotion to any post in public service conducted by examination authority."*
s.2(1)(c)(iii) makes "Board, University, Authority or Body under any law for the time being in force" an examination authority — capturing the Examination Regulatory Authority, Prayagraj (UPTET) and UPBEB. **This is the only one of the six statutes whose definition reaches eligibility tests as a class, and it is almost certainly a direct response to the UPTET 2021 leak.**
Penalties (T1, s.13): solver gang 7–14 years + ₹10–25 lakh; **repeat offence by a solver gang — life imprisonment + ₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore**; obstruction by threat to life or wrongful restraint of examination staff — **life imprisonment + ₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore**; s.14 wilful neglect of duty causing a leak — 2–7 years.
**Correction:** the cabinet approved the **Ordinance on 25 June 2024**, not ~4 June 2024.

**Gujarat — Gujarat Act No. 2 of 2023. T1 (direct).** https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19402/1/2_of_2023.pdf
Assented **2 March 2023**, gazetted 3 March 2023. Correct title is **"Prevention of Unfair Means"**, not "Unfair Methods". **THE SCHEDULE (see s.2(c)), entry 5: "The State Examination Board"** — the Rajya Pariksha Board that conducts Gujarat TET-I, TET-II, HTAT and TAT. Entry 8 adds the Gujarat Secondary and Higher Secondary Education Board; entries 3, 4 and 6 cover recruitment bodies. **Gujarat closed the eligibility-test gap by naming a body rather than broadening a definition.** Penalties: examinee up to 3 years + fine not less than ₹1 lakh; conspiracy/abetment **5–10 years + ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore**. **The s.1(2) commencement notification was not retrieved — the date the Act actually came into force is unknown.**

**Haryana — Act 25 of 2021. T1 (direct).** https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/18320/1/haryana_public_examination_%28prevention_of_unfair_means%29_-_cs.pdf
Assented **4 September 2021**, gazetted 10 September 2021 (Notification No. Leg. 25/2021). **There is no Schedule.**
> *s.2(1)(f): "'public examination' **means** examination for the purpose of **recruitment to any post** under the State Government including autonomous bodies, authorities, boards or corporations."*
**HTET is not covered.** It is conducted by BSEH, Bhiwani and confers eligibility only. **Uttarakhand — Act 13 of 2023. T1** (via PRS reproduction of the State Gazette). https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/uttarakhand/2023/ActNo13of2023UKD.pdf
Assented **26 April 2023**; gazette notification No. 168/XXXVI(3)/2023, 27 April 2023; s.34 repeals the Ordinance of **11 February 2023**. Correct title: **"…(Measures for Control and Prevention of Unfair Means in Recruitment) Act, 2023."**
s.2(1)(d): *"'Competitive examination' means the examinations as specified in Schedule-2 conducted for **selection to any post**…"* Schedule-1 lists only UKSSSC, UKPSC, the Medical Selection Board, and "any other institution authorised by the State Government". **UBSE, Ramnagar — which conducts UTET — is absent. UTET is outside unless notified under s.32; no such notification was found.** PRS's comparative analysis flags two design features: a fixed 3-year minimum for a first offence with no gradation, and **debarment on the filing of a chargesheet rather than on conviction**.

**Jharkhand — Act 15 of 2023. T1** (via PRS reproduction of the Jharkhand Gazette Asadharan, 29 November 2023). https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/jharkhand/2023/Act15of2023Jharkhand.pdf
Correct title again uses **"Control and Prevention"**. The drafting is the sharpest illustration in the set: **Schedule-I lists *bodies*** (including, at entry 5, the **Jharkhand Academic Council**, which runs JTET), but **Schedule-II lists *examinations* and inserts the word "recruitment" into six of its eight entries** — including entry 5, *"Any **recruitment** examination conducted by the Jharkhand Academic Council."* Combined with the s.2(1)(d) chapeau ("conducted for selection to any post"), **JTET is most likely outside**; the counter-argument runs through unqualified entry 3, but the specific governs the general. **Contested, not settled.** JSSC teacher recruitment is covered (Schedule-II entry 2 carries no "recruitment" limiter).

**Rajasthan — two distinct Acts; the brief conflates them.** There is **no** "Rajasthan Public Examination (Measures for Prevention of Unfair Means in Recruitment) Act, **1992**."
- **The Rajasthan Public Examination (Prevention of Unfairmeans) Act, 1992 (Act 27 of 1992, assented 8 Nov 1992). T1** — https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/18813/1/rajasthan_public_examination_(prevention_of_unfairmeans)_act,_1992.pdf Schedule-based; entry 3 covers RPSC (hence RPSC teacher grades); entry 1 covers BSER examinations **"under the Rajasthan Secondary Examination Act, 1957"** — and **REET is not conducted under that Act**, so REET's coverage under the 1992 Act is arguable, not plain. Maximum penalty as originally enacted: **3 years / ₹2,000**.
- **The Rajasthan Public Examination (Measures for Prevention of Unfair Means in Recruitment) Act, 2022 (Act 6 of 2022, assented 5 April 2022, in force 12 April 2022 vide S.O. 8). T1** — https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/18812/1/in_english.pdf s.2(e): *"'public examination' means examination for the purpose of **recruitment** to any post under the State Government including autonomous bodies… as specified in Schedule-II."* **Schedule-II entry 6: "Any recruitment examination conducted by the Rajasthan Board of Secondary Education."** So **state teacher recruitment is unambiguously covered** (RPSC entry 1, RSSB entry 3, BSER entry 6); **REET, as a pure eligibility test, again sits at the edge** of the recruitment framing. Penalties: s.10(2) 5–10 years + ₹10 lakh to ₹10 crore; s.14 cognizable, non-bailable, non-compoundable; s.15 investigation by ASP and above; ss.16–17 Designated Courts.
- **2023 amendment (Bill No. 18 of 2023, T1** — https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_states/rajasthan/2023/Bill18of2023Rajasthan.pdf**)** substitutes s.10(2) to **"not less than ten years but which may extend to imprisonment for life"**. The Statement of Objects records that the existing penalties had *"no deterrent effect"* on "organised mafias". The enacted instrument is reported as Rajasthan Act 17 of 2023 — **Act number T4; the enacted text and gazette were not retrieved.**
- **Timing point:** the 2022 Act came into force **12 April 2022 — after** the REET 2021 leak and **after** the Level-2 cancellation. **The REET 2021 leak could not be prosecuted under it.** The December 2022 RPSC Senior Teacher leak was the first major teacher-exam leak fully within its reach, eight months after commencement.

**Cross-cutting conclusion.** **No state statute contains an express exclusion of school-teacher eligibility tests.** In every exclusion case the gap arises from framing "examination" around *hiring* rather than *licensing* — and all three exclusion statutes retain a notification power that, so far as could be found, has never been used to close it.

## CORRECTIONS TO THE BRIEF

- Public Examinations Act 2024: assent **12 February 2024** (brief correct); **commencement 21 June 2024**; **Rules made 23 June 2024 (G.S.R. 342(E)), published 24 June 2024** — not "rules notified 21 June 2024".
- The Rajasthan statute is **two Acts**, not one: the 1992 Prevention of Unfairmeans Act and the 2022 Measures for Prevention of Unfair Means **in Recruitment** Act. There is no 1992 "in Recruitment" Act.
- The 24 December 2022 Rajasthan leak was the **RPSC Senior Teacher Grade-II GK paper**, not REET-Mains/3rd-grade.
- Rajasthan's paper-leak SIT was constituted **December 2023**, not 2024.
- Uttarakhand and Jharkhand Acts are "**Measures for Control and Prevention** of Unfair Means in Recruitment", 2023.
- Gujarat's Act is "Prevention of Unfair **Means**", not "Unfair Methods"; Gujarat Act No. 2 of 2023.
- UP's Ordinance was approved by cabinet **25 June 2024**, not ~4 June 2024; the Act was assented **6 August 2024**, deemed in force **15 July 2024**.
- The 2021 lifelong-validity change was effected by an **NCTE letter of 9 June 2021**, six days after the PIB announcement.
- The Odisha teacher-exam annulment on record is **OTET**, not OSSTET.
- Assam's fake-TET-certificate case is **2021** (CID Case 10/2021), not 2022.
- Two items materially missing from the brief and added here: ***Devesh Sharma* (2023 INSC 704, 11 Aug 2023)** — a nationwide eligibility shock that voided B.Ed eligibility for primary posts — and **Tripura's 10,323 teachers** (HC 7 May 2014, SC 29 Mar 2017), the second-largest annulment in the window.
