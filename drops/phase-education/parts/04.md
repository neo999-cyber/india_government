## 4. ENROLMENT AND THE UDISE+ BASE SHIFT

This is the textbook reporting-base shift the brief asked for, and it is worse than the brief assumed: **there are three stacked base changes, not one, and only two of them are disclosed.**

### 4.1 What changed, and when

Individual **student-level** data collection began with reference year **2022-23**, replacing school-wise consolidated returns. Two identifiers were introduced: **Aadhaar, collected voluntarily**, and a mandatory portal-generated unique educational ID (EID) for every child.

> "To revolutionize the school data collection mechanism further and align the data to monitor the recommendations of NEP, **w.e.f., 2022-23, individual student wise data collection has been started** which will help in tracking of the drop out students and saving to the Govt. by way of accurate number of students in the school. This is being done for the first time at the National level." — UDISE+ 2023-24 Report, ¶1.5, p. 10

> "Through, unique student and teacher Identity… credentials of individuals… can be established more accurately leading to **weeding out of duplicate or ghost entries** resulting into precise identification of beneficiaries for benefit transfers of Samagra Shiksha scheme, PM POSHAN Scheme, National Scholarship scheme etc." — ¶1.7, p. 11

**Direction of bias: downward on the numerator, by construction.** Deduplication can only remove records, never add them.

**And the intensity of the correction is a map of administrative capacity, not of schooling.** Aadhaar seeding reached only **79.4% nationally in 2023-24** (19.7 of 24.8 crore students), with enormous state variance — **Meghalaya 24.2%, Bihar 38.8%, Manipur 51.8%** against **Andhra Pradesh 98.1%**. By 2025-26 seeding reached ~90.2%. **If deduplication is the mechanism, then the measured "fall" is larger where Aadhaar penetration is higher, which has nothing to do with children.** This should be stated wherever state-level enrolment changes are used.

### 4.2 THE MINISTRY'S OWN INCOMPARABILITY STATEMENT — verbatim

**UDISE+ 2023-24 Report, DISCLAIMER, p. 7:**

> "Post implementation of NEP 2020, requisite reforms in data collection system has been necessitated and UDISE+ 2023-24 is aligned with the recommendations of NEP 2020 by shifting focus on individual student for the first time at the national level a departure from school wise consolidated data till 2021-22 and hence **data from the year UDISE+ 2022-23 data is not strictly comparable with the previous reports on various educational indicators such as GER, NER, Dropout rate etc.** Similarly, **population projections used for generating various educational indicators have been moderated** based on the latest trends of birth rate as recorded by SRS system, saturation reports of UIDAI based on Aadhaar enrolment and the trends of adjoining States in case of smaller States and UTs. Therefore, educational ratios of UTs and few smaller State have undergone changes and strictly not comparable with previous year's figures."

And ¶1.8, p. 11 — note the bridge claim in the last sentence:

> "…this exercise of maintaining a separate student base is **totally different, unique and incomparable to 2021-22 or prior years**… hence the UDISE+ 2022-23 data is not strictly comparable with the previous reports on various educational indicators like GER, NER, Dropout rates etc. **However, 2022-23 and 2023-24 are comparable.**"

The formula recurs, extended, in each later report: 2024-25 — "However, 2022-23, 2023-24 & 2024-25 are comparable"; 2025-26 — "…for the years 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25 & 2025-26."

> Source: *2023-24 Report on Unified District Information System for Education Plus (UDISE+)*, Ministry of Education, Department of School Education & Literacy; reference date 31 March 2024; released **30 December 2024**. Canonical URL now dead; retrieved via Wayback capture of the ministry PDF: https://web.archive.org/web/20250331172900id_/https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/statistics/report_in_PDF/udise_report_existing_23_24.pdf — **T1.**

### 4.3 THE COMPOUNDING BREAK — the denominator moved in the same year as the numerator

The disclaimer says it in the same breath, and this is the part most coverage misses. The population projections were not simply taken from the National Commission on Population's Technical Group; they were **"moderated"** by the Ministry using three ad-hoc inputs: SRS birth-rate trends, **UIDAI Aadhaar-saturation reports**, and "the trends of adjoining States in case of smaller States and UTs."

Two consequences:

1. **GER after 2021-22 is a ratio in which numerator and denominator were re-based in the same year.** A GER change across 2021-22 → 2022-23 is uninterpretable as a behavioural signal.
2. **Aadhaar appears on both sides of the ratio** — the numerator is deduplicated using Aadhaar, the denominator is "moderated" using UIDAI saturation reports. **That is a genuine circularity and should be recorded as one.**

For contrast, the pre-break denominator was named and external: UDISE+ 2019-20, fn. 2, p. 12 — "As per the latest population projection released by the **Ministry of Health and Family Welfare in July 2020**", i.e. the *Report of the Technical Group on Population Projections 2011–2036*. Published 2023 denominators (UDISE+ 2023-24, Table 10.1, p. 180): **age 6-10 = 116,013,877; age 11-13 = 70,348,227; age 3-5 = 73,664,432.** Definition (p. 186): "GER primary = Enrolment in class 1 to 5 ÷ projected population in age group 6-10 years."

**And no Census since 2011 (`P-04`), so every post-2011 denominator is a projection — and after 2022-23 it is a projection the Ministry of Education has additionally adjusted itself.**

### 4.4 What happened to the 2022-23 report

**A 2022-23 report exists** (185 pp, retrieved in full) — so the premise that no comparable 2022-23 figure was published is not quite right. What actually happened is more interesting:

- UDISE+ 2021-22 was released around November 2022. **There was then no UDISE+ release for roughly two years.**
- **The 2022-23 and 2023-24 reports were released together**, end-December 2024 / January 2025 — the 2022-23 data appearing **18–24 months late, and only alongside the year that superseded it.**
- Each year is published in **two parallel volumes**: an "Existing Structure" (10+2) edition and an "NEP Structure" (5+3+3+4) edition.
- **The 2022-23 report carries the incomparability language in a weaker form** — ¶1.8 says only "incomparable to previous years", with **no bridge sentence and no mention of the population-projection moderation.** The explicit "However, 2022-23 and 2023-24 are comparable" claim appears only retrospectively, in the 2023-24 report.

> **Reading: the break was disclosed, but the disclosure was constructed after the fact, in the document that benefited from having a comparator. The break-year datum entered the public record already paired with a successor, which structurally discourages anyone from treating 2022-23 as the break year.**

### 4.6 THE SIZE OF THE FALL — and a correction to the figure in circulation

| Comparison | Pre-primary to HS | Primary to HS |
|---|---|---|
| **2021-22 → 2023-24** | **−17,190,002 (−1.72 crore, −6.5%)** | **−20,777,592 (−2.08 crore, −8.1%)** |
| 2018-19 → 2023-24 | ≈ −1.23 crore | ≈ −1.35 crore |
| 2022-23 → 2023-24 | −3,745,894 | −6,660,879 |
| 2023-24 → 2024-25 | −1,113,148 | −2,077,431 |

> **The widely-quoted "1.2 crore fall" is the 2018-19 → 2023-24 comparison, not 2021-22 → 2023-24. The 2021-22 → 2023-24 fall is substantially larger: 1.72 crore on the total and 2.08 crore on Classes I–XII.** These get conflated constantly and the instrument should not repeat the conflation.

**The fall is concentrated in primary.** Primary enrolment 121,842,250 (2021-22) → 107,837,711 (2023-24) → 104,381,347 (2024-25) — **a 14.4% fall in two years** — while higher secondary was roughly flat (28,579,050 → 27,135,514). **A pure-deduplication story has to explain why ghosts were so concentrated in Classes I–V.** It is plausible (mid-day-meal and scholarship incentives attach most strongly there) but it is an assumption the ministry has never evidenced.

### 4.7 Is there a bridge or back-series? No.

**No back-cast, no dual-basis year and no reconciliation table appears in any of the five UDISE+ reports retrieved.** The ministry's position is purely declarative: pre-2021-22 and post-2022-23 are two series and you may not join them. **There is no published estimate of how much of the fall is deduplication and how much is real decline.** That quantity — the one thing that would let anyone close the ledger — has never been published. `not-published` (the ministry holds both the old and new bases and could produce a dual-run for one year).

**And the ministry's presentation omits the number entirely.** The PIB release for UDISE+ 2025-26 (Release ID **2282141**, 7 July 2026 — https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2282141, **T1**, retrieved in full) reports teachers, PTR, dropout, retention, GER, transition, single-teacher and zero-enrolment schools, computers, internet, electricity, toilets and female representation — **every one framed as an improvement — and contains no total enrolment figure at all.** The total appears only inside the report PDF.

### 4.8 A SECOND STRUCTURAL BREAK, in 2025-26 — with a back-cast that contradicts previously published values

> "With effect from 2025-26, the school ecosystem is reclassified into the new pedagogical and curricular structure i.e. Foundational (Pre-Primary + Primary), Preparatory (partly Primary), Middle (Upper Primary), Secondary (Secondary + Higher Secondary) in line with NEP 2020 recommendations. As a result, education indicators are also re-classified in the 5+3+3+4 structure format from the previous 10+2 academic structure." — UDISE+ 2025-26, ¶1.9

The PIB release then **back-casts 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25 onto the new stages — and the restated values do not match the originally published ones for the same years:**

|---|---|---|
| Dropout, secondary | **14.1** | **10.9** |
| Dropout, primary / preparatory | **1.9** | **3.7** |
| **GER, secondary** | **77.4** | **66.5** |

> **Same year, same source system, two official values, differing by up to 11 GER points and 18 retention points.** A second reporting-base shift layered on the first, three years later. **Any series built here must be tagged by *structure* as well as by year.**

### 4.10 Dropout, retention, transition — and which year is actually corrupted

**UDISE+ definitions, verbatim (2023-24 report, Concepts and Definitions, p. 187):**

> "**Dropout Rate** — Proportion of pupil from a cohort enrolled in a given level at a given school year who are **no longer enrolled at any grade in the following school year.** … Promotion Rate + Repetition Rate + Dropout Rate = 100."
> "**Retention Rate** — Percentage of a cohort of pupils enrolled in a first grade of a given level… who are expected to reach the last grade of the level."

**Dropout is a year-on-year differencing operator.** It compares year *t* against year *t+1*. **Therefore any year whose comparator sits on the other side of a base shift is mechanically corrupted.**

All-India, existing structure, **T1**:

| Year | Dropout Pri | Dropout UP | Dropout Sec | Transition P→UP | UP→Sec | Sec→HS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020-21 | 0.8 | 1.9 | 14.6 | 92.1 | 91.3 | 73.3 |
| 2021-22 | 1.45 | 3.02 | 12.61 | 93.18 | 88.81 | 78.41 |
| **2022-23** | **7.8** | **8.1** | **16.4** | 87.9 | 86.7 | 71.3 |
| **2023-24** | **1.9** | **5.2** | **14.1** | 88.8 | 83.3 | 71.5 |
| 2024-25 | **0.3** | 3.5 | 11.5 | 92.2 | 86.6 | 75.1 |

Retention (T1): 2023-24 — primary 85.4, elementary 78.0, secondary 63.8, higher secondary 45.6. 2024-25 — primary 92.4, elementary 82.8, secondary 62.9, higher secondary 47.2.

**THE COMPARABILITY VERDICT — and it contradicts the ministry's own bridge claim.**

Look at primary dropout: **1.45 → 7.8 → 1.9 → 0.3.** A single-year spike to 7.8% that vanishes the next year is not a behavioural event. Apply the definition and the mechanism is transparent:

- **2022-23 dropout** = student-level 2022-23 differenced against school-consolidated 2021-22. **It differences across the break.** The ~1.4 crore records that disappeared in the re-basing were counted as *children who stopped being enrolled* — i.e. **as dropouts.** The 2022-23 dropout rate mechanically absorbed the deduplication.
- **2023-24 dropout** = student-level against student-level. Both sides on the same basis. Internally consistent.

> **So the ministry's claim that "2022-23 and 2023-24 are comparable" is wrong for dropout specifically, on the ministry's own published definition. 2023-24 is the first clean year; 2022-23 is a one-off artefact. For this instrument: treat 2022-23 dropout, transition and retention as a structural NA, not as a data point.**

The 2023-24 report acknowledges the method change without acknowledging the discontinuity it creates: "This is calculated based on actual movement of students from one level to other using individual student wise data and represent actual scenario more precisely" (p. 22). And note ¶1.5 — student-level data was introduced *in order to* track dropouts, **so the dropout series was always going to be re-based by its own instrument.**

**Second-order warning:** the 2025-26 back-cast restates 2022-23 preparatory dropout as **8.7** and secondary as **13.8**, against 7.8 and 16.4 as originally published. **Even the corrupted year now has two official values.**
