## 0. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — THE SIX LOAD-BEARING FINDINGS

1. **The required series exists, is complete 2000-01 → 2021-22, and I retrieved it from the primary document.** ABE Table 4 publishes **two** series. They move in **opposite directions** across the political transition. All-departments rose 3.84% → 4.12% of GDP (FY2013-14 → FY2021-22); education-departments-only fell from 2.97% to 2.75%, and the **Centre's own education-department spending fell from 0.64% to 0.40% of GDP — a 38% decline in share terms.** Which series you pick determines the entire finding.

2. **The headline 4.12% is not "education spending" in any lay sense.** It includes Ministry of **Defence** (₹15,519 cr), **Atomic Energy** (₹16,392 cr), **Space** (₹5,720 cr), Health (₹30,097 cr) and Women & Child Development/ICDS (₹21,661 cr) — and it includes **training**. Strip training: 4.03%. Strip other ministries: **2.75%**.

3. **The series ends at FY2021-22 (a Budget Estimate) and the ABE is no longer on the live Ministry website.** The latest edition was published in 2024; no edition covers FY2022-23 onward. **T3 has zero observations.** All ABE editions had to be recovered from the Internet Archive.

4. **Budget Estimates are systematically revised down ~0.3pp.** FY2019-20: BE 4.39% → RE 4.30% → **Actual 4.04%**. FY2020-21: BE 4.64% → RE 4.36% → Actual never published. Economic Survey 2023-24 quoted **4.64% as "latest available"** — a Budget Estimate presented as fact.

5. **The pre-2011-12 half of the series is spliced onto a different GDP base**, inflating UPA-era ratios by 0.13–0.29pp. I proved this from primary documents and recomputed the series on a consistent basis.

6. **Census 2011's real literacy rate is 72.98%, not 74.04%.** The universally-cited figure — including in this brief — is the *provisional* March 2011 estimate. The final figure, computed from the Registrar General's own Primary Census Abstract, is **72.98%**. And **PLFS *does* publish a literacy rate: 81.1% (age 7+) for calendar 2025**, published March 2026 — the latest actual national measurement.

### Three artefacts that must not be read as policy

**(a) The FY2020-21 spike is a denominator effect.** Nominal GDP *fell* from ₹2,01,03,593 cr to ₹1,98,29,927 cr (−1.4%) in the COVID year. The numerator grew 6.4%; the ratio rose 0.32pp. Roughly a fifth of the apparent rise is the denominator shrinking.

**(b) There is a structural break in "other departments" straddling FY2013-14/FY2014-15.** Other-departments education spend grew **+41.7%** (FY2013-14) then **+49.0%** (FY2014-15), against +11.4% and +8.4% for education departments. It roughly **doubled** between FY2012-13 (₹68,920 cr) and FY2014-15 (₹1,45,537 cr). Both years are Actuals and the values are **identical across three ABE editions**, so this is not a vintage artefact — but **I could not establish its cause from any retrieved document.** Since it sits exactly on the UPA→T1 boundary and is the main driver of the headline "improvement", **the all-departments series should not be used for UPA-vs-Modi comparison without resolving this.**

**(c) Other-departments share of the total rose from 15.9% (2004-05) to 33.3% (2021-22).** The headline ratio is increasingly driven by non-education ministries.

### A2.4 The denominator — **the series is SPLICED, and I proved it**

**The ABE's column header is wrong.** It reads *"GDP AT CURRENT PRICES (AT FACTOR COST)"* for the whole column. That label is true only of the early rows.

**Evidence 1 — the ABE's own footnotes contradict the header.** The 2024 edition cites MoSPI press notes of **02-08-2021** and **28-02-2023** (2011-12 base). The 2010 edition (*ABE 2006-07 to 2008-09*, MHRD, New Delhi 2010) states plainly: *"GDP figures are on the base year 1999-00 series… taken from National Accounts Statistics 2009 published by CSO."*

**Evidence 2 — identical numerators, different denominators across editions.** The 2010 edition gives 2004-05 all-departments as **3.36%** (GDP ₹28,77,701 cr); the 2024 edition gives **3.26%** (GDP ₹29,71,464 cr). The numerator is byte-identical (₹96,694 cr). **The entire difference is the rebasing.**

**Evidence 3 — decisive check against the Economic Survey's own GDP series.** ES 2025-26 Statistical Appendix, **Table 1.6 "Components of Gross Domestic Product at Current Prices", 2011-12 Series** (`https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/Statistical-Appendix-in-English.pdf`, T1):

|---|---|---|---|
| 2004-05 | 29,71,464 | 31,86,332 | **NO** |
| 2009-10 | 61,08,903 | 63,66,407 | **NO** |
| 2010-11 | 72,48,860 | 76,34,472 | **NO** |
| **2011-12** | **87,36,329** | **87,36,329** | **EXACT** |
| 2012-13 | 99,44,013 | 99,44,013 | **EXACT** |
| 2013-14 | 1,12,33,522 | 1,12,33,522 | **EXACT** |

**Conclusion, established from primary documents:**
- **FY2011-12 onward:** GDP at **current market prices, 2011-12 base**. (Confirmed independently by ES 2023-24 Table VII.1 Note (i): *"The ratios to GDP at current market prices are based on 2011-12 base till 2021-22."*)
- **FY2000-01 to FY2010-11:** old-base GDP **at factor cost** — spliced on, not recomputed.

### A2.5 The February 2026 rebasing — **NOT applied, and cannot yet be**

**PIB, Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation, "NEW SERIES OF GROSS DOMESTIC PRODUCT (GDP) ESTIMATES WITH BASE YEAR 2022-23", 27 FEB 2026 4:00PM** — `https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2233518` (T1).

- New base **2022-23** replaces 2011-12. GDP for base year 2022-23 = **₹261.18 lakh crore**; nominal 2023-24 = **₹289.84 lakh cr**; 2024-25 = **₹318.07 lakh cr**; 2025-26 (2nd AE) = **₹345.47 lakh cr**.
- **The back series has NOT been released.** Part C promises only *"Information related to release dates of Back Series"*. Annual estimates on the new base exist for **FY2022-23, FY2023-24, FY2024-25** only.

**Answer to the brief's question — was the published series recomputed on the new base or left spliced?** **Neither, strictly: it was left on the 2011-12 base and cannot be recomputed.** The last ABE (2024) predates the rebasing entirely. Economic Survey 2025-26 (PDFs created **28 January 2026**) also predates it and its Table 1.6 is explicitly labelled "2011-12 Series".

**Direction of the future break:** the new base gives *lower* nominal GDP (2023-24: 289.84 vs 301.23; 2024-25: 318.07 vs 330.68 — about **3.8% lower**). So **every ratio-to-GDP will jump ~+3.9% in relative terms on rebasing.** A 4.12% figure becomes ≈4.28% for no educational reason. **The instrument must record base-year as a field.**

1. **No sub-district granularity.** Census gives village/ward-level literacy; PLFS gives State/UT. Every district- and block-level literacy plan, and all ULLAS targeting, runs on **2011 geography**.
2. **No small-group disaggregation** by caste, tribe, religion and language at fine geography.

## B8. DEFINITIONS — THE GAP THAT MATTERS

| | **Census** | **NSS 75th** | **PLFS 2025** | **CAMS** | **NFHS-5** | **ASER 2024** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Criterion** | Read AND write with understanding, any language | "read and write a simple message … with understanding" | "both read and write a simple message with understanding" | Read+write short statements **OR completed Class I** | Std 9+ **OR** read all **or part of** a sentence | Read a "story" at **Std II** difficulty |
| **How ascertained** | Enumerator asks; **no test** | Informant reports; **no test** | Informant reports; **no test** | **"no test of the ability was performed"** | **Card-read test** (below Std 9 only) | **One-on-one administered task** |
| **Who answers** | Household informant | Household informant | Household informant | Household informant | The respondent | **The child** |
| **Age frame** | **7+** | 7+ | 7+ and 5+ | 15+, 15–24 | W 15–49, M 15–54 | Children **5–16** |
| **Schooling required?** | **No** | No | No | Class I auto-qualifies | Std 9 auto-qualifies | n/a |
| **Coverage** | Complete enumeration | Sample | Sample | Sample | Sample | Sample, **rural only** |

**The Census definition, precisely.** A person aged **7+** who **can both read and write with understanding in any language**. Three properties do the work:
- **No formal schooling required** — informally-acquired literacy counts.
- **Children 0–6 are excluded from the denominator entirely** (not counted as illiterate). This is why the denominator is 1,046,339,724 not 1,210,854,977, and why it is called the *effective* literacy rate.
- **It is asked, not tested.** The enumerator poses the question to a household informant, frequently *about* other members. **No reading or writing task is administered at any point. A Census-literate person is a person someone said was literate.**

**NSS used the same self-declaration — confirmed** (§3.2.1). **Census, NSS and PLFS are one definitional family**; their differences are sampling and coverage, not concept. CAMS is the outlier within the official family (Class I shortcut). NFHS-5 is the only official instrument administering any reading task — and only below Std 9, with partial credit.

**ASER 2024** (ASER Centre / Pratham, released January 2025), method verbatim: *"The ASER reading task assesses whether a child can read letters, words, a simple paragraph at Std I level of difficulty, or a 'story' at Std II level of difficulty… administered one-on-one to each sampled child in the 5-16 age group… The assessment method has remained the same since 2006."*

**% of Std V children who can read a Std II text:**

| Year | Govt | Private | **Govt & Pvt** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2014 | 42.2 | 62.6 | 48.0 |
| 2018 | 44.2 | 65.1 | 50.5 |
| 2022 | 38.5 | 56.8 | 42.8 |
| **2024** | **44.8** | **59.3** | **48.8** |

Std III 2024 = 27.1%; Std VIII 2024 = 71.1%. **Denominator caveat (ASER's own footnote):** the combined column is *"the weighted average for children in government and private schools only"* — excluding other institution types and out-of-school children.

### The concrete point

- Under self-declaration, **81.1%** of Indians aged 7+ are literate (PLFS 2025).
- Under an administered task, **48.8%** of children in **Standard V** can read a **Standard II** text (ASER 2024).
- Under CAMS, completing **Class I** is literacy by definition, unasked.

More than half of Std V children cannot read a text pitched three grades below them. **Every one of them, on reaching 15 with "primary" recorded as attainment, is counted literate by PLFS's ladder, by CAMS's Case II, and by UIS's recoding of PLFS** — and by the Census too, if an informant says so. **The Std II reading task is a harder test than the national literacy statistic.**

**Quantifying the gap** (both **T4** — via Data For India, `https://www.dataforindia.com/measuring-literacy/`; underlying reports not retrieved):
1. **NFHS-3 (2004-05)** ran both methods on the same respondents: **69% self-reported vs 63.4% when required to read the test card — a 5.6pp gap.**
2. **1991 Literacy Survey** re-contact: **over 10%** of below-primary-educated persons reported literate by informants *"were actually illiterate when individually contacted"*; only **70%** of below-primary self-identified literates demonstrated both reading and writing with comprehension.

**5.6pp is a floor, not a ceiling** — NFHS's card test is one sentence with partial credit, skipped entirely above Std 9. The ASER evidence implies far more on any test with real difficulty.
