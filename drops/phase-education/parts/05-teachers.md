## 5. TEACHERS

**The headline finding of this section is a negative one, and it is the answer to Question 1: the Ministry of Education has never established, and does not maintain, a national teacher-vacancy statistic.** What exists are two irreconcilable ad-hoc aggregates, both assembled on request — one for a Lok Sabha question, one for a parliamentary committee — from unaudited state self-returns of visibly variable quality. They differ by about 1.1 lakh posts for the same year. Neither is published as a series, neither is defined, and the Department told the Standing Committee in writing that the data "are maintained by the respective State governments/Union Territory administrations."

The contrast with what IS measured is the point of the whole section: **India counts its teachers to the individual — name, gender, social category, academic and professional qualification, and the nature of their appointment — and publishes almost none of the quantities that would show whether there are enough of them, whether they are qualified for what they teach, or on what terms they are employed.**

The pattern repeats across all six questions, and it has a consistent shape. **The data is collected and not published; or it was published and was discontinued; or the word used to publish it does not mean what the statute means by it.**

- Teacher **vacancies**: never defined nationally; two irreconcilable ad-hoc aggregates (§5.1).
- **PTR**: published, but its numerator was re-based in 2022-23 and its denominator in 2023-24 — and PTR is the one headline indicator the Ministry's own incomparability disclaimer never names (§5.2).
- **Share of schools meeting the RTE norm**: published annually until 2015-16, then discontinued (§5.2.8).
- **Single-teacher schools**: the indicator was introduced in the same year as the base shift, and there is a six-year hole before it (§5.3).
- **Professionally qualified**: published — but the category counts teachers holding a credential that does not authorise the class they teach, which after *Devesh Sharma* is not a lawful qualification at all (§5.4.6).
- **Contract teachers**: a mandatory field on every teacher record since at least 2020, published nowhere; the only national figure in existence was produced by a university centre for UNESCO (§5.6).

**Every one of these is a decision about what to publish, not a limit on what can be known.**

**FOUR DEFECTS IN THIS TABLE, all of which I verified by re-parsing the state rows:**

1. **The 2023-24 all-India total silently excludes Tamil Nadu.** Tamil Nadu's 2023-24 cells are "–" in both the elementary and the secondary table; the 2021-22 and 2022-23 columns include it (elementary 1,45,016 sanctioned, 5,834 vacant in 2021-22). **The printed "Total" row therefore sums a different set of states in the third column than in the first two, with no footnote.** The apparent fall in national vacancies from 10.25 lakh to 8.47 lakh is partly a change in who is counted. **This is a coverage break inside a single published table, undisclosed.**

4. **One row is not credible at all.** Karnataka, elementary 2021-22: sanctioned 182,624, in-position **51,586**, vacant **131,038** — a 71.8% vacancy rate — followed by in-position 153,964 the next year. A state did not triple its elementary teaching force in one year. **India's 2021-22 national elementary vacancy figure of 10.37 lakh contains roughly 1 lakh posts that are a Karnataka reporting error.** Do not carry the 2021-22 point without this caveat.

#### 5.1.5 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — the sanctioned-post base moves, so the vacancy *rate* is not a rate of anything stable

Within Aggregate B, between 2023-24 and 2024-25:

- **Elementary sanctioned posts FALL by 2,58,513** (48.05 → 45.46 lakh, −5.4%).
- **Secondary sanctioned posts RISE by 9,18,066** (15.21 → 24.39 lakh, **+60.3% in one year**).
- Total sanctioned rises by 6,59,553 (+10.4%).

**No education system creates 9.2 lakh secondary teaching posts in twelve months.** This is a reclassification — posts moving between the two rows, plus states re-reporting on a different basis — and it is undisclosed. Its direct consequence: **the reported improvement in the headline vacancy rate from 15.16% to 14.07% is produced by the denominator growing 10.4% while the vacancy count also grew (9.59 → 9.83 lakh). The absolute number of vacant posts went UP by 23,514 and the rate went DOWN.** Anyone quoting the rate as improvement is quoting an artefact of the sanctioned-post base.

Suggested reasonKind for the sanctioned-post series: **never-defined** (no published definition of what "sanctioned" covers, which levels, or which school-management categories).

#### 5.1.6 What the Secretary actually said — the only spoken national number

On 19 May 2025, before the Committee:

> "…there is a fair amount of vacancies in school system across the country. **Around 10 lakhs is a figure which has been mentioned. Out of that, 7.5 lakhs are from the primary sections** … because 'Education' is under the Concurrent List and, **out of 14.8 lakh schools, Government of India administers just about 3,000 schools.** Rest of the schools are either in the private sector or they are managed by the State Governments or they happen to be aided by the State Governments."

> Source: 368th Report, ¶2.17, pp. 13–14 — **T1.**

The Committee's restatement narrows the universe explicitly: "**there are around 10 lakhs vacancies of teachers in Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA) funded schools in various States**" (368th Report, ¶2.20, p. 14). **So even the round "10 lakh" figure in circulation is not a figure for Indian school teaching posts at large — it is a figure for SSA-funded government/aided schools.** The denominator is never stated when the number is quoted publicly.

Note also the Secretary's phrasing — "a figure which **has been mentioned**". The head of the department describes the national vacancy count as something in circulation rather than something his department produces.

#### 5.1.8 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — RTE section 26 sets a statutory 10% ceiling, and every national figure breaches it

> "**26. Filling up vacancies of teachers.**—The appointing authority, in relation to a school established, owned, controlled or substantially financed by funds provided directly or indirectly by the appropriate Government or by a local authority, shall ensure that **vacancy of teacher in a school under its control shall not exceed ten per cent. of the total sanctioned strength.**"

> Source: *The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009* (Act 35 of 2009), s.26. Retrieved from India Code — **T1.**

Against that ceiling: 15.16% (2023-24) and 14.07% (2024-25) on the Department's own figures; 20.8% / 18.0% / 15.5% at elementary on the PAB figures; **17.1% at KVS and 29.6% at NVS, both of which are schools of the Central Government, i.e. schools where the "appropriate Government" under s.26 is the Union itself.** This is not a target missed. It is a statutory duty, owed by the Union in respect of its own schools, breached by a factor of three at NVS. **No report I retrieved — none of the four Standing Committee reports — cites section 26.** The Committee argues from adequacy and from "the spirit of RTE Act"; it does not invoke the binding numeric provision that its own subject-matter statute contains.

#### 5.1.9 The portal that would hold the answer, and does not resolve

In its Action Taken reply of March 2026 the Department stated:

> "The data regarding vacancy of teachers, as received from States/UTs, is also uploaded in the **PRABANDH portal (https://prabandh.education.gov.in/)** for effective monitoring."

> Source: 373rd Report, p. 85 — **T1.**

**RETRIEVAL FAILURE, and I judge it a substantive one.** As at 2026-08-02, `prabandh.education.gov.in` **does not resolve in DNS** (SERVFAIL against 8.8.8.8; connection failure from two independent network paths). Suggested reasonKind: **not-published** (for the portal-held data specifically), sitting on top of the broader **not-collected** at national level.

#### 5.2.1 WHAT THE RTE ACT ACTUALLY PRESCRIBES — and a correction to the "30:1" that everyone cites

**The RTE Act does not contain the figure 30:1.** I searched the full text of Act 35 of 2009 as amended; the string does not occur, and no provision prescribes a 30:1 primary ratio. What the Schedule (referred to by s.19 and s.25) actually says for classes I–V is a **staffing slab, not a ratio**:

> **THE SCHEDULE, item 1(a) — For first class to fifth class:** Admitted children *Up to Sixty* → **Two** teachers; *Between sixty-one to ninety* → **Three**; *Between Ninety-one to one hundred and twenty* → **Four**; *Between One hundred and twenty-one to two hundred* → **Five**; *Above One hundred and fifty children* → **Five plus one Head-teacher**; ***Above Two hundred children* → Pupil-Teacher Ratio (excluding Head-teacher) shall not exceed forty.**

> **item 1(b) — For sixth class to eighth class:** "(1) At least one teacher per class so that there shall be at least one teacher each for — (i) Science and Mathematics; (ii) Social Studies; (iii) Languages. **(2) At least one teacher for every thirty-five children.**"

> Source: RTE Act 2009, THE SCHEDULE (see sections 19 and 25) — **T1.**

**So: "35:1 for upper primary" is in the Act, verbatim. "30:1 for primary" is not.** The 30 is an *implication* of the first slab (60 children ÷ 2 teachers), and the Act's only explicit primary ratio is a ceiling of **40:1** for schools above 200 children. **The Standing Committee itself misstates its own governing statute** ("the RTE's stipulated Pupil Teacher Ratio of 30:1 for primary schools", 363rd Report ¶3.4.67). Suggested reasonKind for the primary PTR norm: **never-defined** as a ratio in statute; it is an administrative gloss on a slab.

Note also the structure this creates: **the Schedule's binding norm for small schools is a minimum of two teachers per primary school.** On that reading, every one of the ~1 lakh single-teacher schools in §5.3 is in breach of the Schedule, independently of any ratio. That is a cleaner legal test than PTR and no official document I retrieved applies it.

Section 25 gives the compliance clock: "**Within three years** from the date of commencement of this Act, the appropriate Government and the local authority shall ensure that the Pupil-Teacher Ratio, as specified in the Schedule, is maintained **in each school**" — i.e. by **31 March 2013**, school by school, not on average. The Act commenced 1 April 2010 (S.O. 428(E), 16 February 2010).

> Sources: PIB, Ministry of Education, PRID 2161543, 28 August 2025 (2022-23 to 2024-25) — **T1**; UDISE+ 2025-26 release, July 2026 (2025-26 column) — **T3** (relayed; I did not retrieve the PIB page directly, see §5.7).

**The two are not alternative presentations of the same quantity.** "Foundational" spans pre-primary through class II; "Primary" spans I–V. A PTR of 10 and a PTR of 21 are both published for 2024-25-adjacent years and describe different populations. **Any series must pick one structure and stay in it. They must never be spliced.**

#### 5.2.3 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — the PTR series crosses the 2022-23 base shift, its numerator is the re-based quantity, and PTR is NOT named in the Ministry's incomparability disclaimer

The brief's suspicion is correct and it is worse than a suspicion.

**PTR = enrolment ÷ teachers.** Its numerator is enrolment — precisely the quantity that was re-based when UDISE+ moved to individual student-level records in 2022-23 and total enrolment fell from 26.52 crore (2021-22) to 25.18 crore (2022-23) to 24.80 crore (2023-24). See §4 of this drop for the base shift.

**The formula did NOT change — which is precisely why the break passes through undamped.** The published PTR was reproduced from each report's own tables (enrolment by level ÷ teachers by classes taught) and **it reconciles exactly, on both sides of the break — to the published second decimal in 2020-21 and 2021-22.** Worked example, 2023-24 primary: 425,074 (pre-primary & primary) + 3,514,975 (primary only) + 1,081,425 (primary & upper primary) = 5,021,474 teachers; 107,837,711 ÷ 5,021,474 = 21.48 → published 21.

**So the formula, the denominator construction and the double-counting convention are identical before and after 2022-23. Nothing was fixed to absorb the re-basing. The enrolment shock therefore transmits into PTR at full strength.**

**Decomposition of the break-year fall.** Primary enrolment (I–V) fell from 121,842,250 to 112,421,298 — **−9,420,952, −7.73%**, far harder than the −5.07% headline. Primary-teaching teachers rose from 4,656,045 to 4,824,726 (+3.62%). Published primary PTR fell 26.17 → 23.30, i.e. **−2.87 points, −10.96%.**

- Hold teachers at 2021-22, apply the new enrolment: 112,421,298 ÷ 4,656,045 = **24.15** — a fall of 2.02 points, **70% of the total, from the enrolment re-basing alone.**
- Hold enrolment at 2021-22, apply the new teachers: 121,842,250 ÷ 4,824,726 = **25.25** — a fall of 0.92 points.
- Log decomposition: **69.3% of the break-year primary PTR movement is numerator, 30.7% denominator.**

**Roughly seven-tenths of the celebrated improvement in primary PTR in the break year is children being deleted from a database.** A child removed as a duplicate was never in the classroom; removing them improves the measured PTR without a single teacher being appointed or a single child being taught differently.

**And the Ministry's own incomparability disclaimer does not name PTR.** In all four editions retrieved (2022-23, 2023-24 Existing, 2023-24 NEP, 2024-25) the named indicators are exactly the same three: "…not strictly comparable with the previous reports on various educational indicators **such as GER, NER, Dropout rate etc.**" **GER, NER and dropout rate are named. PTR is never named, in any edition.** The list is illustrative ("etc."), but note what the three named indicators have in common — each has either a *population-projection denominator* or a *cohort* structure. **PTR is the one headline indicator whose numerator is the raw re-based enrolment count itself, and it is the one left out.**

Suggested classification for the PTR series: **reporting-base shift at 2022-23, undisclosed for this indicator specifically.** **Do not read 2021-22 → 2022-23 PTR movement as a real change. Hold the series as two segments.** The Ministry's own "2022-23 onwards are comparable to each other" is the correct splice rule.

#### 5.2.4 THE DENOMINATOR MOVED TOO — teacher deduplication is explicit, but what actually shifted was the level ALLOCATION

The brief asks whether teachers were also re-based. **They were, and the Ministry says so in the same paragraph as the student EID:**

> "Through, unique **student and teacher** Identity, credentials of individuals (Student /Teacher) in the education ecosystem can be established more accurately leading to **weeding out of duplicate or ghost entries**…"
> — UDISE+ 2023-24, §1.7, p. 11; identical in 2022-23 and 2024-25 — **T1.**

But the visible effect is not where one would expect:

| | 2021-22 | 2022-23 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Total** teachers | 9,507,123 | 9,483,294 | **−23,829 (−0.25%)** |
| **Sum of the four level-teacher counts** (double-counted) | 11,445,660 | 11,647,574 | **+201,914 (+1.76%)** |
| Primary-teaching teachers | 4,656,045 | 4,824,726 | +3.62% |
| Pre-primary-only teachers | 110,700 | 92,595 | −16.4% |

**FIRST-CLASS FINDING: the total teacher headcount barely moved at the break (−0.25%) while the level-summed count rose 1.76%. Teacher deduplication did not shrink the headcount; what changed was how teachers were reported across levels — proportionally more teachers recorded as spanning multiple levels.** Because a multi-level teacher is counted in full in *every* level they touch, this **inflates every level-specific denominator and therefore depresses every level-specific PTR, independently of the enrolment effect.** This is a second, smaller, entirely undocumented discontinuity. **No UDISE+ report discusses it.**

Note also the largest single-year fall in the whole teacher series — **−189,302 (−1.95%) from 2020-21 to 2021-22 — happened BEFORE the break, not at it.** Whatever the 2022-23 reform did to teacher records, it was not the dominant movement in the teacher series.

#### 5.2.5 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — the DENOMINATOR breaks too, in a DIFFERENT year, because guest teachers were not counted until 2023-24

This is the sharpest item in the section and I have found it stated in only one place — a footnote in a research paper, not in any ministry document:

> "**Note on teacher counts in UDISE 2021-22: … UDISE 2021-22 does not include 'guest teachers', i.e. teachers who are on an ad-hoc appointment against a substantive post; this option is available along with part-time teachers from the 2023-24 cycle onwards. These numbers are not easily available but could be up to 10 to 15% of the government and government-other workforce deployed in a state.**"

> Source: CETE (Centre of Excellence in Teacher Education, Tata Institute of Social Sciences), *Teachers in India in 2021-22: The picture from UDISE+*, Background Paper 1 to the *State of Teachers, Teaching and Teacher Education Report for India 2023*; authors Padma M. Sarangapani, Kamlesh Goyal, Mythili Ramchand, Jyoti Bawane; footnote 2, p. 8. https://tiss.ac.in/uploads/files/SOTTTER-23_BP1_UDISE.pdf — **T2**, retrieved directly.

**So the two sides of the PTR ratio were re-based in consecutive but different years:**

| Year | What moved | Direction of effect on PTR |
|---|---|---|
| **2022-23** | **Numerator**: enrolment re-based to individual student records; ~1.34 crore records removed | PTR falls (spuriously) |
| **2023-24** | **Denominator**: guest and part-time teachers admitted to the collection for the first time | PTR falls (spuriously) |

And the published teacher counts are consistent with exactly this: **94,83,294 (2022-23) → 98,07,600 (2023-24), a jump of 3,24,306 (+3.4%) in the first year guest and part-time teachers could be recorded.** I cannot prove the jump *is* the coverage change — a genuine recruitment increase would look the same — **and I state that limit plainly.** But the coincidence of the coverage change with the largest single-year rise in the series is not something a user of this data can be left unaware of.

**The consequence for the brief's question is unambiguous: the PTR series does cross the break, and it crosses TWO breaks, on opposite sides of the ratio, in consecutive years. There is no continuous national PTR series spanning 2021-22 to 2023-24. Treat 2022-23 and 2023-24 PTR as non-comparable with 2021-22 and with each other.**

Note also what this does to §5.6: **before 2023-24, India's official teacher count excluded a category of teacher that a TISS estimate puts at up to 10–15% of the government workforce.** The pre-2023-24 teacher totals are undercounts of people actually teaching, by an unknown amount.

#### 5.2.6 The independent 2021-22 cross-check, and it reconciles

CETE's own computation from UDISE+ 2021-22 microdata: **14,89,115 schools; 95,07,123 teachers (grades I–XII, excluding pre-primary); 25,57,40,623 students; overall PTR 28:1** (28:1 rural, 28:1 urban). By level: **primary 26:1, upper primary 19:1, secondary 18:1, higher secondary 27:1**; by management, government 28:1 and private 26:1.

**Those level-wise figures match the Department's own 2021-22 column in Table-23 of the 363rd Report exactly (26 / 19 / 18 / 27).** An independent researcher working from the microdata reproduces the ministry's published PTR for 2021-22. **The pre-break PTR is therefore solid and reproducible. It is only across the break that the series fails.** — CETE 2023, §1.2 and §4, **T2.**

#### 5.2.7 The share of schools meeting the RTE PTR norm

The brief asks for this, and it is the right thing to ask for, because s.25 imposes the duty **school by school** and a national mean cannot discharge it. The answer has three parts: **it was published until 2015-16; it has not been published since; and the only post-2015 computation of it was done outside government.**

**That sentence is a category error and should be recorded as one.** Section 25 requires the ratio to be maintained "**in each school**". **A note on the arithmetic of "improvement".** Single-teacher schools fell by 17,347 (−14.7%) across three years. Over the same window the total number of schools fell by about 5,000 and zero-enrolment schools fell by 4,631. **Part of the reduction in single-teacher schools is school closure and consolidation, not teacher deployment.** Neither PIB nor the reports decompose it, and I could not establish the split. The PIB gloss — "Due to the conscious and meaningful governmental interventions there has been consistent decline" — asserts a mechanism the published data does not distinguish from closure.

**The Standing Committee's own reading**, which is the sharpest statement of the problem in any official document I retrieved:

> "The Committee notes with concern that there still exists 1,10,971 single teacher schools in the country. … one teacher managing multiple grades simultaneously not only makes the single teacher overloaded with academic and administrative work but also **result in closure of school operations in case the teacher falls ill or remains absent** … the Committee recommends that such schools should be converted into at least two teacher in a time bound manner."

> — 373rd Report, p. 95, **T1.**

And, going to the heart of the PTR question:

> "The Committee notes that single-teacher schools continue to exist despite years after the passage of the RTE. It notes that **the RTE's stipulated Pupil Teacher Ratio of 30:1 for primary schools and 35:1 for upper primary schools took the focus away from the importance of having one teacher per grade.** Thus, the Committee unequivocally advocates for a new policy of requiring one teacher per grade."

> — 363rd Report, ¶3.4.67, p. 34, **T1.**

**That is the mechanism, stated by a parliamentary committee: a ratio norm is satisfiable by a small school with one teacher and thirty children across five grades. India's PTR looks excellent (§5.2) and ~1 lakh schools have one teacher, and both are true at once because the norm is a ratio and not a coverage rule.**

### 5.4 PROFESSIONALLY QUALIFIED TEACHERS

#### 5.4.1 The statutory deadline and what it required

RTE s.23(2), as amended, is the operative provision. The **original** proviso (Act 35 of 2009) gave five years from commencement, i.e. to **31 March 2015**:

> "Provided that a teacher who, at the commencement of this Act, does not possess minimum qualifications as laid down under sub-section (1), shall acquire such minimum qualifications within a period of **five years**"

The **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (Amendment) Act, 2017 (Act 24 of 2017)** inserted a second proviso, and its drafting is the point:

> "Provided further that **every teacher appointed or in position as on the 31st March, 2015**, who does not possess minimum qualifications as laid down under sub-section (1), shall acquire such minimum qualifications **within a period of four years from the date of commencement of the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (Amendment) Act, 2017 (24 of 2017)**."

> Source: RTE Act 2009 as amended, s.23(2), first and second provisos — **T1** (India Code consolidated text).

The amending clause, verbatim, from the Bill as introduced (**Bill No. 75 of 2017**, printed 5 April 2017, identical to the enacted clause):

> "**1.** (1) This Act may be called the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (Amendment) Act, 2017. (2) **It shall be deemed to have come into force on the 1st day of April, 2015.**
> **2.** In the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009, in section 23, in sub-section (2), **after the proviso, the following proviso shall be inserted**…"

> Source: *The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (Amendment) Bill, 2017*, Bill No. 75 of 2017, as introduced in Lok Sabha — **T1.** India Code footnote to the consolidated Act reads: "Ins. by Act 24 of 2017, s. 2 (**w.e.f. 1-4-2015**)."

**Three things follow that are usually elided.**

**First, the 2017 Act did not substitute or extend the original proviso — it INSERTED a second, free-standing one.** The original five-year proviso remains on the statute book, unamended, its deadline of 31 March 2015 permanently unmet. The new proviso runs four years from a commencement *deemed* to be 1 April 2015, expiring **31 March 2019**. The Act was passed in August 2017, **more than two years after the deadline it was curing had already lapsed** — Lok Sabha 21 July 2017, Rajya Sabha 1 August 2017. **A statutory duty was missed and then retrospectively regularised.**

**Second, the Statement of Objects and Reasons contains NO figure for the number of untrained teachers.** It was read in full. It says only that "the State Governments have not been able to continue the training process for in-service untrained teachers. Hence, the State Governments have requested for extension of the period…" **The "11 lakh" figure often attributed to the SOR is not in it. Do not carry any number as sourced to the SOR.**

**Third, the exercise was costed at ₹453.62 crore** — Financial Memorandum, Bill No. 75 of 2017: "a non-recurring expenditure of **rupees 453.62 crore**… to complete the training of untrained teachers by 31st March, 2019", to be met from Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan allocations on the standard 60:40 / 90:10 / 100% Centre-State shares. **T1.** *For scale: ₹453.62 crore across ~14 lakh teachers is roughly ₹3,240 per teacher trained.*

**DATE CONFLICT, unresolved.** Secondary sources and the standard citation give assent as **9 August 2017**; the Supreme Court's own recital in *Kousik Das* (§5.4.6) states the amendment "was notified on **10th August 2017** by way of a Gazette Notification", and NCTE's later bridge-course eligibility keys off 10 August 2017 as the in-service cut-off. **Assent 9 August / gazette 10 August is the most probable reconciliation, but the gazette copy was not retrieved** (India Code has no standalone entry for the amending Act; `legislative.gov.in/sites/default/files/A2017-24.pdf` does not resolve). Rest on different facts? No — on different *events* being described by the same word. **Use 10 August 2017 where in-service status is the operative test, since that is the date the Court and NCTE apply.**

#### 5.4.1a THE NIOS D.El.Ed. — a course reverse-engineered to fit the residual window

On **22 September 2017** NCTE relaxed its own *Recognition Norms and Procedure* Regulations 2014 to **cut the D.El.Ed. (Open and Distance Learning) programme from two years to 18 months, by subsuming the six-month internship into the 18 months**, and recognised NIOS to deliver it via the MHRD SWAYAM portal. The programme opened **3 October 2017**.

The Supreme Court's characterisation of why 18 months: after the 10 August 2017 notification, "close to around **18-19 months were only remaining**" of the statutory window. **The qualification's duration was set by the deadline, not by the pedagogy.** — *Kousik Das*, 2025 INSC 448, ¶15, **T1.**

**Enrolment:** ~15 lakh enrolled as at 3 October 2017 (PIB, MHRD, "All untrained teachers are to be trained by March, 2019 – Shri Prakash Javadekar", 3 October 2017, **T1**); **13,78,979** confirmed registrations (MHRD *Annual Report 2018-19*, **T1**). Delivery: "Around **12,000 Study Centre Coordinators** & about **54,000 Resource Persons**", plus a dedicated SWAYAM PRABHA channel. Four examination sittings: 31 May–2 June 2018; 25–29 September 2018; December 2018; and "the fourth and last exam was conducted on MARCH 2019."

**FIRST-CLASS FINDING — the outcome was never published, and an enrolment number was put in its place.** No official enrolled → appeared → passed → certified breakdown exists for this programme in any retrieved source. What the MHRD *Annual Report 2018-19* says instead is:

> "Around 14 lakh unqualified teachers from Govt., Govt.-Aided and Private-Unaided Schools were **registered and were trained** in the D.El.Ed. course, which completed on 31st March, 2019. **This is the largest initiative of its kind in the world.**"

**"Registered and were trained" conflates the input with the output.** NIOS plainly holds the result data — it published semester results in May 2019. Suggested reasonKind: **not-published** (outcomes never disaggregated), distinct from not-collected. NIOS annual-report paths return 404.

#### 5.4.1b WHAT HAPPENED AT THE DEADLINE — established, including one clean negative

**(a) Was the deadline extended again? NO — established negatively at T1.** The India Code consolidated text of the RTE Act carries amendment footnotes for Act 30 of 2012 and Act 24 of 2017 only. **Section 23 has been amended once, and once only.** There is no third proviso and no post-2019 amendment. **The deadline was never statutorily extended past 31 March 2019.**

**(b) Dismissal was the stated consequence, in writing, before the amendment even passed.** The MHRD letter of **3 August 2017** to all State/UT Education Secretaries stated that this "will be the **last chance** to acquire the requisite minimum qualifications and that **any untrained teacher would not be allowed to continue in-service beyond 1st April 2019**" (recited by the Supreme Court in *Kousik Das*, ¶4.3, **T1**). PIB reinforced it: the Minister "**categorically said that there will be no further extension for this training programme after 31st March, 2019**" (**T1**).

**(c) FIRST-CLASS FINDING — no count of teachers dismissed, or still untrained, on 1 April 2019 was ever published. In either direction.** The Government declared the backlog cleared using a *registration* number as the proof. Suggested reasonKind: **not-collected** — there is no evidence the enforcement outcome was ever compiled, let alone withheld.

**(d) What actually happened is that the question migrated from administration into seven years of litigation** — over whether the 18-month NIOS qualification the Government itself had created counted at all. See §5.4.6.

#### 5.4.3 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — "trained" and "professionally qualified" are two different published measures and nobody reconciles them

UDISE+ publishes **both**, as adjacent tables, for the same teachers in the same year, and they differ at every level by 2.3 to 5.1 percentage points. **No definition distinguishing the two appears in the tables themselves**, and I did not retrieve a Concepts-and-Definitions entry that states the difference. The plain reading is that "trained" is the broader category (any recognised training, including in-service and the NIOS D.El.Ed. route) and "professionally qualified" is the narrower one (holding the qualification prescribed by NCTE for the post held) — **but I could not establish this from a ministry source, and the two are used interchangeably in secondary coverage.**

**The consequence is direct: the compliance question under RTE s.23 is a question about "professionally qualified", and the figure usually quoted in public is the flattering one, "trained" (91–93%), rather than the operative one (87.7–90.4%).** Suggested reasonKind for the distinction: **never-defined**.

#### 5.4.3a THE CONTESTED QUANTITY — 87.7% or 45.7%? Both are computed from UDISE+, and they rest on DIFFERENT DEFINITIONS, not different facts

This is the sharpest disagreement in the section and it must be carried as a dispute, not resolved by preferring a number.

| | Ministry (UDISE+ 2024-25, Table 4.19) | CETE/TISS (UDISE+ 2021-22 microdata) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary-grade teachers with the qualification | **87.7% "professionally qualified"** | **45.72% hold the *appropriate* qualification** |

**The strongest case for the Ministry's 87.7%:** it counts a teacher as professionally qualified if they hold *a* recognised professional teaching qualification. A B.Ed. holder teaching class III has completed a full professional teacher-education degree; they are not an untrained person. On any ordinary meaning of "professionally qualified", they are.

**The strongest case for CETE's 45.7%:** the NCTE prescribes **D.El.Ed. / B.El.Ed. or equivalent** as the qualification for teaching classes I–V. **B.Ed. is not a valid qualification for primary teaching**, and this is not a matter of opinion — it has been affirmed judicially. CETE puts it directly: "The requirement that D.El.Ed and equivalent degrees such as B.El.Ed or BTC are the only valid professional qualification for primary school teaching was recently reasserted by the High Court of Rajasthan and the Supreme Court, which set aside other professional qualifications such as B.Ed to be valid for this level." **That is *Devesh Sharma*, 2023 INSC 704, 11 August 2023, established at T1 and set out at §5.4.6. A teacher whose degree does not qualify them for the class they are teaching is not "qualified" for the purpose the statute cares about.**

**Verdict: different definitions of the same word, not different facts.** They are reconcilable and CETE gives the reconciliation: 45.72% hold D.El.Ed./B.El.Ed.; **a further 30.44% hold B.Ed. or equivalent** (≈76% holding something); and **about 10% of primary and middle school teachers, and 6% of secondary school teachers, hold NO professional qualification at all.** By management, appropriate-qualification rates at primary: government 60.5%, aided 68%, social/tribal/labour-department government schools 66.45%, **Kendriya Vidyalayas 24%** (55% hold B.Ed. instead), private recognised 22% (43% hold B.Ed.), unrecognised 27.5%, madrasas 15%.

At other levels, where B.Ed. **is** the appropriate qualification: **upper primary 57%** appropriately qualified; **secondary 79%** (government 83%, aided 84%, private recognised 73%).

**And note where the unqualified are: "70% of all teachers with no professional qualification are in the private sector."** — CETE 2023, §4.4 and Finding 12, **T2.**

**The instrument should carry BOTH series and never merge them.** The Ministry's is the wider measure and is published annually; CETE's is the statutorily operative measure and exists for one year only. **The gap between them — roughly 42 percentage points at primary — is the size of the B.Ed.-teaching-primary population, and it is a policy fact, not a measurement error.**

#### 5.4.4 The pre-primary hole

**Fewer than half of India's pre-primary teachers — 48.2% — hold the prescribed professional qualification**, and only 53.3% are trained at all. This sits directly against NEP 2020's foundational-stage priority and against the Standing Committee's observation:

> "The Committee noted **a difference in eligibility criteria for teachers and Anganwadi workers to teach a three-year old child.** It recommended that the minimum qualification must be 12th grade plus a two-year diploma degree."
> — 368th Report, as summarised by PRS Legislative Research, *Standing Committee Report Summary: Review of National Council for Teacher Education and Initiatives Taken to Support Teacher Training*, 31 August 2025, https://prsindia.org/files/policy/policy_committee_reports/SCR_Summary_Review_of_NCTE_and_Teacher_Training.pdf — **T2.**

**Note the structural point: RTE s.23 binds teachers of classes I–VIII. Pre-primary is outside the RTE age band (6–14) entirely, so the qualification duty does not reach the level with the worst qualification deficit.** That is a gap in the statute, not in compliance with it.

#### 5.4.6 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — "professionally qualified" is not a legally coherent category

Two Supreme Court judgments, both retrieved and read directly, have left the published statistic measuring a set that does not correspond to any legal status.

**(i) *Devesh Sharma v. Union of India*, 2023 INSC 704, Civil Appeal No. 5068 of 2023, Aniruddha Bose and Sudhanshu Dhulia JJ, 11 August 2023 — T1.** Operative paragraph:

> "**37.** Consequently, the Appeals are dismissed and the judgement dated 25.11.2021 of the Rajasthan High Court is upheld. **The notification dated 28.06.2018 is hereby quashed and set aside.**"

The quashed NCTE notification of 28 June 2018 had made **B.Ed. holders eligible for primary (classes I–V) posts** subject to a six-month bridge course. **B.Ed. is therefore not a qualifying credential for classes I–V**; the Court reasoned from Article 21A that B.Ed. holders "did not pass the basic professional threshold … required for teaching students of the primary classes."

By a subsequent order of **8 April 2024**, the Court declined to unseat B.Ed. holders already appointed and directed NCTE to design a bridge course. The resulting **six-month NIOS bridge course** is live and its own portal states the terms: eligibility runs to those appointed **28 June 2018 – 11 August 2023**; "**Failure to complete the course within timeframe given by the concerned educational authorities will invalidate the appointment of such candidates**"; and "**This Bridge course will not be valid for the teachers seeking subsequent employments as primary teachers**" — it cures the individual appointment, not the credential. **Registration windows on that portal run to 30 August 2026. The remediation is still open as this is written.** — **T1** (programme portal).

**(ii) *Kousik Das & Ors. v. State of West Bengal & Ors.*, 2025 INSC 448, judgment 4 April 2025 — T1.** Held that teachers **in employment as on 10 August 2017** who completed the 18-month NIOS D.El.Ed. (ODL) **before 1 April 2019** hold a valid diploma, at par with the two-year D.El.Ed., for further appointment and promotion; the High Court judgment to the contrary was quashed. **The validity is conditional on in-service status at 10 August 2017 — the 18-month qualification is not a general-purpose credential.** The West Bengal Board had litigated on the ground that the 18-month NIOS programme "is **not at all identical** to" the regular two-year D.El.Ed.

**The consequence.** As at 2026 there are at least **four legally distinct populations that UDISE+ scores identically as "professionally qualified"**:

| | Population | Legal status for primary teaching |
|---|---|---|
| i | Two-year D.El.Ed. holders | Valid |
| ii | 18-month NIOS D.El.Ed. holders **in service at 10 Aug 2017** | Valid (*Kousik Das*) |
| iii | 18-month NIOS D.El.Ed. holders **not** in service at that date | **Not valid** — Bihar was terminating TRE-3 appointees on this ground in 2026 |
| iv | B.Ed. holders in classes I–V appointed 2018–2023 | **Conditional** — appointment stands only if the NIOS bridge course is completed |

**So the 87.7% at §5.4.2 is not a compliance rate. It is a count of people holding some recognised teaching credential, of whom an unquantified share hold one that does not authorise the class they teach.** This is the same defect CETE identified from the other direction at §5.4.3a, and the two findings are the same finding: **UDISE+'s "professionally qualified" systematically over-states RTE s.23 compliance at primary level by a margin nobody has measured.** Suggested reasonKind: **never-defined**.

*(Not established, and not carried: the "1,000 fake B.Ed. colleges" / mass de-recognition line. No NCTE order, no consolidated count and no Supreme Court matter was retrieved; every circulating number is T4/T5. NCTE does not publish a consolidated national register of recognition withdrawals — a further* **not-published** *absence.)*

### 5.5 RECRUITMENT AND THE EXAM-INTEGRITY DIMENSION

This section is deliberately narrower than the others: I report only what I verified from primary documents I retrieved directly. Where the record is thinner, I say so rather than assembling a survey of press reports.

#### 5.5.1 THE ANCHOR CASE — the Supreme Court annulled an entire state teacher-recruitment panel

**State of West Bengal v. Baishakhi Bhattacharyya (Chatterjee) & Others**, judgment of **3 April 2025**, **2025 INSC 437**, per **Sanjiv Khanna, CJI** (Civil Appeal arising out of SLP (C) No. 9586 of 2024 and roughly 200 connected matters).

The Court **upheld** the Calcutta High Court's judgment of **22 April 2024**, which had *en bloc* set aside the **2016 selection process** of the West Bengal Central School Service Commission for **Assistant Teachers for Classes IX–X and XI–XII, and non-teaching Group C and Group D staff**:

> "**we uphold the impugned judgment cancelling en bloc / entire selection process** but have made certain modifications in the directions issued by the High Court."
> — ¶51 — **T1**, judgment text retrieved and read directly.

The findings of fact the Court adopted are the reason the "separate the tainted from the untainted" route was unavailable:

> "(vi) OMR sheets had been destroyed without the scanned mirror images being preserved in the server of SSC — (vii) SSC had provided scanned OMR sheets to RTI applicants in the year 2018 till 2023 claiming that such OMR sheets were from its database although, **CBI did not find any OMR sheets in the server of SSC** — (viii) **appointments higher than the declared vacancies had been given in respect of all 4 categories** — (ix) appointments had been given to **persons who were not even in the panel** — (x) appointments had been given to **persons who submitted blank OMR sheets** — (xi) appointments had been given persons **after expiry of the panel** — (xii) persons placed lower in rank had been given appointment in preference to persons placed higher — (xiii) **merit list containing the marks obtained by the respective candidates had never been published** — (xv) **total beneficiaries of the illegalities are yet to be identified** — (xvi) SSC had applied for permission to **create supernumerary posts to accommodate the illegal appointees** — (xvii) Recruitment Rules governing the four categories **had never been adhered to either in letter or spirit**"

The Commission had "directed the destruction of the physical OMR sheets through its executive decision dated **22.07.2019**." The CBI investigation was ordered by a Single Judge on **15.02.2022**; four FIRs followed.

**The doctrinal point matters for the instrument.** The Court's own line of authority (*Joginder Pal*, *K. Shyam Kumar*, *Vanshika Yadav*) requires every effort to segregate tainted from untainted, and holds en bloc cancellation "incompatible with law" where segregation is possible. **Segregation was impossible here only because the evidence had been destroyed by the recruiting authority itself.** The remedy — mass annulment — fell on candidates the Court did not find to have done anything wrong; it directed that non-tainted candidates may re-sit "with age relaxation and other concessions", and ordered a fresh selection process (¶49).

**Reading for §5.1: this is one mechanism by which vacancies persist. A cohort of secondary and higher-secondary teaching posts in a large state was filled, then unfilled by court order nine years later, and the posts returned to the vacancy pool.** Note the interaction with §5.1.5: West Bengal's *secondary* sanctioned strength is one of the largest in the PAB annexure (1,47,888 in 2023-24).

**On the number in circulation:** the judgment as retrieved does not state a headline count of cancelled appointments; the widely-quoted figure of **~25,753** does not appear in the text I read. **I therefore do not carry it.** What is established is the *scope*: the entire 2016 panel across four categories. **If a count is needed, it must be sourced separately and graded on its own.**

#### 5.5.2 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — the central anti-paper-leak law does not cover state teacher recruitment

The **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024** (**Act No. 1 of 2024**, assented **12 February 2024**) is the legislative response to examination fraud. Its reach is set by s.2(k) read with the Schedule, and the Schedule is short:

> **THE SCHEDULE [See section 2(k)] — ANY EXAMINATION CONDUCTED BY—**
> **1.** Union Public Service Commission. **2.** Staff Selection Commission. **3.** Railway Recruitment Boards. **4.** Institute of Banking Personnel Selection. **5.** Ministries or Departments of the Central Government and their attached and subordinate offices for recruitment of staff. **6.** National Testing Agency. **7.** Such other authority as may be notified by the Central Government.

> Source: *The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024*, Act No. 1 of 2024, Schedule, p. 9 — **T1**, retrieved and read directly.

**Not in the Schedule: any State School Service Commission. Any State Public Service Commission. Any state Teacher Eligibility Test. Any state teacher recruitment board (BPSC, REET, UPBEB, and the rest). And not the Central Board of Secondary Education, which conducts the CTET.** The Act reaches teacher recruitment only where the Central Government is itself the recruiter — i.e. the ~0.2% of schools in §5.1.7 — unless a body is separately notified under item 7.

Parliament said so openly in the Statement of Objects and Reasons:

> "**The Bill shall serve as a model draft for States to adopt at their discretion.** This would aid States in preventing the criminal elements from disrupting conduct of their State level public examinations."
> — Statement of Objects and Reasons, ¶4, Dr. Jitendra Singh, 29 January 2024 — **T1.**

**So: the largest teacher-recruitment fraud in Indian history was a state process; the Union's answer to examination fraud, enacted six weeks before the Calcutta High Court judgment and fourteen months before the Supreme Court's, does not apply to state processes and expressly leaves them to state discretion.** That is not a gap in drafting — it is the constitutional division of §5.1.1 reappearing as a gap in enforcement. **The same federal boundary that prevents the Union from counting teacher vacancies prevents it from policing teacher recruitment.** Record it as the structural finding that ties this section together.

### 5.6 CONTRACT, PARA AND GUEST TEACHERS

#### 5.6.1 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — the data IS collected, for every teacher in India, and is never published

The brief asks whether any national figure exists. **It does not. But the reason is not that the data is absent — it is that the data is collected exhaustively and withheld from publication.**

The **UDISE+ Data Capture Format** records, for every individual teacher record in the country, field **3.3.12 Nature of Appointment**, with the code list given verbatim in the DCF instructions:

> "[For Sl No 3.3.12] **Nature of Appointment: 1-Regular, 2-Contract, 3-Part-Time/Guest**"

And at school level the DCF separately enumerates teaching staff under two headings:

> "(a) **REGULAR TEACHING STAFF** / **NON-REGULAR TEACHING STAFF** (**Contract, Part Time, Guest Teacher, Para Teacher etc.**)"

Both fields are present and identically worded in the DCF for **2022-23** and for **2025-26** — i.e. this has been collected continuously across the period, on both sides of the student-level reform.

> Source: UDISE+ Data Capture Format (school and teacher schedules), Department of School Education & Literacy, editions 2022-23 and 2025-26, items 3.3.12 / 3.3.13 and the Teaching Staff block — **T1.**

**So: for each of the 1.01 crore teachers in UDISE+ 2024-25 the system holds a regular/contract/guest flag, and no UDISE+ report, PIB release, Economic Survey chapter, or parliamentary reply that I retrieved publishes the national regular-vs-non-regular share.** Suggested reasonKind: **not-published** (explicitly NOT not-collected — this distinction is the finding).

The Standing Committee has been asking for exactly this and not receiving it. It sought "the details of appointments of permanent and ad-hoc/contractual teachers, separately since 1st December 2023 till 31 December 2024 … State/UT-wise and year-wise" (363rd Report ¶3.4.29) and received the Concurrent-List reply quoted at §5.1.1 — **the same non-answer that covers vacancies covers contract status.**

#### 5.6.1a THE ONLY NATIONAL CONTRACT-TEACHER ESTIMATE THAT EXISTS — and it is not the government's

CETE computed from UDISE+ 2021-22 what the Ministry has never published:

- **11% of government school teachers are contractual, overall.**
- **16% of government PRIMARY school teachers are contractual** — "indicating that proportionally more posts in primary schools have been filled by contractual teachers."
- **5,18,111 contractual teachers in total, of whom 4,68,820 — 90% — are in rural schools.**
- **Part-time teachers: 1% of teachers nationally.** Delhi is the outlier: 14% of the state's teaching workforce and **25% of its government teachers** are part-time, 72% of them women.
- State shares of government teachers on contract: **Meghalaya 69%, Jharkhand 54%, Arunachal Pradesh 53%, DNH&DD 47%, Mizoram 37%, Sikkim 37%, Chandigarh 35%, Uttar Pradesh 25%, Assam 24%, Tripura 19%.** 19 states/UTs exceed the 11% national level. In Arunachal Pradesh (61%), Jharkhand (77%) and Meghalaya (53%), more than half the contractual teachers are in primary schools.

> Source: CETE 2023, *Teachers in India in 2021-22*, Background Paper 1, §3.3 "Employment terms" and Finding 9 — **T2.**
> *Denominator, stated: government-school teachers in UDISE+ 2021-22. Not all teachers; private-sector employment terms are not comparable.*

**The dedicated paper gives the full accounting and a trend.** CETE, *Public and Private Sector Contract Teachers in India: An Analytical Research Paper*, Background Paper 3 to SOTTTER 2023, lead author Padma M. Sarangapani — **commissioned by the UNESCO Teacher Task Force in 2023**; the parent report was released by **Sanjay Kumar, IAS, Secretary, Ministry of Education, on 18 January 2024**. **T2.**

**Full denominators, UDISE+ 2021-22:** total teachers all managements **9,507,123**; state-government-department schools **4,681,968**; government-aided **796,631**; other-government (societies, army, railways) **200,478** → combined public **5,679,077**. **Contractual teachers within these: 585,698** = 518,111 (state-government schools) + 20,080 (quasi-government societies) + 47,507 (government-aided). Hence **518,111 ÷ 4,681,968 = 11.1%.**

**Feminisation of the contract workforce:** **55% of contractual government teachers are women** (282,544 F / 235,567 M) against **44% female in the overall government teaching workforce**. In government-*aided* schools the contract workforce is 59% female; in aided primary and upper-primary schools **80% female** (9,466 of 11,786). **The contract tier is more female than the regular tier, at every level of aggregation.**

**Qualification of contract teachers:** 2011 to 2014-15, **41–45%** of contractual teachers lacked the RTE/NCTE-mandated qualification; **2017-18: ~30% of government contractual teachers had no professional qualification.** For 2021-22 **the cross-tab could not be computed at all from the public data** — which is §5.6.1 restated: the field exists, the release does not carry it.

**THE TREND — and why it must not be read as improvement.** BP3 reports contractualisation falling **from 13% in 2013-14 to 11% in 2021-22**. **BP3 itself says the fall is substantially reclassification, not regularisation:** the sharp declines in **Madhya Pradesh, Bihar and Delhi** came from extending contracts from two-to-three years to "until retirement age", so the teachers **re-code from UDISE+ category 2 to category 1 while remaining on lower salaries and outside government-servant status.** BP3's own term for the result is "**invisible contract, quasi-regular teachers**", which it says "seems to be persisting in the system."

**So the 13% → 11% series is a definitional migration between two codes, running asynchronously across states. Do not treat it as continuous, and do not read it as a decline in contractualisation.**

**CETE's own warning about this number is as important as the number, and it is the "never-defined" finding in its exact form:**

> "UDISE provides information on the 'nature of appointment' of teachers, and notes: 'The appointment of a teacher may be on a regular basis, on contract, or as part-time/guest teacher'. **Field interaction suggests that the definition of these categories varies contextually — from state to state as well as between government and non-government/private managements.** Moreover, both field data and PLFS indicate that **there are teachers in the system with no formal contract — and are likely employed based on verbal agreement on terms between employer and teacher. Given this, the picture from UDISE+ on employment terms/appointment types is very limited.**"

And the sting in "regular": "at least the term 'regular' is used to indicate employment until retirement — although based on interactions with states, **this does not mean that the teacher is a government servant** — their contracts may be for the entire period until retirement, but not carrying any extensive benefits of being a government employee."

**So the field exists, the codes exist, and the codes mean different things in different states. Suggested reasonKind for the contract-teacher share: never-defined (the categories) compounded by not-published (the aggregate).** Carry the 11% as the single best available national estimate for 2021-22, at T2, with the definitional caveat attached — and do not extend it to other years.

**Remember also §5.2.5: guest teachers were not in the UDISE+ collection at all until 2023-24.** The 11% therefore excludes an entire category of non-regular teacher, and understates non-regular employment by an unknown margin.

#### 5.6.2 What the Committee says the practice is

> "The Committee notes that this continuous process of recruitment of permanent/regular teachers has **almost been stopped** and recruitment of contractual teachers is being opted by the State/UT Governments during recent years … The contractual appointments of teachers **undermines the Constitutional provision reservation in Government jobs to SC, ST, OBC, EWS, PwD etc.**"
> — 363rd Report, ¶3.4.30, **T1.**

> "The dual system of regular teachers and contractual teachers creates a system of different pay for equal work, which goes against the spirit of the constitutional guarantee of equal pay for equal work. … The Committee notes that **the NEP 2020, unlike the draft version released in 2019, does not make any unequivocal statement on discontinuing the practice of hiring contract teachers.** The Committee therefore recommends that the Department direct the utilisation of Samagra Shiksha funds by states towards the hiring of regular teachers alone, or the regularisation of contractual teachers."
> — 363rd Report, ¶3.4.66, **T1.**

**That last point is checkable and load-bearing: a commitment present in the 2019 draft NEP was absent from the 2020 final NEP.** It is a documented policy retreat on contract teaching, identified by a parliamentary committee. (The draft-vs-final comparison itself I did not retrieve; the claim is the Committee's, at **T1** as a statement *of* the Committee.)

## ABSENCES

**A-1 · No national teacher-vacancy statistic — `not-collected`.** The Department states in writing that vacancy data "are maintained by the respective State governments/Union Territory administrations"; the Standing Committee had to ask it, in 2025, to "seek data from the State/UT Governments and maintain it centrally." No defined, published, recurring national vacancy figure exists. What circulates are two ad-hoc aggregates prepared on request. *(§5.1.1)*

**A-2 · Two irreconcilable official national vacancy figures for 2023-24 — `never-defined`.** 8,46,647 (PAB annexure) vs 9,59,148 (DoSEL to Committee); secondary sanctioned posts differ by 6.0 lakh. Neither document defines its levels or its school universe, so the difference cannot be attributed. **Carry as a range or not at all.** *(§5.1.4)*

**A-3 · Sanctioned teaching posts have no published definition — `never-defined`.** The base moves +10.4% in a single year, with secondary +60.3%, undisclosed and unexplained. Every vacancy *rate* rests on it. *(§5.1.5)*

**A-4 · PRABANDH portal data — `not-published`.** The Department names it as where state-reported vacancy data is uploaded; it is a login-gated administrative MIS that has never exposed a public teacher-vacancy output, and as at 2026-08-02 the domain does not resolve. *(§5.1.9)*

**A-5 · Share of schools complying with the RTE PTR norm — `not-published` (post-2015-16).** Published annually by DISE/NUEPA through 2015-16, then discontinued. UDISE+ holds school-level enrolment and teacher counts for every school and has never published it. *(§5.2.8)*

**A-6 · National contract/para/guest-teacher share — `not-published`, compounded by `never-defined`.** UDISE+ collects "Nature of Appointment: 1-Regular, 2-Contract, 3-Part-Time/Guest" for **every individual teacher**, and separately counts regular vs non-regular teaching staff at school level. No national aggregate is published. Additionally the categories mean different things in different states, and teachers with no formal contract at all are recorded somewhere in them. *(§5.6.1, §5.6.1a)*

**A-7 · Zero-teacher schools — `not-published`.** UDISE+ publishes zero-*enrolment* schools; no zero-*teacher* count appears in any edition. *(§5.3)*

**A-8 · "Trained" vs "professionally qualified" — `never-defined`.** Both are published, for the same teachers in the same year, differing by 2–5 points at every level. Neither term appears in the *Concepts and Definitions* annexe of any UDISE+ edition, which defines sixteen other indicators and stops. *(§5.4.3)*

**A-9 · Single-teacher schools and RTE-PTR compliance, 2016-17 to 2021-22 — `not-published`.** DISE stopped publishing after 2015-16; UDISE+ did not begin until 2022-23. A six-year hole in the two sharpest deployment indicators, spanning most of T1. *(§5.3)*

**A-10 · Decomposition of the fall in single-teacher schools — `not-collected`.** How much is teacher deployment and how much is school closure/consolidation is not distinguished by any source, while PIB attributes the whole of it to "conscious and meaningful governmental interventions." *(§5.3)*

**A-11 · PTR omitted from the incomparability disclaimer — `never-defined` (scope of the disclaimer).** All four retrieved editions name GER, NER and Dropout rate; none names PTR, whose numerator is the re-based enrolment count itself. *(§5.2.3)*

**A-12 · The 2023-24 zero-enrolment spike — `not-published` (explanation).** 12,954 schools with no students and 31,981 teachers posted to them, up 26% and 19% year-on-year, then collapsing. No source explains it. *(§5.3)*

**A-13 · Pre-2021-22 teacher vacancies — `not-collected`.** No UPA-baseline vacancy figure was established at any grade. **Do not construct one.** *(§5.7)*

**A-14 · State teacher-recruitment exams outside the Public Examinations Act 2024 — a coverage absence, not a data absence.** Recorded here because it explains A-1's persistence: the same federal boundary that keeps the Union from counting teacher vacancies keeps it from policing teacher recruitment. *(§5.5.2)*

**A-15 · NIOS D.El.Ed. 2017–19 outcomes — `not-published`.** Enrolment was published repeatedly (~15 lakh, then 13,78,979). No enrolled → appeared → passed → certified breakdown exists in any retrieved source, though NIOS published semester results in May 2019 and plainly holds the data. The MHRD Annual Report substitutes "registered and were trained" for a completion count. *(§5.4.1a)*

**A-16 · Number of teachers dismissed, or still untrained, on 1 April 2019 — `not-collected`.** The Government stated in writing that no untrained teacher "would not be allowed to continue in-service beyond 1st April 2019", then declared the backlog cleared using a registration number. **No count exists in either direction.** *(§5.4.1b)*

**A-17 · "Professionally qualified" does not correspond to any legal status — `never-defined`.** Four legally distinct populations are scored identically; B.Ed. holders teaching classes I–V are counted as qualified although *Devesh Sharma* (11 Aug 2023) holds they are not eligible. The published statistic over-states RTE s.23 compliance at primary by an unmeasured margin. *(§5.4.6)*

**A-18 · Trained-teacher share of CONTRACT teachers — `not-published` (post-2015-16).** DISE published it separately (54–67% against 79–82% for regular teachers). UDISE+ collects both fields and publishes neither cross-tab; CETE could not compute it for 2021-22 from the public release. *(§5.4.2, §5.6.1a)*

**A-19 · Untrained-teacher stock: three official figures, three unstated universes — `never-defined`.** 5.12 lakh (govt+aided, Mar 2015) vs ~15 lakh (all-management, Oct 2017) vs 13.79 lakh registered (AR 2018-19). Not errors — but no source states its universe alongside its number. *(series candidate 18)*

**A-20 · NCTE recognition withdrawals — `not-published`.** No consolidated national register of de-recognised teacher-education institutions in any retrievable form. Every circulating count is T4/T5 and none is carried. *(§5.4.6)*

**A-21 · Ministry of Education document corpus, mid-2026 — a retrievability regression worth logging in its own right.** `education.gov.in` migrated to a client-rendered SPA and its entire legacy `/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/` tree — annual reports, statistics, and **the whole pre-2025 parliamentary-annexure corpus** — now returns 404 on every path. Combined with `dsel.education.gov.in`, `udiseplus.gov.in`, `pib.gov.in`, `indiacode.nic.in`, `eparlib.sansad.in` and `prabandh.education.gov.in` being unreachable during this work, **the primary record for this section was reconstructed almost entirely from third-party archives.** This is not a finding about teachers, but it conditions the grade of everything above and will worsen. *(§5.7)*

## LEDGER CANDIDATES

*Suggested by the material; no records authored.*

**L-cand-1 · RTE teacher-qualification deadline lapses and is retrospectively cured.** Range **2015-03-31 → 2019-03-31**. Term: the statutory five-year window for in-service teachers to acquire minimum qualifications expired 31 March 2015 unmet; the RTE (Amendment) Act 2017 (Act 24 of 2017, assent 9 August / gazette 10 August 2017 — see the date conflict at §5.4.1) inserted a second proviso creating a fresh four-year window for teachers in position as on 31 March 2015, ending 31 March 2019. **A statutory duty was missed and then legislatively regularised more than two years after the fact.** *(§5.4.1)*

**L-cand-2 · The RTE section 26 ten-per-cent vacancy ceiling, breached continuously and never invoked.** Range **2010-04-01 → open**. Term: s.26 binds the appointing authority for every government or substantially-funded school to keep teacher vacancies below 10% of sanctioned strength. Measured national rates are 14–15%; KVS 17.1% and NVS 29.6% as at 31 December 2025, in schools where the Union is itself the "appropriate Government". **No Standing Committee report cites the provision.** *(§5.1.8)*

**L-cand-3 · The single-teacher school and the ratio norm.** Range **2013-03-31 → open**. Term: RTE s.25 required the Schedule's PTR to be maintained "in each school" within three years of commencement, i.e. by 31 March 2013. The Schedule's binding norm for a small primary school is **two teachers**, not a ratio. Roughly 1 lakh schools have one teacher; the Department answers the school-level duty with a national mean of 25:1. *(§5.2.1, §5.2.11, §5.3)*

**L-cand-4 · UDISE+ discontinues the RTE compliance measure.** Range **2015-16 → open**. Term: the share of schools breaching the RTE PTR norm was published annually by DISE/NUEPA through 2015-16 and has not been published since, although the underlying school-level data is now collected more completely than ever. *(§5.2.8)*

**L-cand-5 · The Department declines to maintain a national teacher-vacancy statistic; the Committee asks twice.** Range **2025-03-24 → 2026-03-16**. Term: the 363rd Report recommends the Department "seek data from the State/UT Governments and maintain it centrally"; the 373rd Action Taken reply repeats the Concurrent-List position and points to a portal instead. *(§5.1.1, §5.1.9)*

**L-cand-6 · Supreme Court annuls an entire state teacher-recruitment panel.** Range **2016 → 2025-04-03**. Term: *State of West Bengal v. Baishakhi Bhattacharyya*, 2025 INSC 437, 3 April 2025, upholding en bloc cancellation of the WBSSC 2016 selection for Assistant Teachers IX–X and XI–XII and Groups C and D. Segregation of tainted from untainted was impossible because the recruiting authority destroyed the OMR sheets by executive decision dated 22 July 2019. *(§5.5.1)*

**L-cand-7 · Parliament's anti-paper-leak law excludes state teacher recruitment.** Range **2024-02-12 → open**. Term: the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act 2024 Schedule covers UPSC, SSC, RRBs, IBPS, central ministries and NTA only; the Statement of Objects and Reasons states the Bill "shall serve as a model draft for States to adopt at their discretion." *(§5.5.2)*

**L-cand-8 · Guest teachers enter the national teacher count.** Range **2023-24**. Term: guest and part-time teachers became recordable in UDISE+ only from the 2023-24 cycle; before that the official national teacher count excluded a category estimated at up to 10–15% of the government workforce. A coverage change to the denominator of every PTR, undisclosed in any UDISE+ report. *(§5.2.5)*

**L-cand-9 · Contract teaching becomes the standing arrangement in the Union's own schools.** Range **2020-21 → 2026-03-31**. Term: KVS contractual teacher engagements rise from 3,260 to 7,909 and NVS from 2,403 to 3,437 while vacancy rates rise in both; the Committee directs regular appointment "not later than 31st March, 2026" and the position as at 31 December 2025 shows the direction unmet. *(§5.6.3)*

**L-cand-10 · The NIOS mass-training programme.** Range **2017-10-03 → 2019-03-31**. Term: after NCTE relaxed its own regulations on 22 September 2017 to compress the D.El.Ed. (ODL) from two years to 18 months — a duration the Supreme Court records was set by the ~18–19 months remaining in the statutory window — NIOS enrolled ~14 lakh in-service untrained teachers via SWAYAM, at a budgeted ₹453.62 crore, across four examination sittings. **Outcomes were never published.** *(§5.4.1a)*

**L-cand-11 · The Supreme Court removes B.Ed. as a primary-teaching qualification, and the remediation is still running.** Range **2018-06-28 → 2026-08-30 (open)**. Term: *Devesh Sharma v. Union of India*, 2023 INSC 704, 11 August 2023, quashed the NCTE notification of 28 June 2018; the order of 8 April 2024 spared sitting appointees conditional on a six-month NIOS bridge course whose registration windows remain open to 30 August 2026, with non-completion invalidating the appointment. *(§5.4.6)*

**L-cand-12 · The 18-month diploma is held valid — but only for those already teaching.** Range **2017-08-10 → 2025-04-04**. Term: *Kousik Das v. State of West Bengal*, 2025 INSC 448, 4 April 2025, held the 18-month NIOS D.El.Ed. at par with the two-year diploma **for teachers in employment as on 10 August 2017 who completed before 1 April 2019** — and only for them. Seven and a half years after the programme closed, the credential it conferred was adjudicated conditional. *(§5.4.6)*

**L-cand-13 · The Shiksha Mitra absorption is quashed in the same fortnight the untrained-teacher deadline is extended.** Range **2014 → 2017-07-25**. Term: *State of U.P. v. Anand Kumar Yadav*, 25 July 2017, set aside the 2014 Rules absorbing ~1.78 lakh Shiksha Mitras as Assistant Teachers and quashed the consequential orders — between the Lok Sabha's passage of the RTE Amendment Bill (21 July) and the Rajya Sabha's (1 August). **T3 — figure and holding to be confirmed against the judgment.** *(§5.5.4)*
