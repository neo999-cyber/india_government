## RTE Act §12(1)(c) — the 25% private-school quota: seats, non-implementation, and reimbursement arrears

**Bottom line up front:** there is **no published national series of "seats notified vs seats filled"** under §12(1)(c), and the Government of India does not maintain one. The Ministry of Education's own parliamentary answers report a *cumulative stock of children being reimbursed for*, not an annual flow of admissions, and they carry **no seats-available denominator at all**. The only genuine national seats-vs-filled measurement ever made was the DISE-based exercise for 2012-13/2013-14. Everything after that is state-by-state, patchy, and denominator-inconsistent. That absence is itself the most robust finding in this brief.

## (c) Reimbursement: claims, approvals, and the arrears

### C1. The central mechanism (T1, retrieved)

From LS 17/S6 UQ 959 (26.07.2021), `.../annex/176/AU959.pdf?source=lsapps`, and LS 18/S2 UQ 2160 (05.08.2024):

- §12(2) reimbursement is funded under **Samagra Shiksha**, at **per-child cost notified by the State**, for **Classes I–VIII**, "**subject to a maximum ceiling of 20% of the total Annual Work Plan and Budget approved by the Government of India for a State/UT under the Scheme**."
- Sharing: **60:40** Centre:State generally; **90:10** for the 8 North-Eastern states, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand and UT of J&K; **100% central** for UTs without legislature.
- Sequence: **States pay first from their own funds**, then claim in their AWP&B; the PAB approves an estimate; release is conditional on utilisation certificates, expenditure statements, audit reports, **release of the commensurate state share**, and budget availability.
- Critically: "**The Central share is released to State/UTs based on approved funding pattern and not intervention wise**." **The Centre therefore does not know, and cannot report, how much money actually reached schools under §12(1)(c).** Approval ≠ release ≠ payment to schools.

### C5. The most important methodological finding in this brief

The July 2026 Karnataka reply (LS 18/S8 UQ 1260) presents this table, labelled "seats earmarked" and "actual admissions":

| Year | "Total number of seats" | "Actual number of admissions" | Sanctioned (₹ cr) | Reimbursed (₹ cr) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-22 | 446,304 | 446,304 | 199.98 | 199.98 |
| 2022-23 | 160,810 | 160,810 | 191.80 | 191.80 |
| 2023-24 | 345,057 | 345,057 | 199.99 | 199.99 |
| 2024-25 | 233,110 | **44,605** | 200.00 | **85.11** |
| 2025-26 | 167,860 | 159,131 | 156.10 | 148.68 |

Cross-checking against Indus Action's PAB-derived tables for Karnataka (BSR 2023, Table A-2, "No. of children based on approval"): 2020-21 = **446,304**; 2021-22 = **160,810**; 2022-23 = **345,057**.

**The Ministry's "total number of seats" for year Y is exactly the PAB-approved reimbursement headcount for year Y−1.** It matches for three consecutive years. It is a lagged accounting figure, not a count of notified seats. That is why "seats" equals "admissions" perfectly for 2021-22, 2022-23 and 2023-24 — a **100% fill rate by construction**, not by achievement. The apparent 19.1% fill in 2024-25 (44,605/233,110) is the artefact breaking down, not a sudden collapse.

**Practical consequence for the instrument: any figure the Government of India labels "seats" under §12(1)(c) should be treated as a reimbursement-claim headcount until proven otherwise. Do not build a fill-rate series on it.**

## (d) Change in central policy on §12(1)(c) reimbursement

**Central reimbursement has not been discontinued.** It has been narrowed and capped. Evidence, all **T1 retrieved**:

1. **Start date.** "The Central Government is supporting States and UTs for reimbursement of expenditure incurred towards payment made to private schools for admission of children under Section 12(1)(c) **from 2014-15**." — LS 17/S7 UQ 1273, 06.12.2021, `.../annex/177/AU1273.pdf?source=lsapps`. Central co-funding therefore begins **in the first year of T1**, not under the UPA baseline.
2. **Still live in 2026.** The July 2026 Karnataka reply confirms sanction and reimbursement continuing through **2025-26** (₹156.10 cr sanctioned, ₹148.68 cr reimbursed).
3. **The 20% cap.** Reimbursement is "subject to a **maximum ceiling of 20% of the total Annual Work Plan and Budget** approved by GoI for a State/UT under the Scheme" — stated identically in 2021 (UQ 959) and 2024 (UQ 2160). I found **no evidence this ceiling was ever raised**. It is the binding constraint on Karnataka (flat ₹200 cr/year) and plausibly on Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh.
4. **Class restriction.** Support is "for admission in **class 1 and above**" / "**classes I to VIII**". **Pre-primary/nursery admissions under §12(1)(c) are outside central reimbursement**, even though many states admit at pre-primary. This is the substantive narrowing, and it is exactly what the Uran Education Society litigation is about. Similarly, Chhattisgarh's extension of the benefit to **Classes IX–XII since 2019** is funded **entirely from state funds "without Central Government reimbursement"** (Indus Action, June 2026, T3).
5. **No intervention-wise release.** Since 2024 at the latest the Ministry states plainly that central share is released "based on approved funding pattern and **not intervention wise**." There is no central accounting of §12(1)(c) money actually disbursed.
6. **Scope change absorbed into Samagra Shiksha.** SSA was subsumed into **Samagra Shiksha from 2018-19** (LS 18/S2 UQ 2257). The §12(1)(c) line survived the merger.

## Litigation context (relevant to all four questions)

- **Pramati Educational and Cultural Trust v. Union of India (2014)** — Supreme Court exempted **minority institutions** entirely from the RTE Act, including §12(1)(c). This structurally shrinks the seat base and creates an incentive to seek minority status. **Relayed via SPRF (T4); judgment not retrieved.**
- **February 2023** — Supreme Court issued notice on a PIL to enforce 25% reservation; Indus Action records that Manipur notified and Ladakh began notifying **as a direct result**. **Relayed (T4).**
- ***Dinesh Biwaji Ashtikar v. State of Maharashtra & Ors*, SLP (Civil) No. 10105/2017, neutral citation 2026 INSC 56**, bench Justices P. S. Narasimha and Atul S. Chandurkar. Judgment reserved 01.12.2025, delivered **13 or 15 January 2026** (sources conflict: Supreme Court Observer says 13 Jan; 24law says 15 Jan — **unresolved**). The Court issued directions across preparatory (8), application-processing (5) and post-admission (6) stages, including centralised online portals, help-desks, scrutiny by local authorities rather than schools, and timely reimbursement; called the provision a "national mission"; observed that "**all States and Union Territories have neither implemented the mandate of Section 12, nor established a portal dedicated to ensuring a transparent admission process**"; and directed the **NCPCR to file an affidavit by 31 March 2026** confirming rule implementation by all states/UTs. Related matter: *Md. Imran Ahmad v. Union of India*. **Relayed via scobserver.in and 24law.in (T4).
