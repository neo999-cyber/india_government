# TEACHER VACANCIES IN INDIA — is there a single authoritative national figure?

## HEADLINE: the premise needs correcting, and the correction is the finding

**A consolidated national school-teacher vacancy figure DOES exist and HAS been tabled — but not where you'd look for it, and it has been switched off.** Three distinct regimes:

| Period | Behaviour on national vacancy totals |
|---|---|
| **2014 – Jul 2018** | Ministry routinely tables all-India `TOTAL` rows for sanctioned / working / vacant, in **both** Houses, from AWP&B and PAB Minutes |
| **Aug 2018 – Feb 2024** | Totals continue but thin: secondary-only (2019), then a **state-wise table with no total row** (Dec 2023), then a **vacancies-only** total (Feb 2024). Source migrates AWP&B/PAB → PRABANDH |
| **Dec 2024 – Jul 2026** | **Zero.** No sanctioned, no vacancy, at any level, in either House. Every question answered with the Concurrent-List formula plus *teachers in position* from UDISE+ |

- **Last all-India sanctioned + in-position + vacant for state-government school teachers: RS Unstarred Q1460, 02.08.2023.**
- **Last all-India vacancy number of any kind: RS Unstarred Q525, 07.02.2024** (Elementary 7,22,385; Secondary 1,24,262).
- **Most recent living national total: Standing Committee Report No. 368, tabled 8 August 2025** — furnished by the Ministry to the Committee, *not* to either House in answer to a question.

So: not "never tabled." Rather — **the Ministry still computes the number, still gives it to a parliamentary committee, and has stopped giving it in answer to parliamentary questions.** That is a sharper and more defensible finding than "no such number exists."

## 6. UNESCO'S ~1 MILLION IS NOT A VACANCY FIGURE

**UNESCO, *State of the Education Report for India 2021: No Teacher, No Class*.** UNESCO New Delhi; research by the CETE team, TISS Mumbai. `unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000379115` returned **HTTP 403**; retrieved from the UNESCO-hosted Teacher Task Force: `https://teachertaskforce.org/sites/default/files/2022-09/2021_UNESCO_No-teacher-no-class-State-education-report-India_EN.pdf`

**Verbatim, report p. 33:**
> "UDISE captures data pertaining to available teachers in each school… However, sanctioned posts for each school are not reported, and hence vacancy-related information is inferred from the number of teachers in a school. In order to estimate vacancies, a PTR of 35:1 (also reported as the one used by states to estimate vacancies) gives an idea of teacher adequacy and requirements. Based on this, the estimated number of schools with vacancies at the country level stands at 301,166, or 19 per cent of all schools."
>
> "Based on the PTR of 35:1, the **total additional teacher requirement** is found to be about **1,116,846**. 69 per cent of total additional teacher requirement is in rural areas."

- **Base year: UDISE+ 2018/19** (every table captioned so).
- **Method: a modelled requirement** — apply a flat 35:1 norm to every school, sum the deficit. **Not a shortfall against sanctioned posts.** UNESCO says sanctioned posts are not in the data.
- **Coverage: ALL managements** (1,551,000 schools; 9,430,839 teachers) — government, aided and private unaided. Single-teacher schools: **110,971**.
- **Not a Government of India figure.**

**The two ~1 million numbers rest on DIFFERENT FACTS, not different weightings.** UNESCO's 11.16 lakh = modelled additional requirement, all schools, 2018-19, PTR-normative. The Government's 9.59 lakh (2023-24) = vacant *sanctioned* posts, government/SSA-funded schools, PRABANDH/PAB administrative count. Their agreement is a coincidence of construction. Treating "UNESCO says 1 million, Parliament says 1 million" as corroboration is double-counting.

**And the Ministry has twice declined to engage with it in the House.** MPs quoted the UNESCO figure verbatim:
- **LS UN 3482, 08.08.2022**: *"whether according to a report around more than 11.16 lakh posts for teachers are vacant in schools across the country… and upto 69 per cent of posts are vacant in the rural areas"*
- **LS UN 24, 22.07.2024**: *"whether according to a report, more than 11.16 lakh posts of teachers are vacant in the schools of the country… out of which about seventy-nine percent of the posts are vacant in rural areas"*

Both answers gave the Concurrent-List paragraph and **no vacancy number**, answering only the incidental sub-question on female teachers. (Note the two MPs cite 69% and 79% for the same underlying rural share; UNESCO p. 33 says 69%.)

## 7. UDISE+ REPORTS NO VACANCIES — CONFIRMED

Full-text search of the UDISE+ **2023-24** and **2024-25** national reports (both retrieved via Wayback): **`vacan*` = 0 occurrences; `sanction*` = 0 occurrences.** Not in tables, TOC or narrative.

Section 4 ("Details on Teaching & Non-teaching staff") publishes only: teachers by school category (4.1); by management × category (4.2–4.6); by management × classes taught (4.7–4.11); **PTR** (4.12); by management × gender (4.13–4.17); **% trained** (4.18); **% professionally qualified** (4.19).

**Every variable is teachers in position or a ratio derived from them. There is no sanctioned-posts denominator, so no vacancy is computable from UDISE+.** This is exactly the gap UNESCO flagged in 2021 — six years on, unclosed. It is also why the Standing Committee series is a separate administrative collection (State + Samagra Shiksha establishment via PAB/PRABANDH), not a UDISE+ derivative.

**This is why the post-2024 answers are what they are:** the Ministry has migrated its parliamentary sourcing from PAB/PRABANDH (which *has* sanctioned posts) to UDISE+ (which does not). The refusal and the source-switch are the same act.

## 8. WHERE SOURCES DISAGREE

1. **Report 368 prints two contradictory 2023-24 totals one page apart.** Table-5 (p. 13), attributed to *"Annexure… to Lok Sabha Unstarred Question No. 133 dated 4.12.2023"* and cited by civil-society/TISS witnesses: Elementary 46,56,000 sanctioned / 7,06,000 vacant; Secondary 9,20,000 / 1,25,000 — total 55,76,000 / **8,31,000**. Table-6, from the Department: 63,26,207 / **9,59,148**. **Gap: 7.5 lakh sanctioned posts, 1.28 lakh vacancies, for the same year.** The report does not reconcile them. (Table-5's secondary figures round to the LS UN 1202 / RS UN 525 secondary-only numbers — so Table-5 excludes higher secondary and Table-6 includes it. That accounts for most, not all, of the gap.)
2. **Report 368's 2024-25 column has a definitional break.** Elementary sanctioned *falls* 2,58,513 while Secondary sanctioned *rises* 9,18,066 in one year (net +6,59,553). Unexplained. **Do not read 2023-24 → 2024-25 as real establishment growth.**
3. **Frozen data.** 51,03,539 / 42,03,223 / 9,00,316 (as on 31.03.2017) is tabled identically on 10.08.2017, 08.02.2018, 26.07.2018, 13.12.2018 (×2), 30.07.2018 and 04.07.2019. Two years of answers recycle one stock-take.
4. **Instrument migration breaks the series:** AWP&B/PAB Minutes (2015–2019) → PRABANDH (2020–2024) → UDISE+ (2025–2026). PRABANDH's "classes 1–8" is not the same span as AWP&B's "elementary". Do not chain them without a break marker.
