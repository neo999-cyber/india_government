## 10. HIGHER EDUCATION

### 10.1 THE HEADLINE FINDING — AISHE collapsed as a timely series, then reappeared as a double release

**AISHE 2022-23 and AISHE 2023-24 were released together on 8 July 2026.** Before that, the most recent AISHE was 2021-22, released 25 January 2024. **That is a 29-month interval with no AISHE publication at all, and AISHE 2022-23 was published three years and six months after its own reference date.**

**The 2022-23 lag is not a collection problem.** The report itself states: *"The Survey was held between 18th November, 2022 till March, 2024"* (§1.8). **Collection closed in March 2024; the report appeared 28 months later.** The binding constraint sits between scrutiny and publication, not in the field.

**And the lag is era-shaped.** AISHE 2015-16, 2016-17 and 2017-18 each carried a cover year one year after the survey year. **The lag opened in T2 and reached its maximum in T3.**

> **This resolves §8A(1) definitively.** The Economic Survey 2025-26 quoted an AISHE **2022-23** GER of 29.5 while the AISHE 2022-23 report was still unpublished, and the same Survey's statistical appendix — unable to cite an unpublished report — fell back on AISHE 2021-22 and printed 28.4. **The number was in government hands and used in the government's flagship economic document months before the report containing it was released.** `not-published`, with producibility demonstrated rather than inferred.

**Survey administration:** AISHE remains with the Statistics Division, Department of Higher Education, assisted by NIC — **UGC did not take it over.** It runs under the Central Sector scheme **HESPIS**, which the AISHE 2023-24 report says has been *"extended for continuation up to 2025-26 by the Standing Finance Committee"* (§1.3). **No document extending it beyond FY2025-26 was retrieved — a live authority gap worth tracking.**

**Two migrations:** the entire AISHE report archive was re-uploaded to `cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in` in June 2025, so **original publication metadata is no longer recoverable from file paths**; and the July 2026 PIB release directs readers to a new Department of Higher Education portal (`dohe-education.gov.in/documents/reports/aishe-report-...`) which **failed to resolve on repeated attempts.**

**Microdata: none.** What is published is the report PDF plus aggregate Excel tables (`he.nic.in/aishereport`) and an institution directory. **There is no anonymised respondent-level file of the kind NSS/PLFS release.** `not-published`.

### 10.2 GER — and the denominator restatement that breaks the series

**This is the most important methodological fact in the domain, and the reference points in common circulation mix two incompatible series.**

AISHE 2020-21 (published 29 January 2023) states it verbatim:

> "In the year 2020-21, Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) in higher education for age group 18-23 years is estimated to be 27.3, as compared to **25.6 in 2019-20 as per 2011 projection, whereas, it is 27.1 in 2019-20 as per 2001 population projection.**"

**Every GER published by AISHE before the 2020-21 report rests on 2001-Census-based projections. Everything from 2020-21 onward rests on 2011-Census-based projections. The new denominator is roughly 6% larger, which mechanically cuts GER by about 1.5 points at 2019-20 levels.**

Arithmetic check: 2019-20 enrolment 3,85,36,359 ÷ 0.271 ⇒ implied denominator 142.20 million (matches AISHE 2018-19 Table 52's 142,078,501); ÷ 0.256 ⇒ 150.53 million (matches AISHE 2021-22 Table 52's 150,805,600). **The restatement is fully explained by the denominator swap.**

**Official restated series (2011-Census projection basis).** Source: "Gross Enrolment Ratio from AISHE based on Population Projection of 2011 Census", https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s392049debbe566ca5782a3045cf300a3c/uploads/2025/06/202506041164303308.pdf — **T1**; extended to 2022-23 and 2023-24 from the reports released 8 July 2026.

**CORRECTIONS to figures in common circulation:** AISHE 2012-13 published **21.5**, not 21.1 (the 2011-basis restatement for that year is 21.6). The pair "2018-19 = 26.3 / 2019-20 = 27.1" is **2001-basis** while "2020-21 = 27.3 / 2021-22 = 28.4" is **2011-basis** — **they cannot go in the same column.** And 2022-23 is **29.5**, not 28.4.

**Female GER crossed above male in 2017-18** and has stayed above for seven consecutive years — AISHE 2023-24: *"The GPI has crossed 1 since 2017-18… and it continues."* **Note the crossover year is itself basis-dependent**: on the as-published 2001-basis series it reads as 2018-19.

**THE DENOMINATOR IS FALLING, AND IT IS DOING HALF THE WORK.** Published 18–23 projections: 2018 = 142,078,501 (2001 basis); **2021 = 152,444,000; 2022 = 151,161,000; 2023 = 149,879,000** (2011 basis). My arithmetic reproduces the published GER exactly (4,46,32,261 ÷ 151,161,000 = 29.53%; 4,50,01,123 ÷ 149,879,000 = 30.02%).

> **The projected denominator fell 1,282,000 (−0.85%) between 2022 and 2023. Holding it at its 2022 value, the 2023-24 GER would be 29.77, not 30.0. Roughly half of the headline +0.5-point gain is denominator shrinkage, not additional enrolment.** As India's 18–23 cohort contracts, **GER will keep rising on flat enrolment** — and the whole denominator rests on a projection whose base is now fifteen years old (`P-04`). **Male GER was flat at 28.9 in both 2022-23 and 2023-24: all of the 2023-24 increase is female enrolment plus the shrinking denominator.**

### 10.4 Data-quality flags inside the flagship report

1. **The "92% participation rate" is post-pooling, not actual response.** AISHE 2023-24 Box 1: actual response was universities 1,249 (96.9%), colleges 44,380 (92.0%), **standalone institutions 10,513 — 69.1%.** Published totals are reached by "repeating/pooling" the latest available data from AISHE 2021-22 and 2022-23 for non-responders. **Roughly 31% of standalone institutions in the 2023-24 figures are carrying data one to two years old.** The PIB release says only "participation rate is more than 90%."
2. **The Ministry's own caveat, verbatim (PIB, 8 Jul 2026):** *"Participation in AISHE is **voluntary**, and the data is **self-reported** by the HEIs via AISHE web portal."* **This is `P-22` in higher education: a voluntary self-reported administrative return standing in for a measurement.**
3. **Internal contradiction on ST GER inside one document.** AISHE 2023-24 highlights: *"ST student GER is 22.8 in 2023-24. This was **23.5** in 2022-23."* Body text §3.7: *"GER has increased to 22.8 in 2023-24 from **22.6** in 2022-23."*
4. **Narrative contradicts its own table.** §2.3: *"The States with highest GER are Tamil Nadu (52.3), Delhi (53.0), Uttarakhand (48.5)…"* — but Table 19 of the same report shows **Puducherry 72.3** and **Chandigarh 58.5**, both omitted, and lists Tamil Nadu ahead of the higher Delhi.
5. **The GPI time-series file does not reconcile with the GER time-series file**, both published side by side on aishe.gov.in. For 2017-18 the GER table implies a ratio of 1.004 while the GPI table gives **0.97**; for 2019-20, 1.065 against **1.01**.
6. **Table 52 and Table 21 are listed in the AISHE 2023-24 contents but do not appear in the published PDF.**

### 10.5 FACULTY VACANCIES — NOT ESTABLISHED. No figure is offered.

**This is a deliberate refusal, not an omission.** The parallel retrieval stream tasked with the Lok Sabha / Rajya Sabha unstarred-question PDFs on central-university, IIT, NIT, IIM and IISER sanctioned/filled/vacant teaching posts, and on reserved-category (SC/ST/OBC/EWS/PwD) faculty vacancies, **did not return.** Direct attempts failed: `sansad.in` serves its question archive as a client-rendered application with no reachable JSON endpoint, and the session's WebSearch budget was exhausted.

**The widely-circulated ranges — "6,000–11,000 central-university vacancies", "4,000–5,000 IIT vacancies" — were deliberately NOT reproduced**, because under the tier rule a parliamentary reply *cited but not obtained* is T4, and even that provenance could not be confirmed.

> **No vacancy figure should enter the instrument on the strength of this phase. §10.5 must be re-run.** Note that this mirrors §5's finding for schoolteachers: the national teacher-vacancy quantity is assembled from parliamentary fragments and has no standing published series at either level.

### 10.6 UGC FUNDING — resolved from primary budget documents

**The "UGC budget slashed from ₹5,360 crore to ₹2,500 crore" claim is arithmetically true on a BE-to-BE basis and materially misleading.**

*Notes on Demands for Grants 2024-25*, Demand No. 26, item 38 (https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2024-25/doc/eb/sbe26.pdf — **T1**):

| | Actual 2022-23 | BE 2023-24 | RE 2023-24 | **BE 2024-25** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 38.01 UGC | 5,091.62 | **5,360.00** | 6,409.00 | **2,500.00** |
| 38.02 Transfer to MUSK-UGC | … | … | … | **2,000.00** |
| 38.03 Amount met from MUSK-UGC | … | … | … | **−2,000.00** |

**FY2024-25 is the first year in which UGC's provision is split between the Consolidated Fund and MUSK** — the *Madhyamik and Uchchatar Shiksha Kosh*, the education-cess fund. **The ₹2,500 crore figure is the CFI component only; the gross provision for UGC in BE 2024-25 was ₹4,500 crore.**

From *Notes on Demands for Grants 2025-26*, item 32 (**T1**): gross UGC provision — **Actual 2023-24 ₹6,324.12 cr; BE 2024-25 ₹4,500.00 cr; RE 2024-25 ₹5,048.59 cr; BE 2025-26 ₹5,783.23 cr.** From *2026-27*, item 34 (**T1**): **Actual 2024-25 ₹3,678.47 cr; BE 2025-26 ₹3,335.97 cr; RE 2025-26 ₹3,470.00 cr; BE 2026-27 ₹3,709.00 cr** (CFI component, MUSK sub-lines not shown that year).

> **Correct statement: UGC's GROSS provision fell from ₹6,324.12 crore (Actual FY2023-24) to ₹4,500 crore (BE FY2024-25) — a real cut of about 29%, not the 53% the headline implies — and had recovered only to ₹5,783.23 crore by BE FY2025-26, still below the FY2023-24 actual in NOMINAL terms, before any inflation adjustment.**

**A second break makes long comparison invalid.** In FY2013-14 to FY2015-16 the Department's demand had **no separate "Central Universities" head at all** — the UGC line, then ₹9,000–10,000 crore (2013-14 Actual **₹10,091.35 cr**; 2015-16 BE **₹9,615.45 cr**), *carried* the maintenance grants UGC passed through to central universities. After the abolition of the Plan/Non-Plan distinction from FY2017-18, **"Grants to Central Universities" became its own line (₹16,146.11 crore in BE 2025-26).** **The apparent halving of "the UGC budget" between 2015 and 2018 is overwhelmingly a reclassification, not a cut. Any series must break at FY2016-17/2017-18.**

**A third trap: gross versus net.** Department of Higher Education totals *net of recoveries*: Actual 2022-23 ₹38,556.80 cr; Actual 2023-24 ₹55,392.68 cr; Actual 2024-25 ₹45,576.52 cr; BE 2026-27 ₹55,727.22 cr. **Recoveries have reached −₹22,769 crore in BE 2026-27 — gross and net now diverge by roughly 29%, entirely because of MUSK routing.** Reporting gross where the source reports net, or vice versa, produces a spurious step change.

### 10.7 HEFA — the grants-to-loans shift, in the government's own accounts

HEFA appears in two distinct forms, and the transition is the finding.

**As an equity injection** (*Notes on DFG 2019-20*, Demand 58, item 12 — **T1**): HEFA capital — Actual 2017-18 **₹250.00 cr**; BE 2018-19 **₹2,750.00 cr**; RE 2018-19 ₹2,750.00 cr; BE 2019-20 **₹2,100.00 cr**.

**As debt service on institutions' borrowings.** From BE 2019-20 onward, dedicated sub-lines **"Interest under HEFA Loan"** and **"Repayment of Principal of HEFA Loan"** appear under each institutional head. BE 2025-26 (**T1**):

| Head | Interest | Principal repayment |
|---|---|---|
| Grants to Central Universities | 83.06 | 462.14 |
| Support to IITs | 240.00 | 450.00 |
| NITs | 72.00 | 149.89 |
| IISERs / IIITs | 80.60 | 133.00 |
| (item 42) | 10.00 | 12.00 |

> **This is the substantive funding-model change, visible in the government's own accounts: infrastructure capital that was formerly a grant is now a loan carried on the institution's books, with interest and principal appearing as recurring budget lines — roughly ₹1,693 crore of HEFA debt service budgeted in BE 2025-26, from a standing start in FY2019-20, and a claim on the same demand that funds the institutions.**

**NOT retrieved:** HEFA cumulative sanctions and disbursements, institutions covered, the window-by-window split and which windows require repayment from internal accruals versus government servicing, and HEFA's status in 2025-26. **Do not fill these from memory.**

### 10.8 HECI and the UGC Draft Regulations 2025 — NOT ESTABLISHED

The Higher Education Commission of India has been announced repeatedly since 2018. **Whether any HECI bill was introduced or enacted was not established in this phase**; the parallel stream did not return. **No HECI line appears in any of the eight budget documents retrieved**, which is consistent with — but is not proof of — non-enactment. Likewise the January 2025 draft UGC Regulations on Vice-Chancellor appointments and the state objections to them. **Recorded as unestablished, not as an absence finding.** If it turns out to be announced-but-never-legislated across three terms, that is a first-class finding and it should be run down.
