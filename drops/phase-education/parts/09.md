## 9. PRIVATE SCHOOLING SHARE

### 9.1 FRAME WARNING — state this wherever either number appears

**ASER and UDISE+ private-share figures are not interchangeable and must never be placed in the same column.**

| | **ASER** | **UDISE+** |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | **rural only** | all-India, rural + urban |
| Population | **age 6–14** | all grades, pre-primary to XII |
| Instrument | **household survey**, household-reported | **school-reported administrative returns** |
| Frame | Census 2011 village frame, 30 villages/district, 20 households/village | attempted universe of UDISE+-coded schools |
| Incentive | households have no stake in the count | **schools have funding and scheme entitlements attached to headcount** |

**That last row is the analytically decisive one.** ASER's private share is not vulnerable to the ghost-enrolment problem UDISE+ has just corrected for. **ASER is therefore the better control series across the 2022-23 break — and, usefully, ASER shows no discontinuity there at all.**

Sources: ASER 2014 National Findings deck (2006–2014 back-series) — https://img.asercentre.org/docs/Publications/ASER%20Reports/ASER%202014/National%20PPTs/aser2014indiaenglish.pdf; ASER 2024 all-India deck slide 3 — https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2024-All-India-ppt-Jan-27-11am.pdf. **T4** per §0 (NGO dataset, retrieved directly).

**The hypothesis in the brief is confirmed, with three corrections:**
- Private share rose steeply through the UPA years (**18.7% in 2006 → 30.8% in 2014**, a near-doubling) — **this is a baseline-era trend, and it is one of the clearest things in the entire domain.**
- It then **plateaued, not rose**, across T1: 30.8 / 30.6 / 30.9 in 2014 / 2016 / 2018. **The privatisation of rural schooling stopped in 2014 and has never resumed.**
- The COVID trough is **25.1% and it is 2022, not 2021** — there was no nationwide field ASER in 2020 or 2021.
- **The reversal was essentially complete by 2024** (25.1 → 30.6). ASER's own verdict: *"In 2024, private school enrollment has returned to pre-COVID levels. Private school enrollment has been around 30% nationally for rural children (age 6-14) for over ten years."*

The same V-shape appears at age 5: private school or pre-school attendance **37.3% (2018) → 30.8% (2022) → 37.5% (2024)**.

**Wadhwa's interpretation, which the instrument should carry because it bears on §3.2:** *"This almost complete reversal back to 2018 levels is seen across grades as well as gender… This seems to confirm that the increase in government school enrollment observed during the COVID years was driven more by **necessity rather than choice**."* Her candidate mechanisms: low-cost private schools closing during the pandemic; household financial stress; government schools distributing dry rations during closures.

> **DO NOT READ THE 34.4% → 38.8% JUMP AS A BEHAVIOURAL SHIFT.** The 2023-24 report labels these rows "(Primary to Higher Secondary)"; the 2024-25 report labels them "(Pre-Primary to Higher Secondary)". The columns sum to 234,963,031 (= I–XII) in 2023-24 and to 246,932,680 (= including pre-primary) in 2024-25. **Pre-primary is heavily private, and 2024-25 pre-primary enrolment was 14,047,080 — almost exactly the 15.0 million "increase" in private unaided.**
>
> **This is a third base change, and unlike the other two it is NOT mentioned in any disclaimer.** It is precisely the kind of thing that produces a spurious "privatisation surge" headline, and the instrument must not reproduce it.

The 2020-21 → 2021-22 movement (government **+8.1 million**, private **−5.6 million**) is the COVID shift on genuinely comparable pre-break data, **and it corroborates ASER's direction independently.** That agreement between an administrative universe and a household survey, on the one stretch where both are clean, is worth stating: it is the best available evidence that the COVID government-school shift was real and not an artefact of either instrument.

Economic Survey 2025-26 ¶11.10 (**T1**) gives the current headline: "Government schools constitute **69 per cent of all schools, enrolling nearly half of all students**, while private schools account for **26 per cent of schools and 41 per cent of total enrolment**."

### 9.5 FIRST-CLASS FINDING — the government does not publish a school-closure count

Both parliamentary questions asked specifically for **the number of schools closed or merged, year-wise and state-wise**. In both replies the ministry declined:

> "Education is in the Concurrent list of the Constitution and the **opening, closing and rationalisation of schools are within the purview of respective State Government and UT Administration**." — LS U.Q. 112 (03.02.2025), parts (b)–(d); repeated near-verbatim in LS U.Q. 384 (02.02.2026).

> **The Government of India publishes only the STOCK of schools each year. It does not publish the FLOW. The widely-circulated "93,000 schools closed" figure is a stock difference — it nets closures against openings and says nothing about how many were merged rather than shut.**

**Suggested reasonKind: `not-collected` at the Union level** — the ministry's stated position is that it does not hold the flow because opening and closing are state functions. **But mark `reasonDisputed: true`, `disputeKind: evidentiary`**, because the same ministry operates UDISE+, a school-level system with a unique code per school, from which closures and mergers are directly derivable; and because the 2025-26 release *does* publish flow-type counts for **zero-enrolment schools (10,294 → 5,663)** and **single-teacher schools (118,190 → 100,843)** across 2022-23 to 2025-26. **The data is producible from a system the Union runs.** Any instrument entry should record *net change in school stock*, and record the closure count itself as unpublished.

**The ministry's rhetorical move is worth logging:** asked about closures, it answers with **access** and **efficiency**. LS U.Q. 384 (02.02.2026, **T1**, https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU384_rHxI8U.pdf?source=pqals) supplies a **Gross Access Ratio** series — villages/habitations with a school within norm, as % of total: primary **97.15 (2018-19) → 97.83 (2024-25)**; upper primary 96.49 → 96.57; secondary **88.24 → 95.35**; higher secondary **65.05 → 94.97**. The same reply discloses **0.3% of government schools running without buildings, 12.0% in dilapidated building blocks, 0.6% without drinking water, and 13.4% of primary schools running with a single teacher.**

**Corroborating from the independent side:** ASER 2024 finds government primary schools with fewer than 60 children enrolled rose from **44.0% (2022) to 52.1% (2024)** — i.e. the consolidation the government frames as optimisation is happening against a school stock that is becoming *more*, not less, thinly populated.
