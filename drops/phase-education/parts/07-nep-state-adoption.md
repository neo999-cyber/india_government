# TOPIC A — THE TAMIL NADU THREE-LANGUAGE DISPUTE

## A1. NEP 2020 — exact wording (paragraph number verified: **4.13**)

**Source:** National Education Policy 2020, Ministry of Human Resource Development, Government of India, released 29/30 July 2020. Full URL: `https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/NEP_Final_English_0.pdf` — **T1**.

**Para 4.13 (verbatim, complete):**

> "4.13. The three-language formula will continue to be implemented while keeping in mind the Constitutional provisions, aspirations of the people, regions, and the Union, and the need to promote multilingualism as well as promote national unity. However, there will be a greater flexibility in the three-language formula, and no language will be imposed on any State. The three languages learned by children will be the choices of States, regions, and of course the students themselves, so long as at least two of the three languages are native to India. In particular, students who wish to change one or more of the three languages they are studying may do so in Grade 6 or 7, as long as they are able to demonstrate basic proficiency in three languages (including one language of India at the literature level) by the end of secondary school."

The prompt's wording is **confirmed**: paragraph number 4.13 is correct, and the phrase is exactly *"and no language will be imposed on any State"* (capital S on "State").

**Four adjacent provisions that materially change the reading of 4.13** (all verbatim, same source):

- **4.12** — "…There will be a major effort from both the Central and State governments to invest in large numbers of language teachers in all regional languages around the country, and, in particular, for all languages mentioned in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India. **States, especially States from different regions of India, may enter into bilateral agreements to hire teachers in large numbers from each other, to satisfy the three-language formula in their respective States**, and also to encourage the study of Indian languages across the country."
- **4.11** — "Wherever possible, the medium of instruction until at least Grade 5, but preferably till Grade 8 and beyond, will be the home language/mother tongue/local language/regional language."
- **4.17** — "**Sanskrit will thus be offered at all levels of school and higher education as an important, enriching option for students, including as an option in the three-language formula.**"
- **22.15** — "…Sanskrit will be mainstreamed with strong offerings in school — **including as one of the language options in the three-language formula** — as well as in higher education."

**Textual finding:** the word "Hindi" appears **nowhere** in the language chapter of NEP 2020. A full-text grep of the 66-page policy returns only two occurrences of "Hindi", both incidental (a list of subjects for teacher assistance, and none in Ch. 4/22 language passages). Sanskrit, by contrast, is named repeatedly and is twice explicitly nominated as a third-language option.

### THE PRECISE DELTA

| | 1968 §3(b) / 1986 §8.7 / POA 1992 ¶10 | 2020 §4.13 |
|---|---|---|
| Named languages | **Hindi named and compulsory** in non-Hindi states; English named; regional language named | **No language named.** Only constraint: "at least two of the three languages are native to India" |
| Who chooses | Prescribed by the Centre; "State Governments should adopt, and vigorously implement" | "the choices of States, regions, and of course the students themselves" |
| Anti-imposition clause | **Absent** | "**no language will be imposed on any State**" — new sentence, no 1968/1986 antecedent |
| Number of languages | Three, compulsory at the secondary stage | Three, retained ("will continue to be implemented"); switching allowed in Grade 6 or 7; proficiency in three required by end of secondary school |
| Sanskrit | 1968 §3(d) treats Sanskrit separately, *not* as a formula slot; POA 1992 ¶11(b) criticises states that **substituted** a classical language for a modern Indian language | Sanskrit explicitly nominated **as a formula option** (§4.17, §22.15) |
| Central funding lever | POA 1992 ¶13: 100% central assistance for **Hindi teachers** in non-Hindi states | No language-specific funding line; instead a bilateral state-to-state teacher-hiring suggestion (§4.12) |

**Summary of the delta:** what changed is the *identity* of the languages (removed) and the *locus of choice* (moved to states/students). What did **not** change is the *cardinality*: three languages remains the policy, at the secondary stage, in both 1968 and 2020.

## A3. Tamil Nadu's position and its actual legal/policy basis

> THIS HOUSE refuses to accept the scheme of Union Government for the imposition of Hindi.

## A5. **CRITICAL ANALYTICAL QUESTION — different facts, or different weightings?**

**Answer: overwhelmingly different weightings of the same facts, with one genuine and narrow factual dispute.**

### The factual claims that do NOT conflict

1. **"NEP 2020 does not mandate Hindi."** True, and Tamil Nadu has never produced a textual citation to the contrary. The word "Hindi" is absent from NEP's language chapter. Para 4.13 says the choice belongs to "States, regions, and of course the students themselves."
2. **"NEP 2020 mandates a third language."** Also true, and the Union does not deny it. Para 4.13's opening clause is affirmative: "The three-language formula **will continue to be implemented**." The flexibility clause ("However…") modifies *which* languages, not *how many*. NCF-SE 2023's R1/R2/R3 with proficiency in R3 by age 14 makes the cardinality operational (T1, RS AU1933).
3. **"Tamil Nadu currently teaches two languages."** Undisputed by both sides — the Union's own RS answer AU2210 records the question's premise without contesting it.
4. **"Tamil Nadu has not signed the PM-SHRI MoU and has not received Samagra Shiksha funds."** Both sides agree; the Union states it in writing (LS AU2597; LS Starred Q.9). The disagreement is over whether that is a legitimate conditionality, not over whether it happened.

So the two governments are not asserting incompatible facts about the text. They are asserting incompatible *significance*.

### Tamil Nadu's argument is a valid inference from the Union's own text, not a counter-factual

The strongest version of the TN case is **arithmetical, and it is derivable entirely from NEP 4.13 without any empirical claim about teachers**:

- 4.13 requires three languages, of which **at least two must be native to India**.
- In a Tamil-medium/Tamil-and-English school, the existing two are Tamil (Indian) and English (not native to India).
- Therefore the mandatory third language **cannot be a foreign language** — it must be an Indian language other than Tamil, otherwise the "at least two native to India" floor fails.
- NEP then names, in the entire policy, exactly one language as a preferred third-slot option: **Sanskrit** (§4.17, §22.15).
- Hence: for Tamil Nadu specifically, "no language will be imposed" delivers a *choice among Indian languages other than Tamil*, not a choice that includes "no third language."

That is not a misreading. It is a correct reading of the text's own constraint structure. The Union's reply (AU2210, AU1933) never denies it; it answers a different question — *which* language — while TN is objecting to *whether a third at all*.

**The secondary, empirical TN argument** — that teacher availability makes Hindi or Sanskrit the only feasible third language — is *anticipated by NEP itself*. Para 4.12 concedes the supply problem and proposes a remedy: "States… may enter into bilateral agreements to hire teachers in large numbers from each other, to satisfy the three-language formula." A policy that proposes interstate teacher-import treaties has already conceded that the local supply of non-Hindi, non-Sanskrit Indian-language teachers is inadequate. The historical record confirms the direction of failure: POA 1992 ¶11 recorded that "no provision exists for the teaching of South Indian languages… in the Hindi speaking States" and that "a classical language has been substituted for a modern Indian language in some States." The formula has failed asymmetrically for 57 years, always in the direction of Hindi/Sanskrit.

### Where there IS a genuine factual dispute — and it is narrow

**One, and only one:** whether Tamil Nadu officials previously agreed to implement NEP in full and then reneged. Pradhan asserted this in the Lok Sabha on 10 March 2025; Kanimozhi denied it ("We had clearly said that the three language policy is not acceptable to Tamil Nadu"). This is a resolvable factual question about the content of a meeting, and it is unresolved in the public record. **T4 both sides.**

A second, softer factual dispute concerns **burden asymmetry**: TN argues Hindi-speaking students learn two functional languages (Hindi + English) while non-Hindi students learn three. The 1968 formula explicitly tried to close this by requiring a southern language in Hindi states; POA 1992 ¶11(c) recorded that this never happened. NEP 2020 **removed the corrective requirement while retaining the three-language obligation** — it deleted "preferably one of the southern languages" along with everything else language-specific. This is a factual asymmetry visible on the face of the two documents, and the Union has not addressed it in any parliamentary answer I retrieved.

### Verdict

The dispute is **not** about what NEP says. Both sides quote 4.13 accurately; the Union quotes it verbatim in at least five parliamentary answers, and TN does not contest the wording. The dispute is about what a **de-specified mandate** means when it lands on a state that objects to the mandate's *cardinality* rather than its *content*:

- **The Union weights the choice clause** ("no language will be imposed") as dispositive, and treats the three-count as an uncontroversial residue of 1968.
- **Tamil Nadu weights the count clause** ("will continue to be implemented") as dispositive, and treats the choice clause as cosmetic because the choice set, for TN, is non-empty only in ways TN objects to.

Both readings are textually available. The removal of language names in 2020 is a real, substantive change from 1968 — but it changes the *identity* of the imposed language, not the *fact* of a third-language requirement, and Tamil Nadu's 1968 resolution was directed at the requirement, not only at Hindi ("**That the Three-Language Formula shall be scrapped**", 23 Jan 1968). TN's position in 2025 is therefore continuous with its 1968 position and is not a mischaracterisation of NEP 2020; it is a rejection of the element NEP 2020 retained.

**The coercion question is separate and is not textual at all.** Nothing in NEP 2020 authorises conditioning Samagra Shiksha funds on NEP adoption; the linkage runs through the PM-SHRI MoU (Sept 2022) and the 2021 realignment of Samagra Shiksha to NEP. The Union's own formulation — "implementation of Samagra Shiksha is implementation of NEP 2020" (LS Starred Q.9, 21 July 2025; LS AU2597, 09 Mar 2026) — is a *policy-design* claim, not a claim about the NEP text. This is where the two sides come closest to a genuine conflict of fact, and it is where the Supreme Court declined to intervene (see B7).

## B1. Is adoption legally required at all? — **No.**

**Constitutional position (T1, stated by the Union itself in multiple parliamentary answers):**

- "Education is in the concurrent list of the Constitution and majority of the schools are under the domain of respective State and UT Governments." — Lok Sabha Starred Q. No. 9, answered 21.07.2025, **Minister of Education Shri Dharmendra Pradhan**. `https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/185/AS9_3P6rhK.pdf?source=pqals`
- "The subject 'Education' is in List-III (Concurrent List) of the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution of India." — Lok Sabha Unstarred Q. 2597, answered 09.03.2026.
- "**since Education is a subject under the Concurrent List of the Constitution, the respective State and Union Territory (UT) Governments may decide on the modalities of implementing the Three Language Policy**" — Rajya Sabha Unstarred Q. 1933, answered 06.08.2025.
- "Education being in the concurrent list, Centre and States are equally responsible…" — PIB release, Ministry of Education, "Implementation of NEP, 2020", 07 FEB 2024, MoS Dr. Subhas Sarkar, written reply in Rajya Sabha. `https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2003660`

**NEP 2020 is a policy, not a statute.** It was approved by the Union Cabinet; no Act of Parliament enacts it. There is no legal instrument compelling a state to adopt it. This was confirmed judicially — see B7.

**What the Centre does have is a fiscal lever, not a legal one:** the Samagra Shiksha scheme was "revamped in 2021 to align with the provisions of NEP, 2020", and the Union's stated position is that "implementation of Samagra Shiksha is implementation of NEP 2020" (LS AU2597, T1). Participation in PM-SHRI requires signing an MoU committing to NEP.

## B7. The judicial position — states cannot be compelled

**Supreme Court, order dated Friday 9 May 2025 (reported 9–12 May 2025).** Bench: **Justice J. B. Pardiwala and Justice R. Mahadevan**. Petitioner: advocate **G. S. Mani**, seeking directions to Tamil Nadu, Kerala and West Bengal to implement NEP 2020 including the three-language formula. Plea **dismissed**.

Verbatim (as reported by PTI):
> "The Supreme Court, through Article 32 of the Constitution, can issue directives to ensure that the fundamental rights of citizens are protected. **It cannot directly compel a state to adopt a policy like the National Education Policy, 2020.**"
> "The court may, however, intervene if a state's action or inaction related to the National Education Policy violates any fundamental rights or any other legal rights. We do not propose to examine this issue in this writ petition filed under Article 32 of the Constitution."

The Court also held the petitioner lacked standing ("The petitioner has nothing to do with the cause he proposes to espouse").

Source: PTI via ETLegalWorld, `https://legal.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/litigation/sc-dismisses-plea-seeking-implementation-of-nep-in-tamil-nadu-other-states/121102155` (Published 12 May 2025 13:25 IST). Corroborated by Deccan Herald `https://www.deccanherald.com/india/court-cant-force-any-state-to-adopt-nep-supreme-court-rejects-plea-for-implementation-3532976` (09 May 2025 10:06 IST). **T4** — I could **not** retrieve the order itself; `main.sci.gov.in` did not resolve. ## B8. Maharashtra — the test case for the "functionally means Hindi" claim

Maharashtra **has** adopted NEP 2020. In April and June 2025 it issued Government Resolutions introducing **Hindi as the third language from Class 1**. After sustained opposition, CM Devendra Fadnavis announced withdrawal of both GRs on **29 June 2025**, and constituted a committee under **Dr. Narendra Jadhav** to recommend how the three-language formula should be implemented.

Sources (T4): `https://www.siasat.com/facing-backlash-maharashtra-withdraws-hindi-language-policy-orders-...`; The Hindu, "Maharashtra scraps three-language policy for primary education"; ThePrint (28 July 2025) confirms: "In June, the Mahayuti-led Maharashtra government withdrew its decision of introducing Hindi as the third language, following strong uproar by the Opposition. The state has otherwise adopted NEP 2020."

**Analytical weight:** this is the single most important empirical datum for A5. A BJP-led state that adopted NEP in full, when it operationalised the "flexible" three-language formula, selected **Hindi** as the third language from Class 1 — and had to reverse under public pressure. It is direct evidence that Tamil Nadu's inference about what the flexible formula converts into in practice is not paranoid. **T4.**
