# 06 — CONTESTED: are encounter operations lawful counter-insurgency or extrajudicial killing? The Amshipora case

Amshipora is the case the operator asked for by name, because it is the one where the Army itself found that its own personnel had staged an encounter. The primary facts and the disposal are below. **A note on evidence grade throughout: the court-martial record is not public, the Court of Inquiry has never been published, and the Armed Forces Tribunal order was read through press accounts. Almost everything here is RELAYED, mostly through Scroll.in's sustained reporting, which is the fullest account located. Nothing in this part is T1.**

---

## The primary facts

**18 July 2020.** Three men were shot dead at Amshipora village, Shopian district, and presented as unidentified militants. They were later established by DNA to be **Imtiyaz Ahmad (20)** and **Abrar Ahmed (16)** of Dharsakri, and **Mohammed Ibrar (25)** of Tarkasi — all of Rajouri district, seasonal labourers who had gone to Shopian for orchard work and were last in phone contact with families on 17 July 2020.

**The official version on the day.** J&K Police said the operation followed "a specific input by 62RR about the presence of terrorists". FIR No. 42/2020, Heerpora police station, was registered on the written report of Major Kush, adjutant of 62 Rashtriya Rifles. The Army's report claimed recovery of two pistols, magazines, cartridges and "other objectionable items", and described "three unidentified hardcore terrorists got neutralised" following a cordon and search operation launched the previous day. Brigadier Ajay Katoch, Commander 12 Sector RR, held a press conference the same day.

**The disposal of the bodies — the load-bearing fact.** The bodies were taken to **Baramulla district, 125 km away**, and buried there after "medico-legal formalities including collection of their DNA". **No photographs were released and the three were not identified.** This is the mechanism by which a staged encounter was, for eleven weeks, indistinguishable in the record from a genuine one.

**How it unravelled.** 9 August 2020, three Rajouri families filed missing-person complaints. 15 August, Capt Bhoopendra Singh placed in close arrest. 18 August, Army announced a Court of Inquiry. Late September, DNA matched. First week of October 2020, bodies exhumed from Baramulla and handed over.

## The Court of Inquiry

**18 September 2020** — the Army stated its inquiry had found "prima facie evidence that during the operation, powers vested under the AFSPA [Armed Forces Special Powers Act] 1990 were exceeded", and that disciplinary proceedings under the Army Act had been initiated. It also found the "Do's and Don'ts of Chief of Army Staff (COAS) as approved by the Supreme Court have been contravened".

**COULD NOT ESTABLISH: the Court of Inquiry document itself, or any finding beyond the two sentences the Army chose to publish. The full CoI has never been published.**

## The police investigation and chargesheet

Filed **28 December 2020** before the Chief Judicial Magistrate, Shopian. Three accused: Capt Bhoopendra Singh (alias "Major Basheer Khan"), 62 RR; Tabish Nazir Malik, civilian; Bilal Ahmad Lone, civilian, who turned approver and was pardoned. Offences: murder, abduction, destruction of evidence. Alleged motive: prize money of Rs 20 lakh — the chargesheet says Singh "furnished false information to mislead the senior officers and for getting lodgment of FIR done to tailor fit his motive for grabbing prize money in furtherance of criminal conspiracy."

**The Army contradicted the motive on the record**, 11 January 2021: "It is clarified that Indian Army has no system of cash awards for its personnel for any acts in combat situations or otherwise in the line of duty."

**Documented gaps in the police investigation.** No financial trail for the Rs 20 lakh was traced. The source of the planted weapons was never established. The statements of Major Kush and Major Amit Kumar Rai were never recorded, the chargesheet citing "in view of their transfer to the frontiers". The four soldiers who admitted firing on Singh's orders were listed as witnesses, not accused. The owner of the car used said "some soldiers" took it; those soldiers were never identified.

Chargesheet length is reported as "more than 200 pages" by the outlet that examined it and as 1,400 pages elsewhere. **Unreconciled — treat page count as unestablished.**

## The disposal — the crux

| Step | Date |
|---|---|
| Killings | 18 July 2020 |
| Army announces AFSPA powers "exceeded" | 18 September 2020 |
| Police chargesheet | 28 December 2020 |
| Court-martial proceedings announced | early April 2022 |
| Proceedings conclude | 16 January 2023 |
| Guilty on six charges including murder; life imprisonment | 17 January 2023 |
| **AFT suspends the life sentence and grants bail** | **9 November 2023** |
| **Army confirming authority confirms findings, conviction, cashiering** | **11 November 2023** |

**Note the sequence: the Tribunal suspended the sentence two days before the Army's confirming authority confirmed it.** The AFT refused to stay the conviction or the cashiering; only the custodial element was suspended.

The form of court martial is inconsistent in the record — announced in April 2022 as a General Court Martial, reported at verdict as a Summary General Court Martial. Unresolved.

**What the AFT held** (bench: Justice Rajendra Menon, Chairperson; Lt Gen C. P. Mohanty), as quoted in press readings of the order:
- "Prima facie, based on the material available on record we are convinced that the likelihood of the applicant being acquitted after hearing of this appeal cannot be ruled out."
- The confession was recorded in close arrest and "has not been recorded in accordance to the requirement of law", in breach of ss. 24-27 of the Indian Evidence Act.
- "The statement of the applicant in the Summary of Evidence recorded under Rule 22 is totally different from the statement recorded in the COI."
- On the approver: "based on the statement of a co-accused, a conviction cannot be ordered." The second civilian co-accused had turned hostile.
- On the senior officers: with 391 rounds of AK-47, 125 rounds of LMG and grenades expended, the Commanding Officer's claim of ignorance "seems to be totally untrustworthy"; and "there cannot be any motive for the applicant to eliminate three civilians and conduct such an operation without the knowledge of PW-3 [Commanding Officer]."
- On deleted evidence: testimony pointing to "prior information of the operation and permission from the CO" and to "deletion of chats from WhatsApp group".
- The bench expressly limited itself: its findings are for bail and "shall not cause any prejudice to any of the parties at the time of trial".

**Singh's own case:** that he "obediently complied with the orders of his commanding officer" and was "made the sacrificial lamb due to intense political pressure, media scrutiny and also to ensure the position and promotions of senior Army officers did not get affected".

**CURRENT DISPOSAL, as precisely as the record allows.** Conviction stands. Cashiering stands and was confirmed 11 November 2023; Singh loses pension and all benefits. Life sentence suspended since 9 November 2023; Singh at liberty on bail. **COULD NOT ESTABLISH any final judgment on the substantive appeal.** The last established position is a suspended sentence and a pending appeal.

## AFSPA section 7 and the civil trial

Section 7 of the AFSPA (J&K) Act 1990 requires prior Central Government sanction to prosecute. The police investigation "did not lead to any arrest, as the armed forces have immunity from prosecution in a civilian court"; the chargesheet itself records that AFSPA gave the captain immunity from police arrest though he was interrogated.

**COULD NOT ESTABLISH — whether a section 7 sanction request in respect of Capt Bhoopendra Singh was ever forwarded to the Ministry of Defence, and if so its outcome. This is the single most important unfilled gap in this part. It must be treated as unknown, not as refusal.**

**Civil trial:** last established position is July 2021 — thirteen hearings since February 2021, **charges not yet framed**. The father of Mohammed Ibrar: "I don't even know the government lawyer who is fighting my case." No subsequent status established.

**Compensation:** Rs 5 lakh ex-gratia to each family plus a promise of government employment; families reported still being moved between offices three years later. RELAYED. **The government order under which the ex-gratia was sanctioned could not be established** — which connects directly to part 10, where a question about delays in SRO-43 ex-gratia to families of victims of violence was deleted from the J&K Assembly in October 2025 because the Home Department is under the Lieutenant Governor.

## CASE FOR — as the Army and the defence would put it, in their own terms

The institution investigated itself and did so seriously. Within a month of the families coming forward the Army convened a Court of Inquiry; within two months it publicly announced that its own personnel had exceeded AFSPA powers and contravened the COAS Do's and Don'ts — an admission no external body compelled. It then ran the matter to a court martial, convicted the officer on six charges including murder, awarded the maximum sentence available, cashiered him dishonourably, and confirmed both conviction and sentence. That is a functioning accountability chain, and it moved faster than the civil trial, which after several years had not framed charges.

On the merits, the prosecution's theory does not hold. The alleged motive was contradicted on the record by the Army, which has no cash-award system, and no money was traced. An independent judicial forum presided over by a former Chief Justice examined the record and found the conviction rested on a confession taken in close arrest contrary to the Evidence Act and on the statement of a co-accused, which cannot found a conviction — with nothing left if both are discarded. Suspending a custodial sentence pending appeal while leaving conviction and cashiering intact is ordinary criminal-justice practice, not impunity.

## CASE AGAINST — as the families and their supporters would put it, in their own terms

Three labourers were taken, shot, dressed with planted weapons, stripped of their identities, labelled "hardcore terrorists", and buried 125 km away without photographs or names. Had three families 160 km away not filed missing-person reports, the record would have shown three dead militants for ever. That is not an aberration in the accounting; it is a demonstration that the accounting can be manufactured.

Accountability then stopped exactly where it began to threaten anyone senior. The two majors most central to the case could not be interviewed because they had been transferred "to the frontiers". The four soldiers who admitted firing were made witnesses, not accused. The soldiers who took the car were never identified. The source of the planted weapons was never traced. The court martial was closed, so no one outside the Army can test its evidence. And when the Tribunal found the Commanding Officer's ignorance "totally untrustworthy" and found evidence of deleted battalion chats, the consequence was not that the inquiry widened upward — it was that the one convicted man walked out on bail. Meanwhile the civil prosecution, the only forum families can participate in, is immobilised by section 7. The compensation is Rs 5 lakh and a job that took years. Whatever else this is, it is not a case that closed.

## DIFFERENT FACTS OR DIFFERENT WEIGHTINGS?

**Mostly different weightings of agreed facts, with one genuine factual dispute — and it is not the one people argue about.**

- **No side disputes the core fact.** That the three dead were civilian labourers and the encounter was staged is common ground between the Army's Court of Inquiry, the police chargesheet, the court-martial verdict, and the AFT, which nowhere suggests the three were militants. **This is unusual and must be recorded as such: the state's own institution established the staging. Treating this as a contested killing would be behind the evidence.**
- **The genuine factual dispute is the scope of responsibility** — did Capt Singh act alone, or on the CO's orders with battalion knowledge? Here the two sides assert incompatible facts. The Army's prosecution theory (rogue captain, cash motive) and Singh's defence (obeying orders, scapegoat) cannot both be true. Note the awkwardness stage 3 must preserve: the AFT's order sits on the *defence* side of this dispute while being cited by critics as vindication.
- **The remaining disagreement is weighting.** Both sides accept: conviction confirmed, cashiering confirmed, custodial sentence suspended, appeal pending, civil trial stalled. One side weights that the conviction happened at all; the other that nobody is in prison and nobody above a captain was touched.
- **A methodological warning.** The AFT's reasons are evidentiary and procedural, not exculpatory on the underlying facts, and the AFT said so itself. Using the order as evidence that the encounter was genuine would be a misreading. Using it as evidence that the officer escaped consequence is accurate but incomplete, since conviction and cashiering stand.
