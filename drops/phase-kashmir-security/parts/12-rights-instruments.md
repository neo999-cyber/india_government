# 12 — RIGHTS INSTRUMENTS: custodial deaths, NHRC, AFSPA sanction, JKCCS, APDP, OHCHR

This part carries quantity **(i) custodial deaths and deaths in security-force custody** for the source-instrument map, plus the instruments that alone ever carried perpetrator attribution for civilian deaths.

**Unusually well-sourced.** Most of what follows is RETRIEVED primary: the Protection of Human Rights Act from NHRC's own server, the J&K Reorganisation Act Fifth Schedule, NCRB table footnotes, NHRC's 1993-1997 custodial-death guidelines, the JKCCS/APDP Annual Human Rights Review 2019, the SHRC's 2011 unmarked-graves enquiry report, and the OHCHR reports. Exceptions are marked.

## 1. NCRB — how a custodial death is bounded

**Where it lives:** *Crime in India*, Chapter 16A, titled "Custodial Crimes & Complaints Against **Police** Personnel". Table 16A.6 in the 2022 volume is titled "Cases Registered against **State Police Personnel** for Human Right Violation".

**Bounding notes, verbatim, identical across the 2019, 2020 and 2022 volumes:**
> "Note: Persons Not on Remand includes Persons Arrested and yet to be produced before court"
> "Note: Persons in remand includes Persons in Police/Judicial Remand"
> "● As per data provided by States/UTs"
> "●States/UTs may not be compared purely on the basis of crime figures" (2020 onward)

**What this establishes.** NCRB's custodial-death universe is **police custody and lockup only**. The remand/not-remand split is a *procedural* split inside police custody, not a police-versus-judicial split — judicial-remand deaths sit inside the police chapter because the police were the reporting unit. And the data are not NCRB's observations; they are what State and UT police forces returned.

**Does it include suicide, illness and natural death? YES — and they dominate.** Table 16A.3 "Reasons of Custodial Deaths" column heads, verbatim: "Suicide | Death due to illness/ Death in Hospitals during treatment | Injuries sustained prior to police custody | Injuries sustained during the police custody due to physical assault by police | While Escaping from Custody | Road Accidents/Journey Connected with Investigation | Others | Total".

All-India 2019: suicide 33, illness/hospital 36, prior injuries 1, **assault by police 2**, escaping 5, road accidents 4, others 4 — total 85.
All-India 2022: suicide 31, illness/hospital 32, prior injuries 1, **assault by police 1**, escaping 7, road accidents 0, others 3 — total 75.

**So the single category corresponding to what most readers mean by a custodial death — killed by police in custody — is one column of seven, registering one or two cases a year nationally.** Any authored series must name which column it is using.

**ARMY AND CAPF CUSTODY IS OUTSIDE THE FRAME ENTIRELY.** There is no category, column, row or footnote anywhere in Chapter 16A for deaths in the custody of the Army, Rashtriya Rifles, CRPF, BSF or any other central armed police force. The unit of account is the State/UT police and every row is a State or UT. **A death in Army or Rashtriya Rifles custody in J&K has no cell to be recorded in.** NCRB nowhere *says* it excludes armed-forces custody — it simply has no such category. This is a silent structural absence, and stage 3 must not attribute a stated reason to NCRB, because NCRB has not stated one.

**THE J&K SERIES IS BROKEN AT 31 OCTOBER 2019.** J&K appears as a **STATE** in the *Crime in India* 2019 tables and as a **UT** thereafter, with Ladakh split out as a separate UT. The break is in both *Crime in India* Tables 16A.1-16A.3 and *Prison Statistics India* Table 8.1. **Any J&K custodial-death series must break at this date**, and the post-2019 J&K figure covers a smaller territory than the pre-2019 one.

## 2. NHRC — a statutory ceiling on what it can count in J&K

### Section 19 of the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 — verbatim, RETRIEVED from NHRC's own hosted copy

> "**19. Procedure with respect to armed forces.**—(1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, while dealing with complaints of violation of human rights by members of the armed forces, the Commission shall adopt the following procedure, namely:—
> (a) it may, either on its own motion or on receipt of a petition, seek a report from the Central Government;
> (b) after the receipt of the report, it may, either not proceed with the complaint or, as the case may be, make its recommendations to that Government."

**What section 19 removes.** The general investigative machinery of the Act — s.13 civil-court powers, s.14 investigation, s.17 inquiry — is disapplied by the opening words "Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act". For armed-forces complaints NHRC has exactly two moves: ask the Central Government for a report, then either drop it or recommend. **It cannot investigate, cannot summon, cannot compel, and cannot itself establish a fact. Its output rests on the account supplied by the government whose forces are complained of.**

**"Armed forces" is defined widely.** s.2(1)(a), verbatim: "'armed forces' means the naval, military and air forces and includes **any other armed forces of the Union**". That tail pulls the CRPF, BSF, ITBP, SSB, CISF, NSG and Assam Rifles into s.19's restricted procedure alongside the Army. **The forces most present in J&K are the forces NHRC may not investigate.**

### Before 31 October 2019, the Act barely applied to J&K

PHRA s.1(2) as originally enacted, verbatim: "Provided that it shall apply to the State of Jammu and Kashmir only insofar as it pertains to the matters relatable to any of the entries enumerated in List I or List III in the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution as applicable to that State."

Since police and public order were State List subjects in J&K, ordinary policing complaints there were outside NHRC and inside the State Commission. Section 21(5) drew the corresponding line on the State Commission.

**Net effect pre-2019: a jurisdictional pincer.** NHRC covered Union and Concurrent subjects; the SHRC covered State and Concurrent subjects; and s.19 removed the armed forces from NHRC's investigative reach on either side of that line. **Nobody with investigative powers could reach the Army or the CAPFs.**

### The change on 31 October 2019, with the legal instrument identified

J&K Reorganisation Act 2019, Fifth Schedule Table-1, entry 86, verbatim: "The Protection of Human Rights Act, 1994. Proviso to sub-section (2) of section 1 shall be omitted."

So the J&K carve-out was deleted on the appointed day and NHRC acquired ordinary jurisdiction over the two new UTs — **while s.19's armed-forces restriction remained untouched.**

### The 24-hour custodial-death intimation regime, and why it does not reach the armed forces

NHRC letter No. 66/SG/NHRC/93 of 14 December 1993, verbatim: a direction to "the **District Magistrates and Superintendents of Police of every district** that they should report to the Secretary General of the Commission about such incidents **within 24 hours of occurrence**… **Failure to report promptly would give rise to presumption that there was an attempt to suppress the incident.**" Extended to judicial custody by letter of 21 June 1995.

**The addressees are District Magistrates and Superintendents of Police.** No Army formation, Rashtriya Rifles unit, CRPF or BSF sector command is an addressee. **A death in Army or CAPF custody in J&K triggers no 24-hour intimation obligation** — and even if reported, s.19 confines NHRC to seeking a report from the Central Government.

Worth carrying for its own sake: NHRC's Chairperson wrote in 1995 that "a systematic attempt is being made to suppress the truth and the report is merely the police version of the incident", and in 1997 that "post-mortem reports appear to be doctored due to influence/pressure to protect the interest of the police/jail officials". **The body responsible for the count says the inputs are unreliable.**

## 3. The termination of the J&K State Human Rights Commission — a first-class finding

**The instrument:** J&K Reorganisation Act 2019, Fifth Schedule **Table-3** ("State laws… which are repealed"), entry 117, verbatim: "The Jammu and Kashmir Protection of Human Rights Act, 1997. XV of 1997". That is the SHRC's enabling statute. Its repeal on 31 October 2019 extinguished the Commission. The same Table repeals the J&K Accountability Commission Act 2002.

**The Act contains no transitional or savings provision for pending complaints.** Roughly **630 pending complaints** — alleging murder, enforced disappearance, rape and other violations by security forces — abated. Their records have reportedly been physically locked in the former SHRC premises since 31 October 2019, established by an RTI reply. **RELAYED — the RTI reply itself was not retrieved.**

Contemporaneous corroboration from the counting body, JKCCS/APDP *Annual Human Rights Review 2019*, RETRIEVED, verbatim:

> "Thousands of complaints and petitions regarding cases of enforced or involuntary disappearances were pending before the SHRC and enquiries into these cases were under process. One of the complaints about 506 cases of disappearances from North Kashmir (filed in 2012) was being monitored SHRC and another complaint about 150 cases from Banihal was also under the observation of Commission but subsequent to the abrogation of special status of Jammu and Kashmir and disbanding of SHRC, hundreds of these families were dejected and deprived of justice. So far the government did not clarify its position on fate of thousands of cases of disappearance filed before the SHRC."

**And the SHRC could not reach the Army either.** OHCHR June 2018, para 95, verbatim: "SHRC did not send a notice to the Indian Army as it does not have jurisdiction over forces controlled by the central government operating in Kashmir."

## 4. AFSPA section 7 — sanction for prosecution

**The figures: 50 requests · 0 granted · 47 refused · 3 pending. Grant rate zero.**

Source: Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 1463, answered 1 January 2018, Ministry of Defence. **The reply PDF itself could NOT be retrieved** (Parliament's file server returned HTTP 500 for that question across the plausible sessions). Its content is established from two independent documents that quote it: OHCHR's June 2018 report (fn 31, 38, 62, 271) and Venkatesh Nayak's RTI application reproduced verbatim in his CIC second appeal, which is RETRIEVED. **Grade this as strongly corroborated but not primary-retrieved. The Annexure listing the 47 individual cases has not been seen.**

OHCHR June 2018, para 53, on the parallel MHA figure, verbatim: "In February 2018, the Union Ministry of Home Affairs informed the Parliament that since 1990 the Jammu and Kashmir Government had sought the sanction of the central government for prosecution of members of the security forces in 50 cases. The central government refused to sanction prosecution in 47 cases, while decisions remained pending in relation to 3 cases as of April 2018."

**A retrieved MHA reply on the Act itself** — Lok Sabha Unstarred Q.4063, 20 March 2018, RETRIEVED from Parliament's file server, verbatim: "There is no proposal to amend the Armed Forces (Jammu and Kashmir) Special Powers Act, 1990. There is no proposal under consideration of Government of India to withdraw the Armed Forces (Jammu and Kashmir) Special Powers Act, 1990 from Jammu and Kashmir." No sanction figures in it.

### The decision rule is held by nobody, on both ministries' own account

From the CIC second appeal, RETRIEVED. The RTI asked for the *procedure, norms, criteria and standards* used to assess evidence — the decision rule, not the case files.

- CPIO, Ministry of Defence, 22 February 2018: "information sought in your RTI application… is **not under the custody of the undersigned CPIO**… The requisite information as sought on behalf of the undersigned CPIO may be treated as **'Nil'**."
- Under Secretary (AG-I), MoD, 26 February 2018: "The requisite information are **not maintained/available in D(AG) section**."
- For CPIO, Indian Army, 16 April 2018: "concerted efforts were made to obtain the information sought by you, however, the same is **not available/held with the concerned agency of this Headquarters**."

**Both ministries denied custody of the same records.** The appellant's own summary, verbatim: "the 1st Respondent Public Authority is claiming that the 2nd Respondent Public Authority is the custodian… whereas the latter has issued a categorical denial that it is the custodian".

### The CIC then sealed the files

CIC final order, 5 June 2020. **RELAYED** — substantial verbatim extracts recovered through press; the order itself was not retrieved. The CIC permitted access to the refusal orders in the 47 cases but refused the case files under s.8(1)(a), verbatim:

> "the facts of the instant case require a **dynamic attribution to the meaning of larger public interest** within the framework of RTI Act by associating **non-disclosure with larger public interest**"
> "**Indisputably, the larger public interest of the State (vis-a-vis its susceptibility to unrest) prevails over that of the alleged victims.**"

**This is the cleanest `withheld` in the phase.** The data exists, disclosure was specifically requested through the statutory route, and it was refused by the appellate body with reasons on the record.

## 5. JKCCS and APDP — the only instruments that ever attributed a perpetrator

### The attribution rule, verbatim from the Annual Human Rights Review 2019 (RETRIEVED via mirror)

> "The year witnessed extrajudicial executions of at least 80 civilians in J&K, besides killings of 159 militants and 129 armed forces."

> "Out of the 80 civilians killed this year, **19 were killed by armed forces**, 17 were killed in cross LOC shelling between Indian and Pakistani armed forces (We have not been able to ascertain the number of killings of civilians in Pakistan administered Kashmir.) While **28 civilians were killed by unidentified gunmen**, 6 were killed by militants, 7 were killed in explosions, one person died after being allegedly hit by a stone and one person (a non-local) died in cross-firing between armed forces and militants."

**Note the discipline this shows.** The single largest bucket is "unidentified gunmen" (28 of 80). JKCCS does not force an attribution it cannot support, and it flags its own out-of-scope boundary in parentheses. **That is better practice than either MHA or SATP.**

Their category architecture includes **Cordon and Search Operations and Raids** as a counted category — the quantity no official instrument counts at all (part 02, A2).

### Their stated method

No formal methodology annex, but a repeated provenance formula: "According to the data compiled by JKCCS and APDP"; "based on media reports and independent verification"; "As per data obtained by JKCCS and APDP **through J&K High Court Registry**"; "In most of the villages and towns that JKCCS and APDP **researchers went**". So: media monitoring, field verification, court-registry records, and own fact-finding, with an annexed case list for detentions.

### They declare their own measurement failure — which is why the 2019 figures are a floor

Verbatim: "Since August 5 2019, no proper research or documentation could be undertaken about the fresh reported cases of enforced or involuntary disappearances… Due to the state imposed clampdown and terror there was **complete information collapse in Kashmir**." And: allegations of destruction of civilian property during night raids "**were not reported**".

**Stage 3 must carry this: the August-December 2019 JKCCS figures are a floor stated as such by the body that produced them.**

### APDP on enforced disappearance, and the definitional switch

APDP: "more than 8000 cases of enforced or involuntary disappearance in Jammu and Kashmir since the inception of conflict in 1989".

The official counterpart, OHCHR June 2018 para 97, verbatim: "the state and central governments say **around 4,000 are missing**, most of whom they allege crossed over to Pakistan-Administered Kashmir. In January 2017, Chief Minister Mehbooba Mufti told the Jammu and Kashmir Assembly that **4,008 'missing persons'** from the state were in Pakistan-Administered Kashmir for arms training."

**This is a definitional switch, not a numerical dispute.** The official instrument counts "missing persons" and assigns a cause (voluntary crossing for arms training). APDP counts "enforced disappearances" and assigns responsibility to state agencies. **They are not measuring the same object.**

**And no official instrument counts enforced disappearance at all**, because it is not an offence. OHCHR 2018 para 100 quotes NHRC's own 2012 observation, verbatim: "enforced disappearance was not codified as a criminal offence in domestic law, nor were extant provisions of law used to deter the practice." India signed the ICPPED on 6 February 2007 and has not ratified it, and "has never allowed a visit" by the UN Working Group on Enforced or Involuntary Disappearances despite a 2011 standing invitation. **This meets `never-defined` on the strict test — the quantity has no legal existence, so it could not be collected even in principle.**

### The SHRC 2011 unmarked-graves report — PRIMARY TEXT RECOVERED

Header, verbatim: "Office of the Sr. Superintendent of Police, J&K SHRC Srinagar. NO: SHRCPIW/2011/1000 Dated: 02.7.2011. Subject: Enquiry Report of Unmarked Graves in north Kashmir."

Method, verbatim: the Commission "took **suo-moto cognizance** of the matter on 9.4.2008"; the team "visited almost all the places in North Kashmir"; and, crucially, "At some places, though the witnesses corroborated verbally the existence of un-marked graves, but **were not ready to record their statements in black and white due to their security concerns**… **We have not included any statistics of un-marked graves given by such people in our report.**" **So the SHRC's figure is itself a floor, by its own stated rule.**

The finding, verbatim summary:
> "Total number of un-identified dead bodies (Claimed to be dead bodies of unidentified militants by local Police & handed over to locals for their burial) in various un-marked graveyards of North Kashmir = **2730**
> Dead bodies identified later on as of locals out of the preceding figure = **0574**
> Number of dead bodies identified as locals & shifted to their native graveyards after exhumation = 017
> Number of dead bodies identified as locals but still buried in the above mentioned graveyards = 0557
> Number of dead bodies still buried in the above mentioned graveyards as un-identified = **2156**"

**Note for stage 3: ledger record L-0004 already covers this** and currently cites SATP at T4. **The primary SHRC text is now recoverable and the record should be upgraded, not duplicated.** The OHCHR-cited URL for the report is dead; the text survives at Kashmir Life.

The SHRC also recorded an absence in its own words: "Even for 464 unidentified dead bodies which the Government is accounting for… they have not maintained any **identification profile**, so that same could be compared with identification profiles of NOKs of various disappeared persons in the Valley to negate/establish any link." And on its written enquiries to police station heads: "**no reply has been received till this time.**"

## 6. OHCHR, and India's rejection

**The stated limitation, in OHCHR's own words:** "As OHCHR was denied access to Kashmir, it was not possible to directly verify allegations" (2018); "As OHCHR has continued to be denied access to Kashmir…" (2019).

**India's rejection:** the reports are "fallacious, tendentious and motivated" and "a selective compilation of largely unverified information". RETRIEVED from mea.gov.in.

**The structure of that exchange should be recorded exactly, because it is circular:** the rejection's premise — that the information is unverified — is downstream of the access denial by the rejecting party. Neither side is being dishonest; the point is that **no procedure exists by which the dispute could be resolved**, and stage 3 should record it as a dispute about access, not about numbers.

**A dating that belongs in the record.** India's rejection rested in part on the sufficiency of J&K's own human rights commission. The OHCHR update was published 8 July 2019. **The J&K State Human Rights Commission was abolished on 31 October 2019 — 115 days later.**

**B5 deserves emphasis because it is the phase's clearest `never-defined`.** It is not that nobody has studied enforced disappearance in J&K — a great deal has been documented. It is that the quantity has no legal existence in India, so no official instrument could count it even in principle, and the official instrument that does exist counts a *different object* ("missing persons") with a *built-in causal attribution* (crossed over for arms training).
