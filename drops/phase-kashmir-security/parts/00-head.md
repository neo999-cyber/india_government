# 00-head — INDEX, LIMITS, AND THE LEDGER QUESTION

This is the head note for stage 2 of phase 11, `kashmir-security`. It carries three things and nothing else: an index of the parts that exist on disk; a consolidated statement of what stage 2 could not establish; and the answer to the ledger question, per quantity, which the operator reserved for a decision of their own.

**There is no assembled `research.md`, by design.** The research for this phase exists only as the parts listed below. Nothing summarises them, and this head note does not attempt to — it indexes them and answers one question across them. **A downstream reader must read the parts.** Any claim taken from this phase must be traced to the part that holds it and to the document that part cites, because the evidence grade in this subject varies by more than it usually does: some findings rest on ministry PDFs retrieved and re-checkable from a URL, and some rest on a press account of a press conference. Those must never be treated alike, and only the parts record which is which.

## 2. WHAT STAGE 2 COULD NOT ESTABLISH

Consolidated from every part's own could-not-establish section plus part 11. **The three classes below are different facts and must not be merged downstream.** A source that does not exist supports an absence record. A source that exists but was unreachable from this environment supports nothing at all. A source that has been taken down is a finding in its own right.

## 3. THE LEDGER QUESTION

**The question, as the operator framed it.** For each quantity, does **any single instrument** place **both sides' facts on one ledger** — one document or dataset recording, **on the same page and the same basis**, both the harm attributed to militants and the harm attributed to the state?

**The overall answer, stated before the detail and without rounding.** Three instruments in this phase are genuinely two-sided in some respect. **Two of them have stopped, and the third counts the state's side as activity rather than as harm.** Everything else is one-sided by construction. But the qualifications matter more than the headline, so the per-quantity answers below are the operative ones, and each is grounded in a named part and a named document as the routing decision requires.

### 3.0 The trap cases — instruments that appear two-sided and are not

These must be settled first, because three of the per-quantity answers below turn on them and each of these tables will read as two-sided to anyone who meets it without the definitional work.

**(i) MHA's "Civilians killed" column is one number covering two causes, and attributes neither.** From AR 2023-24 the footnote reads, verbatim: *"[Civilians killed includes civilians killed in terrorist-initiated incidents and civilians killed in encounter/counter Terrorism operations]"*. That is not a two-sided ledger; it is a **merger**. A reader sees one figure and cannot recover what share of it the state caused. The arithmetic residual between the pre- and post-restatement bases (16, 5, 1, 0 for 2018-2021) **is not a bridge and must never be published as the count of civilians killed in security-force operations** — the 2021 residual is zero, and the security-force figure for 2020 also moved across the same restatement, so the restatement carries ordinary revisions too. **This is the exact case the ledger question is asking about, and the answer is that the appearance of comprehensiveness is produced by discarding the attribution.** (Parts 09 PC-2, 01 break 2b, 06b §3)

**(ii) SATP's fatalities table carries three populations and no perpetrator breakdown at all.** Its columns are `Year | Incidents of Killing | Civilians | Security Forces | Terrorists/Insurgents/Extremists | Not Specified | Total`. Three populations on one line reads as a balanced account. **It is not one: SATP never says who killed the civilians, publishes no definition of "civilian", and publishes no methodology of any kind.** Its entire methodology statement is the datasheet footer: *"Source: Compiled from news reports and are provisional."* (Part 06b §6)

**(iii) MHA's pre-2022-23 "Incidents" column is a composite named for only one of its two components.** `TII + Encounters/CT Ops = "Terrorist Incidents"` exactly, four years out of four. So the state's own operations were inside a number labelled for militant activity, invisibly. This is the mirror image of the trap: an instrument that is covertly two-sided while presenting as one-sided, which makes the series respond to the state's operational tempo as much as to threat. (Parts 09 PC-1, 01 break 2a)

**(iv) Even a nominally two-sided table here is compiled by one of the parties.** PIB attributes the series verbatim to **"(Source: CID, J&K"** and MHA's parliamentary reply of 24 July 2024 to **"(Source: UT of J&K)"**, while the Annual Reports carrying the identical figures state no source at all. **The producer is the Criminal Investigation Department of the J&K Police — a party to the operations being counted — and the publisher is the ministry to which it ultimately reports.** Two-sidedness of format does not survive one-sidedness of production, and stage 3 should not let it. (Parts 06b §2, 09 PC-10)

### 3.1 Per-quantity answers

**(a) Incidents — PARTIALLY, and only from a document posted 11 October 2023.**

From MHA Annual Report 2022-23 the J&K table carries `Terrorist Initiated Incidents` and `Encounters/Counter Terror operations` as separate columns, same table, same basis, same years. That **is** a single instrument recording militant-initiated events and state-initiated events side by side, and it is the closest thing to a two-sided incident ledger that exists. Three qualifications, none of them optional:

1. **It is two-sided as to *activity*, not as to *harm*.** An encounter count records that the state acted. It records nothing about what the state's action did.
2. **It begins in 2018.** No decomposition is published for any earlier year, and before AR 2022-23 the two were merged (trap (iii)).
3. **The state's most common form of contact with the civilian population is in neither column.** Cordon-and-search operations are uncounted by anyone. **The instrument counts the outcomes of an activity it does not count.** (Parts 02 A2, 06b §1)

Named documents: AR 2022-23 `AnnualReportEngLish_11102023.pdf`, AR 2023-24 `AnnualReport_27122024.pdf`, AR 2024-25 `AREnglish_24032026.pdf`. Held in parts 01, 03, 09.

**(b) Civilians killed — YES, one instrument, and it is non-official and it stopped in 2020.**

**The instrument is the JKCCS/APDP *Annual Human Rights Review*.** Its 2019 edition, RETRIEVED, records in one passage, verbatim: *"The year witnessed extrajudicial executions of at least 80 civilians in J&K, besides killings of 159 militants and 129 armed forces"*, and then splits the 80 by perpetrator: *"19 were killed by armed forces, 17 were killed in cross LOC shelling… 28 civilians were killed by unidentified gunmen, 6 were killed by militants, 7 were killed in explosions…"*. **That is both sides' harm, on one page, on one basis, with the perpetrator named — and with the discipline of leaving the largest bucket unattributed rather than forcing it.** It is the only such instrument found in this phase for this quantity.

**No official instrument is two-sided on this quantity, in any year.** MHA has never published a column for civilians killed by security forces under any heading in any Annual Report 2008-09 to 2024-25; its parliamentary series is bounded to deaths "due to terrorist attacks"; and its current Annual Report column is the merger at trap (i). GTD cannot carry it (non-state actors only). UCDP records Government-of-India one-sided violence in three country-years ever, none after 2002. ACLED could in principle and was not retrieved.

**And the two-sided instrument terminated.** JKCCS ceased publishing after 2019, and its own 2019 edition declares a measurement failure for the period after 5 August 2019 — *"complete information collapse in Kashmir"* — so even its final figures are a floor stated as such by the body that produced them. **After 2019 no instrument of any provenance publishes the perpetrator split for civilian deaths in J&K.** Kashmir Law and Justice Project is a live candidate successor and was not investigated at all; that check should be run before this is authored as settled. (Parts 12 §5, 00 §3(b), 00 §3b)

**(c) Security-force personnel killed — NO in the strict sense, and this is the answer that most needs stating carefully rather than rounded.**

MHA's J&K table places `SFs killed` and `Terrorists killed` on the same page, on the same basis, for the same years. On a loose reading that is two-sided: harm suffered by the state and harm inflicted by the state, in one document.

**It fails the strict test for two reasons and stage 3 should adopt the strict reading.** First, `Terrorists killed` is not a neutral record of harm inflicted by the state — it is a record of harm the state has already classified as legitimate, and the classification is made by the force that did the killing, before independent identification (part 08). Second, **MHA's own summary of the conflict was explicitly one-sided in the opposite direction**: the cumulative ledger published from at least AR 2008-09 to AR 2020-21 ran *"14,091 Civilians and 5,356 Security Force (SF) personnel"* and **never had a militants-killed term in any of the thirty years it was published**. When the ministry compressed the conflict into one sentence, it dropped the population its own annual table counted. (Parts 01 break 3, 09 PC-6)

**What must nonetheless be carried, because a record treating every measure here as equally compromised would be wrong:** this is the one quantity on which the instruments agree. MHA against SATP runs 91/95, 80/78, 62/56, 42/45 for 2018-2021 — within about 10% every year — because the dead are named, pensioned and publicly honoured. (Part 00 §3(c), part 03)

**(d) Militants / terrorists killed — NO.**

No instrument places the killing of militants beside any accounting of whether the killings were lawful, correctly identified, or inquired into. The relevant counter-facts exist and are in **different documents held by different ministries**: AFSPA s.7 sanction (50 requests, 0 granted, 47 refused, 3 pending — a Ministry of Defence reply); magisterial inquiries (506 since 1990, one completed, that report never submitted — an advocacy petition to the SHRC, not a government return); and the identification record (the SHRC's 2011 finding of 2,730 unidentified bodies, of which 574 were later identified as locals). **None of these has ever appeared on the same page as a "Terrorists killed" figure**, and MHA has never disaggregated the column by local and foreign in any of seventeen years.

**And the error in this column is structurally one-directional.** Bodies wrongly recorded as militants are buried far away and unphotographed; bodies wrongly recorded as civilians would be claimed immediately. Amshipora put three civilians in the 2020 "terrorists killed" figure for eleven weeks and was corrected only because three families 160 km away filed missing-person reports. (Parts 08, 12 §4-5, 09 PC-16)

**(e) Infiltration — NO, and the question is close to inapplicable.**

Every infiltration quantity measures an adversary flow: `Infiltration attempts` and `Net Estimated infiltration` (MHA), `Infiltration Bid Eliminated` (MoD, LoC-only, Army-only). There is no state-side term and no document proposes one. The nearest analogue, ceasefire violations, is one-sided in the same way: MHA's table counts *"incidents of Ceasefire Violation/Cross Border Firing"* with the other party implicitly the actor, and **no Indian instrument counts firing initiated from the Indian side.** MHA's series is discontinued after 2022 and the counted CFV series was replaced by a prose clause in 2021-23. (Parts 00 §3(e), 01 breaks 4 and 7, 09 PC-5, PC-15)

**(f) Local recruitment — the question does not arise, because there is no ledger.**

Zero occurrences in seventeen MHA Annual Reports and in every parliamentary reply retrieved. What circulates are press-conference claims that contradict each other — 2018 as 187, 218 and "over 200"; 2022 as 100, 110 and 130; 2023 as 10 and 22. **There is no instrument to be one-sided or two-sided.** (Parts 04, 02 A3)

**(g) Encounter operations — NO, and this is the sharpest one-sidedness in the phase.**

The `Encounters/CT Ops.` column from AR 2022-23 is the only column in MHA's entire J&K instrument that counts what the state did. **Nothing counts what those operations produced.** There is no count of civilians killed in them separately from the merged column; no count of cordon-and-search operations; no count of magisterial inquiries ordered, completed or published; no count of AFSPA s.7 sanction requests in the same instrument; and the J&K chapter of AR 2024-25 contains **zero occurrences** of "human right", "NHRC", "custody", "custodial", "compensation", "ex-gratia", "inquiry" or "magisterial" across roughly 46,000 characters. **MHA's annual accounting of the J&K conflict contains no accountability vocabulary of any kind.** An instrument that counts 731 operations over seven years and has no term for their consequences is one-sided by construction, not by omission. (Parts 00 §PENDING, 02 A2 and A10, 06b §1, 09 PC-13)

**(h) Pellet and crowd-control injuries — YES, once, for deaths only, and it was never repeated. This is the finding that most resists rounding.**

**The instrument is MHA's answer to Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 511, 7 February 2018**, RETRIEVED at `https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2018-pdfs/rs-07022018-ENG/511.pdf`. Annex-I is a single month-by-month grid, calendar 2015 to 2017, with two columns, headed verbatim:

> "No. of protestors/ persons killed by pellets | No. of Security Force personnel killed during Law & Order protests"

Totals 17 and 2. **That is both sides' harm, in one table, on one basis, on one page, published by the Union government** — harm done to civilians by a state crowd-control weapon and harm suffered by the state's personnel in the same protests. **It is the only official Indian document located in this phase that does this.**

Four qualifications, all of which must travel with it:

1. **It counts deaths only.** The same answer refused the injury figure — the question asked for "injured or killed" — with the words *"Further details in the matter cannot be disclosed in the interest of the national security."* **The two-sided ledger exists precisely to the extent that both terms are small.**
2. **It covers three calendar years and was not continued.** No later MHA reply on this quantity was located.
3. **The civilian column has no counterpart on the injury side, and the injury side is where the harm is.** The J&K Chief Minister told the Assembly that 6,221 people were injured by pellets in under eight months; the state's health administration filed a district-wise injury list on affidavit in the High Court; neither was ever published, and the assembly route terminated in 2018.
4. **The word "pellet" occurs zero times in every MHA Annual Report 2013-14 to 2024-25.** The two-sided ledger exists in a parliamentary answer and nowhere in the flagship. (Part 07 throughout; part 09 PC-7)

**(i) Custodial deaths — NO, and the one-sidedness runs in the opposite direction from everything else, which is why the two can never be combined.**

NCRB's *Crime in India* Chapter 16A — "Custodial Crimes & Complaints Against **Police** Personnel" — is a state-harm-only instrument. It records deaths in custody and, in Table 16A.3, their causes, including the column *"Injuries sustained during the police custody due to physical assault by police"*. There is no militant-attributed term anywhere in it and there could not be. **It is the mirror image of MHA's instrument: MHA counts harm to the state and harm the state calls legitimate; NCRB counts harm by the police and nothing else.**

They cannot be joined into a ledger for four reasons: the unit of account is the State/UT police, so **Army and CAPF custody has no cell to be recorded in**; the "assault by police" column registers one or two cases a year nationally, out of seven cause-columns dominated by suicide and illness; the data are not NCRB's observations but *"As per data provided by States/UTs"*; and **the J&K series breaks at 31 October 2019**, when J&K appears as a State in the 2019 volume and as a UT thereafter with Ladakh split out. (Part 12 §1)

### 3.2 What follows, for the routing decision

**Summarised:** two-sided instruments exist for (b), for (h), and in a weak activity-only sense for (a). The instrument for (b) is non-official and stopped in 2020. The instrument for (h) is official and retrieved but covers deaths only, for three years, and was never repeated. The instrument for (a) counts the state's side as operations and omits the state's commonest operation entirely. **(c), (d), (e), (g) and (i) have no two-sided instrument at all, and (f) has no instrument at all.**

**The consequence, stated plainly.** For six of the nine quantities, and for the post-2020 period of a seventh, **a two-sided account of the Jammu and Kashmir conflict is an assembly the researcher makes. No source makes it.** That is not a claim that the sources are wrong. It is a claim about what they are: each was built to answer one party's question, and the joining of them — deciding that MHA's "civilians killed" and JKCCS's perpetrator split and NCRB's custody column describe parts of one thing — is an act performed by whoever builds the ledger, on their own authority, and it should be visible as such wherever this instrument renders it.

**And the corollary that stage 3 should carry with equal weight.** Because the assembly is the researcher's, the instrument must be unusually explicit about which side each source can speak for. Part 03's fairness finding is the model: security-force deaths are the one measure both families of instrument agree on, and a record treating every quantity in this domain as equally compromised would be as wrong as one that treated them as interchangeable.
