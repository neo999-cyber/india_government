# 08 — CONTESTED: the disputed identity of those killed, and burial away from home districts

This part carries the mechanism that makes part 03's counting dispute concrete. Everything here is RELAYED unless marked otherwise; the primary instruments — magisterial inquiry reports, the burial order if one exists — are precisely what does not exist in retrievable form, and that is the finding.

---

## 1. The burial practice, from April 2020

**Start date: April 2020.** First visible in the Keran sector operation, Kupwara, 1-6 April 2020, in which five militants were killed and, departing from usual practice, neither police nor Army identified them; all five were buried at an undisclosed location in Kupwara.

### The critical finding on FORM: there is no published order

Inspector General of Police, Kashmir, **Vijay Kumar**, April 2020, in his own words:

> "If we allow for identification at encounter sites and permit burial at their native places, huge gatherings might spread infection of Covid-19."

> "As per several orders of the government of the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir in view of the Disaster Management Act, we have to ensure strict lockdown even during burial. To avoid such situations we are doing burial at safer and isolated places."

And, in the same reporting, a senior police officer speaking anonymously:

> "It's not a policy now but it should become a policy."

**Assessment.** No dedicated written order or circular establishing a burial policy has ever been published. What exists is (i) generic Disaster Management Act 2005 lockdown orders which nowhere on their face concern the disposal of encounter dead, and (ii) an operational practice which the police chief described as flowing from them, and which a senior officer inside the system said in the same month was *not* a policy. **A practice applied to hundreds of bodies for six years rests on no published instrument.** A lawyer acting for a family seeking exhumation was told: "No written order has been given to us. They just told us verbally to move an application before the DC, Baramulla."

**This is recorded as an absence, not a retrieval failure**, because the responsible officials themselves point to lockdown orders rather than to a burial instrument. No J&K Home Department order, police circular or SOP number governing the burial of encounter dead could be established.

### The stated reasons, in officials' own words

- **COVID-19 and lockdown compliance** — IGP Vijay Kumar, above. Asked whether it would outlast COVID: "I can't comment on any future course of action but presently we are doing this in view of the Covid-related lockdown and for the safety of the people."
- **Preventing glamorisation** — the same officer, later: "We have not only stopped the spread of COVID infection but also stopped glamorising terrorists and avoided potential law and order problems."
- **The rationale predates COVID.** A confidential J&K Crime Branch report of 2017 to the DGP: "[The] massive gatherings that are being witnessed in funerals of killed local militants post-2016 unrest is a serious concern which has to be addressed… These gatherings romanticise and glamourise the terrorists and give boost to militancy." Public funerals for *foreign* militants had already been prohibited since October 2015.
- **As put to the Supreme Court, 29 August 2022:** J&K police counsel said returning bodies was "not feasible", warning that "if this is allowed, the high court will be filled with similar prayers seeking to perform last rites", and that burial in remote border areas prevents glorification and avoids influencing "young minds".

**The stated reason moved from public health to public order without the practice changing.** That shift is itself evidence and both sides use it.

### How many bodies — three counts that do not reconcile

- **158 (2020) and ~180 (2021)** — figures put before the Supreme Court, 29 August 2022.
- **~140 in 2020** — Pulitzer Center reporting.
- **"at least 580" since April 2020**, "according to official records", as at February 2023 — Al Jazeera.

**All RELAYED, all attributed to official sources, none reconcilable. No published series exists.** Do not author a figure.

Burial sites named across sources: Baramulla; Zachaldara and Wudder Payeen, Handwara, Kupwara; Sonamarg; Ganderbal; Uri (foreign militants, pre-2020).

### Whether families were permitted last rites — a live factual dispute

**The police account (IGP Vijay Kumar, April 2020):** bodies brought to Srinagar for post-mortem and DNA; families given a travel pass to attend identification; a second chance at the burial site if not identified in Srinagar; "If they identify [the bodies] in the presence of a magistrate, we allow them to participate in burial as per religious practices… We take photographs for record. Even unidentified killed terrorists are being buried as per religious practices." He gave dated worked examples (Melhoora, 22 April 2020; Pulwama, 25 April 2020).

**The families' account, in the same reporting:** families of the Kupwara five and the 17 April Shopian dead said they were not told where the burials took place and no DNA samples were taken from them; the Baramulla DC said he had "no proof" they were related; families doing the rounds of offices were themselves booked under an FIR for lockdown violation. Rafi Ahmad Dar: "The police are 100% wrong when they say the militants are unidentified."

**These cannot both be generally true.** The most defensible reading is that the safeguards exist on paper and are applied inconsistently — but **the instrument should record this as disputed fact and not resolve it.**

### Exhumations, and whether the policy was relaxed

- **Amshipora, October 2020** — exhumed and handed over after DNA matching, administratively, not on court order.
- **Hyderpora, 18 November 2021** — bodies of Mohammad Altaf Bhat and Dr Mudasir Gul exhumed hours after the LG ordered a magisterial inquiry.
- **Aamir Magray** — J&K High Court ordered exhumation plus Rs 5 lakh compensation and grave access; the **Supreme Court, 12 September 2022 (Surya Kant and J. B. Pardiwala JJ), set aside the exhumation direction while upholding compensation and grave access**; review dismissed January 2023. The Court's reasoning as quoted: "After a body has been buried, it is considered to be under the custody of law… Once buried a body should not be disturbed"; "A court will not order disinterment of body unless it is shown that disinterment is in the interest of justice". **The primary judgment could not be retrieved — indiankanoon returned 403. Retrieval failure, not absence.**
- **Rashid Ahmad Mughal, Ganderbal, 2026** — buried at Zachaldara; exhumed and handed over **13 June 2026**, about 75 days after death.

**COULD NOT ESTABLISH any announcement, order or date of relaxation.** The affirmative evidence points the other way: the Ganderbal case of March-April 2026 occurred six years on and under an elected J&K government, with roughly six or seven people present at the burial and a 75-day wait for the body. **The practice is established as still operative in 2026.**

## 2. Why this bears on COUNTING — demonstrable, not inferential

Three retrieved instances.

1. **Amshipora.** Three civilians entered the official record as "unidentified hardcore terrorists" and were buried 125 km away with no photographs released. They were removed from that category only because families 160 km away filed missing-person reports and DNA was eventually run. Absent that, MHA's 2020 "terrorists killed" figure of 221 would have contained three civilians permanently.
2. **April 2020 generally.** The IGP's own protocol makes identification conditional on a family (a) learning of the death, (b) obtaining a lockdown travel pass, (c) reaching Srinagar in time, and (d) being believed by two different District Commissioners. **Each is a filter that can fail without anyone recording that it failed.**
3. **Ganderbal 2026.** Police were able to identify the dead man only because identity papers happened to be on his body — a police officer: "It is only because of these papers that we were able to identify the deceased, otherwise the process would have taken more time."

**The implication for the instrument.** "Terrorists killed" and "civilians killed" are populated at the point of an encounter, by the force that conducted it, before independent identification, and the body is then removed to a location that makes subsequent contestation costly. That does not prove the series is wrong. **It means the series' error is structurally one-directional**: mistakes flow from "civilian" into "terrorist" and not the reverse, because a body wrongly recorded as a militant is buried far away and unphotographed, whereas a body wrongly recorded as a civilian would be claimed immediately.

## 3. Hyderpora, 15 November 2021 — the case that was never adjudicated

Four dead: **Mohammad Altaf Bhat** (hardware shop owner, owner of the building); **Dr Mudasir Gul** (dentist turned businessman, tenant); **Aamir Magray** (described by police as an associate, by his family as an employee); and a man police identified as a Pakistani militant.

Police first said Bhat and Gul were shot dead by militants, then said they might have died in crossfire.

**Magisterial inquiry** ordered by LG Manoj Sinha on 18 November 2021, report to be submitted within 15 days. **THE REPORT HAS NEVER BEEN MADE PUBLIC.** Families met the LG on 7 December 2021; he sought two days to publish it and the time lapsed. **This is a finding, not a gap: an inquiry ordered under public pressure produced no published outcome.**

**What was published instead was the police's own SIT report** (28 December 2021, DIG Sujith K. Singh): Gul killed by the foreign terrorist inside the building; Magray died in crossfire after being used as a human shield and was "closely associated with the foreign terrorist"; Bhat "volunteered to go inside" and was killed in crossfire as a human shield; security forces given a clean chit, having "did not go to the attic at all during searches".

## 4. The wider record on inquiries

**506 magisterial inquiries** ordered in J&K since 1990; **one** completed, and that report never submitted; **zero** convictions arising. Advocate Shafqat Nazir: "The inquiries die their own death."

**CAVEAT, and stage 3 must carry it:** the 506 figure comes from an advocacy organisation's count via a 2018 State Human Rights Commission petition, not a government return. **It is a claimed figure with a named source, not an audited one, and must be graded as such.** It should not be authored as an official statistic.

## CASE FOR — the security establishment's case, in its own terms

Between 2018 and 2024 the security forces conducted 731 encounters and counter-terror operations and killed 1,142 militants, while 369 security personnel died in action. Against that volume, the number of cases in which identity has been seriously contested and subsequently established as wrong is very small — Amshipora is the case everyone names, precisely because it is exceptional. It is exceptional in a second way: the state established it. The Army opened the inquiry itself, published the AFSPA finding, and court-martialled the officer. That is not a system that conceals; it is one that corrects.

The other cases do not stand up the same way. In Hyderpora the SIT, headed by a DIG and reporting within six weeks, reconstructed the sequence with physical evidence — fall positions, distances, the fact that no searcher entered the attic — and found the two undisputed civilians were killed by the militant and in crossfire while being used as human shields. The bodies of the two civilians were exhumed and returned within three days, without litigation. And the Supreme Court, on full argument, declined to disturb the burial and accepted that last rites had been performed.

On the burial practice specifically: between 2016 and 2020 militant funerals were the single most effective recruiting instrument in the Valley — not private grief but staged public events, with crowds mobilised across a wide radius. The Crime Branch said so in writing in 2017. Ending them is the most direct life-saving measure available. Religious washing and shrouding are performed; post-mortems and DNA are taken; families may identify and participate before a magistrate; where identity is later established, exhumation and handover follow, as in Amshipora and Ganderbal.

## CASE AGAINST — as families, lawyers and human-rights reporting put it, in their own terms

The base-rate argument assumes the denominator is knowable. It is not, and the state has arranged matters so that it is not. The dead are declared militants by the force that killed them, buried in a distant graveyard, unnamed and unphotographed. The check on that declaration is a family that hears in time, travels, is believed, and gets DNA run. Amshipora is not a demonstration that the system self-corrects; it is a demonstration of what correcting it takes — three simultaneous missing-person complaints, sustained press attention, and eleven weeks. Ganderbal 2026 shows the alternative: identification happened only because papers were in a pocket, and even then the family waited 75 days.

Nor does the machinery for testing disputes function. 506 inquiries since 1990; one completed; that report never submitted; no convictions. Hyderpora is the model: an inquiry ordered within three days under public pressure (encounter 15 November 2021, inquiry ordered 18 November 2021), a 15-day deadline, an LG who promised publication within two days — and no report, ever. What was published instead was the police's own account of a police operation, clearing the forces. In Ganderbal in 2026, with an elected Chief Minister publicly demanding publication, the seven-day report still did not appear.

And a body is not a public-order problem. Denying a family the right to bury its dead is a punishment imposed without charge, trial or finding, on people who have done nothing. It is imposed by no published order — six years on, nobody can produce the instrument. The COVID justification has outlived COVID by four years, and the police chief said so himself when he listed "stopped glamorising terrorists" alongside stopping infection.

The result is not that every encounter is fake. The result is that no one — including the government — is in a position to say what proportion are, because the only instrument that could tell you has been disabled at the point of burial and at the point of inquiry.

## DIFFERENT FACTS OR DIFFERENT WEIGHTINGS?

**This one is genuinely mixed and the mixture is the finding.**

- **Amshipora: no factual dispute.** Common ground.
- **Hyderpora: irreducibly different facts, never adjudicated.** Whether Aamir Magray was a militant associate or an employee, and whether Bhat and Gul were killed by the militant or by security fire, are contradictory factual claims. The SIT asserts one; the families assert the other; the magisterial inquiry that might have arbitrated was never published; the Supreme Court decided a burial-rights question, not the merits. **Record as unresolved fact. Do not treat the SIT report as settling it — it is the investigating institution's account of its own operation — and do not treat the families' account as settling it either.**
- **On the burial practice: different weightings on the core agreed fact.** Both sides accept that since April 2020 bodies are buried in remote graveyards rather than handed to families. One weights the deterrent effect on recruitment; the other weights collective punishment of non-participants and the loss of a check on identification.
- **On the safeguards: genuine unresolved factual dispute** — asserted in the affirmative by the IGP with named worked examples, denied in the specific by named families in the same article.
- **On the general question of identity, the disagreement is not about weighting the same facts — it is about whether one side's facts exist.** The state's case rests on a base rate. The critics' case is that the base rate is unmeasurable *because* of the burial and identification practice. These are not two readings of one dataset; one side asserts that the dataset is constructed by the interested party and cannot be audited. **That is a claim about the instrument, and it is supported by a retrieved mechanism (section 2 above), not merely asserted.**
- **What both sides agree on, and it is the strongest single fact here:** in the one case where identity was conclusively tested, the official record had been wrong, and wrong in the direction of classifying civilians as terrorists.

## COULD NOT ESTABLISH

- Any written order, circular or SOP establishing the burial practice. Officials point to Disaster Management Act lockdown orders instead. **Classified as an absence, not a retrieval failure.**
- The Hyderpora magisterial inquiry finding — never published.
- The Ganderbal 2026 magisterial inquiry finding — not published as at the latest reporting located.
- How many bodies have been buried under the practice. Three official figures circulate and do not reconcile.
- Any date or instrument of relaxation.
