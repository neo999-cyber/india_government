# 05 — CONTESTED: does the fall in stone-pelting reflect consent or suppression?

## The one primary document

MHA, Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 228, answered 24 July 2024, subject "LAW AND ORDER IN J&K". RETRIEVED — https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2024-pdfs/RS24072024/228.pdf

> "Description | 2018 | 2023 | 2024 (upto 15th July,24)
> Organized Stone Pelting | 1328 | 00 | 00
> Organized Hartal | 52 | 00 | 00
> …
> (Source: UT of J&K)"

Three things to hold onto before either case is put.

1. **The qualifier is "Organized".** Unorganised stone-pelting is definitionally outside the count, and "organized" is nowhere defined. Both 2023 and 2024 are reported as exactly `00` — a whole-year zero in a category whose boundary is undefined.
2. **This series has never appeared in any MHA Annual Report** in seventeen years, though the Annual Report 2024-25 asserts the conclusion in prose: "Organized stone pelting and protests have become things of the past" (RETRIEVED, para 15.9). The assertion is published; the count behind it is not.
3. **"(Source: UT of J&K)"** — MHA is relaying the territorial administration's returns.

## Other figures each side prefers

**Government's, RELAYED and internally inconsistent.** Organised stone-pelting 2,654 (2010) → 0 (2024); civilian deaths in stone-pelting 112 (2010) → 0 (2024); civilian injuries 6,235 (2010) → 0 (2024); hartal calls 302 pre-abrogation → 0. Earlier series: 730 (2015), ~2,653 (2016), 1,412 (2017), 1,458 (2018), ~2,000 (2019, of which 1,193 after 5 August 2019), then an 87.13% fall in 2020.

**Note an unreconciled contradiction inside the government's own numbers:** MHA is reported as giving both **759** and **1,458** for 2018 stone-pelting incidents, against **1,328** in the primary reply above. Three official figures for one year. RELAYED and unreconciled — stage 3 must not pick one.

**Critics', RELAYED.** 765 people arrested in 190 cases for stone-pelting and law-and-order offences between 5 August and 15 November 2019 alone — MHA's own answer to Parliament. Plus the structural points in part 10 and the press-contraction points in part 03.

## CASE FOR — the consent reading, in its own terms

Nothing prevents a Kashmiri from throwing a stone in 2024 that did not equally prevent it in 2016, when roughly 2,653 incidents occurred under a considerably larger and more aggressive security deployment. Repression does not explain the pattern: the periods of heaviest coercion produced the *most* stone-pelting, not the least. What changed is that the political economy of the street collapsed — the hartal calendars, the overground-worker networks that mobilised crowds, and the funerals that supplied the occasions.

The consequences of that collapse fall on ordinary people and are measurable: zero civilian deaths and zero civilian injuries in stone-pelting incidents in 2024, against 112 dead and 6,235 injured in 2010. Those are Kashmiri lives and Kashmiri eyes. Schools and businesses that shut on 302 hartal calls a year now stay open. 2.36 crore visitors came in 2024; the Amarnath Yatra ran with 5.12 lakh pilgrims; an assembly election was held and President's Rule was revoked in October 2024. People who are being suppressed do not turn out to vote and do not host three crore visitors. Absence of stone-pelting plus presence of participation is consent.

## CASE AGAINST — the suppression reading, in its own terms

The fall did not follow a political settlement. It followed a communications blackout, a mass detention drive, and the removal of the region's statehood. 765 people were arrested in 190 cases in the fourteen weeks after 5 August 2019. A fall in an activity for which the immediate penalty became preventive detention, passport and government-job denial, and FIRs against whole crowds, tells you about the penalty, not about the sentiment.

More importantly, the instrument that would have registered continuing dissent was itself removed. From 2018 to October 2024 J&K had no Legislative Assembly. It now has one that cannot legislate on Police or Public Order, and whose questioning of the Lieutenant Governor's discretionary functions can be restricted by rules the Lieutenant Governor makes. The pre-2019 figures that critics and journalists rely on exist because MLAs asked and Chief Ministers answered in writing; the post-2019 figures exist because the executive chose to announce them. The Kashmir Press Club closed in January 2022 and at least seven named journalists were jailed under anti-terror law between 2018 and 2023, one of them for reporting a contested encounter.

Nor is participation the same as consent: high turnout in 2024 was widely read within Kashmir as a demand for the restoration of statehood — that is, as a vote against the arrangement whose success it is now cited to prove.

## DIFFERENT FACTS OR DIFFERENT WEIGHTINGS?

**Almost entirely different weightings of the same facts.** This is the cleanest weighting case in the phase and should be recorded as such — `differentFacts` is FALSE here.

Both sides accept: organised stone-pelting has gone to approximately zero; hartal calls have gone to zero; 765 people were arrested in the first fourteen weeks after 5 August 2019; the Assembly did not sit for six years and cannot legislate on police and public order; journalists were jailed; turnout in 2024 was high. **Neither side disputes any of these.**

The disagreement is about what the absence of protest is evidence of. The same observation is read as revealed preference by one side and as revealed cost by the other. **There is no additional fact that decides it, because both accounts predict the identical observation.**

**One genuine factual gap could discriminate between them**, and it is the highest-value follow-up here: an independent measure of grievance not mediated by the state — an unmanipulated protest-event dataset, an independent survey of political attitudes, or a reading of the 2024 mandate through manifestos and vote shares. **COULD NOT ESTABLISH any of these.** Until one exists the argument is unresolvable from the data both sides use, because both sides use the state's data.

**A caution against a lazy synthesis.** "Some of both" is not a finding and must not be authored as one. The two accounts make one divergent prediction: the suppression account predicts that grievance persists and resurfaces when cost falls; the consent account predicts it will not. Critics read Pahalgam 2025 as the resurfacing; the government reads it as externally supplied and therefore not a test of local sentiment. **That divergence is testable over time. This record should hold the question open rather than close it, and should carry a revisit trigger rather than a verdict.**
