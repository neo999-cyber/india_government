# 09 — CANDIDATE PROVENANCE RECORDS: definitional divergences

Operator's rule being applied: "If two sources count 'incidents' on different definitions, that is a provenance record, not a series footnote." Each item below is written as a candidate dispute record — the divergence is the finding, not a caveat hung on a number.

This part covers divergences established from **primary MHA documents I retrieved myself**. Divergences involving SATP, J&K Police, NCRB, NHRC, JKCCS, APDP and OHCHR are in the parts that carry those instruments.

---

## PC-1 — MHA's headline "terrorist incidents" figure includes MHA's own operations

**The divergence.** MHA publishes two incident quantities that are routinely both called "incidents", and one is the sum of the other plus security-force-initiated operations.

- MHA Annual Report 2022-23 onward, column headings verbatim: "Terrorist Initiated Incidents (TII)" and "Encounters/CT Ops."
- MHA reply to Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 371, answered 20 July 2022, column heading verbatim: "Terrorist Incidents".

The identity, checked on every year where both are available:

| Year | TII | Encounters/CT Ops | Sum | RS US Q.371 "Terrorist Incidents" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 228 | 189 | 417 | 417 |
| 2019 | 153 | 102 | 255 | 255 |
| 2020 | 126 | 118 | 244 | 244 |
| 2021 | 129 | 100 | 229 | 229 |

Four for four.

**Why this is a dispute and not an arithmetic curiosity.** The 417→229 fall is the figure the government uses to demonstrate that terrorism declined. The reply of 20 July 2022 says so in its own words: "There has been substantial decline in terrorist attacks from 417 in 2018 to 229 in 2021." But roughly 45% of the 2018 figure (189 of 417) is counter-terrorism operations conducted by the state. A quantity that moves when the state conducts more or fewer operations is not a clean measure of militant activity, and it is presented as one. Conversely, an intensified operational tempo *raises* the number the government elsewhere wants low.

**Direction of bias.** `obscures` — the composite cannot be read as a measure of either thing. It is not that the post-2014 figure is too high or too low; it is that two populations are added together under a name that names only one of them.

**Bridge exists: YES, and this is unusual.** From AR 2022-23 onward MHA publishes both components, so the composite can be decomposed for 2018 onward. For years before 2018 no decomposition is published and the bridge does not exist.

**Competing accounts.**
- *MHA / Government of India*: the table is labelled, the components are published from AR 2022-23, and "incidents of terrorist violence" has conventionally included encounters because an encounter is an incident of terrorist violence — the militants are present and armed in both cases.
- *The counter-position*: an operation the state chooses to launch is not an event that happened to the state, and combining them makes the series respond to policy as much as to threat. Note this position is available without disputing a single MHA number.

**Sources.** AR 2022-23 (`AnnualReportEngLish_11102023.pdf`), AR 2023-24 (`AnnualReport_27122024.pdf`), AR 2024-25 (`AREnglish_24032026.pdf`), all retrieved from mha.gov.in — T1. RS US Q.371 of 20.07.2022, https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2022-pdfs/RS20072022/371.pdf — retrieved, T1.

## PC-2 — "Civilians killed" means two different things in two MHA instruments, and changed meaning mid-series

**The divergence.** For the same years, MHA has published two civilian-death series under the same column name.

- **Bounded basis** — RS Unstarred Q.371, 20 July 2022, introduces its table verbatim as: "The number of security forces and civilians who died **due to terrorist attacks** since 2018 are as under". Values: 2018 = 39, 2019 = 39, 2020 = 37, 2021 = 41.
- **Composite basis** — MHA Annual Report 2023-24 and 2024-25 footnote, verbatim: "*[Civilians killed includes civilians killed in terrorist-initiated incidents and civilians killed in encounter/counter Terrorism operations]". Values: 2018 = 55, 2019 = 44, 2020 = 38, 2021 = 41.

The composite basis was introduced in AR 2022-23 (posted 11 October 2023) **without the footnote**; the footnote first appears in AR 2023-24 (posted 27 December 2024).

**What this settles, and what it does not.** The operator asked whether MHA's "civilians killed" means civilians killed by militants only. The answer is: **it depended on which MHA document you were reading and in which year, and MHA has now settled on a composite that merges both causes into one undifferentiated column.** So MHA does not exclude civilians killed by security forces — it absorbs them. That is a different and in some ways worse failure than exclusion, because a reader of the current Annual Report sees a single "Civilians killed" number and has no way to know what share of it the state caused, while a reader of the 2020-21 report saw a number that excluded them entirely and equally could not tell.

**MHA has no column for civilians killed by security forces, and never has had one, in any report year 2013-14 to 2024-25.** That stands.

**Direction of bias.** `obscures`.

**Bridge exists: NO.** The residual between the two bases (16 for 2018, 5 for 2019, 1 for 2020, 0 for 2021) is *not* a bridge and must not be published as the count of civilians killed in security-force operations. The 2021 residual of zero, and the fact that the SF-killed figure for 2020 also moved (62 → 63) across the same restatement, show the restatement carries ordinary revisions as well as the definitional change. The residual's basis is unestablished. This is a hard stop.

**Competing accounts.**
- *MHA*: the composite is disclosed in the footnote; it is a complete count of civilian deaths connected to the conflict, which is more informative than a count restricted to one perpetrator.
- *The counter-position*: a count that names no perpetrator cannot answer the question the count exists to answer, and the disclosure arrived fourteen months after the redefinition.

**Sources.** As PC-1, plus AR 2019-20, AR 2020-21, AR 2021-22, all retrieved — T1.

## PC-3 — MHA restated the incident series for 2017-2019 and never said why

**The divergence.** Same column name, same ministry, same reports one year apart.

| Year | AR 2020-21 (posted 22 Apr 2022) | AR 2021-22 (posted 24 Nov 2022) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 342 | 279 | −63 |
| 2018 | 614 | 417 | −197 |
| 2019 | 594 | 255 | −339 |
| 2020 | 244 | 244 | 0 |

No note accompanies the restatement in either report.

**Why it is a dispute.** The restated series is the one that supports the decline narrative most strongly, and the years cut hardest are 2018 and 2019 — the years immediately before and including the abrogation of Article 370. The pre-restatement figures are not obviously wrong; they are simply no longer published. **What the 614 and 594 counted is not established from any document.**

**Direction of bias.** `degrades-precision`, or `disputed`. I would not assert a direction: I cannot show which basis is the better measure, only that they are different and the change was undisclosed.

**Bridge exists: NO.** No MHA document reconciles them.

**Note for stage 3.** The pre-restatement values must be carried as basis-unestablished rather than dropped. Dropping them is the instrument adopting the state's later revision by default, which is exactly the move the never-splice rule exists to prevent.

## PC-4 — the restructuring was applied to J&K alone

**The finding.** The AR 2022-23 split of the J&K incident count into militant-initiated and security-force-initiated columns was **not** mirrored in the Left Wing Extremism tables, which keep an unchanged header — "Years | Incidents | Extremist | Extremist | SFs | Civilians | Extremist | Arms | Arms | Persons" — across AR 2020-21, AR 2021-22 and AR 2022-23, and are never split by initiator.

**Why it matters.** It removes the innocent explanation. Had all internal-security theatres been restructured together, breaks 1 and 2 would be house-style. They were not. One theatre's table was rebuilt, in one report year, in a way that changes what the headline number for that theatre means.

**Direction of bias.** `disputed`. I am not asserting motive and stage 3 should not either. The established fact is the asymmetry.

**Sources.** AR 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, retrieved — T1.

## PC-5 — "Infiltration attempts" and "Net Estimated infiltration" are different quantities in the same table

**The divergence.** MHA's infiltration table has always carried two rows, labelled verbatim "Infiltration attempts" and "Net Estimated infiltration". The second is labelled an estimate; the first is not, and MHA nowhere states how an attempt is detected or counted. "Successful infiltration" is not published under that name by MHA at all.

The published series diverge sharply in level and in shape — attempts 419 (2017) → 53 (2022); net estimated 136 (2017) → 14 (2022). Any commentary that says "infiltration fell from 419 to 53" and any that says "from 136 to 14" are quoting different rows of the same table.

There is also a quiet restatement: net estimated infiltration for 2019 was published as **138** in AR 2019-20, AR 2020-21 and AR 2021-22, and as **141** in AR 2022-23. No note.

**Direction of bias.** `degrades-precision`.

**Bridge exists: NO** for the attempts/net relationship — MHA publishes no interception or attrition figure that would link them.

**Note.** The two must be separate series and must never share a line. The 2019 net value carries a restatement.

## PC-6 — a cumulative death ledger that ran for decades, then stopped, and never counted militants

**The finding.** MHA Annual Reports from 2013-14 to 2020-21 open the J&K section with a cumulative toll since 1990. Verbatim, AR 2020-21: "Since the advent of militancy in J&K in the 1990s, 14,091 Civilians and 5,356 Security Force (SF) personnel had lost their lives (upto 2020)." Earlier: 13,880 / 4,879 to 31.03.2014; 14,024 / 5,273 to 31.03.2019; 14,054 / 5,294 to December 2019.

From AR 2021-22 onward the sentence is absent from all four subsequent reports.

**Two separate things to record.**
1. **A stock series was discontinued** after 2020, terminal value 14,091 civilians / 5,356 SF personnel. No successor.
2. **It never had a militants-killed term.** For thirty years MHA's cumulative ledger ran two categories — civilians and security forces — and omitted the third population its own annual tables counted. Whatever one thinks of that omission, it means the cumulative figure was never a total of conflict deaths and should never be presented as one.

**Direction of bias.** `obscures`.

## PC-7 — MHA's flagship instrument does not contain the word "pellet"

**The finding.** The string "pellet" occurs **zero times** in every MHA Annual Report I retrieved: 2013-14, 2018-19, 2019-20, 2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25. There is likewise no stone-pelting or law-and-order incident table for J&K in any of them; every occurrence of "stone" in those reports is foundation-stone laying.

This is not a divergence between two instruments — it is the flagship instrument's silence, and it belongs in the absences part as well as here. It is recorded here because it bears directly on the ledger question: MHA's annual accounting of the J&K conflict contains no term of any kind for harm done by crowd-control weapons.

**Sources.** All eight Annual Reports, retrieved from mha.gov.in — T1.

## PC-8 — the 2016 clincher: MHA counted six civilian deaths in the deadliest civilian-death year of the period

**The divergence, in one document.** Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 2670, answered **10 August 2016**, asked by Shri Narendra Budania, answered by MoS Hansraj Gangaram Ahir. RETRIEVED — https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2016-pdfs/rs-100816/2670%20E.pdf

Annexure-II, introduced verbatim as "The number of terrorist incidents in Jammu and Kashmir and the number of security personnel/ civilians killed/injured **in these incidents** during the last three years and current year are as under". Columns: "Year | No. of terrorist incidents | Civilians Killed | Civilians Injured | Security personnel Killed | Security personnel Injured". The 2016 row, to 31 July 2016: incidents 163, **civilians killed 6**, civilians injured 12.

That answer was given five weeks into the unrest that followed the killing of Burhan Wani on 8 July 2016, during which large numbers of civilians were killed in security-force firing on protesters. MHA's count for the same window, given to Parliament in the middle of it, is six. The MHA Annual Report 2016-17 later put the full year at 15 and wrote, verbatim: "However, there is 11.76% decrease in casualties of civilians in comparison to the corresponding period of 2015."

**Why this is the strongest single item in the phase.** It is not an argument about framing. It is one ministry, one document, one date, in which the instrument's boundary is visible without any interpretation: the deadliest year for civilians in the period is recorded by the responsible ministry as an 11.76% improvement for civilians, because the deaths it does not count are the ones the state caused.

**A necessary discipline.** The civilian death toll of the 2016 unrest is not established in this phase from any primary document. Do not pair MHA's 15 with a specific counter-figure unless that figure's own basis has been established. **The finding here is the instrument's boundary, not a rival number**, and it survives intact without one.

**Direction of bias.** `understates-post-2014` on civilian deaths, and `obscures` on the question of who caused them.

**Bridge exists: NO.**

## PC-9 — "incidents" is defined once, in Parliament, and never in the Annual Report

**The finding.** Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 210, answered **12 December 2018**, asked by Dr T. Subbarami Reddy and Shrimati Ambika Soni, answered by MoS Hansraj Gangaram Ahir. RETRIEVED — https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2018-pdfs/rs-12122018-ENG/210.pdf

The only definitional sentence for "incidents" found anywhere in the MHA corpus, verbatim:

> "The number of incidents includes the violence committed by terrorists during anti militancy operations."

**Why it matters.** It is the mechanism behind PC-1 and PC-3: under the old basis an encounter generated an incident whenever the militants fired back. It appears once, in a parliamentary answer, and never migrated into the Annual Report series it describes — which carries no definition of "Incidents", "Terrorist Initiated Incidents" or "Encounters/CT Ops." in any of the seventeen report years checked.

**Still not established, and this is the honest limit:** whether a *fatality* is required for an incident, and whether firing, grenade throwing, IED recovery or non-fatal attacks count. No MHA document says. Any comparison of MHA's incident count with another instrument's is therefore a comparison in which one side's definition is unknown.

## PC-10 — an official stone-pelting series exists, and only in Parliament

**The finding.** Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 228, answered **24 July 2024**, asked by Shri Neeraj Shekhar, subject "LAW AND ORDER IN J&K", answered by MoS Nityanand Rai. RETRIEVED — https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2024-pdfs/RS24072024/228.pdf

The table, verbatim:

> "Description | 2018 | 2023 | 2024 (upto 15th July,24)
> Organized Stone Pelting | 1328 | 00 | 00
> Organized Hartal | 52 | 00 | 00
> Terrorist Initiated Incidents | 228 | 46 | 11
> Encounters/CT Operations | 189 | 48 | 21
> Security Forces killed | 91 | 30 | 10
> Civilians killed | 55 | 14 | 14
> (Source: UT of J&K)"

Four separate findings ride on this one table.

1. **A year-wise official stone-pelting count exists and has never appeared in any MHA Annual Report.** MHA holds the data. Its absence from the flagship instrument is an editorial choice, not a data gap — which changes the absence classification from `not-collected` to `not-published`.
2. **The qualifier is "Organized".** Unorganised stone-pelting is definitionally outside the count and "organized" is nowhere defined. Both 2023 and 2024 are reported as exactly `00` — a whole-year zero in a category whose boundary is undefined.
3. **Stone-pelting was never inside the "Incidents" count at any vintage.** 1328 organised stone-pelting events in 2018 against a total "Incidents" figure of 614 for the same year. This kills the most natural explanation of the 614→417 restatement in PC-3. **What the 197 removed events were remains unestablished.**
4. **"(Source: UT of J&K)".** This is the only explicit source attribution on a J&K casualty table found anywhere. **MHA is publishing the UT administration's returns, not its own.** The Annual Report prints identical figures with no attribution, so its readers cannot tell that the Union ministry is relaying territorial-administration numbers — and after 31 October 2019 the UT administration reports to the Lieutenant Governor, who reports to MHA. The producer and the publisher are two ends of one chain.

**Direction of bias.** `obscures`.

## PC-11 — three bounding formulas, none of them in a table label

**The finding.** MHA has bounded "Civilians killed" three different ways across its instruments, and in every case the bounding sits in surrounding prose rather than in the column heading:

- **Bare label**, no bounding at all — MHA Annual Reports, every year from 2008-09 to 2022-23.
- **"who died due to terrorist attacks"** — RS Unstarred Q.371, 20 July 2022.
- **"in these incidents"** — RS Unstarred Q.2670, 10 August 2016.
- And from AR 2023-24, a footnote that broadens rather than bounds: "includes civilians killed in terrorist-initiated incidents and civilians killed in encounter/counter Terrorism operations".

**Why this is a provenance record and not a style note.** Every automated extraction, every data aggregator, and every chart built from these tables carries away the label and discards the prose. **The definition is systematically stored in the one part of the document that does not survive transcription.** That is the mechanism by which a bounded figure enters public circulation as an unbounded one, and it is reproducible: the widely-circulated compilations of MHA J&K data carry the column name and not the sentence.

**Direction of bias.** `obscures`.

## PC-12 — parliamentary replies carry columns the Annual Report has never had

**The finding.** Quantities MHA has published in Parliament and never in an Annual Report, in any of seventeen years:

| Quantity | Where it appeared | Never in AR |
|---|---|---|
| `Civilians Injured`, `Security personnel Injured` | RS Q.2670, 10 Aug 2016 | ✓ |
| `No. of Terrorists/suspects arrested` | LS Q.198, 24 Feb 2015 | ✓ |
| `No. of incidents of encounter between terrorists and Security Forces` | LS Q.198, 24 Feb 2015 | first appeared in AR only in 2022-23 |
| `Organized Stone Pelting`, `Organized Hartal` | RS Q.228, 24 Jul 2024 | ✓ |

**Consequence for absence classification.** Several quantities that look like `not-collected` when you read only the Annual Report are demonstrably `not-published` — MHA has produced them on request. That distinction is the difference between an absence with no route and an absence with a working parliamentary route, and getting it wrong in either direction is a downstream error.

**One caution on the encounter counts.** LS Q.198 gives 2014 encounters = 57; the Annual Report's `Encounters/CT Ops.` series starts at 2018 = 189. Neither is defined. **Do not splice them and do not read a rise.** They are two undefined quantities sharing a word.

## PC-13 — AFSPA is reported annually for the North East and never for J&K

**The finding, from an exhaustive check of the retrieved Annual Reports.** The Armed Forces (Special Powers) (Jammu and Kashmir) Act, 1990 is **never named in any MHA Annual Report** I retrieved — 2013-14, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2020-21, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25 all return zero occurrences of "Special Powers) Act, 1990", "AFSPA, 1990" or "AFSPA 1990". No disturbed-area statement for J&K appears in any of them.

Meanwhile AFSPA 1958 in the North East is reported every year, in detail, as a measurable achievement. AR 2024-25 para 2.39, verbatim:

> "Because of considerable improvement in the security situation of NE States, imposition of AFSPA, 1958 has been significantly reduced. As on 01.04.2025, AFSPA is applicable only in 3 districts and 3 police stations of another district in Arunachal Pradesh."

**Why this is a provenance record.** The instrument publishes, annually and at district granularity, the extent of extraordinary-powers legislation in one internal-security theatre and is silent on its extent in the other — the theatre in which the same ministry reports 731 counter-terror operations over seven years, and in which section 7 of the 1990 Act is the provision that governs whether any of those operations can be prosecuted. A reader of the Annual Report can track the retreat of AFSPA in Manipur year by year and cannot learn from the same document that AFSPA applies in J&K at all.

**Direction of bias.** `obscures`.

**Note.** This is not a claim that MHA conceals AFSPA's application in J&K — it is widely known and is stated elsewhere. The finding is about the *instrument*: the flagship annual accounting treats the two theatres asymmetrically, and a comparison of internal-security theatres built from this document is therefore built on unlike coverage.

## PC-14 — five "incident" counts for one year, and the most-cited independent counter publishes no definition

**The divergence.** For calendar 2018 in J&K, five separately published and separately labelled incident counts exist, all retrievable: 614 ("Incidents", MHA Annual Reports), 597 ("Number of Terrorism Related Incidents", SATP), 417 ("Terrorist Incidents", MHA RS Q.371), 228 ("Terrorist Initiated Incidents", MHA AR 2022-23 onward and RS Q.228), 206 ("Incidents of Killing", SATP fatalities datasheet).

**The definitional differences, stated precisely.** "Incidents of Killing" gates on a **death**. "Terrorist Initiated Incidents" gates on **who initiated**. "Terrorist Incidents" drops the initiation requirement. "Incidents" is the widest official category, widened by the rule in PC-9. "Number of Terrorism Related Incidents" gates on a *relation* rather than an act and **carries no published definition whatsoever**.

**The near-coincidence of 228 and 206 is arithmetic accident, not agreement** — one filters on outcome, the other on initiator. Stage 3 must not treat them as corroborating.

**The deeper structural point, which is the actual finding.** The two families of instrument fail in **opposite** directions. SATP gates on **press reportability** ("Compiled from news reports"); MHA gates on **administrative reportability** — an incident is what a police station and the UT administration book and forward. Press misses routine, undramatic, remote-sector and blackout-period events. Administrative reporting misses events the state has an interest in not booking, and can re-book one operation as several. **Neither is a superset of the other and no reconciliation exists.**

**And SATP publishes no methodology.** Its site structure was enumerated: eleven J&K datasheet pages, no methodology page, no codebook, no note-on-data, no inclusion criteria, no definitions page. The nearest thing is a site disclaimer, verbatim: the data rests on "publicly available information, **internally developed data** and other sources believed to be reliable", and "www.satp.org does not guarantee the accuracy, adequacy or completeness of any information". **SATP asserts a non-press component to its data without ever specifying what it is or which figures it touches.** An independent 2015 review by Action on Armed Violence — an organisation whose business is casualty recording — likewise records no SATP methodology.

**Direction of bias.** `degrades-precision`.

**Bridge exists: NO**, and cannot exist, because one side of the comparison has no stated definition.

## PC-15 — ceasefire violations: the instrument was replaced at the point the number became favourable

Full evidence in part 01, break 7. As a dispute record: MHA published a month-by-month integer series of ceasefire violations covering the LoC and the International Border, ending 2,140 / 3,479 / 5,133 / 664. MoD subsequently published, in place of any count, the prose clause "there have been just three incidents of ceasefire violations and no incident of shelling along the Line of Control (LoC)" — a different ministry, a narrower geography, an uncounted unit, and no year attribution.

**Direction of bias.** `obscures`.

**Bridge exists: NO.**

**Competing accounts.** *Government*: the ceasefire held, so there is nothing to count, and the reply answers the question asked. *Counter*: a fall to near-zero is exactly the moment at which a maintained series becomes most informative, and it is the moment the series stopped being maintained; the statement is also silent about the International Border, which the previous series covered.

**A necessary fairness.** The underlying claim — that ceasefire violations collapsed after February 2021 — is corroborated inside the pre-existing instrument itself: of 664 violations reported for January-June 2021, 658 fall in January and February and six in the four months March-June. **The collapse is real on MHA's own basis. What is unsupported is any comparison of the post-2023 "three" with the earlier integers.**

## PC-16 — the local/foreign militant split was abandoned by both instruments

**The finding.** MHA has never published it. SATP published it from 1990 and stopped at 15 November 2001, along with its stated qualifier "only identified terrorists killed". Consequence: for the entire post-2001 period the local/foreign composition of the militant dead exists only as unsourced assertion.

**Why it is a dispute and not merely a gap.** The local/foreign share is the single most-used measure in the argument about whether militancy is indigenous or externally supplied — it is cited on both sides of part 03 and part 04. **It is quoted constantly and measured nowhere.**

**Direction of bias.** `obscures`.

## Note on what makes these authorable

Every divergence above is established from documents I retrieved and can be re-checked from the URLs. None of them rests on a press account of an official figure. That is unusual for this subject and stage 3 should exploit it: these are the T1-graded spine of the phase, and the contested material in parts 03 onward is far more thinly sourced by comparison.
