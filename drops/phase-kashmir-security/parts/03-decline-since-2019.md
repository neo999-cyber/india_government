# 03 — CONTESTED: has militancy declined since 2019, and on what measure?

Both cases are put in their own terms, with each side's own preferred data. The verdict on different-facts versus different-weightings is at the end and is deliberately split, because this question does not have one answer across all its measures.

## CASE FOR — the government's case, in its own terms

Terrorist-initiated incidents fell from 228 in 2018 to 28 in 2024, an 87.7% decline, with 46 in 2023 against 129 and 125 in the two preceding years. Security-force deaths fell from 91 to 31. Civilians killed — on the broader definition that now includes deaths in counter-terrorism operations — fell from 55 to 14 in 2023. Terrorists killed fell from 257 to 67, which is what a shrinking adversary looks like. Infiltration attempts fell from 419 in 2017 to 53 in 2022, and net estimated infiltration from 136 to 14.

The government's own summary, verbatim from MHA Annual Report 2024-25 para 15.9, RETRIEVED:

> "The security situation of UT of J&K has improved significantly. Organized stone pelting and protests have become things of the past. Subsequently, UT of J&K has witnessed overall development in all sectors. Tourism Sector has witnessed unprecedented growth in the year 2023 as more than 2.11 crore tourist visits were recorded in the UT of J&K. During 2024, 2.36 crore tourist visits were recorded."

Tourism is offered as the normalcy proxy: 1.13 crore (2021, RELAYED), 1.85 crore (2022, RELAYED), 2.11 crore (2023, RETRIEVED), 2.36 crore (2024, RETRIEVED), with the 2024 Amarnath Yatra completed by 5.12 lakh pilgrims (RETRIEVED). Institutional normalisation is offered alongside it: President's Rule revoked by S.O.4484(E) dated 13 October 2024 following assembly elections (RETRIEVED, AR 2024-25 para 15.5).

The framing, verbatim from RS Unstarred Q.384, 6 December 2023, RETRIEVED: "The Government has a policy of zero tolerance against terrorism. The approach of the Government is to dismantle the terror eco-system."

## CASE AGAINST — in its own terms

**1. The instrument was rewritten mid-series by the party being measured.** MHA has published 614, 417 and 228 as the number of incidents in 2018, and 594, 255 and 153 for 2019 (part 01, breaks 1 and 2). No methodology note accompanied either change. The fall from 228 to 28 is read in public and in Parliament as continuous with a series that used to report 614 for the same year.

**2. The frame excludes one side's harm — in the instrument most often quoted.** In the parliamentary series the civilian figure counts civilians "who died due to terrorist attacks" (RS Q.371, 2022). *In fairness to the government case, and this must be stated:* the Annual Report series from 2022-23 onward does include civilians killed in CT operations, per its footnote. So the objection holds against the parliamentary instrument and against most political usage; it does not hold against the current Annual Report table. What holds against both is that neither ever separates the two causes, and neither has ever counted civilians killed by security forces outside CT operations at all.

**3. The count is generated at the point of the killing, by the force that did the killing, and the body is then removed.** See part 08. Amshipora put three civilians in the "terrorists killed" column for eleven weeks.

**4. The verification machinery is inert.** 506 magisterial inquiries in J&K since 1990, one completed, that report never submitted, no convictions — a figure from an advocacy petition to the State Human Rights Commission, not a government return, and it should be carried as a claimed figure with a named source. The Hyderpora inquiry report was promised for publication by the Lieutenant Governor and never appeared; the Ganderbal seven-day report of 2026 had not appeared weeks later.

**5. The answerability that used to generate independent counts was removed.** See part 10.

**6. Press capacity to produce an independent count contracted.** Kashmir Press Club closed January 2022. Journalists held under anti-terror law over the period include Aasif Sultan (2018), Sajad Gul (January 2022), Fahad Shah of The Kashmir Walla (4 February 2022, following the site's reporting on a Pulwama shootout), Abdul Aala Fazili (April 2022), Irfan Mehraj (March 2023), Majid Hyderi (September 2023). RELAYED throughout — CPJ, HRW, RSF, IFJ.

**7. The trend is not monotonic, and the categories that rose are the ones the trend claim is meant to exclude.** MHA's own series shows 2021 (129) above 2020 (126). On SATP's tabulation — RELAYED at two removes, since SATP itself was unreachable (part 11) — incidents run 369 (2019), 415 (2020), 460 (2021), 457 (2022), 267 (2023), 210 (2024), and civilian deaths 42, 33, 36, 30, 12, 31. Targeted killings of minorities and migrant workers rose in 2021-22: 14 people from minority communities including 3 Kashmiri Pandits killed between 1 January and 30 November 2022, per MoS Home Nityanand Rai in the Rajya Sabha (RELAYED).

**8. Pahalgam, 22 April 2025.** Militants attacked tourists in the Baisaran Valley near Pahalgam, Anantnag district, killing 26 civilians and injuring more than 20; eyewitnesses reported victims were questioned about religion before being shot. Claimed by The Resistance Front. RELAYED throughout — the official NIA/MHA return and the government's own incident classification for 2025 COULD NOT BE ESTABLISHED, and MHA Annual Report 2025-26 was not located. The force of the point in critics' terms: one incident produced more civilian deaths than MHA's entire recorded civilian toll for 2023 (14), in the district and the sector the government uses as its normalcy proxy.

## DIFFERENT FACTS OR DIFFERENT WEIGHTINGS?

**Split, and the split must be preserved rather than collapsed into one verdict.**

**Different facts, established beyond dispute.** MHA has published 614, 417 and 228 for the same object — incidents in 2018 — without a methodology note. These are not weightings of one number; they are different numbers. Any comparison across the 2021-22 / 2022-23 boundary compares unlike things. The instrument should refuse to splice.

**Different facts, unresolved.** Whether the "terrorists killed" column is accurate at the individual level. Amshipora proves it can be wrong. Nothing in the public record establishes how often, in either direction.

**Different weightings, on facts both sides accept.** Both accept 28 terrorist-initiated incidents in 2024, 2.36 crore tourist visits, and 26 dead at Pahalgam. One side reads a structural decline punctuated by an outlier; the other reads a suppressed surface with intact capability that surfaced once, catastrophically. Neither reading is compelled by the numbers.

**A disagreement about instruments, not facts — and this is the real one.** The government's position is that incidents, fatalities, infiltration, recruitment and tourism are the right measures and all point one way. The critics' position is that all five are produced or curated by the executive being assessed, and that the two independent registers — an assembly that could question police and public order, and a press that could report contested killings — were removed in the same period. **This is not settleable by adding more of the government's data.**

**Fairness to the government case, and stage 3 must carry this.** The decline in *security-force* deaths, 91 to 31, is the government's strongest number. Security-force fatalities are the series the state has least incentive and least ability to understate: the dead are named, pensioned and publicly honoured. No critic located in this phase offers a competing count of security-force deaths. **This measure survives the objections that damage the others, and a record that treats every measure as equally compromised would be wrong.**
