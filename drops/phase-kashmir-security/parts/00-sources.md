# 00 — THE SOURCE-INSTRUMENT MAP

Built by DEFINITION, not by number. For each quantity: which instruments carry it, what counting definition each uses (verbatim where retrievable), and where two instruments diverge **by definition** rather than by number.

**How to read the evidence grades.** RETRIEVED means I or a subagent downloaded and read the document; the URL is given. RELAYED means the figure or wording is known only through a press account or a third party. **The grade attaches to the document held, not to the institution the subject belongs to.** A J&K Police figure known only through a report of a press conference is RELAYED however official the speaker.

**Standing caution before anything below is used.** The three J&K *territorial* sources — J&K Police, J&K Home Department, J&K Legislative Assembly — were unreachable in this session (part 11). Everything attributed to them here arrives via MHA or via press. Stage 3 must not upgrade any of it.

## 2. THE HEADLINE DEFINITIONAL FINDINGS

Three findings dominate everything below.

**(A) MHA's headline "terrorist incidents" figure is the sum of militant-initiated incidents and security-force-initiated operations.** Established arithmetically on four years: TII + Encounters/CT Ops = "Terrorist Incidents" exactly, for 2018 (228+189=417), 2019 (153+102=255), 2020 (126+118=244) and 2021 (129+100=229). The mechanism is stated once, in RS Unstarred Q.210 of 12 December 2018, verbatim: **"The number of incidents includes the violence committed by terrorists during anti militancy operations."**

**(B) For calendar 2018 in J&K there are five separately published, separately labelled "incident" counts, all retrievable.**

| Number | Label, verbatim | Publisher | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **614** | "Incidents" | MHA Annual Reports 2018-19 to 2020-21 | widest official category; basis never stated |
| **597** | "Number of Terrorism Related Incidents" | SATP | widest SATP category; **no published definition at all** |
| **417** | "Terrorist Incidents" | MHA, RS US Q.371, 20 Jul 2022 | armed contacts either side initiated |
| **228** | "Terrorist Initiated Incidents" | MHA AR 2022-23 onward; RS US Q.228, 24 Jul 2024 "(Source: UT of J&K)" | militants must have initiated |
| **206** | "Incidents of Killing" | SATP fatalities datasheet | a death is required |

These are not measurement noise. **They are four different questions and one undefined one.** The near-coincidence of 228 and 206 is arithmetic accident: one filters on *initiator*, the other on *outcome*.

**(C) The two families of instrument fail in opposite directions.** SATP gates on **press reportability** — its tables say "Compiled from news reports". MHA gates on **administrative reportability** — an incident is what a police station and the UT administration book and forward. Press reporting misses routine, undramatic, remote-sector and communications-blackout events. Administrative reporting misses events the state has an interest in not booking, and can re-book one operation as several incidents. **Neither is a superset of the other, and no reconciliation exists.**

## 3. QUANTITY BY QUANTITY

### (a) Incidents / "terrorist incidents"

**Carried by:** MHA Annual Reports (every year); MHA parliamentary replies; SATP (two series); MoD (a separate "Terrorist Initiated Incidents" restricted to the LoC).

**Definitions, verbatim.**
- MHA Annual Reports 2008-09 to 2021-22: column heading is bare — "Incidents". **No definition in any of seventeen reports.**
- MHA Annual Reports 2022-23 onward: "Terrorist Initiated Incidents (TII)" and "Encounters/CT Ops." — **neither defined anywhere.**
- MHA, RS US Q.210, 12 Dec 2018: "The number of incidents includes the violence committed by terrorists during anti militancy operations."
- SATP fatalities datasheet: "Incidents of Killing" — the fatality requirement is on the face of the column.
- SATP incidents datasheet: "Number of Terrorism Related Incidents" — a *relation*, not an act. No definition, no source line, no note.
- MoD, PIB 7 Feb 2018: "Terrorist Initiated Incidents (TIIs) … along the Line of Control (LC) under operational control of Army" — LoC-only, Army-only.

**Does an incident require a fatality?** SATP's "Incidents of Killing": YES, stated. SATP's "Terrorism Related Incidents": unstated. **MHA: NOT ESTABLISHED for any of its three series.** No MHA document says whether firing, grenade attacks, IED recoveries or non-fatal attacks are inside the count.

**Do MHA and SATP draw the line in the same place? NO, and on two orthogonal axes** — the fatality gate and the provenance gate (finding C above).

**Stone-pelting is outside every incident count at every vintage.** Organised stone-pelting alone was 1,328 in 2018 against a widest-basis total of 614.

### (b) Civilians killed

**This is where the phase's central answer sits, and the answer is not the expected one.**

**MHA does not exclude civilians killed by security forces from its Annual Report table. It absorbs them into an undifferentiated column.** MHA Annual Report 2023-24 and 2024-25 footnote, verbatim:

> "*[Civilians killed includes civilians killed in terrorist-initiated incidents and civilians killed in encounter/counter Terrorism operations]"

But that is the position only from AR 2022-23, when the values were restated upward (2018: 39→55) **without a footnote**; the footnote arrived one report later. And it is not the position in MHA's parliamentary replies, which use two different narrower bounding formulas:

- RS US Q.371, 20 Jul 2022: "The number of security forces and civilians who died **due to terrorist attacks** since 2018" — 2018 = 39.
- RS US Q.2670, 10 Aug 2016: "…the number of security personnel/ civilians killed/injured **in these incidents**" — 2016 to 31 July = 6.

**Three bounding formulas, and none of them is in a table label.** Every automated extraction carries away the column heading and discards the sentence.

**MHA has never published a column for civilians killed by security forces**, in any Annual Report, in any year, under any heading. The broadened definition reaches only deaths in encounters and CT operations — not protest firing, crowd control, checkpoints, or crowd-control munitions. Those deaths appear in MHA Annual Reports only as *prose*: AR 2013-14 narrates "a clash between the CAPF and civilians at PS Gool in District Ramban in J&K on 18.07.2013 in which 4 persons were killed and 41 injured" and "the death of civilian protestor in CRPF firing at Gagran, Shopian on 11.09.2013" on the same pages as a table showing 15 civilians killed for that year.

**Which instrument does carry civilians killed by security forces?** Worked through exhaustively:

| Instrument | Carries perpetrator attribution for civilian deaths? |
|---|---|
| MHA | **No.** Bounded to terrorist attacks, or absorbed into an undifferentiated composite. |
| SATP | **No perpetrator breakdown at all.** |
| GTD | **Structurally cannot** — non-state actors only, by definition. |
| UCDP | **Three country-years ever** for Government-of-India one-sided violence (1990, 1993, 2002), none after 2002. |
| ACLED | **Yes in principle** — but India coverage begins 1 January 2016, and it **defaults to 0 fatalities when the source is unclear**. Not retrieved this session (API key required). |
| **JKCCS / APDP** | **Yes — and they are the only bodies that ever published it.** |

**The single most important number in this map.** JKCCS's perpetrator split for J&K civilian deaths in 2018: **71 killed by armed forces, 25 by unidentified gunmen, 18 by militants.** RELAYED.

Set that against the same year's other counts: MHA gives **39** civilians "due to terrorist attacks"; MHA's later composite gives **55**; SATP gives **86**; **JKCCS's militant-attributed subtotal is 18.**

**These four numbers are not one quantity measured with error. They are disjoint subsets, all called "civilians killed".** That sentence should govern how stage 3 treats every civilian-death figure in this phase.

**And JKCCS ceased publishing in 2020.** So **after 2019 no instrument of any provenance publishes the perpetrator split for civilian deaths in J&K.** ACLED is the only candidate successor and was not retrieved.

**The three-way disagreement for 2018, all official or near-official:**

| Source | Civilians killed, 2018 |
|---|---|
| MHA Annual Reports 2018-19 to 2021-22, and RS US Q.371 | **39** |
| MHA Annual Reports 2022-23 onward, and RS US Q.228 "(Source: UT of J&K)" | **55** |
| SATP | **86** |

**A discipline that must survive into authoring.** The 39→55 restatement looks like it isolates "civilians killed in CT operations" as a residual of 16. **It does not, and that residual must not be published as a figure.** The residual is 0 for 2021, which would mean no civilian died in any operation that year; and the SF-killed figure for 2020 also moved (62→63) across the same restatement, so the restatement carries ordinary revisions too. The residual's basis is unestablished.

**And SATP's excess over MHA is unexplained.** The obvious hypothesis — that SATP catches protest and encounter-site deaths MHA omits — is **contradicted by 2016**, the year of the largest protest death toll in the period, where SATP's count (14) is *below* MHA's (15). Ratios run 2.21 (2018), 1.08 (2019), 0.89 (2020), 0.88 (2021). **Do not treat the two civilian series as interchangeable in any year on the strength of their agreement in others.**

### (c) Security-force personnel killed

**Carried by:** MHA Annual Reports; MHA replies; SATP; MoD (LoC fatal casualties).

**Definitions.** MHA's column is "SFs killed" (or "Security Forces killed"); AR 2015-16 alone used "SFs who lost their lives". **The composition is never stated** — whether it covers Army/Rashtriya Rifles, CAPFs, J&K Police and Special Police Officers, and whether that composition changed. One partial data point: RS US Q.2628 of 11 December 2019 counts an SPO inside the total. And the 2012 figure was silently revised from 15 to 38 between AR 2013-14 and AR 2014-15 with incidents unchanged at 220 — which implies a change in which forces count, never stated.

**THIS IS THE ONE QUANTITY WHERE THE INSTRUMENTS AGREE.** MHA against SATP: 91/95 (2018), 80/78 (2019), 63/56 (2020), 42/45 (2021) — within about 10% every year. (CORRECTION, stage 5: the 2020 MHA figure is given here as 62 in an earlier draft of this line; 62 is the AR 2021-22 value and MHA's currently published value from AR 2022-23 onward is **63**, per part 06b §2 — the restatement noted at part 01 break 2b. Use 63.) A press-derived register and an administrative register both capture "a soldier or policeman died" reliably, because the dead are named, pensioned and publicly honoured. **Stage 3 should carry this explicitly: a record treating every measure in this domain as equally compromised would be wrong.**

### (d) Militants / terrorists killed

**Carried by:** MHA Annual Reports ("Terrorists killed"); SATP ("Terrorists/Insurgents/Extremists"); J&K Police via press.

**Are "militants killed" and "terrorists killed" the same population?** Effectively yes across instruments — the labels differ, the referent does not. What differs is who declares membership, and the declaration is made by the force that conducted the killing, before independent identification (part 08).

**Local vs foreign split — the answer is that BOTH sides abandoned it.**
- **MHA has never disaggregated "Terrorists killed" into local and foreign, in any Annual Report, in any of seventeen years.**
- **SATP had the split and dropped it.** Its old table "Local and Foreign Terrorists Killed in Jammu and Kashmir" runs 1990 to 15 November 2001 and stops. Its note, verbatim: "The data includes only identified terrorists killed in J&K". The current SATP datasheet family has no local/foreign breakdown at all.

So the local/foreign split for the entire post-2001 period — the whole period of analytical interest — **exists only as unsourced official assertions in press conferences.** The widely-quoted "42 of 68 killed in 2024 were foreign nationals" has no retrievable basis. Note also SATP's abandoned qualifier: the old series counted **only identified** militants, a constraint SATP no longer states anywhere.

**And the two instruments disagree by ~60% on the current year.** For 2025 the Army Chief stated 29 militants eliminated; SATP's datasheet gives 46. Both RETRIEVED. The likely definitional cause: the Army counts eliminations **in operations under its own operational control**, while SATP counts all deaths of persons it classifies as militants in J&K from any cause and any agency — police-led operations, CRPF, BSF at the International Border, and deaths in incidents rather than operations. **Two "militants killed in 2025" figures, both real, counting different populations.** This is a live divergence, not a historical one.

### (e) Infiltration

**Four distinct published quantities, from three bodies.**

| Quantity | Verbatim label | Publisher |
|---|---|---|
| Attempted | "Infiltration attempts" | MHA |
| Net / successful | "Net Estimated infiltration"; earlier "Successful"; earlier "Net infiltration (Estimated)" | MHA |
| Eliminated | "Infiltration Bid Eliminated (IBE)" | MoD, LoC-only, Army-only |
| Unlabelled | "Total" (one row, 2005-2013) | MHA |

**Does the published series switch between them? YES, repeatedly, and MHA has never said whether they are the same quantity.** The row-label history: `Total` (to AR 2013-14) → `Total Infiltration Attempts` (AR 2014-15, with "(Source MAC)") → adds `Successful` (AR 2015-16) → `Net infiltration (Estimated)` (AR 2016-17) → `Net Estimated infiltration` (AR 2017-18 to 2022-23) → **no table at all (AR 2023-24, AR 2024-25)**.

**MHA does not publish "successful infiltration" under that name** after 2015-16. In AR 2013-14 a successful-infiltration figure appears only in prose, never in the table: "the number of successful infiltration attempts has declined from 121 in 2012 to 97 in 2013."

**Source attribution appears in exactly two years** — "(Source MAC)", the Multi Agency Centre, an Intelligence Bureau body — then is dropped while the series continues.

**A quiet restatement:** net estimated infiltration for 2019 was published as 138 in three successive reports and as 141 in AR 2022-23, with no note.

**The series is discontinued.** No MHA infiltration figure exists for 2023 or later. The policy paragraph about containing infiltration survives in AR 2024-25; the measurement does not.

**And into that vacuum the Army speaks, verbally, about a quantity it does not publish.** The then GOC-in-C Northern Command declared 2023 the "year of zero infiltration" (RELAYED). That is an assertion about the **net** quantity, made by a body that publishes only **Infiltration Bid Eliminated**. **The Army's public infiltration claims and MHA's published infiltration series are not the same variable, and the Army's are in no document.**

**Why a naive series is guaranteed to be wrong.** The three quantities move in *opposite* directions under one underlying reality: if interception improves, attempts may be unchanged, IBE rises, and net falls. A series built by taking "the infiltration figure" from whichever body published one in a given year will show a decline that is partly an artefact of switching from attempts (MHA Annual Reports, dominant 2001-2010) to net (which only begins later) to Army "zero infiltration" statements (2023 onward). **All three must be separate series, and a gap in one must never be filled from another.**

Two further unresolved divergences inside MHA's own numbers, both RELAYED and both undated because the conflicting primaries could not be retrieved: net infiltration for 2019 published as 138 and as 141, and for 2021 as 34 and as 24.

### (f) Local recruitment into militancy

**Carried by: nothing that qualifies as an instrument.**

- **Zero occurrences in seventeen MHA Annual Reports**, and none in any parliamentary reply retrieved. Established by exhaustive grep.
- **No published definition of "recruitment" was found anywhere**, by any body. Confirmed joining, missing-with-weapon, and intelligence estimate are different quantities and no source distinguishes them.
- What circulates are DGP press-conference claims, which conflict: 2018 as 187, 218 and "over 200"; 2022 as 100, 110 and 130; 2023 as 10 and 22.
- The pre-2018 figures exist because a Chief Minister answered an MLA in writing in the J&K Assembly (66/88/126 for 2015/2016/2017). That route is now closed (part 10).
- The **"hybrid militant"** category, introduced October-November 2021, is defined by police as someone "not on the official list of militants". **Whether such persons enter the recruitment count could not be established.**

**This is the quantity that is politically central, constantly quoted, and has no stable official series at all.**

### (g) Encounter operations and cordon-and-search operations

**Encounters: counted, but only recently and only in one instrument.**
- MHA Annual Report "Encounters/Counter Terror operations", from AR 2022-23: 189 (2018), 102, 118, 100, 117, 48 (2023), 57 (2024). **Undefined — no note says what constitutes one.**
- A much earlier one-off: LS US Q.198, 24 February 2015, column heading verbatim "No. of incidents of encounter between terrorists and Security Forces" — 2014 = 57. **The capability to count operations preceded its publication by seven years.**
- **Do not splice these.** 2014 = 57 and 2018 = 189 are two undefined quantities sharing a word.

That reply also carries a column MHA has never published in an Annual Report: "No. of Terrorists/suspects arrested" — **which merges a designated terrorist with an unproven suspect into one number.**

**Cordon and search operations: NOT COUNTED ANYWHERE.** No published count was found from MHA, the J&K Police or the Army, for any year, by two agents searching independently. **The most common form of contact between the security forces and the civilian population is uncounted — while the instrument counts the encounters, detentions and property damage that CASOs produce.**

### Ceasefire violations on the Line of Control — an instrument the operator did not name, and it matters

**Two ministries count, split by geography.** MHA counts the BSF-held International Border; MoD counts the Army-held Line of Control. MoD's own word for its categories is "Nomenclature", and it names three: CFVs, Terrorist Initiated Incidents, and Infiltration Bid Eliminated — all scoped "along the Line of Control (LC) under operational control of Army".

**There is no agreed definition of one violation.** Per Lt Gen (retd) Syed Ata Hasnain, former GOC 15 Corps (RELAYED): "no kind of parleys between the two sides by which you could come to a formal understanding of what could constitute a CFV"; "There is a great deal of subjectivity"; "much depends on the perceptions of the Commanders on the ground". The operative unit is **sector × firing-episode, with episodes separated by a gap of roughly two to four hours, adjudicated by the local commander.** An earlier Indian rule excluded "ineffective" firing and counted only firing "for effect" — if that filter was relaxed, part of the 2013→2017 rise is a rise in what got logged. **The Army instruction could not be retrieved.**

**Even official Indian counts of the same year contradict each other:** 2017 is 881 (Government of India to Lok Sabha, LoC+IB) against MoD's own 860 for the LoC alone — and the LoC component of the 881 is 771. 771 ≠ 860, same quantity, same year, both official.

**The February 2021 understanding is retrieved as a primary document** — the DGsMO Joint Statement of 25 February 2021, via the UN Peacemaker repository. Operative sentence verbatim: "Both sides agreed for strict observance of all agreements, understandings and cease firing along the Line of Control and all other sectors with effect from midnight 24/25 Feb 2021." **It is a joint statement, not a treaty; it contains no definition of a violation and no monitoring or reporting mechanism.**

**AND THEN FOUR INSTRUMENT CHANGES HAPPENED AT ONCE, at the point the number became favourable.** Compare:
- **MHA to Lok Sabha, 3 August 2021:** a twelve-row month-by-month integer table of "Number of incidents of Ceasefire Violation/Cross Border Firing", four years wide — 2,140 / 3,479 / 5,133 / 664.
- **MoD to Lok Sabha, 11 August 2023:** **no count of violations at all.** A casualty table, mostly em-dashes, plus one prose clause: "Ever since the Director General of Military Operations understanding came into existence in February, 2021, there have been just three incidents of ceasefire violations and no incident of shelling along the Line of Control (LoC)."

The respondent ministry changed (MHA→MoD), the geography changed (LoC+IB→LoC only, so the post-2021 statement makes no claim about the International Border at all), the period basis changed (monthly→cumulative with no year attribution), and the unit changed (counted integer series→prose assertion). **A researcher assembling a CFV series across 2020→2023 is not observing a fall from 5,133 to 0; they are observing an instrument being replaced.** No continuously-maintained CFV series of any provenance exists after 2021.

## 3b. THE INSTRUMENTS THE OPERATOR DID NOT NAME

### UCDP (Uppsala Conflict Data Program) — the only instrument shipping a native uncertainty interval, and it carries almost no state-perpetrated violence for Kashmir

Datasets RETRIEVED and parsed at v26.1 (current as at August 2026). Definitions verbatim from the GED codebook:

- **State-based armed conflict:** "A contested incompatibility that concerns government and/or territory where the use of armed force between two parties, of which at least one is the government of a state, results in at least 25 battle-related deaths in one calendar year."
- **One-sided violence:** "The deliberate use of armed force by the government of a state or by a formally organised group against civilians which results in at least 25 deaths in a year."
- **GED event:** "An incident where armed force was used by an organized actor against another organized actor, or against civilians, resulting in at least 1 direct death at a specific location and a specific date."

**The gate that decides everything for Kashmir, verbatim:** "Only events linkable to a UCDP/PRIO Armed Conflict, a UCDP Non-State Conflict or a UCDP One-Sided Violence instance are included." **GED is not a general incident register.** Violence outside a recognised dyad is invisible to it.

**How UCDP codes Kashmir, established from the datasets themselves:**
- Interstate conflict 218 (India vs Pakistan, territory "Kashmir") — active in 28 calendar years; **v25.1 ends it at 2020, v26.1 adds 2025.** A J&K instrument built on v25.1 will be wrong about 2025.
- Intrastate conflict 364 (India vs "Kashmir insurgents") — active **every year 1990-2025, 36 consecutive years**. **Exactly one dyad.** UCDP does not disaggregate Hizbul Mujahideen, LeT, JeM or JKLF. **Per-organisation battle-death series cannot be obtained from UCDP.**
- One-sided violence, actor "Kashmir insurgents" — 22 non-contiguous years. **Missing years are NOT zeros**; they are years below the 25-death threshold. This is the most common misreading of UCDP.
- **One-sided violence, actor "Government of India" — only three country-years ever: 1990, 1993, 2002.** UCDP has recorded no Government-of-India one-sided violence anywhere in India since 2002. **UCDP therefore carries essentially no state-perpetrated civilian-killing series for Kashmir in the modern period — it is not the missing two-sided instrument.**

**Two cautions.** UCDP ships low/best/high estimates and the interval can be enormous — 1993 one-sided violence is best 34 against high 654. Reporting `best` alone can understate uncertainty by a factor of 20. And UCDP **explicitly rejects source-consistency**, verbatim: "[UCDP is not] 'source-consistent' — i.e. we do not aim to use the exact same set of sources to generate the [data]". That sentence must appear in any longitudinal limitation note built on UCDP.

**Operational note:** the UCDP REST API now requires an access token and returns 401 anonymously; bulk CSV downloads remain open. Any pipeline on the old open API is broken.

### GTD (Global Terrorism Database) — coverage ends

Reported as having ceased coverage and been defunded, with the series ending around 2020. **NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED in this phase — the end year should be confirmed before any GTD figure is used, and GTD must not be relied on for anything after 2019 without that check.**

### Kashmir Media Service — a claimed-quantity publisher, not a dataset

Pakistan-based. All figures attributed to an unnamed internal "Research Section". Published claims include "96,480 Kashmiris were killed, including more than 7,400 custodial deaths" since January 1989.

**No methodology exists, and this was positively established** rather than assumed: the site's full page list was enumerated via its own API and contains no About page, no Methodology page and no Sources page. It publishes a cookies policy and no statement of what a killing is.

**Demonstrable internal drift, from KMS's own outputs:** killed-since-1989 quoted as 96,480 (December 2025) and 96,498 (June 2026) — a rise of 18 — while its own flow reporting gives 21 killed in seven months of 2026. Stock and flow do not reconcile. "Women molested" is quoted at 11,269, 11,275 and 11,285 within one year. A separate KMS claim of "500,000 Kashmiris sacrificed since 1947" is an order of magnitude above its own since-1989 figure.

**Assessment: no unit of analysis, no inclusion criteria, no source disclosure, no event-level record, no revision log, demonstrable drift. Its own terminology is its coding scheme. Usable only as an object of study — what the Pakistani state-aligned narrative asserts in a given year — never as a measurement. Stage 3 must not admit any KMS number as a figure.**

### Legal Forum for Kashmir — the strongest non-Indian compiler, and partly derivative

Annual Review 2023 RETRIEVED. Unlike KMS it states a method, verbatim: "This report is based on the primary and secondary sources of information collected by the research team… The report has also compilation from official sources of IOJK, recognized media groups, NGO fact finding reports, as well as the information received from the victim families."

Thin — no inclusion criteria, no counting unit, no source list — but it is a *stated* basis, and it **admits reliance on Indian official sources**, so it is not independent of the instrument it is used to challenge. That admission is the useful finding.

### Leads identified and NOT worked — flagged so they are not mistaken for absences

- **Kashmir Law and Justice Project (kljp.org)** — publishing monthly human-rights briefings on Indian-administered Kashmir as recently as April 2026. **Potentially the most important live successor to JKCCS**, and therefore potentially the only current instrument carrying perpetrator attribution. Not investigated at all: no methodology, no counting basis, no independence assessment. **Recommend a dedicated pass before any claim that nobody publishes the perpetrator split after 2019 is authored as settled.**
- **ACLED** — India coverage from 1 January 2016; can in principle carry perpetrator attribution. Export requires a registered API key; not obtained. ACLED's own methodology piece on improving its India coverage 2016-2018 has been **de-published**, so whether it retroactively revised those years is unknown — meaning the earliest years of any ACLED J&K series may carry an undocumented within-series discontinuity. ACLED also quantises verbal tolls ("dozens"→12, "hundreds"→100) and **defaults to 0 fatalities when the source is unclear**.
- **GTTAC/GRID** — GTD's practical post-2020 successor, US State Department funded. J&K coverage not established.
- **UCDP definitions** came through a summarising pass rather than a raw parse and should be verified byte-for-byte before being quoted as verbatim.
- **MoD Annual Reports** — `mod.gov.in` unreachable; the year-wise ceasefire tables in them were never retrieved. All MoD material here is from PIB releases of MoD parliamentary answers via `web.archive.org`.
- **data.gov.in J&K catalogue** — three resources known by URL; the full catalogue is unknown because the site's backend API returned empty.

## 4. THE LEDGER QUESTION

See part 00-head, which carries the per-quantity answer as the operator required.
