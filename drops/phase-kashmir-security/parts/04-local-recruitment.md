# 04 — CONTESTED: did local recruitment fall, or was it reclassified?

## The finding that comes before the argument: there is no series

**No MHA Annual Report has ever published a local-recruitment figure.** Established by exhaustive grep across seventeen retrieved reports, 2008-09 to 2024-25: zero occurrences of "joined militancy", "joining militancy" or "local recruitment" as a counted quantity. RETRIEVED and certain. The subject appears only as a policy aspiration in near-identical prose for over a decade — AR 2012-13: "It has also encouraged policies to mainstream the youth and discourage the local youth from joining militancy"; AR 2018-19: "…to wean them away from militancy."

No such figure was found in any retrieved parliamentary reply either.

**A quantity the Ministry describes itself as managing for fifteen consecutive years is not measured in the Ministry's own annual report.**

## What exists instead

Figures announced at press conferences, principally by the J&K Director General of Police, plus one pre-2018 assembly answer. **RELAYED throughout — no primary document was retrieved for any of these.**

**The variance is the finding.** For 2022 the same authority is reported saying 100, 110 and 130; for 2023, 10 and 22 — a 30% and a 120% spread. For 2018, three official attributions give 187, 218 and "over 200". **This is a set of press-conference claims, not a statistical series, and it must not be authored as one.** The direction of travel — a large fall — is consistent across every version. The magnitude is not establishable to better than a wide band. Any point value entering the instrument would be laundering.

Note the shape of the loss: the 2015-2017 figures exist because an MLA asked and a Chief Minister answered **in writing**. The post-2019 figures exist because the executive chose to announce them. The instrument changed from an answer to a claim.

## The "hybrid militant" category

**Introduced October-November 2021**, following the killing of two non-Muslim teachers in a Srinagar government school on 7 October 2021. RELAYED.

Definitions, in the police's own words, RELAYED:
- IGP Kashmir Vijay Kumar, November 2021: "part-time terrorists **who were not on the official list of militants**", looking like ordinary civilians, in contact via digital devices, trained locally.
- DGP Dilbag Singh: "hybrid terrorists" have **no previous record of involvement in terrorism** and become identified as terrorists only **after they carry out their first attack**.

A second adjacent category, "militant associate", covers those providing support. J&K rights activist Muhammad Ahsan Untoo, RETRIEVED (The Week, 18 November 2021): "Action can be taken against anybody by labeling him as hybrid militant."

J&K Police publicly called hybrid terrorists their biggest challenge for 2022. More than 70% of 55 targeted killings since 2021 were attributed by J&K Police to hybrid militants. RELAYED.

**MHA's Annual Reports never use the term.** I checked: every occurrence of "hybrid" in the seventeen reports is a hybrid-mode meeting, a hybrid surveillance system, or a solar-wind hybrid street light. RETRIEVED. So the category that reorganises the population being counted exists in police usage and not in the flagship instrument at all.

## The counting question — and why it is not a matter of opinion

**COULD NOT ESTABLISH: whether people classified as "hybrid militants" are, or are not, included in the year-wise local-recruitment figures.** No official statement either way was found, and no journalist appears to have put the question directly.

What *can* be established is suggestive and should be stated at exactly that strength. The category is defined, in the police's own words, by **absence from the official list of militants**. Whatever the recruitment count is, it is a list. On the plain meaning of the officials' own definitions, a hybrid militant is by construction someone the recruitment count did not have. Whether such a person is retrospectively added after his first attack is precisely what could not be established.

**Timing.** The category was introduced in October-November 2021. The recruitment figures then fall from ~125 (2021) to ~100-130 (2022) to 10-22 (2023) to ~7 (2024). The steep fall postdates the category's introduction by one to two years. **That is equally consistent with a genuine collapse and with a redefinition absorbing part of the flow. The evidence does not discriminate, and the instrument must say so rather than pick.**

## CASE FOR — the security establishment's case in its own terms

Local recruitment has collapsed and every corroborating indicator moves with it. Terrorists killed fell from 257 in 2018 to 67 in 2024, of whom 42 were foreign nationals — fewer than 30 locals killed in a year in which the forces conducted 57 encounters. You cannot redefine your way to a smaller number of bodies carrying foreign passports. Infiltration attempts fell from 419 to 53 and net infiltration from 136 to 14. Over 1,900 overground workers were detained between 2019 and 2023, dismantling the recruitment pipeline itself.

The hybrid-militant category was not invented to hide recruits. It was coined to describe a real and novel adversary tactic, adopted *because* conventional recruitment had become suicidal — and the police announced it publicly, repeatedly, as their single biggest challenge. That is not how an organisation behaves when it is concealing a category.

## CASE AGAINST — the critics' case in its own terms

The recruitment "series" is not a series. It is a set of numbers announced at press conferences by the officer whose performance they measure; unpublished in any statistical return; absent from seventeen consecutive MHA Annual Reports and from every parliamentary reply retrieved; and internally inconsistent — 100, 110 and 130 for one year, 10 and 22 for the next. There is no document to audit.

And in the middle of the fall, the same police force introduced a category whose defining property is *not appearing on the militant list*. The men in it are, by the police's own definition, people who joined militancy without being counted as having joined militancy. Whether they are added back is a question nobody has answered — and nobody has been in a position to compel an answer since the J&K Assembly stopped sitting. The Assembly, when it sat, is exactly where these numbers used to be tabled: by a Chief Minister, in writing, to a named MLA.

## DIFFERENT FACTS OR DIFFERENT WEIGHTINGS?

**Three distinct things, and they must not be merged.**

1. **Different facts, established.** The official recruitment numbers for 2018, 2022 and 2023 conflict with each other. There is no single official fact here to weight — the state has published mutually exclusive claims.

2. **An open definitional question, not a weighting.** Whether hybrid militants are counted in the recruitment series is a factual question with a determinate answer that exists in someone's hands and that I could not obtain. It is not a matter of interpretation, and it should not be recorded as a dispute. **It is an absence with a route** — see part 02.

3. **Different weightings, on shared facts.** Both sides accept the fall in terrorists killed, in the foreign-national share, and in infiltration. The government reads these as independent corroboration; critics read them as further measures produced by the same institution and subject to the same identification problem.

**The strongest corroborating fact for the government's case, which critics have not answered:** foreign nationals were 42 of 68 militants killed in 2024. A shrinking *local* share of the militant dead is hard to produce by reclassifying recruits, and no critic located in this phase offers a competing account of it. RELAYED (CTC West Point). **Stage 3 should carry this as an unanswered point rather than as a settled one — the underlying local/foreign split is not published by MHA in any year (part 01), so the 42-of-68 figure's own basis is not established.**

- The provenance of the 42-of-68 local/foreign split for 2024 — MHA has never disaggregated "Terrorists killed" by local and foreign in any Annual Report.
