# 02 — ABSENCES

Classified against the live schema's four values, applied as written:
- **not-collected** — never gathered; if compelled tomorrow the holder would have nothing to produce.
- **not-published** — exists in a holder's hands, not released. Test is producibility under compulsion, not whether anyone asked.
- **withheld** — exists, release was specifically requested or legally required, and was refused. Requires an IDENTIFIABLE REFUSAL.
- **never-defined** — no agreed definition exists, so it could not be collected even in principle. NOT "nobody has studied it".

**Rule observed throughout: for `not-published` and `withheld` a real route is given, or the classification is reconsidered.** Where no route exists I say so rather than inventing one. A placeholder route is worse than none — it enters a verification queue and cannot be worked.

**Rule observed throughout: the STATED reason is the responsible body's, not mine.** Where the body has stated no reason, I say that, rather than supplying one.

## A1 — Civilians killed by security forces outside counter-terrorism operations

**What is not measured, stated positively:** the number of civilians killed by security forces in circumstances other than encounters and counter-terrorism operations — protest firing, crowd control, checkpoint shootings, and deaths from crowd-control munitions.

**Why no figure exists.** MHA has never published a column for this, under any heading, in any Annual Report from 2008-09 to 2024-25. The broadened definition introduced in AR 2022-23 and disclosed in AR 2023-24 reaches only civilians killed "in encounter/counter Terrorism operations" — it does not reach protest firing or crowd control. And MHA's Annual Reports *narrate* such deaths in prose while omitting them from the table: AR 2013-14 records "a clash between the CAPF and civilians at PS Gool in District Ramban in J&K on 18.07.2013 in which 4 persons were killed and 41 injured" and "the death of civilian protestor in CRPF firing at Gagran, Shopian on 11.09.2013", on the same pages as a table showing 15 civilians killed for that year.

**STATED REASON: none. MHA has never stated a reason, because MHA has never acknowledged the category.** This is important and must not be papered over — the absence has no stated reason at all, which is a different situation from a refused or explained one.

**Classification: `not-collected`, with LOW CONFIDENCE in the classification itself.** The honest position: MHA does not publish it and has never acknowledged holding it. Whether the J&K Police hold it at police-station level and could produce it under compulsion is unknown, and could not be tested because `jkpolice.gov.in` was unreachable (part 11). If they do hold it, the correct value is `not-published`.

**Route.** A parliamentary question to MHA asking specifically for civilians killed by security-force action in J&K, disaggregated from the composite "Civilians killed" column — i.e. asking MHA to publish the two components of a column it has already told Parliament is composite. This is a real and precisely-framed route: the footnote in AR 2023-24 is the hook, because it concedes the column contains two populations.

**`reasonDisputed`: TRUE, `disputeKind`: evidentiary.** MHA's implicit position — that this quantity is not part of its accounting — is contradicted by its own prose, which describes individual such deaths in the same section as the table that excludes them. The Ministry demonstrably knows of the events and declines to count them.

## A2 — Cordon and search operations (CASO)

**What is not measured:** the number of cordon-and-search operations conducted in J&K.

**Why no figure exists.** No published count was found from MHA, the J&K Police or the Army, in any document, for any year. CASO was revived as a routine tactic around 2017 and is named constantly in reporting of individual operations. MHA counts encounters from AR 2022-23; it has never counted cordon-and-search.

**STATED REASON: none given by anyone.** No body has been asked on the record and refused, and none has explained the omission.

**Classification: `not-collected`, provisionally.** Two agents searched independently and found nothing. **But this is a negative proved by absence of evidence, and it should be recorded at that strength.** An operation requiring deployment orders and after-action returns is very unlikely to be genuinely uncounted internally, which argues for `not-published` — but nothing was found either way.

**Route.** An RTI to the Director General of Police, Jammu and Kashmir, and separately to the Ministry of Defence in respect of Rashtriya Rifles and Army formations, for the year-wise number of cordon-and-search operations conducted. **Caveat that must travel with this route:** J&K Police's own web presence was unreachable in this session, so the correct RTI addressee designation could not be verified. The route is real but the addressee needs confirming before it is filed.

**Why this matters more than its obscurity suggests.** CASO is the most common form of contact between the security forces and the civilian population. It produces the detentions, the property damage, and the encounters that MHA does count. **The instrument counts the outcomes of an activity it does not count.**

## A3 — Local recruitment into militancy

**What is not measured:** a defined, published, year-wise count of local residents joining militant organisations.

**Why no figure exists as a series.** Zero occurrences in seventeen MHA Annual Reports and in every parliamentary reply retrieved. What circulates are police press-conference claims which conflict with each other — 2018 given as 187, as 218 and as "over 200"; 2022 as 100, 110 and 130; 2023 as 10 and 22 (part 04).

**STATED REASON: none.** No body has explained why the quantity it manages is not published.

**Classification: `not-published`.** The state plainly holds *something* — officials quote figures — but has never published a return, and the internal inconsistency shows that what is quoted is not a maintained series.

**A second, distinct absence sits inside this one, and it is `never-defined`:** there is no agreed definition of what counts as joining militancy. Confirmed joining, missing-with-weapon, and intelligence estimate are different quantities, and no official definition was located. The introduction of "hybrid militant" in October-November 2021 — defined by the police as someone "not on the official list of militants" — makes the boundary worse, not better. **This meets `never-defined` on the strict test: not that nobody has studied it, but that no agreed definition exists, so the quantity could not be collected consistently even in principle.**

**Route for the `not-published` part.** A parliamentary question to MHA for the year-wise number of J&K residents recorded as having joined militant organisations, **with the definition used**, and asking expressly whether persons categorised as "hybrid militants" are included. The definitional rider is what makes this route worth filing — the bare number would reproduce the existing confusion.

## A4 — Organised stone-pelting: published once, to Parliament, never in the flagship

**What is not measured *in the flagship instrument*:** law-and-order and stone-pelting incidents in J&K.

**Why.** No stone-pelting or law-and-order incident table has ever appeared in the J&K section of any MHA Annual Report. But the series exists: RS Unstarred Q.228 of 24 July 2024 gives "Organized Stone Pelting" 1328 (2018), 00 (2023), 00 (2024).

**Classification: `not-published`** as regards the Annual Report. **MHA holds the data and produced it on request.** The omission from the flagship is an editorial choice, not a data gap, and must be recorded as such.

**Route.** A parliamentary question to MHA — the identical route that produced RS Q.228 — for the full year-wise series rather than the three sampled years. This is the strongest route in this part because it has already worked once.

**A separate absence inside it, `never-defined`:** "organized" is not defined anywhere. Unorganised stone-pelting is outside the count by construction, and both 2023 and 2024 are reported as exactly `00`. A whole-year zero in a category whose boundary is undefined is not a measurement.

## A5 — Infiltration after 2022

**What is not measured:** infiltration attempts and net infiltration into J&K for 2023 onward.

**Why.** MHA published a two-row infiltration table in every Annual Report from at least 2010-11 through 2022-23 and **discontinued it**. AR 2023-24 and AR 2024-25 contain no infiltration figures at all, while retaining the policy paragraph describing the "multi-pronged approach to contain cross border infiltration".

**STATED REASON: none. MHA did not announce the discontinuation.**

**Classification: `not-published`.** The border-management apparatus that generates the count continues to operate and is described at length in the same reports.

**Route.** A parliamentary question to MHA for year-wise infiltration attempts and net estimated infiltration for 2023, 2024 and 2025 — a request to resume a series the Ministry itself published for over a decade, which is a strong framing. A parallel question to the Ministry of Defence would test whether the Army holds it independently.

## A6 — Cumulative conflict deaths since 1990

**What is not measured:** the running total of deaths in the J&K conflict since 1990.

**Why.** MHA published it in the opening sentence of the J&K section from at least AR 2008-09 to AR 2020-21 — terminal value "14,091 Civilians and 5,356 Security Force (SF) personnel had lost their lives (upto 2020)" — and discontinued it from AR 2021-22.

**STATED REASON: none.**

**Classification: `not-published`.** The components are still published annually; only the running total stopped.

**Route.** A parliamentary question to MHA for the cumulative figure updated to the current year. Trivially answerable from MHA's own published rows, which makes a refusal informative in itself.

**A distinct, permanent absence inside this one, `not-collected`:** the cumulative ledger **never had a militants-killed term**, in any year it was published. For three decades MHA's cumulative account of the conflict ran two categories and omitted the third that its own annual tables counted. So there has never been an official cumulative total of conflict deaths, only of two of the three populations.

## A7 — The basis of the pre-2021-22 incident series

**What is not measured:** what the "Incidents" figures of 614 (2018), 594 (2019) and 342 (2017) actually counted.

**Why.** MHA restated them to 417, 255 and 279 with no note (part 01, break 1). RS Q.210 of 12 December 2018 states that "The number of incidents includes the violence committed by terrorists during anti militancy operations", which accounts for part of the gap. RS Q.228 of 24 July 2024 rules out stone-pelting, since organised stone-pelting alone was 1328 in 2018 against a total of 614. **What the ~197 removed events for 2018 were is unestablished.**

**Classification: `not-published`.** MHA performed the restatement, so MHA knows what it removed.

**Route.** A parliamentary question to MHA asking for the definitional basis of the "Incidents" figures published in the Annual Reports up to 2020-21 and the reason for their revision in the Annual Report 2021-22. Narrow, answerable, and it names the two documents.

## A8 — Any definition of "terrorist incident"

**What is not measured:** whether a terrorist incident requires a fatality, and whether firing, grenade attacks, IED recoveries and non-fatal attacks are inside or outside the count.

**Why.** No MHA Annual Report in seventeen years defines "Incidents", "Terrorist Initiated Incidents (TII)" or "Encounters/CT Ops.". The single definitional sentence anywhere in the corpus is in RS Q.210 (2018) and is partial.

**Classification: `not-published`.** MHA operates a counting rule; a counting rule exists somewhere in writing for the returns to be compiled.

**Route.** An RTI to the Ministry of Home Affairs, Department of Jammu, Kashmir and Ladakh Affairs, for the instructions or proforma issued to the UT of J&K for compiling the terrorist-violence returns reproduced in the Annual Report. **This is the highest-value single route in the phase**, because it would resolve A7, A8 and much of part 09 at once.

## A9 — The written order governing burial of encounter dead

**What is not measured — indeed, what does not exist:** any published order, circular or SOP establishing the practice, in force since April 2020, of burying those killed in encounters in remote graveyards rather than releasing bodies to families.

**STATED REASON, and here the responsible body has spoken.** The Inspector General of Police, Kashmir, in April 2020, verbatim: "As per several orders of the government of the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir in view of the Disaster Management Act, we have to ensure strict lockdown even during burial. To avoid such situations we are doing burial at safer and isolated places." A senior police officer in the same reporting: "It's not a policy now but it should become a policy." A lawyer acting for a family was told: "No written order has been given to us."

**Classification: `not-collected`** — on the police's own account there is no such instrument to produce. The practice is presented as a consequence of generic lockdown orders that say nothing about burials.

**No route is offered, and that is deliberate.** An RTI for a document that the responsible officials say does not exist would return a nil reply, which is not the same as closing the absence. **If a route is wanted it must be framed differently** — an RTI to the J&K Home Department for *the legal authority under which* bodies are buried without release to next of kin, which asks for the basis rather than for a document. I flag that the correct addressee could not be verified because `jkhome.nic.in` was unreachable.

**`reasonDisputed`: TRUE, `disputeKind`: normative.** Nobody contests the fact that no written burial order has been published. What is contested is the characterisation: the administration treats lockdown orders as sufficient authority, and critics treat the absence of any instrument as the defect. **This is normative, not evidentiary — there is no dispute about what exists.**

## A10 — Magisterial inquiry outcomes

**What is not measured:** the findings of magisterial inquiries into contested killings in J&K.

**Why.** The Hyderpora inquiry, ordered by the Lieutenant Governor on 18 November 2021 with a 15-day deadline, has never been published; families met the LG on 7 December 2021 and he sought two days to publish it, which lapsed. The Ganderbal inquiry of 2026, ordered with a seven-day deadline and with the Chief Minister publicly calling for publication, had not appeared weeks later. Advocacy figures put 506 inquiries since 1990 with one completed and that report never submitted — a claimed figure with a named source, from an SHRC petition, not a government return.

**STATED REASON: none. The Lieutenant Governor undertook to publish and did not.**

**Classification: `withheld`, for Hyderpora specifically.** The test is met: the report exists (an inquiry officer was appointed and reported), publication was specifically requested by the families and publicly promised, and it was not released. That is an identifiable refusal by conduct. **For the general 506 figure the classification is weaker and should be `not-published`**, because no specific request-and-refusal is documented for the others.

**Route.** An RTI to the District Magistrate, Srinagar, for the report of the magisterial inquiry into the Hyderpora encounter of 15 November 2021, conducted by the Additional District Magistrate. A parallel RTI to the District Magistrate, Ganderbal, for the 2026 inquiry report. Both are specific, addressed to the office that holds the document, and name the inquiry.

## A11 — AFSPA in J&K, in the flagship instrument

**What is not measured:** the extent and operation of the Armed Forces (Special Powers) (Jammu and Kashmir) Act, 1990.

**Why.** The 1990 Act is never named in any MHA Annual Report retrieved, and no disturbed-area statement for J&K appears in any of them — while AFSPA 1958 in the North East is reported every year at district granularity as a measurable achievement (part 09, PC-13). The J&K chapter of AR 2024-25 contains zero occurrences of "human right", "NHRC", "custody", "compensation", "ex-gratia", "inquiry" or "magisterial" across roughly 46,000 characters.

**STATED REASON: none.**

**Classification: `not-published`.** MHA administers the Act; the disturbed-area notifications are its own instruments.

**Route.** A parliamentary question to MHA for the areas of J&K currently notified as disturbed under the AFSPA (J&K) Act 1990, with the dates of the notifications in force — exactly parallel to the information MHA volunteers annually for the North East, which makes the asymmetry the framing of the question.
