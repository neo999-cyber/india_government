# 07 — PELLET AND CROWD-CONTROL INJURIES: quantity (h)

This part carries quantity **(h) pellet-gun and crowd-control injuries and blindings** for the source-instrument map (part 00, §3), which defers to it, and supplies the pellet absences that part 02's closing section defers to it. It is written in the same structure as part 00 §3 and part 09, so that it can be read as a continuation of both.

**Retrieval status of this part: unusually good on the state's side, and that is the finding.** Two MHA parliamentary replies and a High Court judgment were retrieved as primary documents, and between them they settle the instrument question. What is RELAYED is almost entirely the *victims'* side of the ledger — the injury aggregates, the hospital figures, the blinding counts. That asymmetry is not an accident of this session; it is the shape of the record.

**Evidence conventions as in part 00.** RETRIEVED means the document was downloaded and read, URL given. RELAYED means known only through a press account or third party. The grade attaches to the document held, not to the institution the subject belongs to.

**Standing cross-reference, not re-derived here.** The string "pellet" occurs **zero times** in every MHA Annual Report from 2013-14 to 2024-25, as do "PAVA" and "shotgun" (one incidental use). That is part 09's PC-7 and part 00 §PENDING, and it is the flagship instrument's silence, established there. This part establishes what happens *outside* the flagship.

## 1. THE INSTRUMENT QUESTION — is any pellet or crowd-control injury figure officially counted, and by whom?

Worked body by body, in the order the operator set.

### 1.1 MHA — the ministry counts the deaths and refuses the injuries, in one document, on one day

**RETRIEVED, and this is the central document of this part.**

Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 511, answered **7 February 2018**, subject verbatim "PROTESTERS KILLED BY PELLET GUNS IN JAMMU AND KASHMIR", asked by Prof. M. V. Rajeev Gowda, answered by MoS Hansraj Gangaram Ahir.
→ `https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2018-pdfs/rs-07022018-ENG/511.pdf`

The question asked, verbatim:

> "(a) the number of protesters and bystanders **injured or killed** by pellet guns in Jammu and Kashmir each month during last three years;
> (b) the number of security personnel injured or killed in protests each month during the above period;
> (c) the number of pellets and Pelargonic Acid Vanillyl Amide (PAVA) shells used each month during the said period;"

The answer, verbatim and in full for (a) to (c):

> "The number of protestors/ persons and the security force personnel **killed** in protests by pellets guns during last three years, month-wise, are at Annex-I. **Further details in the matter cannot be disclosed in the interest of the national security.**"

Annex-I is a month-by-month table for calendar 2015, 2016 and 2017 with two columns, headed verbatim:

> "No. of protestors/ persons killed by pellets | No. of Security Force personnel killed during Law & Order protests"

Every month of 2015 is a dash. 2016: July 3, August 5, September 4, October 1. 2017: April 1, June 1, August 2. **Total 17 and 2.** Security-force column: 2 in July 2016, nothing else.

**Four findings ride on this one reply, and they are load-bearing for the whole part.**

1. **Pellet *deaths* are officially counted, month-wise, and published.** This is the only pellet quantity of any kind that the Union government has published as a series. It is small, it is dated, and it is retrievable.
2. **Pellet *injuries* are refused, explicitly, with a stated reason.** The question asked for injured *or* killed; the answer supplied killed only and then closed the door with a named exemption. **This is an identifiable refusal with an identifiable refuser, date, questioner and stated ground — the `withheld` test met exactly** (see A12 below).
3. **The quantity of munitions fired is refused on the same ground.** Sub-question (c) is answered by the same sentence (A13).
4. **The 17 is widely mis-described in circulation.** The table's window is January 2015 to December 2017. Human Rights Watch renders the figure as "2015–2017" (correct as to the table's window); other accounts render it as "July 2016 – August 2017" (correct as to where the deaths actually fall) and one as "13 killed in 2016". **The MHA table itself is unambiguous; the secondary renderings are not.** Any authored figure must cite the table's own window.

**A companion reply the same day, also RETRIEVED.** Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 512, 7 February 2018, subject verbatim "PEOPLE KILLED BY PELLETS IN J&K", asked by Shri Husain Dalwai.
→ `https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2018-pdfs/rs-07022018-ENG/512.pdf`

It asked whether tests were conducted on the effects of the pellet-firing shotgun. Answer, verbatim:

> "Tests of Pellets Gun including the impact of lead-based pellets were conducted in TBRL Chandigarh. Details regarding procurement processes and the authorities responsible for testing and selection of the metal pellets and the details of the metal used to make the pellets used in these shotguns **cannot be disclosed in the interest of the national security**."

> "The test trial of munitions was conducted by TBRL Chandigarh on Gelatin blocks prepared from the ballistic grade transparent Gelatin material that react to bullets in a manner similar to human tissues as regards elasticity, capacity to absorb, energy, strength etc."

So the weapon was ballistically tested; the test results, the metal composition and the procurement chain are all withheld under the same formula. **Three distinct quantities refused in two replies on one afternoon, all under one exemption.**

**The other MHA instrument, and why it will mislead anyone who finds it.** MHA *does* publish a column called "Civilians Injured" — once, in Rajya Sabha Unstarred Q.2670 of 10 August 2016 (RETRIEVED; established in part 09, PC-8). Its introductory sentence bounds it verbatim to "the number of security personnel/ civilians killed/injured **in these incidents**", the incidents being terrorist incidents. Its value for 2016 up to 31 July is **12**.

That is five weeks into the unrest in which the state's own later count puts pellet injuries in the thousands. **The number is not wrong; it is a different quantity.** It measures civilians injured in terrorist incidents. Crowd-control injuries are outside it by construction, and nothing on the face of the table says so. This is the mechanism of part 09's PC-11 operating on the injury series rather than the death series.

**Negative results, established rather than assumed.** A search of MHA's own document store returns no SOP, guideline or specification for the pellet-firing shotgun. A PIB search for pellet-gun or PAVA material returns nothing on this subject. **No MHA publication of any injury figure was located for any year.**

### 1.2 The J&K government and the J&K Legislative Assembly — the only aggregate injury figure any government has ever given, and the route that produced it is closed

The figures: in a written reply to a National Conference MLA in the J&K Legislative Assembly, Chief Minister Mehbooba Mufti stated that in the period **8 July 2016 to 27 February 2017**, in the Kashmir division, **51 people were killed**; **9,042 people were injured**; of those **6,221 were injured by pellets**; **782 suffered eye injuries**; and **510 were hospitalised**. Reported 12 January 2018.

**RELAYED, and the caution part 10 attaches to it stands unchanged: I did not retrieve the assembly record, `jklegislativeassembly.nic.in` was unreachable (part 11), and the counting basis — whether the count is of admissions, of persons, or of treatments — is not established.** Do not launder any of these into a clean number.

**Two divergences inside the relayed renderings of this single answer, and they matter more than they look.**

- **Eye injuries are rendered as 782 by some accounts and 728 by others.** Both are attributed to the same assembly reply. One of them is a transposition and I cannot establish which. **Neither figure should be authored.**
- The sub-figure **54** appears in press renderings of this reply as the number who "suffered some form of visual impairment", and separately in press renderings of the J&K State Human Rights Commission's material. It also appears, RETRIEVED, in the High Court judgment as the number of persons paid ex-gratia for **permanent disablement** (§1.5 below). **Three predicates, one integer. I cannot establish that they are the same 54 and I do not assert it.** The coincidence is recorded so that a later reader does not silently treat one as corroborating another.

**Part 10 already establishes what happened to this route.** The pellet-injury figure exists in the public record because an MLA asked and a Chief Minister answered in writing. The assembly was in suspended animation from 20 June 2018, dissolved 21 November 2018, and the assembly restored in October 2024 is barred by s.32(1) of the Reorganisation Act from Police and Public Order. **This quantity's only legislative route terminated in 2018 and did not resume.** It is not a gap.

### 1.3 SMHS Hospital and Government Medical College Srinagar — figures exist, reach the public through doctors and journals, and were never published as a government dataset

This is the question the operator posed most sharply, and the answer is clean.

**No hospital or health-department pellet-injury dataset has ever been published by the holder.** What exists is of three kinds, and they must not be run together.

**(a) Peer-reviewed clinical series — RETRIEVED at abstract level, and these carry the only stated definitions in the whole subject.**

- Wei X, Ang BCH, Nazir A, Shah FQ, Qureshi T, Jain A, Natarajan S, "A series of 777 pellet gun ocular injuries over a 4-month period in Kashmir", *Indian Journal of Ophthalmology* 2022 Oct;70(10):3465-3469, DOI 10.4103/ijo.IJO_721_22 → `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36190028/`. Retrospective; **777 patients** admitted to a tertiary hospital in Srinagar **between July and November 2016**. Verbatim: "76.3% of the eyes had open globe injury"; "Final best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) after treatment was **counting fingers or worse in 82.4% of the eyes**."
- A prospective follow-up study at SMHS Hospital, Srinagar, **July 2016 to June 2018**: of 1,066 patients presenting with ocular trauma, **664 eyes of 643 patients** were included; **59.3% of eyes had visual impairment of varying degrees** and **10.8% were completely blinded**, defined as no perception of light. **RELAYED — the abstract was located through search summarisation and the paper itself was not opened.** Grade accordingly.
- A Government Medical College Srinagar psychiatry study of 380 pellet-injured patients, August 2016 to August 2018, reporting 85% with psychiatric morbidity and 79% with major depressive disorder. **RELAYED.**

**(b) Figures filed on affidavit in court and never reproduced.** See §1.5. The hospitals *did* produce their numbers, to a court, under order. The numbers are in the court record and are not in the judgment.

**(c) Figures quoted by doctors and health officials to journalists.** These circulate widely — "over 1,000 treated at SMHS in two years", "446 pellet eye injuries in a month" — and none has a retrievable basis. **Not authorable.**

**The finding, stated positively.** The most-cited source for pellet blindings in Kashmir is the ophthalmology department of a government hospital. It has never published a count. Its figures reach the public either as clinical research, where the definitions are stated but the denominator is a study population and not a census, or as remarks to reporters, where there are no definitions at all. **There is no published health-department series, and none was located for any year.**

### 1.4 The J&K State Human Rights Commission — a figure, a recommendation, and then abolition

The SHRC is reported to have put **1,726 persons injured by metal pellets in 2016**, to have categorised victims by severity (both eyes / one eye / other body parts / mental effects), and in April 2018 to have asked the government to frame a rehabilitation policy for pellet victims. **RELAYED throughout. The SHRC's own report was not retrieved.**

Note against §1.6: a separate relayed figure has the J&K government planning to rehabilitate **1,725** pellet victims. **1,725 and 1,726 are almost certainly the same list, but I did not establish that and do not assert it.**

**The instrument no longer exists.** The J&K Protection of Human Rights Act 1997 was repealed by the J&K Reorganisation Act 2019, Fifth Schedule Table-3, entry 117, on 31 October 2019, extinguishing the Commission with no savings provision (part 12, §3). **The one body that had produced a pellet-injury figure with a severity taxonomy was abolished, and its records are reported to be physically locked in its former premises.**

### 1.5 The courts — where the numbers actually are, and where they stopped

**RETRIEVED, and this is the second key document of the part.** *J&K High Court Bar Association v. Union of India & ors.*, WP(C)(PIL) no. 14/2016, High Court of Jammu and Kashmir at Srinagar, reserved 10 February 2020, **pronounced 11 March 2020**, Ali Mohammad Magrey and Dhiraj Singh Thakur JJ.
→ `https://images.assettype.com/barandbench/2020-03/3313c8f2-877c-4245-921d-1141b9c0a9b1/J_K_High_Court_Bar_Association_v_Union_of_India___Ors_pdf.pdf`

**The petitioner's figures**, as recorded by the Court, verbatim: "It is alleged that **about 4000 persons were injured and about 100 persons were blinded**. The petitioner in para 7(f) of the petition has given particulars of **46 persons** whose eyes, according to him, were damaged by pellet injuries." These are the Bar Association's pleaded figures, recorded but not adopted by the Court.

**Government figures on affidavit — these ARE government figures and they are RETRIEVED at one remove, through the judgment.**

- **Secretary to Government, Home Department**, reply affidavit: "**there is no specific policy or rule in vogue relating to compensation to the alleged victims of pellet guns**."
- **Government Order No.61-Home of 2017 dated 27.01.2017** constituted a committee headed by the Divisional Commissioner, Kashmir (with IGP Kashmir Zone, Principal GMC Srinagar, Director Health Services Kashmir, a SKIMS representative and a CID representative) "to identify the persons who were **permanently / partially disabled** during the law and order disturbances in Kashmir Valley **from 08.07.2016 to 02.11.2016**".
- The Divisional Commissioner submitted a list by communications dated **23.10.2017** and **25.01.2018**.
- Government orders dated **05.01.2018** and **26.02.2018** sanctioned **Rs 0.75 lakh to 54 persons who had suffered permanent disablement** and **Rs 0.50 lakh to 10 persons who had suffered partial disablement**.
- **Director, Health Services, Kashmir**, on affidavit in response to the Court's order of **11.05.2018**, gave "the **district-wise list of the persons who had received pellet injuries**", and "According to the statistics stated in the affidavit, **510 patients had been referred to other Hospitals in the country**."
- **Medical Superintendents, SMHS Hospital and Bone & Joint Hospital** (respondents 26 and 28) and the other hospital authorities "have filed their respective responses to the petition, **detailing out the number and the particulars of the injured persons** who had reported at their respective Hospitals during the period in question, the nature of injuries received by them, the treatment provided to them and the procedures, wherever necessary, conducted on them."

**Read those last two together and the finding is stark.** A district-wise list of persons who received pellet injuries exists, sworn to by the Director of Health Services. Hospital-wise counts with the nature of injuries exist, sworn to by Medical Superintendents. **All of it was filed in a court, and the judgment reproduces none of the numbers.** The state produced under compulsion what it refused to Parliament three months earlier — and the compelled version reached a case file rather than the public. This is the strongest `not-published` in the phase and it comes with a real route (A16).

**Note the 510 collision.** The judgment has 510 as patients *referred to other hospitals in the country*. Press renderings of the CM's assembly reply have 510 as those *hospitalised* out of 782 with eye injuries. One is RETRIEVED and one is RELAYED. **They are two different predicates on one integer and I cannot establish whether they share an origin.**

**What the Court held on the weapon itself.** The judgment reproduces paras 17-30 of the Division Bench's earlier order of **21 September 2016**, which are the operative reasoning. Verbatim:

> "It is true that so many persons were injured due to use of Pellet Guns or by use of force, some of them seriously. It is also true that **because of use of Pellet Guns even though more protests become violent the loss of life is less**, as stated in the reply filed by respondent nos. 1, 3 and 4."

> "…so long as there is violence by unruly mobs, use of force is inevitable. What kind of force has to be used at the relevant point of time or in a given situation/place, has to be decided by the persons in charge of the place where the attack is happening."

> "Having regard to the ground situation prevailing as of now and the fact that Government of India, Ministry of Home Affairs **has already constituted a Committee of Experts through its Memorandum dated 26.07.2016** for exploring other alternative to Pellet Guns, before filing the report by the Expert Committee and a decision taken at the Government level, **we are not inclined to prohibit the use of Pellet Guns in rare and extreme situations**."

**And the SOP, which is where the judgment goes quiet.** Verbatim: "the Director General of Police **on 25.10.2010** issued the SOP which was circulated amongst the lower formations… The said SOP was framed under Section 127 to 132 Cr. P. C. read with Section 24, 32 and 33 of the J&K Police Act. **Clauses A to T of the SOP read thus: … … …**" — the twenty clauses are elided in the text as published, as is the concluding private-defence passage. **The only judicial citation of the governing crowd-control SOP omits its text.** The SOP itself was not retrieved from any source.

**A flat contradiction between two official respondents in the same proceeding, and it is the sharpest factual dispute in this part.** The Court records, verbatim: "the Director General of Police in his reply has specifically stated that **the guidelines stated in the SOP are being strictly followed**." The CRPF, in its own affidavit in the same litigation, is reported to have told the Court that it was **"difficult to follow the standard operating procedure given the nature of the protests"** (RELAYED). **Two arms of the state, one court, one question, two incompatible answers.** The Court did not resolve it: "Whether at a particular place the guidelines issued in the SOP are being followed or not, can be individually decided and as in general no one can be in a position to give a finding."

**Disposal.** Prayers (a) and (b) — ban and prosecution — rejected. Prayers (iv) and (v) — hospital data and outside surgeons — held satisfied. Prayer (iii) — compensation — held satisfied by the ex-gratia payments, with the Court adding, verbatim: "strictly speaking, it is **not a case where compensation is being sought or claimed for wrong doing of any security force personnel**… but for **discharge of public duty** by such security force personnel who were being attacked by violent mobs during the relevant period." **PIL dismissed, 11 March 2020.**

**The Supreme Court leg, RELAYED throughout.** The Bar Association appealed the interim order of 21/22 September 2016. In 2017 a bench headed by CJI J. S. Khehar (with Chandrachud and Sanjay Kishan Kaul JJ) declined to ban the weapon, directed the Attorney General to consider alternatives, received an interim report **in a sealed cover**, and pressed the Bar Association to come forward with "positive suggestions" — indicating it would ask the government not to use pellet guns if there were no violence and no stone-throwing. Later a bench headed by CJI Ranjan Gogoi remitted the matter to the High Court with a direction to decide within six weeks, which produced the judgment of 11 March 2020. **No Supreme Court order banning or restricting the weapon was located, and the sealed-cover interim report of the Expert Committee has never been published.**

**And the most recent judicial position, RELAYED, 30 July 2026.** A Supreme Court bench of CJI Surya Kant with Bagchi and V. Mohana JJ, hearing a petition about alleged pellet use during the 20 July 2026 NEET protests in Delhi, **declined a blanket ban**, on the ground that such weapons cannot be prohibited while an existing Bureau of Police Research and Development advisory permits their use in exceptional crowd-control circumstances. → `https://kashmirobserver.net/2026/07/30/pellet-guns-cant-be-banned-while-advisory-permits-their-use-sc/`. **The BPRD advisory was not retrieved and its date and text are not established.**

### 1.6 The CRPF — the force that fires the weapon holds figures, disclosed some to a court, and refused them under RTI

**RELAYED throughout; the CRPF affidavit itself was not retrieved.**

To the J&K High Court in August 2016 the CRPF stated that between **8 July and 11 August 2016** — 32 days — it had used **1.3 million pellets** (through about 3,000 pellet cartridges), **8,650 tear-smoke shells** and **2,671 plastic pellets**, alongside fourteen types of "less lethal and non-lethal" munitions including oleoresin grenades, pepper balls and stun grenades. Its stated justification, verbatim as reported: **"Pellet guns were introduced in 2010 as an accepted weapon of riot control. In case this (pellet shotgun) is withdrawn, the CRPF would have no recourse in extreme situations but to open fire with rifles, which may cause more fatalities."**

**So the force keeps consumption returns.** That is decisive for absence classification: the quantity MHA refused under sub-question (c) demonstrably exists in a holder's hands and was produced once, to a court, when the disclosure served the force's own argument.

The CRPF is reported to have **refused an RTI application on its use of pellet guns in Kashmir** in January 2017. **RELAYED thinly — the article was returned by search but the page itself gave HTTP 403 and could not be read, so the exemption clause invoked is NOT established.** Do not author the exemption.

**No CRPF publication of any injury figure was located, for any year.** No annual statistical return of the force carrying crowd-control injuries was found.

### 1.7 NHRC — structurally barred from the force that fires the weapon

**No NHRC pellet-injury count was located, for any year.** Searches for NHRC cognizance, notices or reports on pellet injuries in J&K returned nothing retrievable.

The structural reason is already established in part 12, §2, and applies here with unusual force. PHRA s.2(1)(a) defines "armed forces" to include "any other armed forces of the Union", which pulls the CRPF inside s.19. Under s.19, for complaints against those forces the Commission may only seek a report from the Central Government and then drop the matter or recommend — it cannot investigate, summon, compel or itself establish a fact. **The pellet-firing shotgun in Kashmir is deployed by the CRPF and the J&K Police. NHRC's investigative machinery reaches the second and not the first.** And the 24-hour custodial-death intimation regime is addressed to District Magistrates and Superintendents of Police, so it generates no reporting duty for a CRPF sector command in any case.

Before 31 October 2019 the position was worse: the PHRA proviso to s.1(2) confined NHRC in J&K to List I and List III matters, leaving ordinary policing to the SHRC — and the SHRC, on OHCHR's account, had no jurisdiction over centrally controlled forces either (part 12, §3). **For the whole of the 2016 unrest, no human-rights body with investigative powers could reach the CRPF.**

### 1.8 NCRB, SATP, UCDP, GTD, ACLED — no crowd-control injury category exists in any of them

- **NCRB.** *Crime in India* Chapter 16A is "Custodial Crimes & Complaints Against **Police** Personnel"; the unit of account is the State/UT police (part 12, §1). There is no injury-by-crowd-control category anywhere in it, and CAPF conduct is outside its frame entirely.
- **SATP.** Its J&K datasheets count **"Incidents of Killing"** and fatalities by category (part 06b, §6). **There is no injury column of any kind, and no crowd-control category.** Its incident series does not reach stone-pelting or its policing either (part 00, §3(a)).
- **UCDP.** GED requires "at least 1 direct death" and admits only events linkable to a recognised conflict dyad (part 00, §3b). **Non-fatal injury is invisible to it by definition**, and Government-of-India one-sided violence is recorded in only three country-years ever, none after 2002.
- **GTD.** Non-state perpetrators only; state crowd-control injuries are outside by construction.
- **ACLED.** Can in principle carry protest events with state actors, but India coverage starts 1 January 2016, it defaults fatalities to 0 where the source is unclear, and it was not retrieved this session (part 00). **It is a candidate and nothing more.**

## 2. VERBATIM COUNTING DEFINITIONS, AND WHERE THE BOUNDARY IS DRAWN DIFFERENTLY

**Definitions actually stated, verbatim, by their publishers.**

| Source | Verbatim label or bound | What it gates on |
|---|---|---|
| MHA, RS US Q.511, 7 Feb 2018 | "No. of protestors/ persons killed by pellets" | **death**, caused by pellets, in a protest context |
| MHA, RS US Q.511 | "No. of Security Force personnel killed during Law & Order protests" | **death**, of a member of the forces, in a law-and-order protest — no causal agent named |
| MHA, RS US Q.2670, 10 Aug 2016 | "Civilians Injured" … "in these incidents" | injury **in a terrorist incident**. Excludes crowd control by construction |
| GO No.61-Home of 2017, via HC judgment | "persons who were **permanently / partially disabled** during the law and order disturbances in Kashmir Valley from **08.07.2016 to 02.11.2016**" | **disablement**, within a 118-day executive window, cause not restricted to pellets |
| Director Health Services Kashmir, affidavit 2018, via HC judgment | "persons who had received **pellet injuries**" (district-wise); "**referred to other Hospitals** in the country" | injury, and separately a **referral** |
| *IJO* 2022 (n=777) | "final best-corrected visual acuity … **counting fingers or worse**" | a **clinical acuity threshold** |
| SMHS prospective study (n=664 eyes) | "**completely blinded**" = "**no perception of light**"; "visual impairment of varying degrees" | two different clinical thresholds, both stated |
| J&K Assembly reply, 12 Jan 2018 (RELAYED) | "injured", "injured due to pellets", "eye injuries", "hospitalised" | four nested categories, **none defined** |
| Amnesty International, 2017 | "88 people whose **eyesight was damaged** by metal pellets … between 2014 and 2017" | damage to eyesight, **case-documented**, not a census |
| SHRC (RELAYED) | "injured by metal pellets", with severity categories both eyes / one eye / other body parts / mental | injury with a **severity taxonomy** — the only official taxonomy located |

**Now the boundary questions the operator posed, answered exactly.**

**Is the counted thing injuries, or persons injured?** Unresolved for every official figure. The assembly reply's "6,221 injured due to pellets" reads as persons; the hospital material reads as admissions and as eyes. The clinical studies are explicit — 664 **eyes** of 643 **patients**, and 21 patients had both eyes injured — and are the only sources that state which. **No official source states its unit.** A person with two injured eyes, admitted twice, referred once, is one person, two eyes, two admissions and one referral, and no official document says which of those it is counting.

**Is it eye injuries or blindings?** Distinct everywhere they appear, and the ratio between them is not stable: 782 eye injuries in the assembly reply against 54 "visual impairment"; 664 eyes in the SMHS series of which 59.3% impaired and 10.8% with no light perception; 777 patients in the *IJO* series of which 82.4% of eyes ended at counting-fingers-or-worse. **Three studies of overlapping populations at one hospital produce three incompatible impairment shares because each uses a different threshold.** That is not error; it is three different questions.

**Is "blinded" total or partial, one eye or both?** **No official instrument answers this, because no official instrument uses the word with a definition.** The clinical sources answer it and answer it differently: "no perception of light" (an absolute threshold, per eye), "counting fingers or worse" (a functional threshold, per eye), "visual impairment of varying degrees" (a band). The widely-quoted "139 blinded" carries none of these. **The government's own vocabulary — "permanent disablement", "partial disablement", "visual impairment" — has no stated threshold anywhere.**

**Do any two sources draw the line differently?** Yes, everywhere, and the divergences are written up below as candidate provenance records rather than as footnotes, per the operator's rule.

## 6. THE CONTESTED QUESTION

Both cases in their own terms, with each side's own preferred data, as parts 03 to 06 and 08 do it.

### CASE FOR — the pellet-firing shotgun as a less-lethal alternative to live fire, put as its most capable advocate would put it

The choice is not between the pellet gun and nothing. It is between the pellet gun and a rifle. **The CRPF said so on affidavit to the High Court, and nobody has met the point:** "In case this (pellet shotgun) is withdrawn, the CRPF would have no recourse in extreme situations but to open fire with rifles, which may cause more fatalities." Before 2010, that was the recourse. The weapon exists because the alternative killed people.

The comparison the case rests on is the state's own and it is stark. In the summer of 2010, over roughly four months of unrest policed largely without this weapon, **around 112 civilians died**. In the far larger and longer unrest of 2016-17 — nearly eight months, with the pellet shotgun as the primary instrument and, on the force's own account, 1.3 million pellets fired in the first 32 days alone — the J&K Chief Minister told the Assembly that **51 people were killed** in the Kashmir division. **More unrest, more force applied, fewer deaths.** The High Court found the same thing on the pleadings before it, verbatim: "because of use of Pellet Guns even though more protests become violent **the loss of life is less**".

The lethality figure is not an estimate; it is the government's own published series, month by month. **Seventeen deaths over three calendar years**, against 1.3 million pellets in one month. A weapon that fires more than a million projectiles and kills seventeen people over three years is functioning as designed. Two members of the security forces were killed in law-and-order protests in the same period, which is what the crowds were doing.

Nor was the weapon adopted casually or left unexamined. It was ballistically tested at the Terminal Ballistics Research Laboratory, Chandigarh, on ballistic-grade gelatin blocks selected to react to projectiles as human tissue does. When it became controversial, the Union government did not defend it reflexively: within **eighteen days** of the unrest beginning it constituted a seven-member Expert Committee — a joint secretary, a CRPF IG, a BSF IG, a J&K Police IPS officer, the Ordnance Factory Board's small-arms DDG, the director of TBRL and an IIT Delhi professor — which reported within five weeks. Its recommendations were accepted, and the result is an escalation ladder that MHA has stated in Parliament: **PAVA-Chilli shells and grenades, STUN-LAC shells and grenades, and tear-smoke shells are to be used to disperse violent protesters *before* the pellet gun.** The Director General of Police told the High Court that the SOP is being strictly followed.

Two independent judicial forums have examined the demand for a ban on full argument and refused it — the High Court in 2016 and again finally in 2020, and the Supreme Court in 2017 and again, most recently, on **30 July 2026**, which declined a blanket ban while a police-research advisory permits use in exceptional circumstances. On the compensation limb the state acted without being ordered to: a committee under the Divisional Commissioner identified the disabled, and ex-gratia was sanctioned. The Court's conclusion, verbatim, was that this is "not a case where compensation is being sought or claimed for wrong doing of any security force personnel… but for **discharge of public duty** by such security force personnel who were being attacked by violent mobs".

### CASE AGAINST — the pellet-firing shotgun as an indiscriminate weapon producing mass blinding of bystanders, in its own terms

The weapon cannot be aimed. A single cartridge releases between 360 and 600 metal pellets which scatter in unpredictable directions; targeted firing is functionally impossible. That is not a critics' characterisation, it is the mechanism. The most a senior officer on the government's own Expert Committee would claim is that **around 90% of pellets would strike below the waist** — which concedes that roughly one in ten does not, at a rate of 1.3 million pellets in 32 days. The eyes are what is above the waist.

The consequence is measurable and it has been measured, by government doctors, in peer-reviewed journals, using stated definitions — which is more than any government count offers. In one Srinagar hospital, in four months of 2016, **777 patients** were admitted with pellet-related ocular injuries; **76.3% of eyes had open-globe injury**; and after expeditious surgery, most of it on the day of admission, **final best-corrected visual acuity was counting fingers or worse in 82.4% of eyes**. In the prospective SMHS series, **10.8% of eyes had no perception of light at all**. A separate GMC Srinagar study found **85% of pellet-injured patients had psychiatric morbidity**. The mean age in both ophthalmic series is about 22.

The injured are not, or not only, the people the weapon is aimed at, because it is not aimed. Amnesty International documented 88 people whose eyesight was damaged between 2014 and 2017, of whom **14 were women hit while inside their own homes**. The Bar Association's petition named 46 individuals and pleaded about 4,000 injured and about 100 blinded. The Chief Minister's own answer to the Assembly put **6,221 injured by pellets and 782 with eye injuries** in under eight months.

And "non-lethal" is the state's own word for a weapon that, on the state's own count, **killed seventeen people**. The trade-off argument requires knowing how many deaths were avoided. **Nobody has measured that, ever.** What is measured is the harm — and the ministry that publishes the deaths refused to publish the harm, on national-security grounds, twenty-six days after its own state government had published it in a legislature.

That refusal is the heart of it. The state has arranged the accounting so that the benefit side of its own trade-off is asserted and the cost side is unavailable: the deaths are counted month by month; the injuries are withheld from Parliament, filed in a court and left there; the blindings are never defined; the munitions expended are a security secret; the Expert Committee's report, which supposedly restricts the weapon to rare cases, has never been published, so nobody outside the government knows what the rule actually is. The two bodies that might have produced an independent count are gone — the State Human Rights Commission abolished on 31 October 2019, and the Assembly that extracted the only aggregate figure barred since 2019 from asking about police and public order. The one human-rights body that survives may not investigate the CRPF at all.

Finally, on compliance: the escalation ladder exists on paper and the CRPF told the same High Court, in the same litigation in which the DGP said the SOP was being strictly followed, that it was **difficult to follow the SOP given the nature of the protests**. The Court declined to decide between them. Sixteen years on, the SOP's twenty clauses have never been published in full, and in July 2026 the Supreme Court refused a ban partly because an advisory nobody has seen permits the weapon's use.

### DIFFERENT FACTS OR DIFFERENT WEIGHTINGS?

**Split, and the split is not the usual one. The core of this dispute is different *facts*, and the reason is that the two sides' key quantities are not the same quantity and neither is measured on a stated basis.**

**Different weightings, on facts both sides accept.** Both accept: 17 deaths on MHA's month-wise table; the weapon was introduced in 2010; 1.3 million pellets in 32 days; the weapon was tested at TBRL; an Expert Committee was constituted on 26.07.2016 and reported; an escalation ladder is stated in Parliament; two courts declined to ban it; ex-gratia was paid to 64 people. **On these, the disagreement is genuinely one of weighting** — whether seventeen deaths from a weapon called non-lethal is a vindication or an indictment; whether 1.3 million projectiles in a month is evidence of restraint per projectile or of saturation. Neither reading is compelled.

**Different facts, and this is where the weight of the dispute actually sits.**

1. **The counterfactual.** The security case's load-bearing quantity is *deaths avoided by substituting the shotgun for the rifle*. **No instrument of any provenance has ever measured it.** The 2010-versus-2016 comparison is the closest thing to evidence and it is not clean: 112 deaths over four months in 2010 against 51 over nearly eight months in 2016-17 compares different windows, different unrest intensities, different deployments, and two figures neither of which has an established basis (L-0003 cites SATP at T4; the 51 is a relayed assembly answer). **The advocate's central number is unmeasured; the critic's central number is withheld. This is not one dataset weighted two ways.**
2. **Whether the SOP was followed.** The DGP told the High Court it was being strictly followed (RETRIEVED). The CRPF told the same Court it was difficult to follow (RELAYED). **These are incompatible factual assertions by two official respondents in one proceeding, and no forum has resolved them.** Record as unresolved fact; do not treat the DGP's statement as settling it merely because it is the better-graded document.
3. **Who was hit.** The security case's premise is that those hit were participants in violent mobs; the critics' evidence includes documented cases of women hit inside their homes and a mean victim age of about 22. **Both cannot be generally true, and the district-wise list that would bear on it is the document in A16.**
4. **What the rule actually is.** The government says the weapon is restricted; the restriction lives in an unpublished report. **Whether a "rarest of rare" rule exists as an instrument is a question of fact that could not be established.**

**The finding that should govern stage 3's treatment of this quantity.** The pellet dispute is usually presented as a values disagreement about proportionality. **It is not, or not mainly.** It is a disagreement in which the state holds the measurements for both sides of its own trade-off, publishes the term favourable to it, refuses the term unfavourable to it, and has never generated the counterfactual on which its case depends. **A record that classes this as `differentFacts: false` would be wrong, and one that classes it as a simple factual dispute between two measured claims would also be wrong. The accurate statement is that the facts differ because the measurement of one side of the comparison does not exist and the other side's was refused.**
