# 01 — PERIODISATION: where the counting basis changed

Rule being applied: a series is never spliced across a definitional change. Every break below is dated, the change in definition is stated, and the series that must be broken there is named. Where I could not establish whether a change occurred, that is recorded as an unestablished break rather than left blank.

**Retrieval status of this part: almost entirely PRIMARY.** All MHA Annual Report material below was retrieved directly as PDF from `mha.gov.in` and text-extracted; the parliamentary reply was retrieved directly as PDF. The exceptions are marked RELAYED inline.

## BREAK 1 — MHA "Incidents" restated downward for 2017, 2018, 2019
**Date of the change: the Annual Report 2021-22, posted 24 November 2022.**

First, the series as published contemporaneously, year by year, from the reports themselves. I retrieved AR 2013-14, 2014-15, 2015-16, 2016-17, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2019-20, 2020-21 and read each J&K table directly.

| Year | Value as first published | Reports carrying it |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 340 | AR 2013-14, 2014-15, 2015-16 |
| 2012 | 220 | AR 2013-14 → 2015-16 |
| 2013 | 170 | AR 2013-14 → 2017-18 |
| 2014 | 222 | AR 2014-15 → 2020-21 |
| 2015 | 208 | AR 2015-16 → 2020-21 |
| 2016 | 322 | AR 2016-17 → 2020-21 |
| 2017 | 342 | AR 2017-18 → 2020-21 |
| 2018 | 614 | AR 2018-19 → 2020-21 |
| 2019 | 594 | AR 2019-20, 2020-21 |
| 2020 | 244 | AR 2020-21 |

Then the restatement. Same series, same column name, different values, published one report apart:

| Year | AR 2020-21 (posted 22 Apr 2022) | AR 2021-22 (posted 24 Nov 2022) |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 342 | 279 |
| 2018 | 614 | 417 |
| 2019 | 594 | 255 |
| 2020 | 244 | 244 |

**And a trap that follows from the two tables together.** AR 2021-22 does not restate 2014, 2015 or 2016 — it *drops* them, starting its window at 2017. So the values 222 / 208 / 322 for 2014-2016 stand unrevised on the old basis, while 2017 onward stands on the new one. Anyone assembling a 2014-2024 MHA incident series from the Annual Reports — which is the obvious thing to do, and which several secondary compilations have done — **splices two incompatible bases between 2016 and 2017 with nothing on the page to warn them.** The seam is invisible because it falls in a gap rather than at a restated value.

Both tables carry the identical heading structure — "Year | Incidents | SFs killed | Civilians killed | Terrorists killed" — and both are introduced with the identical sentence form, "The trends of terrorist violence in J&K during the last few years are shown in the table given below". **No footnote, no note, and no explanatory sentence accompanies the restatement in either report.** I read the surrounding paragraphs in both; there is nothing.

WHAT CHANGED: unestablished. The 2020 figure is identical across both bases while 2018 falls by 197 and 2019 by 339, which rules out a uniform rescaling and points to a category being removed that was large in 2018-19 and negligible in 2020. **I could not establish from any MHA document what the 614 and 594 figures counted.** The obvious candidate — that the earlier series included law-and-order/stone-pelting incidents, which peaked in 2016-2019 and collapsed after August 2019 — is a hypothesis I could not confirm, because MHA publishes no law-and-order incident table for J&K in any report year (see part 02).

WHICH SERIES MUST BREAK HERE: any `jk-terrorist-incidents` series. The 2014-2019 values as published contemporaneously (222, 208, 322, 342, 614, 594) and the values for the same years as published from AR 2021-22 onward are **not the same series** and must not share an axis. This is a straight P-01-shaped case.

STATUS OF THE PRE-RESTATEMENT FIGURES: they are official, were published by MHA, and their basis cannot now be established. Per the evidence rule they must be carried as basis-unestablished, not quietly dropped in favour of the restated series. Dropping them would be the instrument silently adopting the state's later revision.

## BREAK 2 — "Incidents" split into two columns, and "Civilians killed" redefined
**Date of the change: the Annual Report 2022-23, posted 11 October 2023.**

The four-column table becomes a five-column table. Column headings verbatim from AR 2022-23:

> "Year | Terrorist Initiated Incidents | Encounters/Counter Terror operations | Security Forces (SFs) killed | Civilians killed | Terrorists killed"

and from AR 2023-24 and AR 2024-25, abbreviated:

> "Year | Terrorist Initiated Incidents (TII) | Encounters/CT Ops. | Security Forces killed | *Civilians killed | Terrorists killed"

Two distinct things changed in this one table.

### 2a. The incident count was split — and the split reveals what the old total was
Values from AR 2022-23 onward, checked against the restated single-column series in AR 2021-22 and against the parliamentary reply of 20 July 2022:

| Year | Terrorist Initiated Incidents | Encounters/CT Ops | Sum | "Incidents" per AR 2021-22 / "Terrorist Incidents" per RS US Q.371 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 228 | 189 | **417** | 417 |
| 2019 | 153 | 102 | **255** | 255 |
| 2020 | 126 | 118 | **244** | 244 |
| 2021 | 129 | 100 | **229** | 229 |
| 2022 | 125 | 117 | **242** | — |
| 2023 | 46 | 48 | **94** | — |
| 2024 | 28 | 57 | **85** | — |

Four years, four exact matches. The identity is established, not inferred loosely: **MHA's headline "terrorist incidents" figure is the sum of incidents initiated by militants and operations initiated by the security forces.** The consequence for the series is in part 09; the consequence for periodisation is that the post-2022-23 "TII" column and the pre-2022-23 "Incidents" column are different quantities and cannot be joined.

WHICH SERIES MUST BREAK HERE: any series named for "terrorist incidents". Two separate series should exist from AR 2022-23 onward — militant-initiated incidents, and security-force operations — and neither continues the pre-2022-23 "Incidents" line.

### 2b. "Civilians killed" was silently rebased in AR 2022-23 and disclosed in AR 2023-24

| Year | Civilians killed, AR 2019-20 / 2020-21 / 2021-22 | Civilians killed, AR 2022-23 onward |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 39 | **55** |
| 2019 | 39 | **44** |
| 2020 | 37 | **38** |
| 2021 | 41 | 41 |

The AR 2022-23 table carries **no footnote**. The footnote appears for the first time one report later, in AR 2023-24, and is retained in AR 2024-25, verbatim:

> "*[Civilians killed includes civilians killed in terrorist-initiated incidents and civilians killed in encounter/counter Terrorism operations]"

So the definition changed in the report posted 11 October 2023 and was disclosed in the report posted 27 December 2024. **For roughly fourteen months MHA published a redefined civilian-death series under an unchanged column name with no notice of the change.** That gap is itself the finding, and it is dateable to the day at both ends.

WHICH SERIES MUST BREAK HERE: any `jk-civilians-killed` series breaks at AR 2022-23. Pre-break values count civilians killed by militants; post-break values count civilians killed by militants plus civilians killed during security-force operations, undifferentiated.

NOT ESTABLISHED, and must not be laundered: the arithmetic residual between the two bases is 16 for 2018, 5 for 2019, 1 for 2020, 0 for 2021. It is tempting to read that residual as "civilians killed in security-force operations". **I do not assert that and stage 3 must not author it as a figure.** Two reasons. First, the restatement may fold in ordinary revisions as well as the definitional change — the SF-killed figure for 2020 also moved, from 62 to 63, between the same two reports, and that cannot be a civilian-definition effect. Second, a residual of 0 for 2021 would mean no civilian died in any encounter or CT operation in J&K in 2021, which is a strong claim no document makes. The residual is a derived quantity whose basis is unestablished, and it should be recorded as such.

## BREAK 1b — two earlier, smaller restatements in the same table

Found while retrieving the contemporaneous series, and recorded because they establish that unannounced restatement is a *habit* of this table rather than a single 2022 event.

**The 2012 row was restated between AR 2013-14 and AR 2014-15.**

| Source | 2012 incidents | 2012 SFs killed | 2012 civilians killed | 2012 terrorists killed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AR 2013-14 | 220 | 15 | 15 | 72 |
| AR 2014-15 | 220 | 38 | 11 | 50 |

The AR 2013-14 value is not a transcription error on my part: that report's own prose depends on it, saying "casualties of security forces (SF) personnel have increased by 253.33% in comparison to the last year" — and 15 → 53 is exactly +253.33%. So MHA published 15, reasoned from 15, and then published 38 for the same year one report later, with no note. Terrorists killed fell 72 → 50 in the same move.

**The column names changed for one year and changed back.** AR 2015-16 alone uses "SFs who lost their lives" and "Civilians who lost their lives" where every other report uses "SFs killed" and "Civilians killed". Cosmetic, and I record it only so a later reader who meets that report does not treat it as a different quantity.

**Consequence.** The pre-2014 baseline the instrument freezes at May 2014 sits inside this: the 2012 and 2013 figures a contemporaneous reader would have seen are not the figures the later reports carry. If the baseline is to be sourced to MHA, it must be sourced to a *named report year*, not to "MHA".

## BREAK 3 — the cumulative death toll since 1990 was discontinued
**Date: the Annual Report 2021-22, posted 24 November 2022.**

Every MHA Annual Report from 2013-14 through 2020-21 opens its J&K security section with a cumulative sentence. Verbatim, AR 2020-21:

> "Since the advent of militancy in J&K in the 1990s, 14,091 Civilians and 5,356 Security Force (SF) personnel had lost their lives (upto 2020)."

Earlier values in the same sentence form: 13,880 civilians / 4,879 SF up to 31 March 2014 (AR 2013-14); 14,024 / 5,273 up to 31 March 2019 (AR 2018-19); 14,054 / 5,294 up to December 2019 (AR 2019-20).

From AR 2021-22 onward the sentence is gone. I checked all four subsequent reports for "advent of militancy", "militancy in J&K (in 1990)" and "since 1990": zero occurrences in AR 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25. The remaining "lost their lives" occurrences in those reports are all in the North East chapters.

WHAT CHANGED: a stock measure was discontinued. Note also what the sentence never contained even when it was published — **a cumulative militants-killed counterpart.** MHA's cumulative ledger ran civilians and security forces only. The series has no continuation and the last published value is 14,091 / 5,356 up to 2020.

## BREAK 4 — infiltration: the label changed four times, then the table was deleted
**Label changes 2014-15 to 2017-18; restatement in AR 2022-23; DISCONTINUED after AR 2022-23.**

The row label history, established across seventeen retrieved reports:

| Report | Row label(s) verbatim | Source note |
|---|---|---|
| 2010-11 to 2013-14 | `Total` — one unlabelled row | none |
| 2014-15 | `Total Infiltration Attempts` | **`(Source MAC)`** |
| 2015-16 | `Total infiltration attempts` + `Successful` | **`(Source MAC)`** |
| 2016-17 | `Infiltration attempts` + `Net infiltration (Estimated)` | none |
| 2017-18 to 2022-23 | `Infiltration attempts` + `Net Estimated infiltration` | none |
| **2023-24, 2024-25** | **no table — series discontinued** | — |

So the operator's question — attempts, net, or successful — is answered: **the published series is all three in sequence, and MHA has never said whether they are the same quantity.** A row called `Total` for nine years, then `Attempts`, then a `Successful` row that is renamed `Net infiltration (Estimated)` one year later and `Net Estimated infiltration` the year after. "Successful" and "net estimated" are not obviously the same thing, and no report says they are.

**Two source facts worth carrying.** The only two years MHA attributed the infiltration series to anyone attributed it to **MAC — the Multi Agency Centre, an Intelligence Bureau body** — not to the J&K Police and not to MHA's own returns. From AR 2016-17 the attribution was dropped and the series continued. No other J&K table in any year carries a source note in the Annual Report.

**The discontinuation is confirmed and dated.** I checked AR 2023-24 and AR 2024-25 directly: zero occurrences of "Infiltration attempts" or "Net Estimated infiltration" in either, and no infiltration figures anywhere in them. What survives is the policy boilerplate — AR 2024-25 para 15.7 still describes the "multi-pronged approach to contain cross border infiltration", fencing, deployment and surveillance. **The sentence about managing infiltration outlived the measurement of it by two report years and counting.**

"Net Estimated" is labelled an estimate; "Infiltration attempts" is not, and MHA nowhere states how an attempt is detected or counted.

The 2019 net-infiltration figure was restated from 138 to 141 in AR 2022-23 with no note. Small, but it is the same unannounced-restatement pattern as breaks 1 and 2 and belongs in the record.

Also note the table's window shortens: AR 2018-19 through 2020-21 run the series from 2014; AR 2021-22 and 2022-23 start it at 2017. The earlier years are not restated, they are dropped from view. A reader of the current report cannot see 2014-2016 infiltration at all.

WHICH SERIES MUST BREAK: `jk-infiltration-attempts` and `jk-net-infiltration` are two separate series and must never be rendered as one line. The 2019 net value carries a restatement. Both series **terminate at 2022** — there is no MHA infiltration figure for 2023 or later. That termination is an absence, not a data gap, and must render as one.

## BREAK 4b — the statistics changed chapters and changed owners at the reorganisation
**Date: the Annual Report 2019-20.**

J&K security statistics sat in **Chapter II, "Internal Security"** from at least AR 2008-09 through AR 2018-19, alongside the Left Wing Extremism and North East series. From AR 2019-20 they move to a dedicated territorial chapter, "Jammu, Kashmir and Ladakh Affairs", and have stayed there.

The owning department was renamed and rescoped in the same move. AR 2019-20 para 1.19, verbatim:

> "After the erstwhile State of Jammu & Kashmir was reorganized into the Union Territory (UT) of Jammu and Kashmir and Union Territory of Ladakh as per the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, the Department of Jammu and Kashmir Affairs has also been restructured and renamed as the Department of Jammu, Kashmir and Ladakh Affairs."

and para 1.20, verbatim:

> "The Department of Jammu, Kashmir and Ladakh Affairs deals with all matters relating to the UT of Jammu and Kashmir & UT of Ladakh, including Counter-terrorism"

By AR 2023-24 the same department's remit had widened again, verbatim: "…relating to the UT of Jammu and Kashmir, UT of Ladakh & Ayodhya, including Counter-terrorism within Jammu and Kashmir".

WHY THIS IS A BREAK AND NOT TRIVIA: the figures are no longer produced or presented by the division that produces India's other internal-security series, and are no longer printed beside them. A comparison of J&K against LWE or the North East across 2019 is a comparison across a change in producing unit.

**A related retroactivity, unresolved.** From AR 2022-23 the table is headed "trends of terrorist violence in **the UT of Jammu & Kashmir**" while carrying rows for 2018 and 2019 — years in which no such UT existed and in which the reported territory included Ladakh. **MHA has never stated whether the pre-2019 rows were re-cut to exclude Ladakh or simply relabelled.** Unestablished, and material: if they were merely relabelled, the series changes territorial coverage mid-stream without a seam.

## BREAK 5 — the reporting chain changed on 31 October 2019
**Date: 31 October 2019, the appointed day under the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019.**

WHAT CHANGED, structurally: before that date these figures were produced by a state police force answerable to a state Home Department under a Chief Minister answerable to a sitting legislature. After it, "Police" and "Public Order" ceased to be within the legislative competence of the J&K legislature (s.32(1) of the Act) and the police reporting line runs to the Lieutenant Governor and thence to MHA. The producer of the numbers and the consumer of the numbers became the same ministry.

This is a break in *answerability*, not visibly a break in *arithmetic* — MHA's tables run continuously across 31 October 2019 with no seam. That is precisely why it needs recording: the instrument shows no discontinuity at the point where the independent check on it was removed. See part 10 for the evidence on answerability, including the two identifiable refusals in the restored assembly.

WHICH SERIES MUST BREAK: none on arithmetic grounds. This is a break to carry as a provenance record attached to every J&K security series rather than as a seam in any one of them. Stage 3 should resist the temptation to draw a seam here — a seam asserts an arithmetic discontinuity I have not established.

**NOT ESTABLISHED:** whether the underlying collection instrument at police-station level changed at reorganisation — i.e. whether the FIR-to-return pipeline that produces "terrorist initiated incidents" was altered. MHA's tables are continuous across the date, which is consistent either with no change or with a change absorbed silently. I found no document either way. This is an unestablished break and should be recorded as one.

## BREAK 6 — the J&K legislature ceased to sit, 2018-2024
**Dates: assembly kept in suspended animation from 20 June 2018; dissolved 21 November 2018; President's Rule from 20 December 2018; President's Rule revoked 13 October 2024; results of the first UT assembly election declared 8 October 2024.**

The revocation date is confirmed from a primary source — MHA Annual Report 2024-25, para 15.5, verbatim:

> "In exercise of the powers conferred by section 73 of the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganization Act, 2019 (34 of 2019) read with articles 239 and 239A of the Constitution of India, the President's Rule which was imposed in UT of J&K vide notification dated 31.10.2019 has been revoked by the President of India vide S.O.4484(E) dated 13.10.2024."

The June/November/December 2018 dates are RELAYED (a PIB release and press accounts; I did not retrieve the proclamations themselves).

WHAT THIS DID TO THE MEASUREMENT: quantities that had been answerable on the floor of the J&K assembly stopped being answerable there for six years. Two are documented in part 10 with specific pre-2018 assembly answers — pellet injuries, and year-wise local recruitment into militancy. Both were given by the Chief Minister in the assembly; both have no assembly route in J&K today. This is a break in the *set of instruments in existence*, and it bites hardest on exactly the quantities MHA does not tabulate.

WHICH SERIES MUST BREAK: any series sourced to J&K assembly replies terminates in 2018 and does not resume in 2024, because the restored assembly is barred from the subject matter (part 10). This is a termination, not a gap.

## BREAK 7 — ceasefire violations: four instrument changes at one point
**Date: between the MHA reply of 3 August 2021 and the MoD reply of 11 August 2023.**

This resolves the operator's question about whether ceasefire-violation counts are on a stable basis either side of the 25 February 2021 DGMO understanding. **They are not, and the instability is not one change but four simultaneous ones.**

| | MHA to Lok Sabha, 3 Aug 2021 | MoD to Lok Sabha, 11 Aug 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| Respondent | Ministry of Home Affairs | Ministry of Defence |
| Geography | LoC **and** International Border | "along the Line of Control (LoC)" only |
| Period basis | month-by-month, four years wide | cumulative, no year attribution |
| Unit | integer series — 2,140 / 3,479 / 5,133 / 664 | a prose clause: "just three incidents of ceasefire violations" |

Because MHA counts the BSF-held International Border and MoD counts the Army-held LoC, **the post-2021 statement makes no claim about the International Border at all.**

WHICH SERIES MUST BREAK: any `jk-ceasefire-violations` series breaks here, and arguably should not be built at all across the boundary. **A fall from 5,133 to "three" is not an observation about the LoC; it is an observation about a replaced instrument.**

FURTHER INSTABILITY BEFORE THE BREAK, which means the pre-2021 series is not clean either:
- There is **no agreed definition of one violation**. The operative unit is sector × firing-episode with episodes separated by roughly two to four hours, adjudicated by the local commander. RELAYED, from a former GOC 15 Corps.
- An earlier Indian rule counted only firing "for effect" and excluded "ineffective" firing. **If that filter was relaxed, part of the 2013→2017 rise is a rise in what got logged. The Army instruction could not be retrieved and this is an UNESTABLISHED break inside the pre-2021 series.**
- Official Indian counts of 2017 contradict each other: 881 (Government of India to Lok Sabha, LoC+IB, of which 771 LoC) against MoD's own 860 for the LoC alone.

NO SERIES OF ANY PROVENANCE CONTINUES: SATP's own ceasefire series stops at 31 December 2017 and its relay of official CFV data is frozen at "Data till February 29, 2020". **There is no continuously-maintained ceasefire-violation series after 2021 from any source.**

## BREAK 8 — SATP abandoned the local/foreign militant split in 2001
**Date: 15 November 2001.**

SATP's table "Local and Foreign Terrorists Killed in Jammu and Kashmir" runs from 1990 and stops at 15 November 2001; the page footer reads "Copyright © 2001 SATP." Its note, verbatim: "The data includes only identified terrorists killed in J&K". The current SATP datasheet family contains **no local/foreign breakdown at all**, and no longer states the "only identified" qualifier anywhere.

MHA has **never** published the split in any Annual Report.

CONSEQUENCE: the local/foreign composition of militants killed, for the entire post-2001 period, exists only as unsourced official assertions in press conferences. **No series can be built. The widely-quoted "42 of 68 foreign in 2024" has no retrievable basis and must not be authored as a figure.**

**Two coverage-start traps in the other direction.** ACLED's India coverage begins 1 January 2016 while its Pakistan coverage begins 2010 — a "Kashmir region" pull spanning 2010-2015 is a Pakistan-side-only panel. And SATP's J&K base year 2000 is a partial year, starting 6 March.

**A version trap that is not a date at all.** UCDP v25.1 ends the India-Pakistan Kashmir conflict at 2020; v26.1 records it active in 2025. Same conflict id, same publisher, two answers. ACLED states verbatim that "all fatality figures are revised and corrected — upward or downward … months or years after an event has taken place." **Dataset version and pull date are part of the observation.** A 2024 pull and a 2026 pull of "the same" series are two series and must be stored as such — which is the existing P-09 vintage discipline, applying here with unusual force.

## Candidate breaks I could NOT establish

Recorded as unestablished, per the rule that an unestablished break is a finding.

- ~~**Ceasefire violations either side of the 25 February 2021 DGMO understanding.**~~ **ESTABLISHED — see break 7.**
- **Whether SATP changed its classification at any date, other than the 2001 local/foreign abandonment (break 8).** SATP publishes no methodology page, no codebook and no definitions page — the site structure was enumerated and none exists. So SATP classification changes cannot be tracked in principle, not merely in this session. **This is a permanent unestablishable, not a retrieval gap**, and it is the strongest argument for grading every SATP figure conservatively.
- ~~**Whether "Left Wing Extremism", "North East" and "J&K" tables were restructured together.**~~ **ESTABLISHED — the J&K restructuring is specific to J&K.** I ran this check. The Left Wing Extremism violence table keeps an unchanged header across AR 2020-21, AR 2021-22 and AR 2022-23 — "Years | Incidents | Extremist | Extremist | SFs | Civilians | Extremist | Arms | Arms | Persons" — and is never split into militant-initiated versus security-force-initiated columns. So the AR 2022-23 split of the J&K incident count into "Terrorist Initiated Incidents" and "Encounters/CT Ops" was **not** part of a ministry-wide presentational revision applied to all internal-security theatres. It was done to the J&K table alone, in one report year. That makes break 2 a decision about J&K rather than a house-style change, and stage 3 should treat it as such.
- **Whether the pre-2021-22 "Incidents" series included law-and-order events.** See break 1. The most likely explanation of a 197/339 fall in 2018/2019 with no change in 2020, but unconfirmed.
- **Whether local/foreign militant separation started or stopped at a date.** MHA's Annual Report table has a single "Terrorists killed" column throughout, with no local/foreign split in any report year 2013-14 to 2024-25. Whether such a split was ever published in another MHA instrument is not established here.
